# How @vladtenev thinks

### Worldview
The center of his public thinking is access. From the early Robinhood years, he frames finance as a game where "the rich shouldn't get a better deal" and Robinhood's job is to serve many small customers over a few large ones (1383243228187484167, 1383243228938342404). The mission is explicit: democratize finance for all, give more people access to markets, not less (1354900965619425280, 1420872030883352579). This is not just brokerage PR; it stretches as far as private banking, advisory, and private markets (1905369015964553395, 1967569281295716404, 2026028297822588941). By the third batch, it has hardened into a political and social theory: a society of owners, not consumers, is more free, stable, and prosperous; societies fracture when too many are excluded from wealth (2065929194102456363, 2090270253888483790, 2107530981448335641). He pushes this into policy ideas like Trump Accounts, which he treats as a public-private tool to give children an ownership stake from birth (2016963751719600320, 2073453889068695799).

He thinks the settlement layer should be faster and globally accessible. T+2 is antiquated; technology is the answer, not the impediment; real-time settlement is needed (1356687248746455041, 1356687252315803648, 1795867521897115850). The future of investing is 24/7 and not tied to East Coast working hours (1508876330502303746). That view later merges with onchain markets: 24/7 markets are inevitable and will likely run on the blockchain, with settlement in minutes not days (1898178069762064668, 1988039525097173249). Tokenization is the biggest capital markets innovation in a decade and he believes it will eat the global financial system—stocks, real estate, private assets on-chain (1813311081000866005, 1896611500804198759, 2096046875485032709).

Crypto is not a side project. Early on, he highlights self-custody as the key to making crypto useful and safe (1526647871898009601). Later, DeFi and crypto become the infrastructure and backbone of the global financial system (1808560582662181160, 1945942543906271350). He argues crypto got ownership right early and the U.S. needs clear policy, not enforcement theater (1787595554538786977, 1947326228412666186, 2011622052457783432).

The most recent layer is AI and mathematical superintelligence. He says AI and crypto are the two profound technological shifts, and companies that best incorporate both will win (1824547977316512007). He repeatedly pushes formal verification and Lean proofs as the route to trustworthy AI: human proof review will become obsolete, and eventually all software and hardware will be provably correct (1992401666906509404, 2001485557613105175, 2118096448013386109). At the same time, he frames AI as an ownership problem: retail investors need access to private AI companies, or they will fear and resist AI; extreme wealth concentration is a material risk (2001782195934134614, 2016332563879502219, 2056909794972516732).

Prediction markets fit into this worldview as "truth machines"—capitalism applied to the pursuit of truth and the future of news (1895144580007526756, 1901703746033426919, 1991995565950050466).

His company values are themselves a worldview: first-principles thinking, safety first, radical customer focus, and "participation is power" (1407774343900241923, 1407774344726474753). He also says consensus is a lagging indicator, so builders should move on first principles with conviction (2018448321120174340, 2046301753273151996).

### How they reason and decide
The core method is first-principles reduction: strip complex problems to constituent bits, develop hypotheses, design experiments, debate vigorously, and change minds with evidence (1407774347402498048, 1407774348111335425, 1407774349575168006). In the 2025-26 batch, he repeats the same instruction more personally: start from first principles, test assumptions, keep going when the first answer falls apart (2046703249223495820).

He leans on historical analogy and inevitability. He uses cars versus horseless carriages, chess after Deep Blue, Sumerian tablets, and Kekulé's snake to argue adoption curves and information systems (1905778953669026023, 1971999438622413017, 1991338259864224004). He invokes 1776 to argue for broad financial participation (2057980041951363152). That is his dominant rhetorical frame: this is already happening, and the objector is behind the curve.

He also reasons like a product operator. He treats shipping velocity and execution as strategy: "we've been cooking," "the team is cranking," and the roadmap is built from customer conversations (1866207627652505745, 1903488077689176311, 1968466119234072707). Competition is a race to be won by the sharpest, most hardcore team (1933701899708215740, 1964123867502563519).

When he wants to win an argument, he enumerates reasons and pre-empts the strongest counterargument, as in the stablecoin yield thread (2035098172700467279, 2035098179876921595, 2035098183270068713). He also demands receipts: scientific rigor before declaring victory (2001830061591454199). He often frames decisions as a choice between two futures—tools-for-users versus value-capture, ownership versus spectators (2035871150841221156, 2018782718096810200).

He evaluates assets technically rather than purely narratively. The Dogecoin thread works through transaction fees, block time, throughput, and inflation rate before taking a view (1514684174002634768, 1514684177232249033, 1514684185243369486). He thinks investing must be practiced, not just studied: "investing is like playing the violin you can't just learn from a book" (1929678870095212598). More recently, he applies compounding to evaluation: process goals beat performance goals, performance goals beat outcome goals, and the edge is evaluation (2003533729080463698, 2019574512124326012).

### Risk attitude
He does not deny risk; he usually names it and then tries to build around it. In options and trading, he acknowledges steep risks and points people to educational resources (1274074334001131520, 1392947600194621440). When Robinhood launches short selling, he is explicit about unlimited risk (1972796566248530329, 1973087653810626908). In crypto, safety is a recurrent value—especially customer protection—and he warns repeatedly about misinformation, scams, and due diligence (1377737685595348992, 1590587237426946049, 910175111889563648). On market structure, he worries about systemic risk in clearinghouse deposit requirements and argues real-time settlement would reduce it (1356687252315803648, 1356687253054005251).

Later, his risk language expands from product risk to social risk. He warns that extreme economic concentration of AI wealth could trigger backlash or knee-capping of the industry (2016332563879502219). He talks about trust as the scarce asset in banking and private markets (2054334791823360303). He also acknowledges operational risk directly: his X account was socially engineered, bypassing 2FA, and he uses the incident to warn that Robinhood has not issued any coins or tokens (2080483725209649349, 2082125660294713761). In private markets, he flags that exposure differs from public equity in rights, liquidity, and pricing, and says Robinhood works to make those differences easy to understand (2047353331404964341). He also uses AI as a cautionary tale, citing Asimov's "The Feeling of Power" to warn about trading mastery for convenience (1924582379307598255).

### Favorite narratives
- **Democratizing finance** — bullish and foundational. It begins with public equity access and grows into private markets, advisory, and private AI access. Evidence: (1354900965619425280, 1383243228187484167, 1905369015964553395, 2026028297822588941, 2066505950039527712).
- **Crypto and blockchain** — bullish throughout, deepening over time. Early: self-custody as the future (1526647871898009601, 1590587236202188800). Later: crypto as global financial backbone (1808560582662181160, 1945942543906271350, 1988277941277782381).
- **Tokenization of real-world assets** — bullish and increasingly central from 2024 onward. He calls it the foundation of what's next (1813311081000866005, 1950929498356031608, 1973609608892784950, 2096046875485032709).
- **Prediction markets** — bullish and treated as a truth mechanism rather than mere gambling (1901703746033426919, 1974191753021251830, 1991995565950050466).
- **24/7 markets** — bullish. It starts as an access and scheduling argument and later becomes an onchain inevitability (1508876330502303746, 1852503247816417603, 1988039525097173249).
- **Payment for order flow** — bullish and defensive in the early period, arguing it benefits customers via better execution and zero commissions; this fades from the later agenda as crypto and tokenization take priority (1359322133919195139, 1367524416465170432, 1451588667768651777).
- **Dogecoin** — bullish early, with an unusually technical evaluation. It is prominent in 2021 but largely disappears from later notes (1368741841923760132, 1514684174002634768, 1514684185243369486).
- **Regulatory clarity for crypto** — bullish and increasingly policy-specific: from opposing regulation by enforcement to backing the CLARITY Act and stablecoin yield (1787595554538786977, 1947326228412666186, 2035098172700467279).
- **Mathematical superintelligence / formal verification** — bullish, and the defining narrative of the latest batch. He says Aristotle is solving and verifying long-open Erdős problems and predicts all code will be formally verified within two years (1800177883748671809, 1992401666906509404, 2041573811590721647, 2118096448013386109).
- **Great Wealth Transfer** — bullish in 2024-25, framing $124T moving to younger generations as Robinhood's opportunity (1824529083721724212, 1867652186047668257, 1904705891733299429).
- **Broad ownership / society of owners** — bullish and more explicit in the latest batch (2065929194102456363, 2090270253888483790).
- **Trump Accounts** — bullish, presented as giving children ownership from birth (2016963751719600320, 2073453889068695799).
- **AI job fear** — mixed. He acknowledges fear, then argues innovation creates new jobs and ownership turns anxiety into support (2002156180559573460, 2011834003444404424, 2019871947094651215).
- **Agentic trading / Robinhood Chain** — bullish and recent (2059659590745424213, 2066531788550295920, 2074646246476890610).

The change across the notes is a widening of the same access story: the early posts fight over commissions, payment for order flow, settlement, and Dogecoin; the later posts move to private markets, AI ownership, tokenization, and formal proof.

### Recurring mistakes they admit
He is not a confessional poster, but he does own specific failures. He admits he did not do well on the Putnam exam, plainly: "I did not do this well, to put it mildly" (1998943350377034124). He publicly explains the X account compromise factually, without hiding that social engineering beat 2FA, and uses it to warn users (2080483725209649349, 2082125660294713761). The HOOD Holidays giveaway appears in the notes as an apology with an upbeat pivot (2004993499289264254). Earlier, he frames operational problems as learning opportunities: incidents become reasons to improve systems continuously (1182812555267325952, 1346936063709896704, 1486090139671560198).
