# How @vladtenev thinks

## Worldview
Access is the whole game. His longest-running idea is that finance should be open to everyone, not just the wealthy. He states the mission as democratizing finance for all (post:1354900965619425280) and frames Robinhood as serving many small customers over a few large ones (post:1383243228187484167). The rich shouldn't get a better deal; the product should be built for the many (post:1383243228938342404). This is coded as a value system: "Participation is Power," "Radical Customer Focus," and "SafetyFirst" appear as company credos (post:1407774343900241923, post:1407774351160610816). By 2025-2026, the access idea hardens into a political-economic claim: broad ownership is the core social contract. People who own a stake defend institutions; exclusion breeds backlash (post:2001782195934134614, post:2065929194102456363).

He treats market access as infrastructure engineering. T+2 settlement is outdated and unsafe; he wants real-time settlement because technology is the answer, not the obstacle (post:1356687248746455041, post:1356687253054005251). Markets should not be tied to East Coast working hours; 24/7 investing is first a vision, then an inevitability (post:1508876330502303746, post:1656469408984997889). By 2024-2026, that inevitability is blockchain-shaped: 24/7 markets are expected to run on crypto rails and settle in minutes, not days (post:1898177463328686554, post:1988039525097173249). His early defense of payment for order flow comes from the same place: zero commissions and better execution for ordinary customers, with critics often misunderstanding the mechanics (post:1359322133919195139, post:1367524416465170432).

Crypto is a constant but escalating theme. Early on, he says crypto will fundamentally reorganize the financial system, and self-custody wallets are key (post:1526647871898009601, post:1590587236202188800). By 2024-2026, tokenization becomes the big one: "the biggest capital markets innovation in a decade" and the foundation of what's next (post:1813311081000866005, post:1950929498356031608). Stocks, real estate, and private assets all move on-chain (post:1973609608892784950, post:2096046875485032709). Robinhood Chain and stock tokens are not side projects; they are the future of the financial system (post:2074646246476890610).

He treats prediction markets as "truth machines"—capitalism applied to the pursuit of truth, and the future of news (post:1901703746033426919, post:1974191753021251830). Markets, in his view, are information mechanisms held together by incentives, not just places to trade (post:1991995565950050466).

From 2024 onward, he adds mathematical superintelligence. The promise is verifiably correct AI, not probabilistic LLM output: formal proofs eliminate hallucinations (post:1800177883748671809, post:1810768297098649729). By 2025-2026, he says AI is already producing genuine mathematical discovery, and formal verification with Lean removes human referees (post:1994922827208663383, post:2001485557613105175). The two big technology shifts are AI and crypto; the companies that best incorporate both win (post:1824547977316512007, post:1933701899708215740).

On AI and work, his worldview is mixed. Innovation rarely reduces work; it changes what's worth doing, and AI may spawn single-person unicorns (post:2002156180559573460, post:2033784669079277621). But he also names the fear: AI taking jobs is real, and the answer is not to stop AI but to give people a stake. Ownership turns fear into support (post:1988662096503111807, post:2019871947094651215). That is why he argues retail must get access to private AI companies before trillion-dollar IPOs; otherwise wealth concentration will trigger political backlash and "kneecap" AI (post:2016332563879502219, post:2044925534724079661).

On regulation, he wants clarity and pro-innovation policy, not regulation by enforcement. The U.S. should lead in crypto and financial innovation (post:1787595554538786977, post:1894027864799948957). Later this becomes more specific: the CLARITY Act and stablecoin yield are needed for America to lead (post:2011622052457783432, post:2035098172700467279).

## How they reason and decide
His method starts from first principles. In his own words, he reduces complex problems to constituent bits, develops hypotheses, designs experiments, debates vigorously, and changes his mind when the evidence says to (post:1407774347402498048, post:1407774348111335425, post:1407774349575168006). The compressed version is: "start from first principles. Test your assumptions. Keep going when the first answer falls apart" (post:2046703249223495820). This is not lip service. When evaluating Dogecoin, he walks through transaction fees, block time, throughput, and inflation before taking a stance (post:1514684174002634768, post:1514684177232249033, post:1514684185243369486). His sub-penny pricing posts explain market structure step by step in numbered threads rather than asserting a conclusion (post:1408089396880941061, post:1408089398432845832).

He thinks through historical analogy. He reaches for cars versus horseless carriages, chess after Deep Blue, Sumerian tablets, and Kekulé's snake to argue that the future is adoption, not resistance (post:1905778953669026023, post:1971999438622413017, post:1991338259864224004). He reads commission wars and prior tech shifts to predict how competitors will behave (post:2046327072898052197, post:2016196503644033521). New products are framed as inevitable: "it's happening," "as it was written" (post:1808560582662181160, post:1988039525097173249).

He decides by velocity and execution. Strategy is a race, and the sharpest, most hardcore team wins (post:1866207627652505745, post:1933701899708215740). His recurring status is product-speed: the team is cooking, the team is cranking, the job is not finished (post:1903488077689176311, post:1950215604117414101, post:1906617265292009648). Shipping is not a consequence of strategy; shipping is the strategy.

He reasons from incentives and consumer rationality, not ideology. When arguing for stablecoin yield or broad ownership, he asks what a consumer would choose given fees, yield, liquidity, and risk (post:2035098175661645961, post:2044970372987318726). Markets are machines for information because people with money at stake have an incentive to be right (post:1901703746033426919, post:1991995565950050466).

He demands receipts. Announcements of mathematical or scientific results should be accompanied by supporting data for review (post:2001830061591454199). That is exactly why formal verification matters to him: it converts "vibe proving" into machine-checked proof (post:2001485557613105175).

He runs on iteration and compounding. The edge is evaluation—run more experiments, pick better, repeat (post:2019574512124326012). Process goals outrank outcome goals (post:2003533729080463698). He also uses customer feedback to shape the roadmap, treating conversations with users as the best roadmap input (post:1968466119234072707, post:1895274196961521868).

## Risk attitude
He is not risk-averse in the abstract. He calls trading and investing risk a challenge to master, not a reason to stay out (post:1921284692151500972), and he says investing requires practice, not just theory (post:1929678870095212598). But his operating instinct is safety first for customers, especially in crypto and customer protection (post:1377737685595348992, post:1400967679041675268). He warns about misinformation and scams in crypto and tells people to do due diligence (post:910175111889563648, post:1590587241742880769). He is explicit that short selling involves unlimited risk when announcing the feature (post:1972796566248530329, post:1973087653810626908).

His biggest risk concern is systemic. Clearinghouse deposit requirements and slow settlement are fragility points; real-time settlement is the mitigation (post:1356687252315803648, post:1356687253754376193). He also names a human risk pattern: people tolerate slow losses to avoid a hard conversation—chronic pain versus acute pain (post:1973651755562094734).

By 2025-2026, his risk frame becomes political-economic. Extreme wealth concentration is a real, materializing risk that could trigger redistribution or a regulatory backlash against AI (post:2016332563879502219, post:2065929194102456363). He wants private-market products to be transparent about rights, liquidity, and pricing, because opacity is a trust risk (post:2047353331404964341). On stablecoin yield, he argues for consumer safety and against adding counterparty risk and opacity (post:2035098175661645961, post:2035098172700467279). He also acknowledges a personal security risk—an account compromise via social engineering—and commits to safeguards (post:2080483725209649349, post:2082125660294713761).

## Favorite narratives
- **Democratizing finance**: bullish from the earliest posts onward. The mission is constant, expanding from zero-commission access to private markets, advisory, and ownership (post:1354900965619425280, post:1383243228187484167, post:1905369015964553395, post:2026028297822588941).
- **Crypto/DeFi/tokenization**: bullish, and it has grown more specific. Early posts say crypto will reorganize finance; later posts say tokenization is the foundation and will eat the entire global financial system (post:1526647871898009601, post:1813311081000866005, post:2096046875485032709).
- **Dogecoin**: early bullish, argued on technical merits such as fees, block time, throughput, and inflation (post:1368741841923760132, post:1514684174002634768). It fades from the later main narrative as tokenization and RWAs take over; he does not renounce it, but it stops carrying his attention.
- **Payment for order flow**: bullish and defensive in the 2020-2021 period, framed as a consumer benefit that funds zero commissions (post:1359322133919195139, post:1451588667768651777). By 2024-2026, his emphasis has shifted to on-chain settlement, tokenization, and product velocity, so PFOF no longer does the narrative work it once did.
- **24/7 markets**: bullish throughout, first as a market-hours critique and later as a blockchain settlement inevitability (post:1508876330502303746, post:1898177463328686554, post:1988039525097173249).
- **Prediction markets**: bullish from launch onward, defended as truth machines and the future of news (post:1895144580007526756, post:1901703746033426919, post:1993466831252926864).
- **Mathematical superintelligence / AI**: bullish from 2024 onward. It shifts from anti-hallucination formal verification to real mathematical discovery and AI-math convergence (post:1800177883748671809, post:1810768297098649729, post:1994922827208663383).
- **Great Wealth Transfer / broad ownership**: bullish and increasingly central. A ~$124T transfer to younger generations becomes the ownership-and-AI-stakes argument (post:1824529083721724212, post:1932200828041105445, post:2065929194102456363).
- **US crypto regulation / CLARITY Act**: bullish advocacy. He wants clear rules, stablecoin yield, and American leadership (post:1787595554538786977, post:2011622052457783432, post:2035098172700467279).
- **AI job fear / job singularity**: mixed. He worries about AI eroding human mastery or taking jobs, but his prescription is ownership and access, not prohibition (post:1924582379307598255, post:2002156180559573460, post:2019871947094651215).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He has publicly owned operational and security failures. After the X account compromise, he responded seriously and promised added safeguards (post:2082125660294713761, post:2080483725209649349). For the HOOD Holidays giveaway outage, he apologized and detailed remediation, with "I did not do this well, to put it mildly" (post:2004756962890993834).

He also signals a recurring failure of focus: "It's almost always better to just finish the job" implies that the mistake is chasing new things before the current job is done (post:1906617265292009648). And he treats outages and incidents as lessons for better systems—learning from incidents and improving continuously (post:1346936063709896704, post:1486090139671560198).
