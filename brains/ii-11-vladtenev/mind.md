# How @vladtenev thinks

## Worldview

The through-line from 2012 through 2026 is democratizing finance: he does not believe access should be reserved for the wealthy or for institutions. Early on, he framed Robinhood as serving many small customers over a few large ones, saying the rich shouldn’t get a better deal (post:1383243228187484167, post:1383243228938342404, post:1383243229730983944). By 2024–2026 the mission widened from commission-free public markets into private banking, advisory, and private markets, all pitched as “for everyone” (post:1905369015964553395, post:1905829284515877309, post:1915627893226655931, post:1967569281295716404, post:1991325899200033290).

He treats technology as the answer to stale market structure, not an impediment. T+2 settlement is repeatedly called antiquated; he argues for clearinghouse reform and real-time settlement because technology makes it possible and safer (post:1356687248746455041, post:1356687249451020289, post:1356687250226991104, post:1356687250919100419, post:1356687251590184960, post:1356687252315803648, post:1356687254551322627). Payment for order flow is another fixed belief: he defends it as producing better execution and zero commissions while critics misunderstand the mechanics (post:1359322133919195139, post:1359322134930100227, post:1359322135940923393, post:1367524416465170432, post:1451588667768651777).

Crypto moved from an important asset class to the infrastructure thesis. In 2021–2022 he emphasized self-custody and wallets as key (post:1526647871898009601, post:1526648183635562498, post:1526648500188069889, post:1526648826047651840). By 2024–2026 he says tokenization is the biggest capital markets innovation in a decade and will eat stocks, real estate, and private assets on-chain (post:1813311081000866005, post:1884323609440837865, post:1896611500804198759, post:1942632139503116622, post:1973609608892784950, post:1981472545506525478, post:1990502364269429223). DeFi and crypto are called the future backbone of the global financial system (post:1808560582662181160, post:1872456775892701532, post:1894871877857710455, post:1945942543906271350, post:1988277941277782381).

Prediction markets are another core worldview: they are “truth machines,” capitalism applied to the pursuit of truth, and the future of news (post:1854261181902622994, post:1895144580007526756, post:1901703746033426919, post:1974191753021251830, post:1991995565950050466). He linked this to the 2024 election night markets and then to a broader information/truth mechanism.

The AI worldview changed from useful product to formal proof. In 2024 he announced Harmonic AI and mathematical superintelligence, arguing that formally verified mathematics could eliminate hallucinations (post:1800177883748671809, post:1800177885598343660, post:1800177887263535194, post:1800177892636393734). By 2025–2026 he says Aristotle is solving unsolved Erdős problems and that all software and hardware will eventually be formally verified and provably correct (post:1992401666906509404, post:1994922827208663383, post:2011853930968596973, post:2017326503185522912, post:2044923459399197175, post:2047504081493868930, post:2118095496430674085, post:2118096448013386109). Math converges with software: proofs become machine-checked code, and mathematics becomes collaborative like programming (post:1994963677666333024, post:2001485557613105175).

A newer but central worldview is broad ownership. He sees concentrated ownership as a societal fragility, and broad ownership as the real social contract—a society of owners rather than consumers is more stable and prosperous (post:2003150133375172900, post:2065929194102456363, post:2066505950039527712, post:2090270253888483790). He explicitly links AI backlash to people having no stake in the upside: retail exposure to private AI companies turns skeptics into defenders (post:2001782195934134614, post:2009012760194240891, post:2019871947094651215, post:2056909794972516732).

He also repeats that fears of technology ending work are historically wrong; innovation creates new kinds of work rather than ending work (post:2002156180559573460, post:2011834003444404424, post:2019871947094651215). Agentic finance is the next era: AI agents will run portfolios and spending (post:2062198005948923925, post:2066531788550295920, post:2119831112394613137).

## How they reason and decide

He describes his own method as first-principles thinking: reduce complex problems to constituent bits, develop hypotheses, design experiments, debate vigorously, and change minds when confronted with evidence (post:1407774347402498048, post:1407774348111335425, post:1407774348774035459, post:1407774349575168006). Later he compressed this into a builder’s line: consensus is a lagging indicator; builders move on first principles with conviction, not complacency (post:2018448321120174340).

His market-structure arguments are step-by-step technical threads. He walks through sub-penny pricing, settlement cycles, clearinghouse deposit mechanics, and fee structures one tweet at a time (post:1408089396880941061, post:1408089397661102087, post:1408089398432845832, post:1408089399317827591, post:1408089400055975946, post:1408089400831971330, post:1408089401649811459, post:1408089406162886662, post:1408089406930518023, post:1408089407710654471, post:1408089408469749764, post:1408089409128321026, post:1408089409891667969, post:1408089410558586880). The Dogecoin discussion is the same style: he evaluates fee, block time, throughput, and inflation rate as technical merits rather than memes (post:1514684174002634768, post:1514684177232249033, post:1514684178943524871, post:1514684180239564800, post:1514684181468495880, post:1514684182718398487, post:1514684183842471955, post:1514684185243369486, post:1514684186484883476, post:1514684187755757570, post:1514684189026631701, post:1514684190687576065).

He leans heavily on historical analogy and inevitability framing: cars versus horseless carriages, chess after Deep Blue, Sumerian tablets, Kekulé’s snake, Lem on evolution, and 1776 (post:1905778953669026023, post:1974191753021251830, post:1991338259864224004, post:1971999438622413017, post:2010035249967952039, post:2057980041951363152). This is his argumentative move: the new thing looks strange only because the present is too close.

He frames decisions as choosing between two futures—open tools versus closed labs capturing all the value—and then picks the future that distributes ownership and access (post:2035871150841221156, post:2039801627838738926). He also reasons from incentives: people defend what they own, so the solution is to give them ownership (post:2003150133375172900, post:2001782195934134614, post:2018782718096810200).

Execution speed and iteration matter enormously. He describes the strategy as shipping velocity and product execution: “we’ve been cooking,” “the team is cranking” (post:1866207627652505745, post:1903488077689176311, post:1910468832780837057, post:1950215604117414101, post:1971656850316861892). He uses customer feedback to shape the roadmap and treats the best roadmap as built from conversations (post:1968466119234072707, post:1874204170649305111, post:1895274196961521868).

Later he formalizes this as learning speed: run more experiments, pick better, repeat; evaluation is the edge (post:2019574512124326012). He prefers process goals over outcome goals (post:2003533729080463698), and he frames learning as compounding: more experiments per week is the edge (post:2019574512124326012, post:2074329947280195668). He also demands receipts and supporting data when claims of results are made (post:2001830061591454199).

## Risk attitude

His earliest risk language is education-oriented: he acknowledges shortfalls and risks in trading, especially options, and points people to educational resources (post:1274074334001131520, post:1392947600194621440, post:1392947602081992705). He treats safety as a first-class value, especially in crypto and customer protection (post:1377737685595348992, post:1400967679041675268, post:1590587237426946049).

He pays close attention to systemic risk: clearinghouse deposit requirements, margin cascades, and the need for real-time settlement to reduce counterparty and liquidity risk (post:1356687252315803648, post:1356687253054005251, post:1356687253754376193, post:1356687254551322627). He also warns about crypto scams and misinformation, advising due diligence (post:910175111889563648, post:1590587241742880769).

Later his risk talk becomes more psychological and structural. He says investing or trading risk is a challenge to master, not avoid, and that investing requires practice like the violin (post:1921284692151500972, post:1929678870095212598). When Robinhood added short selling, he was explicit that it involves unlimited risk (post:1972796566248530329, post:1973087653810626908). He described chronic pain versus acute pain: people tolerate slow losses to avoid a hard conversation (post:1973651755562094734). He also warned about AI eroding human mastery for convenience, citing Asimov’s “The Feeling of Power” (post:1924582379307598255).

In 2025–2026 the risk lens expands to society. He worries about fragility from concentrated ownership (post:2065929194102456363, post:2016332563879502219). He notes in disclosures that all investments involve risk and loss of principal is possible (post:2047461655026762103, post:2074588631457841308). He directly owns operational and security failures: the X account compromise and giveaway traffic issues are acknowledged plainly with remediation steps (post:2082125660294713761, post:2004756962890993834). He also says absence of problems may mean you are not asking enough questions (post:2017400526032576883).

## Favorite narratives

**Democratizing finance** — bullish from the first batch to the last. Early on it was about not giving the rich a better deal and serving many small customers (post:1354900965619425280, post:1383243228187484167, post:1420872030883352579, post:1420872032120676355). Later it expands to private banking, advisory, and private markets for everyone (post:1905369015964553395, post:1905829284515877309, post:1967569281295716404, post:1991325899200033290).

**Crypto and blockchain** — bullish. Early focus was self-custody and wallets (post:1365023283364589568, post:1526647871898009601, post:1590587236202188800, post:1798680590344126963). By 2024 it becomes DeFi and crypto as global financial infrastructure (post:1808560582662181160, post:1894871877857710455, post:1945942543906271350, post:1988277941277782381).

**Dogecoin** — bullish, but the reasoning is technical. He analyzed fees, block time, throughput, and inflation (post:1368741841923760132, post:1373088518700888064, post:1514684174002634768, post:1514684185243369486). This is an early example of evaluating a meme asset as infrastructure.

**24/7 markets** — bullish from before the IPO through the tokenization era. Markets should not be tied to East Coast hours; settlement should be minutes, not days (post:1508876330502303746, post:1508876332335128576, post:1656469408984997889, post:1678933596889923586, post:1798503518170763744, post:1898177463328686554, post:1898178069762064668, post:1988039525097173249).

**Payment for order flow** — bullish and defensive. He consistently argues PFOF gives better execution and zero commissions, and that critics misunderstand the mechanics (post:1359322133919195139, post:1367524416465170432, post:1451588667768651777).

**Regulatory clarity for crypto** — bullish. Early posts condemned regulation by enforcement and advocated FIT21 (post:1787595554538786977, post:1787595555981668751, post:1793376331423764653, post:1793376333273460864). Later this becomes support for the CLARITY Act and pro-innovation US policy (post:1869469327440880043, post:1872456294076215374, post:1894027864799948957, post:1945942543906271350, post:1947326228412666186, post:2011622052457783432, post:2052511267651453273, post:2090591694399914461).

**Mathematical superintelligence / AI** — bullish and fast-moving. It starts as verifiable proofs making AI trustworthy (post:1800177883748671809, post:1800177885598343660, post:1800177892636393734). Then it becomes a claim that MSI is near and will be the core of AI (post:1810768297098649729, post:1838323106743751101, post:1914489312235938004, post:1943404400711536825, post:1949979787344093495). By 2025–2026 he says Aristotle is solving unsolved Erdős problems and all code and hardware will eventually be formally verified (post:1992401666906509404, post:1994922827208663383, post:2017326503185522912, post:2044923459399197175, post:2118095496430674085, post:2118096448013386109).

**Tokenization of real-world assets** — bullish. It appears strongly in 2024 as the foundation of what’s next, then becomes the central capital-markets thesis (post:1813311081000866005, post:1896611500804198759, post:1942632139503116622, post:1973609608892784950, post:1981472545506525478, post:1990502364269429223, post:2021401110129541373, post:2040059243462537227, post:2074646246476890610, post:2096046875485032709).

**Prediction markets** — bullish. Launched as a major product and defended as truth machines and the future of news (post:1854261181902622994, post:1895144580007526756, post:1901703746033426919, post:1974191753021251830, post:1990520407238324427, post:1991995565950050466).

**Great Wealth Transfer** — bullish. He repeatedly says ~$124T is moving to younger generations and Robinhood will capture it (post:1824529083721724212, post:1828950017702539453, post:1867652186047668257, post:1904235962953286108, post:1904705891733299429, post:1932200828041105445, post:1989060437326090735).

**Broad ownership / society of owners** — a later bullish narrative. Concentrated ownership is fragile; broad ownership is the social contract (post:2065929194102456363, post:2066505950039527712, post:2074587444595560870, post:2107530981448335641).

**Private markets / Robinhood Ventures** — bullish from 2025 onward. Private markets have been closed to everyday investors too long (post:2026028297822588941, post:2026436165440778404, post:2044925534724079661, post:2046951776780439823, post:2047353335536357724, post:2052887141894799538).

**Stablecoin yield and agentic trading** — bullish. Stablecoin yield should be allowed, and agentic finance is the next era (post:2035098172700467279, post:2035098184801034642, post:2041940987912871982, post:2063368679866208664, post:2066531788550295920, post:2083311105888165900).

**AI job fears** — mixed. He acknowledges AI taking jobs as a concern, but argues innovation creates new work and ownership turns skeptics into supporters (post:2002156180559573460, post:2011834003444404424, post:2019871947094651215).

The biggest narrative changes: crypto went from asset and wallet to full on-chain capital-markets infrastructure; AI went from useful tool to formal verification and proof; prediction markets went from product launch to information/truth foundation; democratizing finance went from public-market access to private-market access and broad ownership.

Later he mocks bears and short sellers, especially around short-selling product decisions (post:1971311763972087889, post:1972796561890607591, post:1973087649603743851). He teased @vikvang1, a trivia winner, in a dry way (post:1898181150146986254, post:1902177193566073342).

In 2025–2026 he targets Bernie Sanders’ data-center moratorium push (post:2001782195934134614), Dario Amodei/Anthropic essay reasoning (post:2016332563879502219), legacy brokerages charging ETF fees (post:2046327072898052197), and legacy banks blocking stablecoin yield (post:2035098177079304309).

His mockery style is usually dry and playful, not brutal. He often frames the target as behind the curve or defending an old system, then moves to first-principles rebuttal.

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He does not often post personal trading mistakes, but he does own operational and educational shortfalls. Early on he acknowledged shortfalls and risk in options trading and pointed to education (post:1274074334001131520, post:1392947600194621440, post:1392947602081992705). He admitted platform/security failures directly: the X account compromise is explained as a serious incident with a stated fix and remediation (post:2082125660294713761), and giveaway traffic issues are acknowledged (post:2004756962890993834). His broader posture is that absence of problems may mean you are not asking enough questions (post:2017400526032576883), which functions as a standing invitation to find and own mistakes before they compound.
