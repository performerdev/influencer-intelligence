# How @vladtenev thinks

## Worldview

The through line is democratizing finance. The earliest posts already frame it as a distribution problem: the rich shouldn't get a better deal, and market access should not be reserved for a few large customers (post:1383243228187484167, post:1383243228938342404, post:1383243229730983944, post:1383243230620180486). That remains present, but it broadens over time. In the middle period he is still arguing for access to investing, crypto, and 24/7 markets (post:1354900965619425280, post:1420872032120676355). In the latest period the view becomes larger: access includes private markets, advisory, private banking, and ownership itself. He says the core social contract is broad ownership—people who own a piece of the future become its defenders (post:2003150133375172900, post:2065929194102456363, post:2066505950039527712, post:2090270253888483790). The changed emphasis is not a reversal; it is expansion from "more people can trade" to "more people should own the system itself."

Technology is how he thinks access should be built. He consistently treats markets as infrastructure problems rather than culture problems. T+2 settlement is "antiquated," and real-time settlement is a technological answer, not an impossible demand (post:1356687248746455041, post:1356687252315803648, post:1356687254551322627). Payment for order flow, he argues, benefits customers through better execution and zero commissions, and critics misunderstand the mechanics (post:1359322133919195139, post:1359322134930100227, post:1367524416465170432). The 24/7-market view also starts early and later becomes blockchain-native: trading should not be tied to East Coast working hours, and settlement should be minutes rather than days (post:1508876330502303746, post:1656469408984997889, post:1898177463328686554, post:1988039525097173249). He frames this as inevitable, not optional.

Crypto moves from a bullish theme to his central thesis about the financial system. Early he describes crypto as fundamental reorganization, with self-custody and wallets as key (post:1365023283364589568, post:1526647871898009601, post:1590587236202188800). By the middle and late periods, the argument is that tokenization is the biggest capital-markets innovation in a decade and will eat the entire global financial system—stocks, real estate, private assets, stablecoin yield, and payments all move on-chain (post:1813311081000866005, post:1884323609440837865, post:1973609608892784950, post:1990502364269429223). He names Robinhood Chain, Stock Tokens, and stablecoin yield as the concrete path (post:1940308314144883074, post:2021401110129541373, post:2035098174294303207, post:2040059243462537227). The late view is not just "crypto is future"; it is "the future of finance is onchain."

The wealth-transfer and private-market view follows from democratization. He repeatedly cites a great wealth transfer, roughly $124T, moving to younger generations, and positions Robinhood as the recipient of that flow (post:1824529083721724212, post:1867652186047668257, post:1904705891733299429, post:1989060437326090735). Private markets have been gated too long, and retail should be able to back OpenAI, Databricks, Stripe, and other private companies before the IPO (post:2026028297822588941, post:2046951776780439823, post:2055433428439568819). This becomes a public-policy style claim: if people have no stake in AI upside, they become backlash; if they own it, they defend it (post:2001782195934134614, post:2016332563879502219, post:2019871947094651215). Trump Accounts extend that to children from birth (post:2016963751719600320, post:2073453889068695799, post:2105685079221813349).

He also holds a strong view about prediction markets as "truth machines"—capitalism applied to the pursuit of truth, and the future of news (post:1854261181902622994, post:1901703746033426919, post:1974191753021251830, post:1991995565950050466). AI is the other huge theme. Early he argued Mathematical Superintelligence could eliminate hallucinations with verifiably correct outputs (post:1800177883748671809, post:1800177885598343660). Later this sharpens into formal verification and "vibe proving": AI plus Lean will make human proof review obsolete, math will look more like software, and unsolved Erdős problems will be exhausted within a couple of years (post:1992401666906509404, post:1994922827208663383, post:2001485557613105175, post:2017326503185522912). AI agents running portfolios and cards are the next consumer-finance era (post:2059659590745424213, post:2062198005948923925, post:2066531788550295920).

On work, his view is explicit: innovation creates different work, not less work, and fears of technology ending work are historically wrong (post:2002156180559573460, post:2011834003444404424). But the remedy he proposes is ownership: a society of owners, not consumers, with retail exposure to the AI upside (post:1988662096503111807, post:2019871947094651215).

## How they reason and decide

He reasons from first principles. The company values thread describes reducing complex problems to constituent bits, developing hypotheses, designing experiments, debating vigorously, and changing minds when confronted with the right evidence (post:1407774347402498048, post:1407774348111335425, post:1407774348774035459, post:1407774349575168006, post:1407774350346838017). A late autobiographical version is even more direct: start from first principles, test assumptions, keep going when the first answer falls apart (post:2046703249223495820).

He uses historical analogy and inevitability heavily. New products are framed as cars versus horseless carriages, chess after Deep Blue, Sumerian tablets, or Kekulé's snake; these analogies argue that adoption curves eventually look obvious (post:1905778953669026023, post:1974191753021251830, post:1991338259864224004). He treats consensus as a lagging indicator and says builders move on conviction (post:2018448321120174340, post:2046301753273151996).

He thinks in structural incentives. Consumers are rational, legacy institutions lobby to protect deposits, and retail lacks skin in the game. That framing drives his stablecoin-yield and private-market arguments (post:2016332563879502219, post:2035098175661645961, post:2035098177079304309). He also demands receipts: announcements of results should ship with supporting data for scrutiny (post:1995265513669472492, post:2001830061591454199).

Compounding is a core mental model for both wealth and learning. Small improvements, more experiments, better evaluation, repeat; "the edge is evaluation" (post:2019574512124326012). He prefers process goals over performance goals over outcome goals (post:2003533729080463698). Long-horizon thinking is explicit: "we're playing the long game" (post:1950929498356031608), and he admires finishing the job over shiny objects (post:1906617265292009648).

He frames competition as a race won by the sharpest, most hardcore team, and treats shipping velocity as strategy: "we've been cooking," "the team is cranking" (post:1866207627652505745, post:1903488077689176311, post:1933701899708215740, post:1950215604117414101). He also says the best roadmap is built from customer conversations and community input (post:1874204170649305111, post:1895274196961521868, post:1968466119234072707). When reasoning about AI futures, he explicitly chooses between two possible futures and picks the one that distributes value to users rather than capturing it in-house (post:2035871150841221156).

His market-structure reasoning is step-by-step. The sub-penny pricing thread and the Dogecoin technical thread both work through mechanics—fees, block time, throughput, inflation, settlement cycles—rather than vibes (post:1408089396880941061, post:1514684174002634768, post:1514684182718398487).

## Risk attitude

He does not avoid risk; he treats it as a skill to master. Investing is like playing the violin: you cannot just learn from a book, you need practice and work (post:1929678870095212598). But he is direct about structural risk. Short selling involves unlimited risk (post:1972796566248530329, post:1973087653810626908). Systemic risk from clearinghouse deposit requirements is best mitigated by real-time settlement (post:1356687252315803648, post:1356687253054005251).

Safety is an explicit core value, especially in crypto and customer protection (post:1377737685595348992, post:1400967679041675268, post:1590587237426946049). He warns about misinformation and scams and advises due diligence (post:910175111889563648, post:1590587241742880769). Trust is the scarce asset in finance; deposit growth is a sign of customer trust (post:2015848539994325075, post:2094545159832637884).

The late risk emphasis becomes social and political: concentrated ownership is fragile, excluding people from wealth they helped create fractures societies, and AI backlash comes from having no stake in the upside (post:2016332563879502219, post:2065929194102456363). He also names a personal risk: AI eroding human mastery for convenience, citing Asimov's "The Feeling of Power" (post:1924582379307598255).

He is careful about private-market exposure. Private-market shares differ from public equity in rights, liquidity, and pricing, and he wants that made easy to understand. He does not take shortcuts, does not imply a public market when one does not exist, and respects issuers that do not want Robinhood retail exposure (post:2047353329383309382, post:2047353331404964341, post:2047353333468610723). Stablecoin yield must be unleashed "safely," with value and safety prioritized for consumers (post:2035098172700467279, post:2035098183270068713).

After his X account was compromised by social engineering, he addressed the security risk plainly, reassured that no tokens were issued, and detailed safeguards (post:2080483725209649349, post:2082125660294713761). That is the pattern: risk is named, the fix is explained, and the public gets the receipts.

## Favorite narratives

- **Democratizing finance** — bullish from the earliest posts. It began as access to trading and zero commissions, then expanded to crypto wallets, 24/7 markets, private banking, advisory, private markets, and ownership programs (post:1354900965619425280, post:1383243228187484167, post:1905369015964553395, post:2026028297822588941). No reversal; the definition widened.
- **Crypto and blockchain** — bullish and rising. Early: crypto fundamentally reorganizes the financial system (post:1365023283364589568, post:1526647871898009601). Later: DeFi is infrastructure, tokenization is the foundation, and stablecoin yield is an expected consumer product (post:1808560582662181160, post:1813311081000866005, post:2035098174294303207).
- **24/7 markets** — bullish throughout. Early calls for replacing T+2 with real-time settlement (post:1356687248746455041). Later frames 24/7 as blockchain-driven and inevitable (post:1852503247816417603, post:1988039525097173249).
- **Payment for order flow** — bullish early, with repeated defense of execution quality and zero commissions (post:1359322133919195139, post:1367524416465170432). The later notes do not revisit it, so the record shows no reversal.
- **Dogecoin** — bullish early, but as a technical evaluation of fees, block time, throughput, and inflation rather than as a pure meme (post:1514684174002634768, post:1514684185243369486). The theme fades in later periods without a disavowal.
- **Mathematical superintelligence** — bullish, with the sharpening described above: from "eliminate hallucinations" to formal verification, Lean, and vibe proving (post:1800177883748671809, post:1810768297098649729, post:1992401666906509404, post:2017326503185522912).
- **Prediction markets** — bullish, emerging in the middle period as "truth machines" and the future of news (post:1895144580007526756, post:1901703746033426919, post:1974191753021251830).
- **Tokenization of real-world assets** — bullish from 2024 onward; becomes his strongest market-narrative, including stocks, real estate, private assets, and stablecoins (post:1813311081000866005, post:1973609608892784950, post:1990502364269429223).
- **Great Wealth Transfer** — bullish ambition rather than an asset stance: $124T is moving to younger generations, and he intends to capture it with broader ownership (post:1824529083721724212, post:1867652186047668257, post:1932200828041105445).
- **AI agents in finance** — bullish late: agentic trading and agentic credit cards are the next era, with crypto as the transaction layer (post:2059659590745424213, post:2062198005948923925, post:2067754874813710593).
- **US crypto policy / CLARITY Act** — bullish policy narrative, from FIT21 to the CLARITY Act and clear market-structure rules; he sees regulation by enforcement as harmful and wants America to lead (post:1787595554538786977, post:1793376331423764653, post:2011622052457783432, post:2090591694399914461).
- **Legacy brokers, gatekeepers, and banks lobbying against stablecoin yield** — bearish late. He mocks their fees and frames their lobbying as protect-the-deposit behavior (post:2035098177079304309, post:2046327072898052197).
- **AI job fears / backlash** — mixed. He argues technology creates different work, not less work, but he treats the political backlash as real and solvable only through ownership stakes (post:2002156180559573460, post:2001782195934134614, post:2019871947094651215).

Respect spans builders, technologists, and public voices. Early: Baiju Bhatt, Christine Hall, Johann Kerbrat, Howard Lindzon, Josh Topolsky, and Aparna Chennapragada are the recurring colleagues and operators (post:892938692922228736, post:1385283538413494273, post:1574797816882335744, post:656306372875653121, post:1615444930306134017, post:1372262429560770561). Elon Musk appears early and again late, tied to conviction and building (post:1364985923813838848, post:2104937407539749367, post:2112257387474407832).

In the middle period, his respected names are more finance-crypto macro: Michael Saylor, Cathie Wood, Raoul Pal, Ilya/ilyasergey, tachim, Brad Gerstner, Thomas Farley, and Grigor Dimitrov (post:1898145330140450857, post:1987636012739420357, post:1925690927743414781, post:1943402639657447761, post:1943457476738777431, post:1932200828041105445, post:1956036819864445221, post:1944453260615594367).

The pattern: he respects people who build hard things, prove them with data, or win by going first.

## Who and what they mock

His mockery is often directed at critics, gatekeepers, and people he frames as behind the curve. Early targets include Dave Portnoy, All-In Podcast hosts, and critics of Robinhood (post:1364333873354608641, post:1363626226636623875, post:1359582110550380549). Bears and short sellers become the target around the short-selling feature launch (post:1971311763972087889, post:1973087649603743851). He also has a lighter, almost absurdist mockery for a trivia winner named @vikvang1 (post:1898181150146986254, post:1902177193566073342).

The late targets are more policy-shaped: Senator Bernie Sanders's data-center moratorium, Dario Amodei/Anthropic's AI essay, legacy brokerages and their fees, banks lobbying against stablecoin yield, and Webull (post:2001782195934134614, post:2016332563879502219, post:2046327072898052197, post:2035098177079304309, post:2108002209892085996). He does not usually call them stupid; he frames them as protecting old distribution or misunderstanding incentives.

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He does not dwell on personal trading losses. The mistakes he owns are operational and security-related. He apologized for the HOOD Holidays outage and explained the fix (post:2004756962890993834). After his X account was compromised by social engineering, he said plainly what happened, assured that no tokens were issued, and laid out new safeguards (post:2080483725209649349, post:2082125660294713761). Earlier he was more likely to pivot to progress or joke things off, but in the later notes the pattern is direct admission plus remediation. The underlying habit is treating incidents as learning loops: learn from the incident, improve the system, keep shipping (post:1182812555267325952, post:1346936063709896704, post:1486090139671560198).
