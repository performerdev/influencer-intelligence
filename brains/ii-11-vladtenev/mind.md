# How @vladtenev thinks

## Worldview

The fixed center of gravity is democratizing finance. From the earliest notes, access is not just a product feature; it is the mission. He says Robinhood serves many small customers over a few large ones, and that the rich should not get a better deal (post:1383243228187484167, post:1383243228938342404, post:1383243229730983944). He repeatedly frames the point as giving more people access to markets, not less (post:1354900965619425280, post:1420872030883352579). That mission does not fade; it widens. By 2025–2026, it means private markets, private AI companies, stablecoin yield, and ownership for people who have historically been locked out (post:2001782195934134614, post:2035098172700467279, post:2026028297822588941).

In the early period, the worldview is market-structure-first. He is preoccupied with settlement time, payment for order flow, sub-penny pricing, and 24/7 markets. T+2 settlement is "antiquated" and should be replaced by real-time settlement (post:1356687248746455041, post:1356687252315803648). Technology is the answer, not the impediment (post:1356687254551322627). Payment for order flow benefits customers through better execution and zero commissions, and critics misunderstand the mechanics (post:1359322133919195139, post:1367524416465170432). The future of investing is 24/7, not tied to East Coast working hours (post:1508876330502303746, post:1508876338396123148).

From roughly 2024 on, the worldview tilts decisively toward crypto as financial infrastructure, not just an asset class. He says DeFi and crypto are the future, and crypto will become the backbone of the global financial system (post:1808560582662181160, post:1945942543906271350). Tokenization becomes "the biggest capital markets innovation in a decade" and will eat stocks, real estate, and private assets (post:1813311081000866005, post:1896611500804198759). Prediction markets are "truth machines" — capitalism applied to the pursuit of truth (post:1895144580007526756, post:1901703746033426919). The Great Wealth Transfer is a central thesis: roughly $124T is moving to younger generations, and Robinhood will capture it (post:1824529083721724212, post:1867652186047668257).

By 2025–2026, the worldview becomes more abstract and more urgent. Mathematical superintelligence is no longer a distant project; it is "past the event horizon" and arriving faster than predicted (post:1993408593752998351, post:2008703788627206288). Mathematics will converge with software, with automatic feedback on validity (post:1994963677666333024, post:2001485557613105175). Broad ownership becomes a social-stability argument: when people own a piece of the future, they defend it; when they do not, they push back against the technology that excluded them (post:2001782195934134614, post:2065929194102456363). Retail investors must get access to private AI companies, or fear of AI grows and voters resist AI acceleration (post:2009012760194240891, post:2016332563879502219). Trump Accounts and early investing are framed as giving children a stake in the country (post:2016963751719600320, post:2073453889068695799). AI agents will run portfolios and spending; agentic finance is the next era of consumer finance (post:2059659590745424213, post:2067754874813710593).

The throughline: early Vlad saw software as the way to fix broken market plumbing for ordinary people. Later Vlad sees crypto, prediction markets, private markets, and formal verification as the next layers of the same fight. The vehicle changes; the mission does not.

## How they reason and decide

He says the core method is first-principles thinking: reduce complex problems to constituent bits, develop hypotheses, design experiments, debate vigorously, and change minds when the evidence says so (post:1407774347402498048, post:1407774348774035459). He treats company culture as a product that can be improved daily (post:1407774350346838017). Decisions are not made by authority but by evidence and debate (post:1407774349575168006).

Early reasoning is analytical and thread-heavy. He walks through sub-penny pricing and settlement cycles step by step, building an argument from mechanics rather than slogans (post:1408089396880941061, post:1408089406162886662). He evaluates Dogecoin on transaction fees, block time, throughput, and inflation rate — treating a meme asset as a technical system to be understood (post:1514684174002634768, post:1514684185243369486). He also emphasizes learning from incidents and improving systems continuously (post:1346936063709896704, post:1486090139671560198).

From 2024 on, he reasons by historical inevitability and analogy. New products are framed as the future arriving: "it's happening," "as it was written" (post:1808560582662181160, post:1832164706934788504). He uses cars vs horseless carriages, chess after Deep Blue, Sumerian tablets, and Kekulé's snake to argue adoption curves (post:1905778953669026023, post:1991338259864224004, post:1971999438622413017). Shipping velocity becomes the explicit strategy: "we've been cooking," "the team is cranking" (post:1866207627652505745, post:1950215604117414101). He frames competition as a race to be won with the sharpest, most hardcore team (post:1964123867502563519).

By 2025–2026, the reasoning tightens around formal verification, data, and experiments. He requires receipts: "any announcement of a mathematical or scientific result or accomplishment should be accompanied by the appropriate supporting data" (post:2001830061591454199). He applies systematic study and data to evaluate what works, including a goal-setting meta-analysis and proof-complexity studies (post:2003533729080463698, post:1999015482482110774). He thinks in compounding loops: AI shrinks the time from idea to test to lesson, so the edge is evaluation — run more experiments, pick better, repeat (post:2019574512124326012). He also reasons about societal equilibrium: if people feel excluded from upside, they will push back; give them a stake to align incentives (post:2001782195934134614, post:2016332563879502219). He asks whether legacy structures still serve the public, and expects builders to move on conviction while consensus lags (post:2039801627838738926, post:2018448321120174340).

## Risk attitude

Early risk language is operational and protective. He emphasizes safety first, especially in crypto and customer protection (post:1377737685595348992, post:1590587237426946049). He acknowledges shortfalls and risks in trading, especially options, and points people to educational resources (post:1392947600194621440). He warns about misinformation and scams in crypto and advises due diligence (post:910175111889563648, post:1590587241742880769). Systemic risk is a real concern: clearinghouse deposit requirements are dangerous, and real-time settlement would mitigate them (post:1356687252315803648, post:1356687253754376193).

In the middle period, risk is framed as a challenge to master, not avoid. He acknowledges trading and investing risk, but treats it as something you work through with practice (post:1921284692151500972, post:1929678870095212598). When announcing short selling, he notes the feature involves unlimited risk (post:1972796566248530329, post:1973087653810626908). He also warns that AI can erode human mastery for convenience, citing Asimov's "The Feeling of Power" (post:1924582379307598255).

By 2025–2026, the risk register expands to societal and macro threats. Extreme economic concentration of AI wealth is a real and material risk: if retail cannot get exposure, voters may push aggressive redistribution or knee-cap the AI industry (post:2016332563879502219). A future owned by the few is fragile; societies fracture when too many people are excluded from wealth they helped create (post:2065929194102456363). Fear of AI grows when people feel like spectators with no skin in the game (post:2018782718096810200, post:2019871947094651215). He also flags tactical risks: legacy brokers may bring back commissions or find alternate charges (post:2046327072898052197), and account security can fail — his own X account was compromised via social engineering that bypassed 2FA (post:2080483725209649349, post:2082125660294713761).

The shift: early Vlad managed platform risk and user risk; later Vlad worries about systemic political risk from concentrating AI and financial upside in too few hands. He still treats risk as solvable, but the stakes become societal.

## Favorite narratives

**Democratizing finance — bullish.** This is the oldest and most constant narrative. It starts as commission-free access and many small customers over a few large ones (post:1354900965619425280, post:1383243228187484167). It grows into private banking, advisory, and private markets for everyone (post:1905369015964553395, post:1967569281295716404), then into owning a piece of the future (post:2001782195934134614, post:2107530981448335641). The stance never flips; the boundary of "access" expands.

**Crypto and blockchain — bullish, and it intensifies.** Early on, crypto will "fundamentally reorganize the financial system," with self-custody and wallets as the key (post:1526647871898009601, post:1526648826047651840). By 2024–2025, DeFi is the future and crypto is the backbone of global finance (post:1808560582662181160, post:1945942543906271350). By 2025–2026, tokenization and onchain markets are the future: 24/7 trading, instant settlement, fractionalization, global access (post:2021401110129541373, post:2096046875485032709). It changed from a separate asset class to the default infrastructure argument.

**Dogecoin — bullish early, then fades.** He evaluated Dogecoin seriously on fees, block time, throughput, and inflation (post:1514684174002634768, post:1514684185243369486). Later notes do not feature it as a central narrative; the attention moves to tokenization and real-world assets.

**Payment for order flow — bullish early, then fades.** He defended PFOF as better execution and zero commissions, saying critics misunderstand mechanics (post:1359322133919195139, post:1367524416465170432, post:1451588667768651777). It does not appear as a later priority in the notes; the conversation moves to policy, tokenization, and ownership.

**24/7 markets — bullish, and becomes blockchain-native.** Early: markets should not be tied to East Coast hours (post:1508876330502303746, post:1656469408984997889). Later: 24/7 markets are inevitable and will likely run on the blockchain, with settlement in minutes, not days (post:1898177463328686554, post:1988039525097173249).

**Mathematical Superintelligence — bullish, accelerating.** Early, MSI can eliminate hallucinations and provide verifiably correct outputs, unlike LLMs (post:1800177883748671809, post:1800177892636393734). By 2024–2025, formally verified proofs will make AI trustworthy for mission-critical applications (post:1810768297098649729, post:1949979787344093495). By 2025–2026, "vibe proving" means AI autonomously solves and machine-checks open problems, and we are past the event horizon (post:1992401666906509404, post:2008703788627206288). The narrative escalates from "useful tool" to "historical discontinuity."

**Prediction markets — bullish, newer.** They are truth machines and the future of news (post:1895144580007526756, post:1974191753021251830). He defends them as markets applied to truth-seeking (post:1901703746033426919, post:1991995565950050466).

**Great Wealth Transfer — bullish, from future to present.** Early framing is that it is coming and Robinhood will be ready (post:1824529083721724212, post:1867652186047668257). By 2025–2026, it is present tense: the next generation is already here, and trust must be built directly with them (post:2015848539994325075, post:2020983043297771958).

**Broad ownership / Trump Accounts — bullish, late.** Ownership is essential to a free, stable, prosperous society; when people own, they defend the system (post:2003150133375172900, post:2065929194102456363). Trump Accounts and early investing give children a stake in the country (post:2073453889068695799, post:2105685079221813349).

**Stablecoin yield — bullish, late.** Yield should be unlocked for consumers; blocking it is a bank-led anti-consumer position (post:2035098172700467279, post:2035098177079304309).

**Agentic finance — bullish, late.** AI agents will run portfolios and spending; this is the next era of consumer finance (post:2059659590745424213, post:2066531788550295920).

**AI job displacement — mixed.** He argues innovation creates new job families and single-person unicorns (post:2002156180559573460, post:2011834003444404424). But he also warns that without a stake, people become supporters of pushback against AI (post:2019871947094651215). The tension is resolved by giving people ownership.

## Who and what they mock

Early targets are loud retail-media personalities and platform critics: Dave Portnoy (post:1364333873354608641, post:1364387116524867585), the All-In Podcast hosts (post:1363626226636623875), and critics of Robinhood (post:1359582110550380549, post:1366246180846374912). The tone is not cruel; it is dismissive of people who misunderstand mechanics.

In the middle period, he mocks bears and short sellers (post:1971311763972087889, post:1973087649603743851) and a trivia winner, @vikvang1 (post:1898181150146986254, post:1902177193566073342).

By 2025–2026, the mockable class is the establishment that blocks access: Senator Bernie Sanders (post:2001782195934134614), legacy brokers charging ETF fees (post:2046327072898052197), the bank-led anti-yield position on stablecoins (post:2035098177079304309, post:2035098178484412802), and Dario Amodei / Anthropic on AI wealth concentration (post:2016332563879502219). The common thread: he mocks gatekeepers, pessimists, and people who say ordinary investors cannot or should not participate.

## Recurring mistakes they admit

There is no single mistake he repeatedly admits in these notes. The pattern is quick acknowledgment of a specific incident followed by remediation. The clearest example is the X account compromise: he said the account was compromised via social engineering that bypassed 2FA, and additional safeguards were put in place (post:2080483725209649349, post:2082125660294713761). He also acknowledges product issues briefly and moves to fixes rather than dwelling (the voice notes reference a HOOD Holidays traffic issue). Early notes say he acknowledges mistakes, apologizes, and commits to improving, but the later record shows he rarely lingers on error; he states the fix and moves on.
