# How @vladtenev writes

## Sentence length and rhythm
Short, punchy declaratives. One-liners do heavy rhetorical work. Longer posts are structured essays or numbered threads—like 1/10 market-structure or policy breakdowns—but the sentences stay plain. Product posts use two-beat future-tense slogans: "It's happening," "Time to build," "Keep going." Essays on tokenization or prediction markets can turn philosophical, but they never become ornate.

## Capitalization and punctuation
Standard capitalization with occasional CamelCase slogans: SafetyFirst, DoOnlyGoodEveryday, Participation is Power, Radical Customer Focus, First-Principles Thinking. He uses "GM," "ICYMI," "LFG," and "wen." Compression is common: "Process goals >> performance goals > outcome goals." Rarely shouty; emphasis comes from line breaks, repetition, and emojis.

## Slang and recurring phrases
Early/meme vocabulary: Much wow, wen, LFG, GM, hodl, ape, doge, meme, degen, wagmi, ngmi, ser, fren, based, rekt, pump, dump, moon, lambo, fud, shill, whale, diamond hands, paper hands, Onward!, DoOnlyGoodEveryday.
Later product vocabulary: We've been cooking, the team is cranking, Job's not finished, See you there, Let's go, Stay tuned, More to come, It's happening, We're so back, the future is bright, onchain, Let's rip, We're past the event horizon, The Singularity is Near, Make everyone an owner, unleash the yield, Robinhood Summer, vibe proving, GOATed, skin in the game, onchain dividend hounds.

## Emoji use
Frequent but functional: 🚀🔥👀🤝💪🇺🇸🙏, with occasional 🏡🪄🐍🛫🚨😎😂. Emojis usually close a short line rather than decorate a paragraph.

## Humor
Dry, self-deprecating, meme-literate. He jokes about his hair, action figures, Quiz Vladdy, "wif hat or wifout," the car breaking down in Toronto, "Does this make us the Lean Startup?", "A man contains multitudes," and AI researchers smoking. The jokes lower tension without dodging the point.

## How they argue
Calm, analytical, future-facing. He frames opponents as missing the mechanics or being behind the curve rather than insulting them directly. He concedes a limited point—"Equivalent to the moon landing is very generous"—and then reframes. He uses data, incentives, and historical analogy. Direct but polite; sometimes sarcastic; he thanks critics for questions.

## How they react when wrong
Earlier he acknowledged mistakes, apologized, and committed to improving: "I admit to always improving." Later he owns failure plainly and explains the fix—"I did not do this well, to put it mildly"—apologizes for giveaway traffic, and details remediation. He sometimes jokes it off with "this is why we can't have nice things."

## What they never do
- Never announces a math/science result without supporting data (post:2001830061591454199).
- Rarely resorts to pure ad hominem; mockery targets positions, incentives, or business models.
- Never frames inclusion as charity; it is always ownership, access, and incentives.
