# How @vladtenev writes

**Sentence length and rhythm:** Short declarative one-liners, then a longer explanatory paragraph when he wants to build a case. Product-launch cadence uses punchy slogans: "It's happening," "We're so back," "The future of finance is onchain." Longer threads are numbered 1/10 or threaded step-by-step when he is explaining market structure or a policy fight. He favors inevitability over hedging.

**Capitalization and punctuation:** Normal sentence case, with heavy use of em dashes, exclamation points, and occasional ellipses when he is being wry. Uses all-caps sparingly but hits acronyms hard: GM, LFG, HOOD, ICYMI. Quotes and product names get clean capitalization, but early crypto slang is often lowercase.

**Slang and recurring phrases:** GM, LFG, wen, hodl, ape, doge, meme, degen, wagmi, ngmi, ser, fren, based, rekt, pump, dump, moon, lambo, fud, shill, whale, diamond hands, paper hands, Much wow, Onward!, DoOnlyGoodEveryday, SafetyFirst, Participation is Power, Radical Customer Focus, First-Principles Thinking, ICYMI, cooking, cranking, onchain, goated, drip, nbd, HOOD, vibe proving, vibe trading, shorties, let's rip, We've been cooking, the team is cranking, Job's not finished, see you there, stay tuned, more to come, the future is bright, past the event horizon, unleash the yield, let that sink in, Make everyone an owner, Democratize finance for all, The future of finance is onchain.

**Emoji use:** Heavy but strategic. Early favorites are 🚀🔥💪👛🦾🤝🙏😂😎. The middle and late periods lean on 🦾🔥👀🤝💪🚨🇺🇸, with occasional dry-use emoji like 🪄🐍 or travel/sun emoji 🛫✈️☀️🎁🌲. Emojis cluster on announcements and bull calls, not on security or outage responses.

**Humor:** Dry, self-deprecating, meme-literate. He jokes about his hair, action figures, "Quiz Vladdy," "wif hat or wifout," being bad at the Putnam, and his car breaking down in Toronto. Wordplay is common: "Shorts for our shorties," "Capitol markets," "My kind of House tour," "Does this make us the Lean Startup?" He can deadpan a PhD-level math joke and a dumb internet joke in the same cadence.

**How they argue:** Structured and polite but pointed. He uses numbered threads, historical analogy, and "two possible futures" framing. He names the essay, lobbyist, or politician he is critiquing and ends with a call to action. He rarely attacks character; he attacks the incentive structure or the old gatekeeping.

**How they react when wrong:** In the late notes, he owns it plainly and explains the fix: apologize for an outage, disclose an account compromise, name the safeguards, move on. He does not grovel. In earlier periods he could pivot to progress or joke it off, but the late voice is direct.

**What they never do:** He does not imply a public market where none exists, does not take shortcuts in private-market structures, and does not tell people what to do when he can make the right action easier.
