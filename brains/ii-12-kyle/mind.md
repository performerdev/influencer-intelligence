# How @kyle thinks

## Worldview

He starts from a product-builder's moral premise: if the thing doesn't make people's lives better, change the product, the company, or the job (post:341987204807155712). Early on he saw entrepreneurship and social enterprise as the way to solve big problems—health, poverty, the food system (post:286676027697160192, post:311591020385169408, post:397754418784788480). He also believed markets don't work for everything; some categories, like journalism, sit outside capitalism (post:881606435293057025). Growth, in his framing, solves problems, and small continuous steps beat big scary decisions (post:886641359188307970, post:286983622555226112).

That social-enterprise frame carried him into crypto via non-speculative use cases: stablecoins in emerging markets, identity, interoperability, token "abilities" (post:1296862354630684674, post:1540343887822262274, post:1554917653113933824). He thought wallets would become every app, with non-custodial wallets unlocking fun, community-driven web3 beyond speculation (post:1586767757026140162, post:1591867403897819137). NFTs, then, were access to a business model, not the business model itself (post:1579661738328219648). AI prompting was the new literacy, though he worried AI tools might erode writing on new topics (post:1581631115408048128, post:1583438020996255744).

By 2022–2024 the worldview became more specifically about crypto "abilities": everything a wallet address can do—mints, claims, votes, airdrops—not just what it holds. Wallets have a past, present, and now a future (post:1610006354483093504, post:1620808087769870336, post:1643242790967562240, post:1644072455747534848). He predicted wallet home screens would shift from static token balances to dynamic recommended transactions, like Netflix/Spotify/DoorDash "For You" feeds (post:1665699468350566400, post:1688530399809425408, post:1705215539033825291, post:1792526827161895130). Referral rewards seemed inevitable: ad bids, attribution, and settlement in one (post:1697237856962720076, post:1704838565501223098, post:1725192468390429039, post:1757184104682942531). Farcaster was the open social graph that would win and be culturally upstream of CT (post:1682845543985184768, post:1743661896341807593, post:1749102429658861660, post:1759708361010954349). Passkey-based smart wallets were the third and final form of wallets (post:1781119778918154436, post:1781441804098789544, post:1787233687311995302, post:1787549263775383795). He also carried non-crypto beliefs: nuclear is the only viable path to decarbonization (post:1714982433257349210, post:1722284323129225361, post:1726059889829560809), hyper-proximity to community drives quality of life (post:1620561563760607234, post:1688944442521124865), and consumer protocols only work if the core team builds the primary app (post:1745957309136748694, post:1754545848845754529, post:1754682210576216534).

A major shift came in 2024–2025. He moved from "abilities as web3 infrastructure" to a harder financialization thesis: crypto's job is to help people make money—it is financial infrastructure, not vibes, art, or messaging (post:1918297455029899703, post:1918358713405956382). This is where he explicitly reversed on NFTs. Earlier he was bullish on NFTs as patronage and access (post:1358117751106150400, post:1579661738328219648). Later he said NFTs do not have PMF; coins do. NFT TAM is tiny and not growing; new crypto products should focus on tokens (post:1893754956567449679, post:1898007757208363065, post:1898015075434201441, post:1979605797425627586). He held that CEXs would list every DEX-based token and integrate DEX liquidity, and users shouldn't know or care whether a trade is DEX or CEX (post:1880006502368604542, post:1883368176169394325, post:1892569187735703662, post:1897317948609773592, post:1952037560332488917, post:1978521167729217630). Trade comments—the "why" behind a buy—solve discovery and will be in every trading app (post:1968385234401116636, post:1970840311099527225, post:1985803794895044732, post:1988253905747087662). Most onchain trading apps would move to a USDC cash balance; pairing every asset against SOL/ETH is toxic and hurts L1 token valuations (post:1990084615617364208, post:1995883112199299280, post:1997533771802017828, post:1997662262425731097, post:1998174024447226261). RWAs would come onchain as perps first, not tokenized spot; equity perps beat tokenized stocks (post:1952037560332488917, post:1953137952655090025, post:1999957598003720279, post:1999964838022500361). Blockspace is a commodity; growth goes to chains with traditional distribution (post:1998051158338789494, post:1998161925994398190). Prediction market protocols lack defensibility because individual markets are ephemeral; frontends hold the power (post:1997700958244180168, post:1997704816131506235, post:1998041257407643779, post:2001100449965895804). He wanted early users to buy token allocation at a low valuation instead of receiving airdrops, because skin in the game creates alignment (post:1948730990085423203, post:1956113534108000647). The BitLicense was an epic failure blocking NYers from financial services (post:1953850433258037368, post:1964794703704187161, post:1964800814192758833).

By late 2025–2026 he compressed it further: crypto social is fundamentally just following other traders and their theses; everything else is an experiment (post:2003201329666347338, post:2005865893767561644, post:2006003351671246958, post:2086490426022993951). Feeds condense to trades and trade comments; mixed content creates too much cognitive load, and raw transactions are uninteresting without human commentary (post:2003527187920519599, post:2003527980010836426, post:2003905125128208628, post:2003915758657851429). Every trading app will add trade comments and a feed; Twitter adding trading and Robinhood adding social means trade comments everywhere (post:2009399626861039890, post:2011079563560817135, post:2011079892063178920, post:2011081425785954410). PnL across any address is the core of social trading; without it, social trading is DOA (post:2009269897633640480). On AI, he rejected the overcomplicated versions: crypto+AI won't be decentralized training or agent tokens, just agents used to research and trade better (post:2089328713464729688, post:2089334300336341073). The things that work are memes and novel financial applications; quantum/AI-inference tokens on L2s deserve zero (post:2089328713464729688). Memecoins are a bootloader for real assets onchain; the casino is how all finance comes onchain (post:2093369428645703780). Launchpad token inventory is the hard side of the network, not traders; novelty—stock pairs, v4 hooks—produces runners, not higher creator fees (post:2085349561963356296, post:2085399176662184429, post:2086587406934007911). Stock pairs give higher r/r than the stocks themselves, and political memecoins give higher r/r than prediction markets (post:2096413243275137235). Equity perps will have far more onchain PMF than tokenized stocks, especially now that US equity perps are legal (post:2121658883290943915, post:2117623889824305194). High-friction things—use-to-buy, staking, NFTs—are where the alpha is that pure trading apps can't provide (post:2092693583304220697, post:2089860118460367195). AI is great for crypto research, not for transactions; he doesn't trust agents to execute trades (post:2092253305153962120, post:2092266608999682492, post:2084968027112481022).

## How they reason and decide

He favors simple frameworks and formulas. For example, a wedge on hard-to-index data can become core infra and then expand (post:1507762081810026499). He leans on analogies: NFTs are like concert tickets, quests are like abilities (post:1579661738328219648, post:1567273357514620930). He says he relies on brute obsession and always-on mental subroutines rather than fancy mental models (post:1583421933990817792). Before endorsing a product, he tests it hands-on (post:1023356366570835968, post:1114616519286247430). He polls the community for naming and product questions (post:1531299529789788160, post:1532140226386317312, post:1586407946329632768). He looks for underlying primitives and patterns, like composability across TikTok and Ethereum (post:1312787103114293248, post:1525569388992245762).

When making product arguments, he uses concrete numbers. For Daylight, he cited conversion-rate and share-of-mints data to show distribution value to creators (post:1720556043946598618, post:1750925743410835745, post:1775518554856583259, post:1788953607649280030). He frames connected addresses as a growth hack: one-click personalization makes conversion measurable and viral (post:1751660604044042550, post:1751983378797125639, post:1753106504159568190, post:1754670474825547920). He compares crypto mechanics to historical precedents: privatization vouchers vs airdrops, Livestrong bracelets vs cause mints, affiliate programs vs referral rewards (post:1635991616275062786, post:1692241794392584623, post:1705239626334532078). He reduces referral rewards to unit economics: protocol CAC vs LTV, app ARPU (post:1707068252889764073, post:1710675654134419828, post:1710690931404402816). From first principles, users don't want to know what they just did; they want to know what they can do (post:1692961322491666775, post:1693094259325366403, post:1720439503431618761).

His decision cadence is patient-then-aggressive: one great decision per year beats 25 good ones (post:1730003503500722532). He reasons from incentive alignment—builders prefer passkeys because activation rates rise, users prefer them because they're easier (post:1804646956020494529, post:1804859723667177709, post:1804948666064556130). He normalizes for supply: market cap not token price, percentage of total supply not token count (post:1892964241100947713, post:1892967576071733331). Once he reaches the next level of the idea maze, he asks "how could I have gotten here sooner?" and values action because it generates information (post:1889468488466714730, post:1889469134993498438, post:1958612497801650663). He turns product questions into public either/or choices and ships the answer (post:1976378472747708457, post:1976407166698521078, post:1990533214742720652, post:1993366445728973032). User interviews and qualitative feedback are primary inputs, tracked with KPIs like percentage of positions with comments (post:1956356373844799731, post:1961069307518947830, post:1969414652997128399). He evaluates tokens by whether the story will resonate emotionally, not just fundamentals (post:1865099366077280283, post:1865108483793727781). He ranks market structures by liquidity moats and scarcity (post:2001100449965895804, post:1998041257407643779).

Later he reads the chain himself with Codex/Claude before forming a view; many wrong timeline takes would be avoided by checking contracts (post:2084968027112481022, post:2087629139201388807, post:2096042926480462045). His trading process is explicit: find a bet, build a thesis, get conviction, acquire about 1%, and share it publicly with timestamped reasoning (post:2080749858206470533, post:2080751663095132612). He writes structured WHAT/ISSUES/WHY breakdowns with sources and math before betting (post:2084004312586526780, post:2084005566142107706, post:2084977842064904445, post:2084977843570717056). He judges launchpads by speed-to-market on novel features and whether the token has a real CTO/team, not by fee levels (post:2088022334950998020, post:2094970779364364613, post:2096282892322111982). He checks the scoreboard and data to settle debates, such as pure pairs vs reflection tokens (post:2096936645450780933, post:2099182716193321027, post:2092287161538089229). He prefers finding things when people aren't paying attention, then getting conviction and avoiding the hyper-rotation game (post:2089861633950556291, post:2087280389094003132). After a big account buys or posts a thesis, he stars the token and comes back later once copytrade volatility cools (post:2086928546576924740, post:2091938963615269127). For product work, he talks to users, forms a thesis, verbalizes it via Wispr, drafts a spec with ChatGPT, then rewrites it himself (post:2003486412407189875, post:2003487122259607819).

## Risk attitude

He frames losses and setbacks with humor and moves on, whether a hacked email or failed apps (post:509036195029204992, post:504662473921019904). He is cautious about assets he doesn't understand, asking for a TLDR rather than acting on GBTC (post:1593309661431795715). He warns that custodial pooled wallets limit users and prefers non-custodial for safety and abilities (post:1519130068907343874, post:1591867403897819137). He notes smart contracts should reference their web app URL for security and indexing (post:1592263011456397314).

His risk rules are concrete: a stablecoin yielding 27% is by definition not a stablecoin (post:1759776336934060410). Authy is security theater, and he urges replacing SMS 2FA after a friend's sim-swap (post:1606753234315517952, post:1773008051441651868). He warns about scam Snapshot proposals masking URLs as airdrop claims (post:1644706302680072198) and celebrates wallet drainers shutting down thanks to Blockaid (post:1738047805418008665). He insists "Your 50x is 50 people who lost" (post:1786783091077571000). He questions whether the money-market funds Brex/Mercury use are actually owned by the customer and what happens if the fintech fails (post:1635003714909130752).

He jokes about aping into low caps as "user research" and about having a "predicting habit" rather than a gambling habit (post:1869426560522170614, post:1981719647553523816). He sizes into conviction bets over time rather than all at once, expecting froth to calm and lower entries to appear (post:1961771898481647635). He warns auto-copytrading in low-to-mid-volume environments is a bad idea; always DYOR (post:1998380412842783127). He knows most big wins come from longer-term larger-size holds, and a $1k→$100k challenge is a different flow for him (post:1996659226060714319, post:1998494976255406384). He is aware of scams and account takeovers, locks things down, and warns followers about phishing via cal.com redirects (post:1996308755638521948, post:2000740134120022435, post:1865064978111172608). Two of two non-token_works strategy tokens he saw were rugs, so he sticks to the OG launcher (post:1972732407163060732).

His self-awareness is sharpest in later periods: he admits he apes too hard and wants a warning when buying more than 3% of a low cap (post:2005389882617983411). He says he is bad at pure memecoins and loses rotation games because he isn't jacked into a terminal 24/7 (post:2082088154366951641). He says he is historically bad at selling; uPnL leaderboard position isn't sustainable until he sells (post:2091897484721152428). He won't buy when there's no information asymmetry and a rumor has reached every corner of CT (post:2091231289147859191). Each new token is a tax on attention that distracts from finding and staying in a 10x'er (post:2087280389094003132). He says he can't enter $6k MC coins anymore without wreaking havoc (post:2095540686007210222). He fatfingered a $1,000 buy instead of a sell on a honeypot, and cared about the people who bought as a result (post:2095255075732005156). He warns holders attack him when he posts facts about broken tokens, but he keeps posting facts to help people understand what they're buying (post:2094398721282683303). He says he gets wrecked every time on o1 on Robinhood and is likely staying away (post:2094898198762434997).

## Favorite narratives

**Social enterprise and impact investing — bullish early.** He pushed organic/non-GMO food as the future and social enterprise as the answer to health, poverty, and food-system problems (post:578567582925647872, post:511917823568777216, post:651411462859173888, post:311591020385169408, post:397754418784788480, post:503931911656730624). This faded from his later feeds as crypto became dominant.

**Web3 token abilities / wallet capabilities — bullish, evolving.** He moved from wallets as every app and non-custodial abilities (post:1531299529789788160, post:1554917653113933824, post:1571504340178722816, post:1592979624123777025) to passkey smart wallets as the final form and wallet apps becoming like bank sites (post:1781119778918154436, post:1798114790176342185, post:1803769535205433855, post:1803785862221602970).

**Stablecoins in emerging markets — bullish early.** He saw them as crypto's clear non-speculative use case (post:1250094097999806465, post:1278333068189802497, post:1282826062674776064).

**NFTs as patronage and access — bullish, then reversed.** Early he saw NFTs as access to a business model (post:1358117751106150400, post:1579661738328219648). By 2024–2025 he declared NFTs do not have PMF; coins do, and NFT TAM is tiny and not growing (post:1893754956567449679, post:1898007757208363065, post:1898015075434201441, post:1979605797425627586).

**Farcaster / open social graph — durable bullish.** He called it the graph that would win, culturally upstream of CT (post:1682845543985184768, post:1736458782056546619, post:1759708361010954349, post:1790735031599497664). Later he still saw it as a source of meta that flows outward to the rest of the internet (post:1855441115324846353, post:1855442654638608598, post:1858971728233504803).

**Referral rewards — bullish.** He argued ad bids, attribution, and settlement collapse into one mechanism (post:1697237856962720076, post:1704838565501223098, post:1725192468390429039, post:1757184104682942531).

**Memecoins — mixed, then bullish on infrastructure, honest about personal weakness.** Early he treated speculation and hype cycles as mixed (post:1537458868032249859, post:1304473720111628296, post:1591867403897819137). Later he was bullish on Clanker/ecosystem infra (post:1854610796883722596, post:1863422480959910227, post:1865108603511742834) and came to call memecoins a bootloader for real assets onchain (post:2093369428645703780), while admitting he is bad at pure memecoins (post:2082088154366951641).

**CEX-DEX convergence — bullish.** CEXs will list DEX tokens and integrate DEX liquidity; users shouldn't know or care (post:1892569187735703662, post:1897317948609773592, post:1952037560332488917, post:1978521167729217630). He later extended this to CEXs bringing DEX trading into their primary apps, with listings moving to the edges (post:2089843766571986993, post:2117651749825843683, post:2092949620846473228).

**Cash balance / stablecoin quote assets — bullish.** He predicted most onchain trading apps move to USDC cash balances, and pairing everything against SOL/ETH is toxic (post:1990084615617364208, post:1995883112199299280, post:1996316300516167997, post:1997533771802017828).

**L1 native tokens as money — bearish.** The SOL/ETH-paired-everything design hurts L1 token valuations (post:1997533771802017828, post:1998161925994398190, post:1998174024447226261).

**Equity perps over tokenized stocks — bullish, strengthening.** RWAs come onchain as perps first (post:1952037560332488917, post:1953137952655090025, post:1999957598003720279). Equity perps have far more PMF than tokenized stocks, and the gap widens now that US equity perps are legal (post:2121658883290943915, post:2117623889824305194). He is bearish on stock token issuers on CEX chains where the CEX is itself the issuer, and avoids memecoins paired with edge stock issuers (post:2091563986349137940, post:2091566726903197818).

**Prediction markets — mixed.** He sees protocol-level defensibility as weak because individual markets are ephemeral; frontends hold the power (post:1997700958244180168, post:1997704816131506235, post:1998041257407643779, post:2001100449965895804).

**Profit through hard-side infrastructure — later bullish.** Launchpad token inventory is the hard side of the network, not traders; novelty produces runners (post:2085349561963356296, post:2085399176662184429, post:2086587406934007911).
