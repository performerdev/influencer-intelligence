# How @zachxbt thinks

## Worldview

The core worldview began as: Crypto Twitter is mostly cosplay. "Most CT can't trade" (post:1458480680292212737), and the big accounts are marketers pretending to be successful traders, not actual traders (post:1403719977283227650; post:1482443139524837382; post:1484921421876719616). From 2021 he argued that influencers and VCs run undisclosed collective shilling campaigns for bags they already hold (post:1410356566658793475; post:1430250147422695426; post:1433821925470580737). The same logic carried through to 2023–2026: influencers shill low-cap coins or scams, dump on followers, and face no consequences (post:1699231235426390188; post:1748354861878616242; post:1947641139281965462).

He sees the unregulated space as a recurring extraction machine. Newbies are repeatedly exploited; most influencers only want trust to profit from it (post:1397735891376947209; post:1458483853790695425; post:1467923764394573834). Launchpads are mostly cash grabs that enrich founders and VCs at retail's expense (post:1428065460680003587; post:1446538141653929989; post:1449036867672940550). NFT projects in the 2022 wave were often cash grabs, rugs, or slow rugs (post:1498670121182711811; post:1497370216690503687; post:1509565668852703237).

The positive counterweight is transparency: on-chain evidence is essential to expose wrongdoing (post:1438585272392900617; post:1442916271457112065; post:1468627653469032448). Over time this became less of a moral stance and more of a professional method. Blockchain analysis can identify scammers, link wallets, and sometimes recover funds (post:1516070166643228679; post:1521488749208780806; post:1565358227574980617).

His view of authority shifted. In 2021 he argued the SEC targets legitimate projects while ignoring real scams (post:1435637838398332932; post:1442907527105040384). By 2021–2022 he expected government crackdowns on crypto influencers were coming, albeit slowly (post:1468999547917537283; post:1476267391193653252; post:1483110255630467084). By 2023–2024 he had concrete evidence that law enforcement and legal action can work: arrests and guilty pleas followed investigations (post:1730310284248555940; post:1770681711728177480; post:1805258560705831209). But the newest notes qualify that again: The Com and similar cybercrime groups operate with near-total impunity in some jurisdictions, especially Canada, which rarely prosecutes (post:2005649228408725593). So the mature view is: law enforcement effectiveness is real but jurisdiction-dependent.

By 2025 the worldview hardened into a systemic one. He described a "crime supercycle" enabled by politicians launching meme coins, dropped court cases, and laundering groups winning against Lazarus Group (post:1935178436970168347). He concluded KYC/AML is mostly useless because crypto moves faster than tradfi, so traditional strategies cannot be applied (post:1879983934110593326). He also argued retail is systematically extracted on major centralized exchanges through hidden supply, OTC deals, and market maker coordination (post:2054898749923860819; post:2054898923450564988; post:2061442537273827463). At the same time, he retained a product-level distinction: Circle builds good products and he holds USDC himself, but its compliance failures have real consequences (post:2040056067640709563).

On the community: he wrote that crypto people are too entitled to build products for in its current state, and only private tools for compliance/law enforcement make sense (post:1831598921396687028). Yet he also believes private-sector collaboration is the most effective way to combat illicit activity (post:1944703336411717981).

## How they reason and decide

His primary method is to follow the money on-chain and publish the path. The canonical trace is: theft address → bridges → privacy protocols → exchanges, with transaction hashes and addresses pasted inline (post:1824047438061306338; post:1849071168411607353; post:1866482699730399331). He uses timing analysis to match deposits and withdrawals through mixers and exchanges, and to demix funds (post:1849071180411551777; post:1920845246142861689; post:1978465746142589232). In the 2025–2026 notes, timing analysis extends to locating outputs on other chains after instant exchange swaps (post:2005649194435195201; post:2036430920409030848; post:2091858021270143431).

He cross-references on-chain data with social media, ENS names, OpenSea names, leaked databases, and device indicators to link wallets to identities (post:1516070166643228679; post:1836753409749274895; post:1859233166063472697; post:1902713170998800469). He links separate incidents by finding overlap addresses that commingle funds, proving the same entity (post:1893211577836302365; post:1893256508965822873; post:1894004267553304723).

He identifies suspects through opsec mistakes: leaked names during screenshare, reused pfps, wallet reuse, and flexing stolen funds. Those who flex leave a complete paper trail (post:2005649220657934837; post:2014685303701713321; post:1836753207436927042; post:1861775070424891617). He corroborates on-chain findings with chat logs, recordings, and victim testimony before publishing (post:1844369904218296449; post:1937121757028360405; post:1859233137902891094).

His project red flags are concrete: anon teams without history, faked partnerships, side wallets funded via instant exchanges, and exaggerated user numbers (post:1934616651087102382). He also tests privacy products to find design flaws and then abuses them for investigations (post:1980612190609576229; post:1980612198926868907). He poses as a client to gather intel directly from illicit actors (post:2036430865862201502; post:2107081645321076770; post:2107081656050106519).

He uses archived tweets and deleted content to expose undisclosed shilling (post:1433821991858016262; post:1466202007702806529; post:1468627653469032448). Early on he compiled master threads and lists of bad actors for reference (post:1443583283648872464; post:1451627071034335246; post:1467923764394573834). Tools named across periods: Etherscan, Breadcrumbs, Twitter search, Wayback Machine, Chainabuse, and CoinGlass (post:1496460894997524481; post:1488194787375210497; post:1545843873389449218; post:1540363765165834242; post:2045820638355693683).

He sometimes reaches out to projects or individuals for comment before publishing and posts if no response within 24 hours (post:1496163412644188162; post:1496178247234691072). He also reaches out to exchanges and services to freeze stolen funds (post:1521488749208780806; post:1540441180814581766; post:1541469371859623939).

## Risk attitude

He takes deliberate personal and financial risk. He received threatening DMs and warned his account could be compromised (post:1538522269521166336; post:1541984984407445505). In 2025–2026 he fronted $349.7K and lost 5% per order with no guarantee a counterparty would not disappear, plus unknown personal risk from dealing with a syndicate (post:2107081676795077095). He also gets dust attacks of stolen funds to his public address and sends any stolen funds received to a USG seizure address (post:2014775112361509069; post:2015771036458049648).

His risk guidance is defensive and opsec-oriented. He tells victims to report theft addresses ASAP because delay makes recovery unlikely (post:1979899792000958802). Yet he is clear-eyed that recovery is often impossible: "You cannot press a magic button for the funds to be returned" and funds may already be gambled away (post:1979899788469399679; post:1937122011270275271). He warns about SIM swaps, phishing, LastPass-stored seed phrases, and home invasions targeting crypto holders (post:1694326221511794706; post:1717901088521687330; post:1844369955447324848; post:1871543411695599902).

Trading-wise, he avoids the setups he exposes. He does not advise trading tokens with high insider supply control and does not encourage trading LAB under any circumstances (post:2061442537273827463; post:2074801441500184621). He specifically warns that shorting high-supply-control tokens can give insiders fuel to push price higher (post:2054898923450564988).

## Favorite narratives

**Influencer and VC undisclosed shilling — bearish.** This is his longest-running target from 2021 through 2026. He exposed Scott Melker, CryptoGodJohn, Crypto Birb, Artchick, Kale Abe, Bitboy, Crypto Rover, Crypto Beast, Zopp0, and an influencer price sheet leak (post:1397706289707376648; post:1442872471116554243; post:1461154776612323332; post:1759929050041033012; post:1947641028720398488; post:1962485396597776468). The stance never changed; the target set expanded from named CT personalities to KOLs broadly.

**Launchpad and VC cash grabs — bearish.** Early examples: Solchicks, Firestarter, BlueZilla, Moonrock/Moonwhale, and tokenomics with high private/seed allocations (post:1466201971149479940; post:1470531529889697794; post:1493206361328558082; post:1410356535746772992). Later this became less frequent as focus shifted to hacks, but the heuristic persisted: multiple launchpad investments by one VC, Moon Sheets, and Bluezilla names are reasons to fade (post:1473004131363303427; post:1486401532807499778; post:1493206578543136773).

**NFT rug pulls — bearish, peak 2022.** Pixelmon, Bored Bunny, Winter Bears, Lady Ape Club, Animoon, Players Only, and Gala Games Orbs were investigated as rugs, slow rugs, or wash-traded cash grabs (post:1497370216690503687; post:1509565462727827466; post:1559198760865091585; post:1532390797479403521; post:1553780274428780546). This narrative receded in later periods as he moved to exchange-grade thefts and DPRK.

**Wonderland ($TIME) — mixed, then bearish.** In 2021 he was mixed-to-analytical on Wonderland and respected Daniele Sesta (post:1452737593364426752; post:1452737678240276483). The view flipped decisively bearish when he exposed Sifu as the QuadrigaCX co-founder (post:1486591682728673282; post:1487206199250534401; post:1490329156009222146). That is the clearest example of a view changing because new evidence connected a project to prior fraud.

**Lazarus Group and DPRK hacks — bearish.** From 2023 onward this became central: Lazarus Group laundering, DPRK IT workers infiltrating projects, and specific investigations of Bybit, Phemex, BingX, Poloniex, and DMM Bitcoin (post:1784935501935390930; post:1812466959109521649; post:1940388827392344261; post:1955613912201896113; post:2107081638073319437). He considers DPRK IT workers unsophisticated but persistent, and hiring them a sign a startup will fail (post:1940388908124291553; post:1940388827392344261).

**Coinbase social engineering scams — bearish.** He argued Coinbase fails users on social engineering, restricting accounts instead of stopping $300M+/year losses, and that leadership is out of touch (post:1886411879939031530; post:1880045353648681354; post:1880046118115143858).

**Circle compliance — bearish on conduct, bullish on product.** In 2024–2025 he attacked Circle for failing to act on clear-cut hacks/exploits where competitors did (post:1835931110183768452; post:1835938039907959278; post:1940388873227657687). By 2025–2026 he separated the two: Circle builds good products and he holds USDC himself, but compliance decisions have had real consequences (post:2040056067640709563; post:2040055757211885953). This is a change from mostly critical to a more balanced product-vs-compliance split.

**CEX token manipulation (RAVE, LAB, RIVER, SIREN, MYX) — bearish.** In 2025–2026 he documented hidden supply, OTC deals, and market maker coordination as a retail extraction mechanism (post:2045820638355693683; post:2054898749923860819; post:2061442537273827463; post:2074801441500184621).

**Huione and SEA laundering — bearish.** He named Huione and similar SEA marketplaces among the bigger threats to the longevity of the space (post:1979899778637918374).

**Public goods funding — bullish, with disclosure.** He supported Optimism RPGF but argued VC-backed projects should disclose funding when seeking retroactive public goods money (post:1729195515462500831; post:1729290605711245573; post:1745618562495180938).

**Paradigm advisory role — bullish.** He announced an advisory role with Paradigm with evident satisfaction (post:1894772701639721409).

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He rarely admits error, but when he does it is concrete and correction-oriented. In 2021–2022 he acknowledged mistakes, corrected them, and sometimes apologized (the AU21 Capital correction is noted in the voice notes). In 2022 he used brief acknowledgments like "Bad wording on my part" and corrected threads. Later the pattern became "Update:" posts when new information changed a conclusion, and public typo corrections ("replies really need an edit button for my typos"). In 2025–2026 he issued corrections such as, after further laundering analysis, the sketchy MM / OTC and private key compromise were independent and not related. The recurring mistake is not being wrong often; it is being early or pithy enough that he must walk back wording or update a live case as new on-chain information appears.
