# How @Cupseyy thinks

## Worldview
Cupseyy’s public posts split into three phases: Fortnite competitive grinder (2019–2021), NFT/Solana trader (2021–2022), and memecoin trenches trader (2022–2026). Since newer posts outweigh older ones, where the view changed I treat the later position as current. The clearest changes are from Fortnite competitor to full-time crypto trader around 2022, and from NFT/coin maximalist to platform-mechanics critic by 2025–2026.

Across all phases, the core lens is results. In Fortnite that meant earnings, PR, and placements; in crypto it means PnL and floor movement. He dismisses people who talk without evidence: "No earnings no 2k or" is a complete rejection of a teammate (post:1332969266317025281). He describes Fortnite competitive as full of "egos" and "drones" who haven’t earned (post:1419948745546817538; post:1419950959627837441; post:1423142429666983944). Later he says CT is "hate, ragebaiting, and engagement farming," and prefers people who actually print (post:1942976419073593790; post:1943003193107190037; post:1936797725896368200).

In the Fortnite phase, he saw solos as the worst, most RNG-based mode: duos and trios let you recover from mistakes (post:1223062951952699392; post:1223064044149411840; post:1223116342686912512; post:1292607052154654724). He believed aim assist was overpowered and unfair, even as a controller player, and wanted it nerfed or vaulted (post:1250952120309649408; post:1251925497463930886; post:1263987403552743425). He complained about broken items and formats—heavy snipers, mythic weapons, boogie bombs, crash pads, sharks—and said tournament formats need more games or region-locking (post:1248408644439302145; post:1250903019488604160; post:1299602434839334916; post:1305926675431960576; post:1258584707928698880; post:1287108012214300672). By 2021 he believed West competitive Fortnite was gatekept: Ark controlled scrims, banned people who contested or killed him, and made the game easier for top players (post:1404309781762949125; post:1404318774824783873; post:1404676582749487105). By 2022 he called the game dying: newer seasons and loot pools were bad, and bringing back the old loot pool would make it fun again (post:1480775143743918082; post:1480777609709383681; post:1520634596412428288). He also said people who grind Fortnite all day are losers; he only played tournaments for money (post:1423002002099998730; post:1504330413224779778; post:1446912880205615105).

The crypto phase changed the arena but not the result obsession. Early on he joked about Bitcoin and Dogecoin as money paths (post:1399542940200886274; post:1407393865410105346), then went all-in on Solana: "All of my net worth in sol rn" (post:1505709615178297345). He called Solana the alpha chain and said ETH gas is too expensive (post:1479920160202891268; post:1530328789699309568). In 2022 he saw NFTs as a real way to make money fast, going from first mint in December to thousands up within weeks (post:1481382426660130816; post:1484119834128367618; post:1493749363234852874). By 2024–2025 the center became the trenches: pump.fun/memecoin trading was "the best place to make money," and 12–16 hours a day was the path to generational wealth (post:1808348461739241721; post:1808930111254585656; post:1814052066035204143; post:1817318317553566144; post:1825384141673636070). Later he tempered that with quality over quantity: buy fewer coins, hold longer, stay selective when liquidity is thin (post:1825576040523247882; post:1829231748791841071; post:1835727507917611452).

By 2025–2026 his view became more systemic. He argued memecoin platforms should market their own product instead of relying on individuals, and that prediction markets like Polymarket/Kalshi are the model to copy (post:2000369423019045115; post:2000374466518638957; post:2000389314996507084). The core problem is game mechanics, not traders: launch fees, gated creator fees, block-0 taxes, supply amounts, multi-wallet buys (post:2009841352406905196; post:2009841898626932844; post:2110824446168613242; post:2110826540405584295). He also argued founder/chain support drives memecoin liquidity: CZ and He Yi spawned nine-figure runners on BNB just by tweeting, and when they stopped, BNB memes died (post:2021579881213997269; post:2021581542070628411; post:2021583167493201922).

Outside competition and crypto, he posted some social and political views. In 2020 he supported Trump, said both candidates were bad but Trump was better, and defended him against racism accusations (post:1323881724074512384; post:1324060917550718978; post:1323885507085041664). He thought pride month and other awareness months were overhyped (post:1402737468802281476; post:1402740184375083013), and he thought fatphobia was fine and fat people should be made fun of (post:1447658426784354306; post:1457153815220609032). These mostly fade after 2022.

## How they reason and decide
His primary reasoning mode is rank-by-results. In Fortnite disputes he cited earnings, PR, and placements rather than reputation or ego (post:1307157976772468736; post:1317682024308682754; post:1334664731290898433; post:1404321152307286018). In NFT analysis he evaluated floor movement, sales volume, and whether the team delivered; he called out slow rugs and cash grabs (post:1527363484941332490; post:1551003025635934209; post:1541147521455628288). He also believed marketing and reach drove NFT floor price more than the product itself (post:1551385141649387521; post:1551385227485855750).

In crypto he is an active scanner and pattern matcher. He said he scans pump.fun manually for hours daily, watching charts even when not buying, and uses Twitter/news as trade ideas (post:1808348484061270322; post:1813243389531361377; post:1924466824672075991; post:1813258601785667751; post:1827451656029544909). He takes profits quickly on some plays but aims for big multiples on others depending on how the chart looks (post:1808503923067498642; post:1808505308546769384; post:1825384141673636070). He treats showing up every day as an edge: you never know when the next runner comes, and doomer CT means a comeback is due (post:1835727507917611452; post:1835738453054464256; post:2111092259085013135).

He uses cause-and-effect and analogies. BNB memes proved founder support matters: CZ tweets led to runners, then CZ stopped and BNB memes died (post:2021581542070628411; post:2021583167493201922). PvP/vamp dynamics are like Fortnite FNCS teams griefing each other until everyone dies for nothing (post:2009853057614684297). Platforms that extract billions without reinvesting are worse than traders, because traders pump volume back into the market (post:2001085427604972020). He can also separate personal behavior from systemic critique: he calls himself part of the problem while still pushing for fixes (post:2111058896273932486; post:2110842525028344153).

In early Fortnite he also analyzed his own gameplay in mental terms. His solo problem was mental, not mechanics: choking, shaking, making dumb plays (post:1286126858266980352; post:1286911434996228100; post:1292551560602488833). His advice to others was practical: lower sens, use Kovaaks, work on mechs, and don’t switch sensitivity too much (post:1311353799932555264; post:1321714784899923968; post:1321714962428026880; post:1321715279131561984).

## Risk attitude
He has always taken real risk, but he does not pretend losses feel good. In Fortnite he spent money on controllers and PC parts, accepted replacing broken Elite/Scuf controllers, and entered wagers; he also got nervous and shook during tournaments and wagers (post:1284032853567660032; post:1346574728056565760; post:1346577404291112961). Bad luck—dying off spawn, crashes, internet/packet loss, griefing—made him repeatedly threaten to quit the season (post:1286907159343706112; post:1321641393274703873; post:1305926675431960576).

In crypto he went all-in on Solana, then panicked when it dropped 50% (post:1505709615178297345; post:1524504653513117696). He held through drawdowns: "Same I’m not pulling out any sol till it goes back up" (post:1484663947709935619). He bought an NFT for 9k and called it crazy (post:1493784405327769610). He took profits on mints but regretted paperhanding when they mooned (post:1501867434164195335; post:1519938174461505536). After being drained for $500k, he became a security voice: manual wallet drains are a serious issue, likely leaked private keys (post:1967955259512574345; post:1967957319276441671). He also says losses are your own fault even if someone else told you to buy (post:1921716782743634043).

## Favorite narratives
- **Aim assist / controller meta** — mixed, and it stayed mixed. He called aim assist overpowered and unfair, even as a controller player wanting it nerfed or vaulted (post:1250952120309649408; post:1251925497463930886; post:1263987403552743425). At the same time he hyped controller meta: "Controller on top" and excitement about an aim-assist buff (post:1265185956052770817; post:1305624429934780417). By 2021 he was dismissive the other way: kbm players shouldn’t die to controller box jumps, and controller takes more skill but kbm is the better input (post:1406388743578267658; post:1433516753556934673). The stance is not a clean reversal; he defends controller players as unfairly targeted but never pretends aim assist is weak.

- **Solos vs duos/trios** — bearish from the start and never changed. Solos is the worst RNG mode; duos and trios allow recovery from mistakes (post:1223062951952699392; post:1223064044149411840; post:1292607052154654724; post:1294836162045992966).

- **Fortnite competitive state** — early mixed, later bearish. He criticized broken items and formats but still competed and believed W-keying was necessary in cash cups (post:1248408644439302145; post:1250903019488604160; post:1246110564788531201). By 2022 he said the game was dying and the old loot pool would fix it (post:1480775143743918082; post:1480777609709383681; post:1520634596412428288).

- **Solana / NFTs / memecoins** — bullish, but the form changed. From 2021–2022 he was bullish on Solana as the alpha chain and on high-floor NFTs like Taiyo Robotics, DeGods, Okay Bears, and SoDead (post:1479920160202891268; post:1506111775485403141; post:1518984226925776896; post:1569106770957856768). By 2024–2025 the bullish target became pump.fun and the trenches (post:1808348461739241721; post:1820624903051354541; post:1818479207657210069). By 2025–2026 he still believed in PvE opportunity, but the main narrative shifted to platform-mechanics reform: launch fees, creator-fee gating, block-0 taxes, supply limits (post:2009841352406905196; post:2009841898626932844; post:2110826540405584295).

- **Founder/chain support as memecoin catalyst** — bullish in 2026. CZ and He Yi tweets created BNB runners; without founder support BNB memes died (post:2021579881213997269; post:2021581542070628411; post:2021583167493201922).

- **Prediction markets as marketing model** — bullish in 2025–2026. Polymarket/Kalshi are crypto and gambling but marketed friendly; memecoins need that same friendly funnel for normies (post:2000392113633345956; post:2000389314996507084).

- **CT sentiment bottom / PvE comeback** — bullish and contrarian. Doomer CT only exists when things are quiet; one big runner flips the mood, so doomer sentiment precedes a comeback (post:2111092259085013135; post:2082278366556926293).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
In Fortnite he admitted choking in solos: shaking, dumb plays, mental problems rather than mechanics (post:1286126858266980352; post:1286911434996228100; post:1292551560602488833). He also admitted throwing games and making mistakes in tournaments (post:1353921860405239808; post:1355754568957628417). He often blamed external factors—crashes, internet, griefing, RNG—but the self-diagnosis of choking was genuine and repeated.

In crypto the biggest admitted mistake is paperhanding too early. "Minted 6 but paperhanded 5 all at 3.5, good profit but I’m pressed" (post:1501867434164195335); he bought back into Okay Bears after selling at 28 because they kept going up (post:1519938174461505536). He also said he was buying too many coins in thin markets and needed to be more selective (post:1835727507917611452; post:1835738224376885647). After the $500k drain he treated it as a lesson and warning, not a secret (post:1967955259512574345; post:1967957319276441671). In 2026 he admitted he is part of the PvP problem while still arguing the platform should fix the mechanics (post:2111058896273932486).
