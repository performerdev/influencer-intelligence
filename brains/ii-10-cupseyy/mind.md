# How @Cupseyy thinks

## Worldview
Cupseyy’s mind runs on two tracks: an early Fortnite-competitor frame and a later Solana/trenches frame. The Fortnite side is sour: solos are the worst mode, full of griefers, cheaters, and RNG (post:1223062951952699392, post:1294836162045992966, post:1432548688702758915, post:1447328929874948096); controller aim assist is unfair and should be nerfed (post:1250952120309649408, post:1263987403552743425, post:1266386021790281729); and the “big ego” crowd in the scene annoys him, especially when they are still LFT despite the ego (post:1459289154114637824, post:1459399922697003008, post:1424171191032377345). He eventually concluded competitive gaming for years is wasting time unless you are earning (post:1609390779696570369).

The financial worldview starts with Solana. He is explicit that Solana is the chain to be on and that Solana projects/ecosystem will take over (post:1523824972505919489, post:1567198337111314434, post:1566514075844034560). In the NFT era he believed projects with strong communities, art, and utility would moon, and fading them was a mistake (post:1569125555668402177, post:1574494107757527040, post:1569106788209016832). He hated Magic Eden as a platform and preferred Solport (post:1530328789699309568, post:1572649606898647040), and he called resellers bad (post:1485504382024044544).

By 2024 he had fully shifted into the pump.fun trench identity. He says grinding the trenches for hours daily is the path to wealth, with no insider plays needed (post:1808348461739241721, post:1808348484061270322, post:1814052066035204143, post:1815429904974180676). He calls it the meme coin super cycle (post:1818479207657210069, post:1833255502261678590) and argues one big play is all it takes to change everything (post:1817318822413783280, post:1835727507917611452). He believes you should always show up and trench because there are always plays and you never know when the next is coming (post:1835727507917611452, post:1827386760289980640). He treats pump.fun as the number one news source, instant every time (post:1827451656029544909), and says market conditions don’t matter because the grind doesn’t stop (post:1829888547425567082, post:1863068965309743277). His newer quality-over-quantity view still stays inside this world: time, work, and effort will pay off if you want it bad enough (post:1821359827718648214, post:1913290594769912301).

## How they reason and decide
His early reasoning was heavily external. Tournament losses get blamed on crashes, griefing, bad luck, internet outages, bad teammates, or chokes rather than raw skill (post:1256382670055124992, post:1305274457473376258, post:1318014504182124545, post:1321641393274703873, post:1286911434996228100, post:1292162611711299584, post:1338340751994880001). That pattern is important because it never fully disappears: when something goes wrong, the first move is to find the outside cause.

In crypto/NFTs he started reasoning like a fundamentals trader. He evaluated NFT projects by community activity, art quality, team, utility, and alpha calls (post:1569106775198281728, post:1569106783830163457, post:1569106779447111680). He believed in buying dips and holding for long-term gains (post:1506111775485403141, post:1524504653513117696, post:1569125555668402177). He also used personal experience and PnL to justify strategies, not abstract theory (post:1808348461739241721, post:1812352260929630570, post:1813241269398695937).

As he moved deeper into the trenches, his decision process became more about scanning and waiting. He scans constantly for hidden gems and asks why a coin is undervalued relative to its narrative (post:1825384141673636070). When markets are ranging, he steps back, looks into what is going on, and sits on his hands instead of forcing trades (post:1829231748791841071). He uses Twitter as a trading journal to reflect and improve (post:1825576040523247882, post:1923434570852012340). He also leans on the idea that one big play like snoofi/wdog will bring everyone back, so missing a session is the real risk (post:1835727507917611452). His most consistent rule is narrative-first: bid fewer coins, good narratives, hold longer.

## Risk attitude
Cupseyy takes conviction risk. He said all of his net worth was in SOL during a crash and still stayed in the trade (post:1505709615178297345, post:1524504653513117696). He acknowledges losses and down periods but stays optimistic about recovery (post:1524504653513117696, post:1527084817266855936, post:1491568233446404099). He also takes retail risk in the trenches: buying NFTs at high prices and trading many tokens (post:1493784405327769610, post:1808348484061270322).

At the same time he warns people about scams and drains and advises safety (post:1554619403790012416, post:1212472412807168000). The risk is not theoretical: he reported getting drained for $500,000 overnight from a manual drain after only connecting his wallet to Jupiter, Axiom, Photon, and Nova; they left $200,000 (post:1967955259512574345, post:1967955345999016361). He can joke about smaller losses, like being down $40 in two days and saying it has to be a prank (post:1819398198231793695), but the big loss clearly hit.

His risk viewpoint tightened after 2024. He notes it is more risky now to buy multiple tokens (post:1835727507917611452), and he frames quality over quantity as the way to trade (post:1829231748791841071). He also acknowledged papering — selling too early — but frames it through PnL rather than despair (post:1825384141673636070).
