# How @mert thinks

## Worldview
From the earliest posts, @mert's worldview is anchored in Solana as the best chain to build on and crypto as a civilizational technology. He sees Solana's advantage as engineering, not narrative: it scales with hardware, has low fees, high throughput, and lets builders focus on product rather than gas optimizations (post:1619045768660385792, 1630772056768774144). He repeatedly says "Only Possible on Solana" — builders should exploit composability, raw performance, and unique tooling (post:1686939818251452416). Over time his conviction only hardened: Solana is "the most usable, most battle-tested, fastest-scaling ecosystem" (post:1954863582945873963) and will be "the internet's capital market" (post:1920459292282351738). Even when Solana had outages and liveness failures, he framed them as implementation bugs, not design flaws; funds were never at risk (post:1777400489488900102, 1754857398617923854). He insists Solana's problems are fixed by competent engineers, not fundamental (post:1533128448893403138).

A second defining view, which becomes dominant later, is privacy. Early on he cared about self-custody and DeFi as the point of crypto (post:1590118372947406849, 1590321177217536000). By late 2025 he argues "crypto without privacy is not crypto" (post:1973335855977680957, 1975942092745584825). He calls privacy "the last remaining 1,000x in crypto" and frames transparent chains as surveillance systems, because stablecoins can be frozen and every move is visible (post:1975451012870586656, 1978608224057045494). Zcash becomes his second high-conviction bet alongside SOL: "there are two major PMFs in crypto: state-free store of value and trading; the market gap that's asymmetrical is encryption" (post:2011143455695913066). This is a view that changed: earlier he rarely mentioned privacy, but by 2025-2026 it is central, and he says the industry slept on the cypherpunk ideal (post:1984989076152263037).

On crypto more broadly, he rejects the "we're still early" complacency: the industry has barely any users and no killer apps beyond speculation and stablecoins (post:1616873771763105793, 1702652187027943748). He sees most crypto activity as short-term games, grifters, and misaligned incentives (post:1669732264052137986, 1824126579418730798). The answer is building real products people want, not infra or narrative (post:1595173795505049641, 1683954694736211971). He often says the bottleneck is not blockspace but product-market fit (post:1628496935555420162, 1635137097266003970). He argues "apps come first, not infra" and the chain that cultivates product-focused builders will dominate (post:1792265762352066757, 1647618106154536962).

His political worldview is libertarian/accelerationist: private money, sovereign networks, unstoppable world computer are the mission (post:1825892618091307144, 1827469272685199390). He despises Canada's restrictions and mainstream media, and sees the final boss as existing bureaucracies, not other ecosystems (post:1727100358810640431, 1780332400385683691). He later says "if it can be seen, it can be seized" and treats encryption as the 2nd/5th Amendment of the internet (post:1992352055932170490, 1977534889927811122).

## How they reason and decide
He reasons from first principles and works backward from the problem, not upward from a tech stack. He says: "work backwards from problems, not upwards from a tech stack you feel obligated to sell" (post:1814684270713094448). He constantly asks whether a blockchain is necessary before building (post:1643382282642923520) and whether it solves a real problem (post:1636755645495119873). His product thinking is market-first: "The market in product-market-fit is more important than the product" (post:1772423337009987679).

He distrusts data without logic. He says "you can do logic without stats but you can't do stats without logic" (post:1766870816220696665) and "misinterpreting data is worse than having no data" (post:1895936975418601785). He warns against averages for skewed data and insists on medians (post:1741004640299409680). He often calls out cherrypicked metrics like TVL, TPS, and daily transactions as useless or gameable (post:1727768876941820076, 1751013215234904209). At the same time, he uses empirical data aggressively when comparing chains, e.g. Solana vs Ethereum fees and TPS (post:1768434227488423960, 1725010095082258434). The key is data must serve a coherent theory, not replace it.

He uses thought experiments and analogies but warns analogies always miss nuance and must be checked by first principles (post:1644511746017869824, 1661893956492312577). He steelmans opposing views and often asks community for feedback (post:1607780814267326469, 1617260237861396480). He believes communication is the highest leverage skill; if you can't explain an idea at three levels of abstraction, you don't understand it (post:1730093668206309608, 1740606261857546338).

His decisions are shaped by long time horizons and a refusal to predict non-linear events. He says humans are peasants when it comes to non-linearity and you'll never reliably time a market (post:1749622892776538489, 1762987969445527773). Instead he focuses on qualitative metrics and inputs: "if you focus on the inputs, the price takes care of itself over a non tourist time horizon" (post:1801687998834065823). He often asks what will not change in 5–10 years (post:1762216180616409529).

Incentives are his master lens: "Focus on incentives: show me the incentives, I'll show you the outcome" (post:1824477062616277233). He reasons that misaligned incentives explain most crypto failures, from L2s to VCs to memecoin casinos (post:1824126579418730798, 1783504046688440617).

From 2025 on, he increasingly frames markets through reflexivity and connectivity: "crypto turns information into value and value into information, so flows lead fundamentals" (post:1977460439509246322, 1977469313759244402). He uses physics metaphors (increase bandwidth, reduce latency) and momentum p = mv as models for startups and markets (post:1957209819167043906, 1897999761489183216).

He ships and says marketing is not enough: "you must both ship and yap — you can not neglect the other" (post:1851632832386400294). He believes the best way to learn is to post the wrong answer and update (post:2004184812827902314, 1992213999060955553).

## Risk attitude
@mert takes concentrated, high-conviction risk. He admits being "over invested, by a lot" in Solana RPC infrastructure and converted ~90% of his crypto holdings into SOL to stake with Helius (post:1737958166074138931, 1782423777831186725). He buys fear: "fear is at all-time highs just bought an unhealthy amount of SOL" (post:1596555289142374400). He jokes about leverage and being down bad, but after actual crashes he says "stop using leverage lol" and caps himself at 2x (post:1995308693844566221, 1976838108663722481).

He is not afraid to lose money and frames losses as tuition or alignment: "you are not soylana aligned until you've lost a tremendous amount of money" (post:1727497721660002576). He shares buying COIN at $300+ and SOL at $220+ as self-deprecating mistakes (post:1710334501916680347, 1724645039433748578). At the same time he repeats risk management rules: use a ledger, exchange, or multisig (post:1554879738480656385); gamble only with what you can afford to lose (post:1764190945120080070); don't time the market (post:1749622892776538489). Later he becomes explicitly security-obsessed: never use single-signer wallets, use hardware MFA, YubiKeys, aliases (post:2087897699341525260, 2114331240224510094). He also warns about privacy risk, stablecoins being frozen, and opsec (post:1980616575938941368, 2034640944893415639).

He treats volatility as noise and often says panic is the wrong response; the edge is a longer time horizon than others (post:1986464233249513586, 2095247433576088049). But he also says if you can't stomach a 20% drawdown, quit crypto (post:1987223256794120523).

## Favorite narratives
- **Solana**: bullish throughout, from early 2021 to 2026. He defended Solana during outages, FTX fallout, and FUD, saying "Solana is the best place to build apps because it already works and scales" (post:1819767810261741689). The view didn't change but deepened with each milestone: compression, Firedancer, DePIN, memecoin stress tests, disinflation. He now says "SOL under 500 is a free gift" and "trillions is not unreasonable" (post:1985629614786728332, 1967243667506372728).
- **Ethereum**: mixed and evolving. Early he said Ethereum and Solana both necessary and the real enemy is the traditional system (post:1723477898219180540). He respects Ethereum as battle-tested but calls its modular roadmap overhyped and L2s centralized. By 2023-2024 he was bearish on L2 proliferation, arguing integrated L1s win (post:1765032085058683218, 1775678636923695280). He never wrote Ethereum off entirely, but the tone became much more critical.
- **L2s/modularity**: increasingly bearish. He calls L2s premature optimizations, marketing terms, and sometimes jokes they're centralized servers (post:1774320167326978345, 1765854451074584840). This started as skepticism and became outright mockery.
- **NFTs**: mixed. Early he defended Solana NFTs and creator royalties (post:1578857602716692483). Later he calls many PFP projects not real brands (post:1621339250015838208) and says measuring NFT success by market cap should be a felony (post:1651437387346591745). However he remained bullish on NFT compression/cNFTs (post:1626771750699638786).
- **Memecoins**: mixed. He called them culture coins and crypto-native gaming, useful stress tests (post:1740486555473522844, 1765232464031596896). But he also calls celeb coins and scams net negative, and says memecoins are not an excuse for not building (post:1795309442105504013, 1802386246309745063). Later he says memecoins are crypto-native gaming / MMORPG (post:1886452551891235213).
- **DePIN, compression, Firedancer, ZK**: bullish throughout. These are Solana-native advantages.
- **Privacy/Zcash**: new dominant narrative from late 2025. He did not emphasize privacy early, but by 2025 it is "the last remaining 1,000x in crypto" and Zcash is "the most asymmetric opportunity in crypto" (post:1973335855977680957, 1976997313731088460). He says the industry slept on cypherpunk ideals and privacy is the third and final step after store of value and programmability (post:2013015373386567954).
- **Bitcoin**: mixed. He respects it as store of value but says its transparency is a bug, not a feature, and Bitcoin will not remain the only internet-native SoV (post:1991530704191255004, 1989731289621311870).
