# How @mert thinks

## Worldview
He is a builder first, not a pure trader. The oldest notes are soaked in Solana conviction: "stop obsessing over price and build some cool fucking things" (1595173795505049641). His base belief is that crypto's real problem is a shortage of useful products, not blockspace: "Crypto's biggest problem is that builders are influenced by short-term price and noise rather than building things people want" (1718382942396027173). He repeatedly says product and engineering matter more than narratives, but he also learns that narrative is a force: "Money flows as a function of story" (1914013385701655003).

His Solana thesis has been constant but the frame matured. Early he says "Solana is the best blockchain to build on" (1544721417652027394), and "try building things Only Possible on Solana" (1686939818251452416). Later he argues performance and decentralization are not a tradeoff because incentives align them: "Solana is the most performant and will also be the most decentralized over time" (1919547782853001721). By 2025 the pitch is bigger: "Solana will be the internet's market" (2007516882455482535), and "Solana is the most undervalued asset in all of finance" (1881864847610220753).

The clearest ideological shift is privacy. Early notes barely mention it. From late 2025 it becomes his dominant crusade: "crypto without privacy is not crypto" (1975942092745584825), "Privacy is the last remaining 1,000x in crypto" (1996194139453714779), "Blockchains are about verifiability, not transparency" (1981776875304132697). He says transparent chains create surveillance and seizure: "If it can be seen, it can be seized" (1992352055932170490). Zcash becomes the answer: "BTC is internet gold, SOL is internet capital markets, ZEC is the internet private Swiss vault" (1986422485513121904).

Crypto's ultimate purpose, in his view, is to upgrade capitalism. He calls it "capitalism 2.0" or "the universal API for money, finance and capitalism" (1920251576624574951, 2036041120497471741). Blockchains are rails for value like the internet is rails for information. He also holds that crypto is a technology, not a scam: "Crypto is a technology; calling it a scam is like calling wireless tech a scam because it facilitates fraud" (1745618772491182537).

He changed on the early/late question. In 2023 he said "the 'we're still early' narrative is harmful" (1616873771763105793). By 2025 he says the opposite: "It's still extremely early — scalable programmable chains are only ~3.5 years old" (1938545906699182410). The difference is that early meant "no users yet" back then; later it means "the golden period is now building."

## How they reason and decide
He works backwards from problems, not forwards from tech: "work backwards from problems, not upwards from a tech stack you feel obligated to sell" (1814684270713094448). He asks whether a blockchain is necessary: "when building a product in crypto — please ask yourself 'is a blockchain necessary for this?'" (1643382282642923520). He also says the market matters more than the product: "The market in product-market-fit is more important than the product" (1772423337009987679).

His reasoning is first-principles and physics-flavored. He repeatedly says data alone is not logic: "you can do logic without stats but you can't do stats without logic" (1766870816220696665). He distrusts cherry-picked metrics and benchmarks: "Data makes you draw worse conclusions in non-linear contexts; there is no substitute for pure reasoning and logic" (1765828640976064764). He likes Peter Thiel's definite vs indefinite optimism lens: Solana is definite optimism, Ethereum is indefinite optimism (1901725436297499134).

He treats incentives as the hidden structure: "show me the incentives, I'll show you the outcome" (1824477062616277233). He also thinks in reflexive loops: crypto turns information into value and value into information, so flows can lead fundamentals and cycles compress (1977460439509246322). He uses historical analogies but warns they miss nuance (1644511746017869824).

He actively tries to disprove himself: "the easiest way to get smarter is to try proving yourself wrong instead of trying to prove yourself right" (1923726027110404208). He asks the community for steelmans and feedback, and says "consistency of ideas is overrated if your ideas haven't evolved" (1794775091487891659).

He judges chains by builders, talent density, real revenue, and executable products, not TVL or price: "The best way to assess a blockchain ecosystem is the number of quality startups building on it without incentives" (1739889951477576012). Later: "revenue (REV) is a direct measure of economic demand for blockspace" (1901959394234884559).

## Risk attitude
He is high-conviction and concentrated. Early: "diversification is dumb" (1784298467768070302). He says he converted about 90% of holdings to SOL (1782423777831186725), and by 2026: "all in on ZEC + SOL" (2019140937314890067). But he also prescribes a barbell: "use 5-20% of your cash for high risk fun shit if you want" and hold the rest in high-conviction names (1974843290865238200). He says concentrate on ~3 assets max with extreme research (1989047146302394844).

Leverage is a live contradiction. He jokes "there is this thing called leverage" (1957858854957359428), and once says "if you're not getting liquidated here, you weren't trying hard enough" (1909149100659912720). After the October 10 crash he writes he "reduced all leverage to max 2x" (1976838108663722481). Later he is plain: "ok can you guys like stop using leverage lol" (1995308693844566221).

He stresses time horizon: "the biggest edge in crypto is having a time-horizon longer than one cycle" (1862898721383444540). Volatility is the price of admission: "if you can not stomach volatility you should not be here" (1995302939603177492), and "if you cant stomach a 20% drawdown, you should completely quit all crypto markets" (1987223256794120523).

Security and privacy are part of risk. He says use a ledger, exchange, or multisig to avoid custodial risk (1554879738480656385). Later he hardens: "never use a single-signer setup for your money. just do not do this. it does not matter if it's a hardware wallet" (2087897699341525260). He advises opsec audits: email aliases, hardware MFA, no SMS, revoke connected apps (2087897699341525260). He says privacy reduces attack surface: the less you leak, the less hackers can use (2046319222352167199).

He buys fear and dips, not tops: "fear is at all-time highs just bought an unhealthy amount of SOL — ama" (1596555289142374400); "buying some SOL here" (1869915799781290146); "been buying the dips for like 3 weeks now lmao irresponsibly overweight" (1997258213352505434).

## Favorite narratives
**Solana — bullish, with a maturing frame.** Always his base. Early: technical superiority and "Only Possible on Solana" (1686939818251452416). Middle: app ecosystem and builder support. Late: "Solana is the internet's market" and "internet capital markets" (2007516882455482535, 1882174607194730644). He does not say ETH must die: "ETH is not going to die; it's the most battle-tested thing in production" (1738082638232006834).

**Privacy/Zcash — the late conversion, now the loudest view.** In 2021-2024 notes he rarely mentions privacy. By 2025-2026 he is evangelical: "Privacy is the last remaining 1,000x in crypto" (1996194139453714779), "Zcash is the most obvious mispricing in crypto" (1976997313731088460), and calls private money "the 5th Amendment of the internet" (1977534889927811122).

**Ethereum/L2s — mixed to bearish.** He respects Ethereum's history but sees it as academically slow; he repeatedly attacks L2/modular scaling as marketing and centralization. However he is not a pure hater: "Ethereum and Solana are both necessary and pursue different paths" (1723477898219180540).

**Memecoins — mixed.** He does not moralize against them: "memecoins/culture coins are a new category enabled by crypto rails" (1740486555473522844), and later "Memecoins are crypto-native gaming" (1886452551891235213). But he criticizes celeb coins and PVP excess.

**Bitcoin — mixed.** Early he says "Bitcoin is the most egalitarian thing we've ever seen" (1762330881794990287). Later he says it is "too big to fail but will likely see slow directional decline" and won't remain the only internet-native SoV (1991530704191255004).

**AI — bullish as a tool, skeptical of hype.** He says AI is best thought of as a programming language (2010056034925326605) and is bullish on it finding/fixing vulnerabilities (2063914405922746783), but mocks AI slop and AI-coin narratives.

**XRP/Cardano — bearish throughout.** They are zombie chains and overvalued to him (1993040384419938449, 1772430809204424882).
