# How @mert thinks

## Worldview

The foundation of @mert's worldview is Solana exceptionalism, but it evolves. In the 2021–2023 era he was a pure Solana missionary: Solana is "the best blockchain to build on," its issues are mostly "buggy software, not fundamental flaws" that competent validators can fix, and price is a misleading signal compared with users, activity, and fundamentals (post:1533128448893403138; post:1612494699046252547; post:1591078397060730880). By 2023–2024 he sharpens this into a builder thesis: the bottleneck in crypto is not blockspace or scaling, it is "a lack of useful products with product-market fit," and the chain that cultivates the most product-focused founders wins (post:1628496935555420162; post:1629294554858831873). From mid-2024 onward he layers on a bigger frame: Solana is becoming "the internet's capital market" or "decentralized NASDAQ," the place stocks, stablecoins, RWAs, prediction markets, and every asset eventually trade (post:1920459292282351738; post:1978071038702309577; post:1994194795695108363). He says Solana is "the Silicon Valley of finance" and "the most undervalued asset in all of finance" (post:1967231022686409180; post:1881864847610220753).

His other major shift is toward privacy. Earlier he is a self-custody and DeFi advocate, warning about CEXes and custodial risk (post:1590118372947406849; post:1590321177217536000). From late 2025 onward privacy becomes the center: "crypto without privacy is not crypto," "the point of blockchains is verifiability, not surveillance," and "if it can be seen, it can be seized" (post:1975942092745584825; post:1981776875304132697; post:1993138992670556218). He reframes the history as "the first step was proving internet-native money worked, the second scaling it, the third and final step will be encrypting it" (post:2013015373386567954). Zcash becomes his main new thesis alongside SOL: "Zcash is the most obvious mispricing in crypto" and the best-prepared privacy system for quantum risk (post:1976997313731088460; post:2038920947831394774). He still insists this is not anti-Solana: "the top 5 will be BTC, SOL, ZEC, HYPE, and one more spot" (post:1984287634160750846).

Underneath this is a conviction that crypto is "capitalism 2.0" — the universal API for money, finance, and property rights, more important than AI because coordinating humans is upstream of coordinating robots (post:1920251576624574951; post:1980982083414552700). He routinely argues that crypto evolves capitalism "from the fax age to the internet age," removing middlemen and making markets planetary-scale (post:2019182910704656688; post:2036041120497471741). He regards pessimism as crowded and wrong; believing tomorrow can be better is itself an edge (post:2071349856027754716; post:1825949474675437950).

## How they reason and decide

@mert reasons from first principles, often explicitly rejecting analogies and historical patterns as decisive. He says "the biggest logical fallacy in crypto is 'high hardware requirements imply centralization,'" and that people wrongly apply "linear forecasting in a field dominated by non-linearities" (post:1738961951567249571; post:1667342782074068993). His style is physical and mathematical: "increase bandwidth, reduce latency," "stake-weighted QoS is p = mv," and probability arguments like P(success) = p_i^n for modular systems (post:1729172578244215261; post:1731774254604050823). Later he formalizes this as "physics-based thinking: ask which problems can be solved and which are barred by the laws of physics" (post:1957209819167043906).

He prefers logic to data, but not naively. He says "you can do logic without stats but you can't do stats without logic" and warns that "misinterpreting data is worse than having no data" (post:1817641540053029129; post:1895936975418601785). He distrusts vanity metrics like TVL, TPS, daily active addresses, and raw transaction counts, insisting on revenue, fees, and real production data instead (post:1727768876941820076; post:1817942871456276645; post:1951947975016145326). He often frames debates around time horizons: if a five-year thesis is affected by five-hour price action, reconsider why you are here (post:1979122321467044071; post:1822307691814928759).

He also reasons through reflexive loops. As he puts it, "narrative dominates fundamentals because connectivity makes the fastest-spreading element dominant"; crypto converts information into value and value into information, so flows lead fundamentals in both directions (post:1961257379296940427; post:1977460439509246322). This view grows stronger later: "the idea of a cycle is dead; reflexivity in an increasingly connected world makes narratives violent but short-lived" (post:1979143383286255995). He tries to prove himself wrong rather than right: "the easiest way to get smarter is to try proving yourself wrong instead of trying to prove yourself right" (post:1923726027110404208).

Concretely, he works backward from problems, not upward from tech. His repeated instruction is "work backwards from problems, not upwards from a tech stack you feel obligated to sell" and "build things people want" (post:1814684270713094448; post:1792274339670188135). In business, he evaluates by market gaps, user traction, and revenue, not by narrative or VC enthusiasm (post:1871181017895096557; post:1939309018067194209).

## Risk attitude

He is an aggressive, concentrated risk-taker who buys fear. The early note is explicit: "fear is at all-time highs just bought an unhealthy amount of SOL" (post:1596555289142374400). He celebrates "buying the dips" and "irresponsibly overweight" positions (post:1908475842373525803; post:1997258213352505434). But the sophistication grows: he warns that being bearish on a speculative asset requires more information and risk than being bullish, and that one random variable can prove bears wrong (post:1868618760858321250). He frames risk through asymmetry: the most obvious bet is a high-conviction, undervalued asset over a multi-year horizon (post:1908994824910368775).

He does not romanticize risk. He warns "if you can't stomach a 20% drawdown, you should completely quit all crypto markets," and after the 2026 crash he caps leverage at 2x, saying leverage is a core problem because perps cause total loss (post:1987223256794120523; post:1976838108663722481; post:2017674117164757159). He also gives serious opsec advice: use multisigs, hardware wallets, email aliases, no single-signer setups, and never expose seed phrases online (post:2087897699341525260; post:2034640944893415639). He treats safety as a product problem: stablecoins can be frozen by issuers, CEXes can be pressured, and self-custody is "a VPN for money" (post:1984767763681067174; post:2038588209194549360).

## Favorite narratives

**Solana** — Bullish the entire period, but the reasoning matures. Early it is tech superiority and "only possible on Solana." Later it is product/founder ecosystem and capital markets. He admits Solana has outages, congestion, and implementation bugs, but separates those from design flaws (post:1646163360302481409; post:1777400489488900102). He says competitors "obsess over Solana while Solana obsesses over customers" (post:2110655970674372706).

**Ethereum** — Mixed and often contradictory. He respects ETH and Vitalik ("ETH is not going to die; it's the most battle-tested thing in production"), but is bearish on the modular/L2 roadmap and repeatedly mocks "ETH maxis" for bad-faith arguments (post:1738082638232006834; post:1791511243326361844). He says Ethereum and Solana are both necessary and the real enemy is the traditional system (post:1723477898219180540).

**L2s / modularity** — Loudly bearish. He calls L2s "centralized servers settling on centralized L2s," and says modular architecture multiplies conditional probabilities of failure (post:1774320167326978345; post:1731774254604050823). He is softer on "real L2s" like Arbitrum and Fuel, but mostly treats modularity as a marketing term (post:1727374240671003027; post:1775678636923695280).

**Privacy / Zcash** — Started as a DeFi/self-custody concern, then became the dominant thesis. "Privacy is the last remaining 1,000x in crypto," "encrypt the money," and "the last PvE" are recurring frames (post:1973335855977680957; post:1985438221765873898; post:2010691599538524486). He frames privacy as choice and fungibility, not crime: "TAM for freedom > TAM for crime" (post:1987466906693956091).

**AI** — Cautiously bullish. Early he calls ChatGPT a major breakthrough; later he says AI is the most bullish crypto catalyst because it forces security and privacy work, but also warns of surveillance and that AI agents will dump on each other (post:1598736652473700352; post:2037913622513324099; post:2017597732559720487). He is skeptical of AI coin hype as narrative slop (post:2012624924100448601).

**Memecoins** — Mixed, trending more charitable. He says memecoins are crypto-native gaming and useful stress-tests, but also that celebrity shitters are extractive and most are scams (post:1765232464031596896; post:1802386246309745063; post:1958637084274516149). Eventually he argues finance itself is becoming entertainment and "everything is a memecoin" (post:2082847079978950680; post:2026280868889632910).

**DePIN / prediction markets / Hyperliquid / NEAR** — DePIN early excitement ("the killer use case") fades into the broader capital-markets thesis but never disappears (post:1755751339382956229; post:1801854008581079259). Prediction markets and Hyperliquid become later favorites. By late 2026 he expands to multi-ecosystem pluralism: Solana, Zcash, and NEAR are all doing uniquely exciting work (post:2106745924672495856; post:2108073189087875167).

## Who they respect

He respects builders and first-principles thinkers over pundits. Consistent names: Anatoly Yakovenko (toly) for systems thinking and willingness to be wrong publicly (post:1595176907905159173; post:1689447294578409474; post:1946174706912014480); Vitalik Buterin for technical depth and fairness (post:1608593786392743937; post:1740579967757681086); Steve Jobs for product obsession and communication ("the doers are the major thinkers") (post:1786429914968662534; post:1845133209157190050); Balaji Srinivasan for contrarian vision (post:1543271437242433539; post:1784611455448297883); Nassim Taleb for fragililty, non-linearity, and skin-in-the-game (post:1743104821735928175; post:1868618760858321250); Peter Thiel for definite/indefinite optimism and contrarian thinking (post:1901725436297499134; post:2022023187467579479); David Deutsch for knowledge creation and explanatory depth (post:1824926053242143093; post:2043452963189063861). He also respects operators like Kyle Samani, Armani Ferrante, the Jito team, the Helius cofounders, Zooko and the Zcash cryptographers, ZachXBT, and Sean Bowe (post:1681302524660416516; post:1766518201263780230; post:1984954958639174112; post:2063699063221191019).

He also mocks himself relentlessly: baldness, Canada/Turkey, being overinvested, buying tops, and being a reply-guy.

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He admits buying tops: COIN at $300+ and SOL at $220+ with self-deprecating humor (post:1710334501916680347; post:1724645039433748578). Later he owns roundtripping gains: "look yeah I lost generational wealth by not taking profits but at least I took screenshots when I had money" (post:1971603175678628334). He admits leverage losses and being margin called, then says to stop using leverage (post:1995308693844566221; post:2019413411009118304). He admits being wrong on timeframes: "I was wrong on the timeframe," and that being too toxic cost reputation (post:1800242462734074216; post:1792348573821989103). He also concedes Solana execution could have been better during congestion, calling it iteration and learning (post:1881174078637199607; post:1881162334305210420).
