# How @mert thinks

## Worldview

From the earliest notes, @mert operates from one fixed center: Solana is the best place to build real products, and price is a poor substitute for product reality. He repeatedly says the chain’s advantage is technical composability, raw performance, and a builder culture that ships, while most problems are software bugs that can be fixed rather than fundamental design failures (1533128448893403138, 1520081230972207104). That conviction hardened across the bear market: Solana’s “resilience against technical, economic, and social stressors proves its strength; empiricism beats theory” (1669447354775117824). He later sharpened the same view: Solana’s issues are implementation bugs, not design flaws, and “implementation errors are trivial to swap out while design errors are fundamental” (1777400489488900102, 1777853400610119781).

The second constant is his builder-first view of crypto. He argues the industry’s bottleneck is not blockspace, scaling, or UX; it is “a lack of useful products with product-market fit” (1628496935555420162, 1635137097266003970, 1786899901801992289). Builders should start from real problems and then ask whether a blockchain is necessary, not work upward from a chain (1636755645495119873, 1643382282642923520). That later becomes a fixed style: “work backwards from problems, not upwards from a tech stack you feel obligated to sell” (1814684270713094448).

Decentralization is not a slogan for him. He rejects the claim that high hardware requirements imply centralization; node operation is driven by incentives and resilience, not merely low barriers (1738961951567249571, 1686216246797168640, 1841557960050311382). He frames decentralization as robustness against non-linearity and sees Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana as the only chains that have demonstrated it (1739840312929874339). By 2024 he is even more pointed: decentralization only matters when something bad happens, like security in web2 (1766857965049401614). Yet he also says decentralization for its own sake is a mistake; first you need something worth decentralizing (1774110064250347725).

His architecture view matures from full integrated-chain aggression to a more layered position. Early he treats L2s and modularity as marketing, with each added component multiplying conditional probabilities of failure (1727008463191203947, 1731774254604050823, 1775678636923695280). He mocks L2s for recreating TradFi with worse trust assumptions (1728122980021030941, 1774320167326978345). But he is not purely tribal: by late 2023 he says Ethereum and Solana are both necessary and pursue different paths to the same endgame (1723477898219180540). Later he accepts that multiple chains can win and that crypto attention is not zero-sum (1880692290664157658, 1883287838541963750). Still, Solana remains the center: “Solana is the most usable, most battle-tested, fastest-scaling ecosystem” (1955574289064661005, 1954863582945873963).

From 2025 onward, the largest new layer is privacy. He begins preaching that “privacy is the last remaining 1,000x in crypto” and that “crypto without privacy is not crypto” (1975942092745584825, 1985438221765873898). The point of blockchains, he argues, is verifiability, not transparency; transparency was only the 2008 workaround for double-spend, and ZK now solves it (1981776875304132697, 1986387764557062503). This is tied to his older self-custody instinct: centrally issued stablecoins can be frozen, so self-custody is not enough by itself (1978608224057045494, 1984767763681067174).

Throughout, he describes crypto as the next evolution of capitalism: “crypto is the universal API for money, finance, and capitalism” (2036041120497471741), “internet capital markets” (1882520136776888593), and “crypto is to finance and the economy what the industrial revolution was to manufacturing” (1874267279774670895). He also frames the broader mission as sovereignty: private money, sovereign networks, an unstoppable world computer (1825892618091307144, 1827469272685199390).

## How they reason and decide

His reasoning style is first-principles and engineering-centric. He repeatedly says you can do logic without stats, but you can’t do stats without logic; cherry-picked data is fraud (1766870816220696665, 1817641540053029129). He distrusts averages for skewed data and insists on medians (1741004640299409680). He uses empirical production data and napkin math to compare TPS, fees, volume, and node counts, especially when defending Solana (1725010095082258434, 1768434227488423960).

He reasons through probabilities when attacking modularity: each extra component — DA layer, sequencer, execution environment — is another conditional probability, so success probability keeps dropping (1731774254604050823, 1733970360314265673). He also frames failure in terms of recovery rather than prevention: with the exception of Bitcoin, probability of no liveness/consistency failure on a major chain is 0%; what matters is recovery (1732066255090127176, 1735740938230620439).

A recurring pattern is problem-first product thinking. He asks founders to identify a gap in the market, not to push a tech stack, and says the market is more important than the product in product-market fit (1758546260653687141, 1772423337009987679). He advises founders not to ask users for solutions; users know their problems, not the solution (1790525595748540500). He is comfortable changing his mind when convinced, as when he came around on Toly’s “economic security is a meme” after roughly 200 tweets (1799581312358948979).

He uses analogies constantly — blockchains as countries, Solana as Mac vs Windows, modularity as Voldemort’s horcruxes — but he also warns that analogies miss nuance and must be checked against first principles (1661893956492312577, 1770273500751642938, 1640444479982149643). Later he shifts toward physics, connectivity, and reflexivity as master concepts: ask which problems are barred by physics, and understand that in increasingly connected systems the fastest-spreading narrative becomes dominant (1957209819167043906, 1962462159427760512, 2026280868889632910).

His decisions are heavily time-horizon driven. He treats short-term price as noise and qualitative fundamentals as the edge (1724818966827159849). By 2025 that becomes more explicit: “you can get much richer than other people by simply having a longer time horizon than them” (1986464233249513586). He also evaluates incentives before outcomes: “show me the incentives, I’ll show you the outcome” (1824477062616277233).

## Risk attitude

He is an aggressive, concentrated risk-taker who repeatedly says he is over-invested in Solana. Early on he wrote: “fear is at all-time highs just bought an unhealthy amount of SOL — ama” (1596555289142374400). He converts most holdings into SOL to stake with Helius and says diversification is dumb (1782423777831186725, 1784298467768070302). At the same time, he separates trading from long-term investing: you can capitalize on short-term market stupidity without brainwashing yourself long-term (1742034846573056263).

He is skeptical of market timing and says humans are bad at non-linear prediction (1749622892776538489, 1762987969445527773). But he also posts deliberately absurd risk lines as both jokes and motivational pressure: “if you’re not getting liquidated here, you werent trying hard enough bid more don’t be a pussy” (1909149100659912720). After a real stress, he became more risk-aware: reducing leverage to max 2x after the October 10 crash (1976838108663722481). He tells people to gamble only what they can afford to lose, but also says crypto drawdowns are normal and quitters should leave (1764190945120080070, 1987223256794120523).

His risk thinking is deeply operational. He worries about RPC failures, priority fees, sandwich attacks, MEV, and transaction landing much more than about narrative FUD. He pleads with teams to consult RPC providers and add dynamic priority fees (1737623561525735652, 1882435032133812491). He later extends that to OPSEC: use multisigs, hardware MFA, email aliases, and avoid single-signer setups (2087897699341525260, 2045582183767916789).

His newest risk focus is privacy. He warns stablecoins can be frozen, public onchain behavior can be doxed, and quantum/harvest-now-decrypt-later is a concave risk (1984767763681067174, 2000227211614503397, 1997230876003045808). He frames privacy as insurance: “you’ll wanna have some capital stashed away on a private chain as insurance” (1975451012870586656).

## Favorite narratives

Solana is his central bull narrative from the first batch to the last. It changes from “Solana is undervalued” (1520790104217997313) and “the best blockchain to build on” (1565406988477112321) to “Solana is the best payments infrastructure in production today” (1742248194820710442) and later “the internet’s capital market” (1967231022686409180). At times he warns against “eth killer” framing and says Solana does not need Ethereum to die (1648371410409078785, 1724643431203369308). The stance remains bullish, but the supporting logic evolves from tech specs to liquidity, talent density, and institutional-scale rails.

Ethereum is mixed for most of the record. Early he compares it unfavorably on scaling and culture (1650708656940351490, 1664474869843611649), but he also says ETH will not die and is the most battle-tested thing in production (1738082638232006834, 1744102476633022537). His L2 bearishness is more persistent: L2s are prematurely optimized, centralized, and largely marketing until proven otherwise (1770103008661065829, 1774211198881325397). That softens only slightly into a view that L2s should at least use real proofs and that sovereign blockspace is a legitimate reason to build on Solana L2s (1727374240671003027, 1802166607612924194).

NFTs start as a mixed story: royalties matter, most PFPs are not brands, but compression is a game-changing primitive (1578857602716692483, 1621339250015838208, 1626771750699638786). Compression remains bullish into 2024–2025 via ZK compression and digital assets (1804244166584516865). Memecoins are treated as structurally real speculation, not an insult: they are culture coins and crypto-native gaming, stress-tests for the network, and not the reason useful apps fail (1740486555473522844, 1783504046688440617, 1886452551891235213).

His newest narrative is Zcash/private money. It is absent from early notes and arrives as a large shift in late 2025: ZEC is “the most obvious mispricing in crypto,” Bitcoin is internet gold, Solana is internet capital markets, ZEC is the internet private Swiss vault (1976997313731088460, 1986422485513121904). By 2026 privacy becomes the top-level story, tied to cypherpunk history and the claim that “privacy is the last PvE” (2058544127776633175, 2077527814660927596).

## Who they respect

He respects builders and systems thinkers over influencers. Toly is the most repeated authority figure, cited across every period (1595176907905159173, 1689447294578409474, 1799581312358948979, 2117244773656613115). Vitalik also earns repeated respect for engineering and openness (1608593786392743937, 2008934824195871208). Balaji, Steve Jobs, Taleb, Munger, Bezos, Thiel, and David Deutsch are recurring intellectual references (1376192692506476544, 1620817863593586688, 1743104821735928175, 1766135468305928658, 1821382880721064190). He also respects operators like Jito, Tensor, Phantom, Hivemapper, Superteam, and the Helius team (1610745715281784832, 1630697780527128578, 1661029988072583169, 1787900215703347521). Later he gives strong respect to Zooko and Zcash cryptographers (1983991402418860302, 1984954958639174112, 2063699063221191019).

## Who and what they mock

He mocks bad-faith critics, lazy media, and fake metrics. Recurring targets are Bitcoin maxis, Ethereum maxis, L2 marketers, Cardano, XRP, SBF, Gary Gensler, Elizabeth Warren, and crypto media (1571131111161606145, 1591990452646985731, 1635400223706214401, 1728126535620784530, 1737971787269317069). He treats chart traders as “modern day astrologers” (1674235013833842689, 2101137641601450093). He also mocks himself and his own group: bald manlets, Canadians, reply guys, and people who buy tops (1732165301452652588, 1788970185031836106). His mockery is often structural: TVL, TPS, and daily transactions are called easily gamed and useless as proof of value (1727508219893870862, 1727768876941820076).

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He admits buying tops with self-deprecating humor: COIN at $300+ and SOL at $220+ (1710334501916680347, 1724645039433748578). He later says he lost generational wealth by not taking profits: “look yeah I lost generational wealth by not taking profits but at least I took screenshots when I had money” (1971603175678628334, 1971558252640162243). He admits he is “over invested, by a lot” in Solana RPC infrastructure (1737958166074138931). He owns being too aggressive and costing reputation when correcting misinformation (1800242462734074216, 1792348573821989103). After Solana performance incidents, he says execution could have been better and frames it as iteration rather than pretending nothing happened (1881174078637199607, 1881162334305210420). He also concedes time-call errors and treats updating publicly as normal: a functioning brain updates with new information (1994117951688200224, 1992213999060955553).
