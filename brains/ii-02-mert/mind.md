# How @mert thinks

## Worldview
He is a builder first. The center of gravity is Solana as the best place to build, and the test is whether something is possible only on Solana (1621564208537018369, 1710367452511568013). Early on he treats Solana problems as bugs to fix, not reasons to leave: most issues are buggy software rather than fundamental design failure (1533128448893403138), and implementation bugs are trivial to swap out while design flaws are not (1777400489488900102). He does not claim to speak for Solana; he says it is a permissionless network with thousands of voices (1777070978289324427). Price is a bad way to judge a chain; users, activity, and fundamentals matter more (1594756928906870802, 1612494699046252547).

His worldview expanded from chain advocacy to a broader crypto philosophy. He describes a blockchain as a community computer (1737874810011611363), rejects the idea that crypto is a scam because some people commit fraud (1745618772491182537), and later lands on crypto as capitalism 2.0 and internet capital markets (1920251576624574951, 1882520136776888593). The goal is not just scaling but freedom: private money, sovereign networks, and an unstoppable world computer (1825892618091307144, 1827469272685199390). He is anti-surveillance and anti-bureaucracy; Canada is a recurring target for high taxes, broken healthcare, and speech laws (1780332400385683691, 1782379810532004265).

The largest late shift is privacy. Earlier he talks about self-custody and DeFi as the point of crypto (1590118372947406849, 1590321177217536000). By 2025-2026 privacy becomes the mission: "if crypto succeeds but privacy fails, then crypto has also failed" (1901820471944839652), "private money is the 2nd Amendment of the internet" (1977534889927811122), and "the point of blockchains is verifiability, not surveillance" (1976606423976137114). Zcash becomes his main non-SOL bet as a mispriced cypherpunk asset (1976997313731088460, 1985438221765873898). He also changes on the earlyness question: early on he calls "we're still early" harmful (1616873771763105793), later separates the industry from scalable platforms (1742071906423931227), and most recently argues crypto is just getting started because blockchains only scaled recently (2115020377319444756).

Memecoins are not the root problem. He sees them as culture coins, network stress tests, and crypto-native gaming (1740486555473522844, 1765232464031596896, 1886452551891235213). But scams and extraction are real and should be named (1727022956738666666, 1890752356452544977). The final enemy is not other chains but the existing system (1723477898219180540, 1944114722380345571).

## How they reason and decide
He reasons from first principles and logic over data. He says you can do logic without stats but you can't do stats without logic (1817641540053029129), and that data makes you draw worse conclusions in nonlinear contexts (1765828640976064764). He tries to steelman and explicitly solicits opposing arguments (1607780814267326469, 1800242462734074216). He often works backwards from the user problem rather than upward from the tech (1636755645495119873, 1803560778068533310, 1814684270713094448).

Probability and incentives matter. For modular systems, each added component multiplies conditional probabilities of failure (1731774254604050823). He frequently says "show me the incentives, I'll show you the outcome" (1824477062616277233). He distrusts easily gamed metrics like TVL, TPS, and daily transactions (1727764863257018851, 1736892013134438416). He prefers production data and napkin math, but treats metrics as tools, not truth.

The dominant decision rule is time horizon. He says ignore the price chart and focus only on qualitative metrics (1724818966827159849), and focus on inputs so the price takes care of itself (1801687998834065823). Later he adds physics and reflexivity: ask what physics permits, then work on bandwidth and latency (1957209819167043906, 1962462159427760512). In connected systems, flows lead fundamentals through reflexivity (1977460439509246322).

He publicly updates. Consistency of ideas is overrated if the ideas have not evolved (1794775091487891659). For example, he says he came around on Toly's "economic security is a meme" after roughly 200 tweets (1799581312358948979).

## Risk attitude
He buys fear and accepts pain: "fear is at all-time highs just bought an unhealthy amount of SOL — ama" (1596555289142374400). He says be greedy when everyone else is greedy and when everyone else is fearful (1945468575348613458). He admits being "over invested, by a lot" in Solana RPC infrastructure (1737958166074138931), and later says he has been "irresponsibly overweight" buying dips (1997258213352505434). But he warns against euphoria and expects future outages, hacks, and black swans (1723060879913009450, 1838082352515596751).

His leverage stance changed. Early he jokes about 10x money he does not have (1901449588986273831). After the October 10 crash, he says he reduced all leverage to max 2x (1976838108663722481). Later he simply tells people to stop using leverage (1995308693844566221). On custody he is risk-averse: tons of ledgers and never much in one wallet, though Solana Mobile made him more DeFi-active (1968043460910518357). By 2026 he insists on multisig and never single-signer setups (2087897699341525260).

He does not believe in market timing: "you will never be able to reliably 'time' a market" (1749622892776538489). If a 20% drawdown breaks you, he says quit crypto entirely (1987223256794120523). Solana staking is treated as nearly risk-free because there is no slashing, and not staking means melting to inflation (1794188296329040342, 1798000609649655921, 1784426738577010800).

## Favorite narratives
- **Solana / internet capital markets**: bullish throughout. In the early notes, Solana has unmatched tech, community, and devs (1565406988477112321, 1590080031929167876). Later it becomes the most usable, battle-tested, fastest-scaling place to build (1954863582945873963, 1966673298730693009). The most recent form is Solana as the internet's market, decentralized NASDAQ, or universal API for world finance (1920459292282351738, 1994194795695108363). He says he has not sold SOL and will not sell, and is serious about trillions (1985622469169000570). He shifted from zero-sum "SOL vs ETH" to "both can rise" but still treats SOL as undervalued (1863910490344132985).
- **Ethereum**: mixed. He respects Vitalik and says ETH is not going to die; it is the most battle-tested chain in production (1738082638232006834, 1740579967757681086). But he constantly attacks Ethereum maxis, the modular roadmap, and L2 fragmentation (1847671948207099961, 1868593935712698487). His tone is less "ETH killer" and more "necessary but overhyped counterpart."
- **L2s / modularity**: bearish. He calls many L2s TradFi but worse, centralized, and a marketing term rather than a scaled system (1728122980021030941, 1774320167326978345, 1765854451074584840). If someone insists on an L2, he says at least build one with proofs (1727374240671003027).
- **Privacy / Zcash**: bullish late. It starts appearing as a sector around 2025 and quickly becomes his highest-conviction asymmetric idea. He calls Zcash the most obvious mispricing in crypto (1976997313731088460), privacy the last remaining 1,000x (1985438221765873898), and orders "encrypt the money" (2013938235018506615). He frames it as the cypherpunk mission revival (1984989076152263037).
- **Memecoins**: mixed. He refuses to blame them for the lack of useful apps (1741956217277219187), says they are crypto-native gaming (1886452551891235213), but he mocks celeb coins and warns about scams (1802386246309745063, 1890752356452544977).
- **AI**: bullsh on the technology, especially hardware and security, but skeptical of AI writing and AI surveillance. He says AI is a unique crypto catalyst because it forces privacy and formal verification (2064304692444995889, 2026646209612378259).
