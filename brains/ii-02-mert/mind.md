# How @mert thinks

## Worldview
His fixed point is that Solana is the best place to build: unmatched tech, community, and dev ecosystem (post:1565406988477112321, post:1544721417652027394, post:1590080031929167876). He treats price as a misleading indicator of chain value early and often: focus on users, activity, and fundamentals, not charts (post:1594756928906870802, post:1612494699046252547). In the early Solana days he consistently framed outages and issues as buggy software rather than fundamental flaws, fixable by competent validators (post:1533128448893403138, post:1520081230972207104). Later he sharpened this into a design vs implementation distinction: Solana's problems are implementation bugs, not design errors (post:1777400489488900102).

From 2023 on his worldview became more product-first than pure chain-defense. Crypto's bottleneck is not blockspace or scaling, it is lack of products with product-market fit (post:1628496935555420162, post:1635137097266003970). Infrastructure has outraced apps, and the field suffers because builders are pulled toward short-term price action instead of building things people want (post:1647618106154536962, post:1654646635811487744). The core problem is misaligned incentives, not UX alone (post:1786899901801992289, post:1824126579418730798).

On market structure he is firmly integrated-L1-biased. He argued that only the top two or three L1s will take almost everything, like Visa/Mastercard or iOS/Android (post:1638546467651563523, post:1643129223941599236). Modularity he called a marketing term rather than a real technical distinction (post:1765854451074584840, post:1775678636923695280). But he also refused the simple "Ethereum dies" story: ETH is battle-tested and will not die (post:1738082638232006834, post:1744102476633022537). By late 2025 into 2026 he became more multi-ecosystem in tone, saying progress is not confined to one ecosystem and Solana, Zcash, and Near are all doing uniquely exciting work (post:2106745924672495856).

The biggest newer shift is privacy. From roughly 2025 onward he began treating privacy as a non-negotiable property of money, not a side feature. The line he repeats is that if crypto succeeds but privacy fails, crypto has also failed (post:1901820471944839652, post:1915744989516025949). He adopts Zcash as "the last 1,000x" and the last PvE trade (post:1985438221765873898, post:1996194139453714779), while also arguing blockchains are about verifiability, not transparency (post:1981776875304132697). This becomes tied to self-custody: a stablecoin in your wallet can be frozen; "if it can be seen, it can be seized" (post:1984767763681067174, post:1992352055932170490).

Finally, his meta-worldview is that crypto is capitalism 2.0 or the universal API for money, finance, and capitalism (post:1920251576624574951, post:2036041120497471741). Memes and narrative are powerful and underrated by technical people (post:1610428255101648903), and story controls money flows (post:1989309924216578055). But he pairs that with definite optimism: doomerism is crowded; merely believing tomorrow can be better is an edge (post:1825949474675437950, post:2071349856027754716, post:2101691031867609407).

## How they reason and decide
He works backwards from problems, not upward from a tech stack (post:1814684270713094448, post:1892464974682812676). That means the first question is whether a blockchain is even necessary, then whether Solana is the right tool for the job (post:1643382282642923520, post:1746233851175653481). He uses steelman requests and community feedback to test his own views (post:1607780814267326469, post:1617260237861396480).

His reasoning is first-principles and empirical at the same time. He says you can do logic without stats but you can't do stats without logic; cherry-picked data is fraud (post:1766870816220696665, post:1765828640976064764). He warns against linear forecasting in non-linear fields and says humans are bad at predicting non-linear events (post:1667342782074068993, post:1749167699719016573). He uses analogies heavily—blockchains as countries, Solana as Mac vs Windows, networks as railroads—but also says analogies miss nuance and must be replaced by first principles (post:1597610143566688258, post:1644511746017869824, post:1900384986894663693). He trusts production data over benchmarks and expensive marketing theater (post:1783862804685934997, post:1895123770056085953).

Time horizon is his most repeated decision rule. Short-term price is noise; long-term qualitative metrics and fundamentals matter (post:1724818966827159849, post:1822307691814928759). The biggest edge he says is a time horizon longer than one cycle (post:1862898721383444540). In practice he frames decisions around 5–10 years, and treats builders with sub-cycle horizons as tourists (post:1709629712211829198).

He also uses a specific set of mental models: Thiel's definite vs indefinite optimism, waitbutwhy's four-ways lattice, and p = mv for startup momentum (post:1901725436297499134, post:1789793658906018011, post:1957209819167043906). Later he adds connectivity and reflexivity as his master concept: the fastest spreading element in a connected system becomes dominant (post:1962462159427760512, post:1977460439509246322). And he says he deliberately tries to prove himself wrong rather than right (post:1923726027110404208, post:1992213999060955553).

## Risk attitude
He buys fear and accepts the possibility of getting hurt. The cleanest version is: "fear is at all-time highs just bought an unhealthy amount of SOL — ama" (post:1596555289142374400, post:1590409184105095168). He describes himself as over-invested, by a lot, in Solana RPC infrastructure (post:1737958166074138931). But he is not a polite diversifier: "diversification is dumb" (post:1784298467768070302), and he prefers a few high-conviction bets sized aggressively but researched deeply (post:1989047146302394844, post:2096260236021817530).

He expects bad outcomes and says so in advance. There will be future outages, hacks, and black swans; don't get euphoric over price (post:1723060879913009450). He warned against bull-market luxury purchases and called for staying grounded when things rip (post:1851034531928629265, post:1838082352515596751). But he also mocks pure defensiveness: "the only way to be more retarded than the one sidelined is to be the one shorting" (post:1958983043928269298). After severe drawdowns he admitted capping leverage at 2x and telling people to stop using leverage (post:1976838108663722481, post:1995308693844566221).

Operationally he is security-paranoid. He advises ledger, exchange, or multisig (post:1554879738480656385); later he says never use a single-signer setup, even a hardware wallet (post:2083230604217508015). His opsec rules include email aliases, hardware MFA, revoking connected apps, and never reusing passwords (post:2045582183767916789, post:2053427805325545700). He also treats privacy as risk reduction: being public onchain has infinite downside and no upside; default to anon (post:1993817824951967957, post:2000227211614503397).

## Favorite narratives
- **Solana** — bullish from the start, but the thesis changed. Early on it was tech, speed, and low fees (post:1565406988477112321, post:1620494832081985537). By 2023–24 it became "only possible on Solana" and product-first (post:1686939818251452416, post:1757608967906447517). By 2025 it is internet capital markets: the place where all assets and markets trade (post:1882520136776888593, post:2007516882455482535). He still says Solana can die at any moment and the work is not done (post:1902297641620308437).
- **Ethereum and L2s** — mixed, trending bearish on L2s. He respects Ethereum's battle-testing and Vitalik, and says ETH won't die (post:1738082638232006834, post:1740579967757681086). But he calls modularity a marketing distinction, and L2s/L3s middlemen that recreate web2 with worse UX (post:1765854451074584840, post:1774320167326978345).
- **NFTs** — mixed. He defended creator royalties early (post:1578857602716692483), was bullish on compression and digital assets beyond PFPs (post:1623826644870782977, post:1663325386820788226), but also mocked PFP brands and market-cap thinking (post:1642597823475777536, post:1651437387346591745). By 2026 he is cautiously open to an NFT comeback (post:1923348382719914395).
- **Memecoins** — changed. He went from criticizing crypto's speculative rot to calling memecoins culture coins, stress-tests, and crypto-native gaming (post:1740486555473522844, post:1765232464031596896, post:1886452551891235213). But he still says scams are an unavoidable consequence of the most usable permissionless system (post:1958637084274516149).
- **Privacy/Zcash** — the newest and now loudest conviction. What began as one of several important sectors became the main thesis: privacy is the last PvE, private money is a right, and ZEC is the correct internet-native private vault (post:1973335855977680957, post:1985438221765873898, post:2077527814660927596). He frames this as the final phase of crypto, after bitcoin proved native money and Solana/ETH scaled programmable money (post:1989357185139425477).
- **AI** — early bullish, then two-sided. He called ChatGPT a breakthrough (post:1598736652473700352). Later he says AI replaces midwits, but also gives Solana devs an edge and makes formal verification possible; AI doomerism treats AI as only destructive and never solution-capable (post:1874519528413036674, post:2010445878071545880, post:2108269640329941153).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He is unusually willing to admit when his own positions hurt him, usually with self-mockery. He admits buying COIN at $300+ and SOL at $220+ (post:1710334501916680347, post:1724645039433748578). Later he admits he roundtripped gains and lost generational wealth by not taking profits (post:1971603175678628334, post:1971558252640162243). He admits being over-invested, by a lot, in Solana RPC infrastructure (post:1737958166074138931). He mocks his own leverage and farming blowups as "couldn't be me (it was me)" (post:1976769750551454075, post:1970093402147434633). He also admits personal mistakes beyond money, including pushing himself into health problems with nicotine pouches (post:1962892909537104125). Intellectually, he admits he used to rely too much on analogical reasoning and now tries to update from first principles (post:1640444479982149643, post:1923726027110404208).
