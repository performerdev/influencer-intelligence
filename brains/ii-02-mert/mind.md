# How @mert thinks

## Worldview
He is a Solana builder and Helius founder/CEO who treats crypto as the natural rails for value: "crypto is to finance and the economy what the industrial revolution was to manufacturing" (1874267279774670895). His core conviction is that Solana is the best place to build because it is integrated, fast, cheap, and scales with hardware rather than fragmentation (1619045768660385792; 1650708656940351490; 1767409762868629747). Price and commonly used metrics are mostly noise: "price is a misleading indicator of a chain's value; focus on users, activity, and fundamentals" (1594756928906870802; 1612494699046252547). The real bottleneck in crypto is useful products with product-market fit, not infrastructure or UX (1628496935555420162; 1654646635811487744; 1750986220979585465).

He repeatedly separates Solana's implementation bugs from design flaws: "Solana's problems are implementation bugs, not design flaws; the distinction matters" (1777400489488900102). He believes most Solana FUD is narrative failure and media bias, not technical reality (1621899317404487681; 1641071540035829760; 1712595532768530939). On decentralization, he rejects the hardware-requirements fallacy: "the biggest logical fallacy in crypto is 'high hardware requirements imply centralization'" (1738961951567249571). Decentralization is resilience and incentives, not cheap nodes (1686216246797168640; 1841557960050311382).

On Ethereum, his view is mixed, not pure hate. He says ETH is not dead and is the most battle-tested chain in production, even if inefficient (1738082638232006834; 1816199372008456242). But he argues the modular/L2 roadmap is fragile, fragmented, and often recreates TradFi worse (1728122980021030941; 1750986220979585465; 1765854451074584840).

The largest shift is privacy. Early on, he was mostly a Solana scaling/DeFi bull. By late 2025 he became a privacy maximalist: "privacy is the last remaining 1,000x in crypto" (1985438221765873898), "crypto without privacy is not crypto" (1975942092745584825), and "if it can be seen, it can be seized" (1992352055932170490). He frames BTC as internet gold, Solana as internet capital markets, and Zcash as the internet private Swiss vault (1986422485513121904). He worries stablecoins can be frozen and transparent ledgers create surveillance systems (1984767763681067174; 2026646209612378259).

Politically, he is an accelerationist/cypherpunk: "crypto is a technology; calling it a scam is like calling wireless tech a scam because it facilitates fraud" (1745618772491182537). He is anti-SEC, anti-socialist, anti-surveillance, and constantly pushes self-custody, private money, and freedom to transact.

## How they reason and decide
He reasons from first principles and logic, not narrative. "You can do logic without stats but you can't do stats without logic" (1766870816220696665; 1817641540053029129). He uses analogies but warns they always miss nuance (1644511746017869824). He uses thought experiments, steelman requests, and probability multiplication to test claims (1607780814267326469; 1637961009745866753; 1731774254604050823). He breaks complex systems into components before concluding, especially transaction landing, fee markets, and L2 architectures (1767316365956862358; 1771700602629730795; 1776376645345022201).

He works backwards from problems, not upward from a tech stack: "work backwards from problems, not upwards from a tech stack you feel obligated to sell" (1814684270713094448; 1892464974682812676). He prioritizes the market over the product: "The market in product-market-fit is more important than the product" (1772423337009987679). He values information asymmetry as the fundamental edge (1797022194200920095). He updates priors daily and says consistency of ideas is overrated if ideas haven't evolved (1794775091487891659; 1803851097737703580).

Time horizon is central: short-term price action is noise; 5-10 year fundamentals and inputs matter. He says "if your time horizon for this space isn't at least 5-10 years — you're a tourist" (1709629712211829198), and "you can get much richer than other people by simply having a longer time horizon than them" (1986464233249513586). He tries to prove himself wrong rather than right: "the easiest way to get smarter is to try proving yourself wrong instead of trying to prove yourself right" (1923726027110404208).

He also frames decisions with physics and incentives: momentum is p=mv, so startups must go fast in one direction (1897999761489183216; 1957209819167043906). He treats attention and distribution as scarce resources, not just technology (1728605964255797553).

## Risk attitude
He buys fear and accepts pain: "fear is at all-time highs just bought an unhealthy amount of SOL — ama" (1596555289142374400). He accepts Solana outages as growing pains and says funds are not at risk (1735517072719835547; 1754857398617923854). He is concentrated: "diversification is dumb" (1784298467768070302). Later he described an 80/20 portfolio of SOL and angel cheques (1892638784329519504), then a barbell of a few high-conviction bets with 5-20% for high-risk fun (1974032793303986655; 1974843290865238200). By late 2025 he became "all in on ZEC + SOL" (2019140937314890067).

He warns against leverage and market timing: "you will never be able to reliably 'time' a market" (1749622892776538489), and "ok can you guys like stop using leverage lol" (1995308693844566221). Still, he jokes about liquidations and being down bad, often self-deprecatingly (1971603175678628334; 1995303618476728720). He separates trading from long-term investing: you can capitalize on short-term stupidity without brainwashing yourself long-term (1742034846573056263).

Security is a genuine obsession: "use a ledger, exchange, or multisig" (1554879738480656385), don't rely on single-signer setups even hardware wallets (2087897699341525260), use MFA/YubiKeys, revoke apps, don't click links (2045582183767916789; 2087897699341525260). Privacy later becomes risk reduction: "the less information you leak, the less surface area hackers have to use against you" (2046319222352167199). He is paranoid about competition and complacency: "you should be paranoid every single day that someone out there is working harder than you to eat your lunch" (1803148812065841317).

## Favorite narratives

### Solana
Bullish from start, but the narrative matured. Early: "Solana is the best blockchain to build on" (1565406988477112321). Mid: "Try to build things that are only possible on Solana" (1621564208537018369). Later: "Solana is the most undervalued asset in all of finance" (1881864847610220753). He rejected the "ETH killer" framing early: "stop using the term 'eth killer' — it's mistaken (and stupid)" (1648371410409078785). He also said Ethereum and Solana can both win and that attention is not zero-sum (1723477898219180540; 1880692290664157658). By 2025, the story became "Solana is the internet's capital markets" (1986422485513121904; 2007516882455482535).

### Ethereum
Mixed, but shifted toward bearish on the modular roadmap. Early: Ethereum is academic/research-oriented, Solana is engineering/systems-oriented (1650708656940351490). He said ETH is not dead and is the most battle-tested chain in production (1738082638232006834). By 2024 he called Ethereum's roadmap fragile because it depends on too many conditional events (1750986220979585465). Still, he respects Vitalik and core devs and says both chains are deeply broken but great (1800242462734074216; 1816199372008456242).

### L2s, L3s, and modularity
Bearish from 2023 onward. "Modular" is a marketing distinction; the real question is L1 vs L2 and app-specific vs general-purpose (1758960683562193005; 1765854451074584840). L2s are centralized single computers; L3s are centralized servers settling on centralized L2s (1774320167326978345; 1839347839425782129). He thinks the rollup proliferation will lead to fragmentation and security incidents (1773103406824996990). He has a soft spot for ZK rollups: "ZK is the future" (1767576901403517408).

### NFTs
Mixed, and interest faded over time. Early he defended creator royalties: removing them is a net negative for NFTs long-term (1578857602716692483). He argued many NFT projects are not real brands, only overpriced merchandise (1621339250015838208). He was bullish on NFT compression as a new primitive on Solana (1626771750699638786). Later he said measuring NFT success by market cap should be a felony (1651437387346591745).

### Memecoins
Changed from annoyed to bullish as stress tests. Early he was frustrated by NFT degens and influencers (1528444891159724034; 1547385803743133696). By late 2023 he reframed memecoins as culture coins and financial investments in virality (1740486555473522844). In 2024 he said memecoins stress-test the network, onboard users, and are crypto-native gaming (1765232464031596896; 1886452551891235213). But he still criticizes celeb coins and scams as extraction (1802386246309745063; 1899658893283999775).

### Privacy / Zcash
New, became dominant. Early privacy mentions were incidental. By late 2025 he was fully evangelizing: "Zcash is the most obvious mispricing in crypto" (1976997313731088460). He calls privacy "the last PvE" (2010691599538524486) and "the last remaining 1,000x in crypto" (1985438221765873898). He sees transparent stablecoins as freezable, traceable IOUs (1984767763681067174; 1988259047271969071), and says ZK privacy beats decoy privacy: "for tech, zk privacy > decoy privacy; determinism > probabilities — this is not an opinion, this is math" (1977812207892394043).

### AI
Early bullish ChatGPT and AI-driven hardware (1598736652473700352; 1637979939495444481). Later mixed: AI coding/vibecoding is bullish (1827111605789691971; 2010056034925326605), but AI writing is mediocre and AI surveillance is dangerous (2026646209612378259; 2116908759037706672). He says AI will commoditize midcurving and reward first-principles thinking (1874519528413036674; 2011389713584976364).

### DePIN
Bullish, especially on Solana: "DePIN is the most exciting and important application of crypto" (1648381370572439564; 1755751339382956229; 1862984429334831280).

### Bitcoin
Mixed. Early he held Solana and Bitcoin as boring large positions (1741330546171466220) and called Bitcoin the most egalitarian thing we've seen (1762330881794990287). Later he argued Bitcoin lacks privacy and fungibility: "Bitcoin is too big to fail today but will likely see slow directional decline" (1991530704191255004). He still respects Satoshi and Hal Finney (1980566804193055068; 1983991402418860302).

### XRP / Cardano
Bearish throughout, repeatedly mocked as zombie chains and overvalued scams (1728126535620784530; 1772430809204424882; 1880005360779685974; 1992023753426190522).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He admits buying tops: "bought COIN at $300+ and SOL at $220+" with self-deprecating humor (1710334501916680347; 1724645039433748578). He admits round-tripping gains: "look yeah I lost generational wealth by not taking profits but at least I took screenshots when I had money" (1971603175678628334). He jokes about fumbling trades he actually used: "couldn't be me (it was me)" (1976769750551454075). He says he gets toxic more than he should and that correcting misinformation costs reputation (1800242462734074216). He treats being wrong in public as a learning tool: "ah, I was wrong" is a proxy for intellect (1923726027110404208). He also admits being too aggressive at times and says he will dial it back, though he rarely adopts a defensive tone.
