# How @mert thinks

## Worldview

He is, above all, a Solana maximalist who reasons from engineering reality and first principles. From the earliest notes through late 2024, his central conviction is that Solana is the best place to build and will win because it optimizes for hardware, speed, low fees, and products rather than academic narrative (post:1544721417652027394; post:1619045768660385792; post:1630772056768774144; post:1664807257450610689). He does not pretend otherwise: he says he is "over invested, by a lot" in Solana RPC infrastructure and jokes about how much he has lost for the cause (post:1737958166074138931; post:1727497721660002576).

But his view evolves in stages. Early on he frames the future as duopolistic—top 2–3 L1s, Visa/Mastercard, Windows/Mac (post:1638546467651563523). By mid-2024 he more explicitly frames integrated L1s as the answer, but also accepts that other chains can win and says zero-sum thinking is wrong: "the real enemy is the existing traditional system" (post:1723477898219180540; post:1743306814979014862). By late 2024 he is saying "multiple chains can win" and specifically that Bitcoin + Solana is a boring large position (post:1880692290664157658; post:1741330546171466220). Then from late 2025, Zcash/privacy becomes the dominant new narrative alongside Solana, and he says the top five trillion-dollar assets will be BTC, SOL, ZEC, HYPE, and one more (post:1979329065669791945; post:1984287634160750846).

His worldview has a hard cypherpunk spine. From FTX onward he hammers self-custody and DeFi as the point of crypto; CEXes and custodians cause major failures (post:1590118372947406849; post:1590321177217536000). This grows into a broader anti-surveillance, anti-bureaucracy stance: media and politicians are incompetent and corrupt; the final boss is existing bureaucracies—SEC, banks, politicians (post:1727100358810640431; post:1641071540035829760). By 2025–2026, privacy becomes the central mission: "crypto without privacy is not crypto," "if it can be seen, it can be seized" (post:1975942092745584825; post:1992352055932170490; post:2033131884243275966). He argues transparent chains converge to surveillance and seizure, and stablecoins can be frozen at any time (post:1955554887959527689; post:1978608224057045494).

He is a techno-optimist and accelerationist. He repeatedly invokes e/acc, hardware acceleration, and "increase bandwidth, reduce latency" (post:1711924212774883455; post:1748157122011881904; post:1835151253221130396). He believes crypto is a technology, not a scam; pessimists and doomers are wrong and "pessimism is crowded" (post:1745618772491182537; post:2071349856027754716). He sees AI as a major breakthrough but also says crypto will be more important than LLMs because coordinating humans is upstream of coordinating robots (post:1980982083414552700; post:2019200567009243587). He argues the most dangerous AI outcome is immersive games consuming young people, not nuclear doomsday (post:1875264654781632732; post:1912441691073630719).

On capitalism and markets: he believes crypto is "capitalism 2.0" or an API for capitalism, making markets more efficient and less leaky (post:1920251576624574951; post:2036041120497471741; post:2042027213952884809). He thinks speculation is not good or bad; it just is, and builders should use it for distribution (post:1736440367812166137). He frequently says the killer use case is markets, not a single app (post:1854666358157402580; post:1915458595556147227).

## How they reason and decide

He reasons from first principles and production data, with a strong bias toward shipping over theorizing. A recurring method is "work backwards from the problem, not upwards from the tech" (post:1814684270713094448; post:1892464974682812676; post:1803560778068533310). He argues that most crypto building is backwards: people start with a chain and look for problems, instead of finding real problems (post:1636755645495119873; post:1643382282642923520).

He distrusts loose analogies and linear forecasting in a nonlinear system. He says "people trying to apply linear forecasting in a field dominated by non-linearities" is a banal error, and historical analogies usually miss nuance (post:1667342782074068993; post:1640444479982149643; post:1739001719835480433). His recurring corrective is probability and failure multiplication: modular architectures add conditional probabilities; the probability of success decreases with each added component (post:1731774254604050823; post:1733970360314265673).

He is empirical but not naive about data. He warns that most crypto metrics—TVL, TPS, users, daily transactions—are gamed and useless (post:1727768876941820076; post:1736892013134438416). He repeats "you can do logic without stats but you can't do stats without logic" and says data are not a substitute for reasoning (post:1766870816220696665; post:1817641540053029129). He values qualitative edge, information asymmetry, and "vibes" over easily manipulated numbers once the metrics are exhausted (post:1724818966827159849; post:1728605964255797553).

He thinks in terms of incentives and time horizons. "Show me the incentives, I'll show you the outcome" is a core pattern (post:1824477062616277233). He tells builders to expand their time horizon and not let price do their thinking; short-term price action is noise (post:1533128448893403138; post:1822307691814928759; post:1871741982428864971). He says the biggest edge in crypto is simply having a time horizon longer than one cycle (post:1862898721383444540; post:2095247433576088049).

He also deliberately seeks disagreement and tries to prove himself wrong. He asks for steelman arguments and admits he changes his mind when convinced, such as on Toly's economic security argument after ~200 tweets (post:1607780814267326469; post:1799581312358948979; post:1923726027110404208).

## Risk attitude

He is a degenerate with a philosopher's guardrails. He openly jokes about buying tops, losing money, leverage, and liquidations: bought COIN above $300 and SOL at $220+, "if you're not getting liquidated here, you weren't trying hard enough," "there is this thing called leverage" (post:1710334501916680347; post:1909149100659912720; post:1957858854957359428). But those are jokes. His actual risk rules are much more conservative on custodial risk: use a ledger, exchange, or multisig; avoid protocols without frozen or multisig authorities; self-custody is the entire point (post:1554879738480656385; post:1603784814418599938; post:1590118372947406849).

He accepts that Solana will have outages, hacks, and black swans; what matters is that funds are safe and the system recovers (post:1646163360302481409; post:1667548560718540802; post:1723060879913009450). He frames crypto as a series of survivable catastrophes: "with the exception of BTC, probability of no liveness/consistency failure on a major chain is 0%; what matters is recovery" (post:1732066255090127176). After the October 10 crash, he says he reduced leverage to max 2x, and he tells people not to use leverage (post:1976838108663722481; post:1995308693844566221).

His later risk framework is privacy-first. He warns that public onchain activity has infinite downside and no upside; being doxed exposes you to stalkers, state surveillance, and frozen assets. He tells people to shield ZEC, use multisigs, audit opsec, use email aliases and hardware MFA, and default to anon culture (post:2000227211614503397; post:2034640944893415639; post:2087897699341525260). He also warns against panicking and moving funds during crises: "you are way more likely to lose your money by superficially moving it around than it is for fundamental cryptography to break overnight" (post:2108058959852773442).

On position sizing, he is concentrated: "diversification is dumb" (post:1784298467768070302). But he also offers more measured advice: 5–20% cash for high-risk fun, hold cash for high-conviction opportunities, concentrate on ~3 assets max, double down on winners, and say no to almost everything except a few extremely underpriced bets (post:1974843290865238200; post:1989047146302394844; post:1973823139269492995; post:1969825357865037910).

## Favorite narratives

### Solana
Stance: lifelong bullish. He calls Solana "the best place to build real products" and "the most usable, most battle-tested chain" (post:1565406988477112321; post:1963714813491601687; post:1946901555514327417). His version changes shape over time:
- Early: Solana is undervalued and solves real scaling (post:1520790104217997313; post:1630772056768774144).
- Mid: "Only possible on Solana" and integrated L1 vs modular L2 debates (post:1621564208537018369; post:1765032085058683218).
- Later: Solana becomes "the internet's capital market" and "the liquidity layer" (post:1920459292282351738; post:1994194795695108363; post:2103811340276056170).

### Ethereum and L2s
Stance: started mixed, then increasingly bearish on L2 proliferation, while respecting Ethereum as the most battle-tested chain. He says ETH won't die, but its modular roadmap has captured mindshare and L2s recreate TradFi with worse UX and more trust assumptions (post:1738082638232006834; post:1791511243326361844; post:1728122980021030941; post:1774320167326978345). He rejects "eth killer" language as stupid (post:1648371410409078785). By late 2024 he is firmly "bearish on L2s" as a category, though he has kind words for ZK.

### Memecoins
Stance: mixed and explicitly unfair to himself. He defends them as crypto-native gaming and stress tests, but also says celeb coins are extractive and not mass adoption (post:1740486555473522844; post:1765232464031596896; post:1802386246309745063; post:1886452551891235213). He resists the "memecoins are why useful apps don't get built" argument: "you don't have a choice; people launching shitcoins isn't the problem, not building useful apps is" (post:1741956217277219187).

### Privacy / Zcash
Stance: the big second act. From late 2025 onward he becomes a loud Zcash bull and makes privacy the center of his crypto thesis. He calls ZEC "the most obvious mispricing in crypto" and says privacy is "the last remaining 1,000x" (post:1976997313731088460; post:1985438221765873898). He frames private money as a right: "if it can be seen, it can be seized" (post:1992352055932170490). He previously respected privacy projects but did not make them the main narrative; the change is explicit and huge.

### AI
Stance: bullish but mixed. He sees AI as accelerating hardware and formal verification, making crypto more secure (post:1637979939495444481; post:2064304692444995889; post:2063914405922746783). But he also says AI writing is mid and outsources thinking, and crypto will matter more than LLMs (post:1949225207870411188; post:1980982083414552700). He is skeptical of AI doomer panic and says AI can both break and fix systems (post:2108269640329941153).

### XRP, Cardano, and zombie chains
Stance: consistent bearish. He mocks ADA's "zombie" state and says XRP is overvalued and a scam in valuations (post:1727898976035176891; post:1918253104325083231; post:1993040384419938449; post:1978482187755573521). He treats their market cap as noise, not product.

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He admits to buying tops: COIN above $300, SOL above $220 (post:1710334501916680347; post:1724645039433748578). He openly says he was wrong or "lied" when he thought something would happen and it did not. He jokes that he got a speeding ticket because a marketing campaign was so good, and that he was "defeated by a fking robot" (post:1678442568848220162; post:1874519528413036674). He says he is toxic more than he should be, that correcting misinformation costs reputation, and that he can be too aggressive and will dial it back (post:1800242462734074216; post:1792348573821989103). After the Jito mempool shutdown, he says the ecosystem "failed to prepare and relied on the altruism of a single team" (post:1766518201263780230). He admits he let price do his thinking at times, and that he roundtripped generational wealth by not taking profits (post:1971603175678628334; post:1971558252640162243). Later he is even more explicit: "looks like I was wrong — 4 comments deep" and "I was wrong on the timeframe" but always reframes as learning (post:1907858136611827948; post:2018726611940749801).
