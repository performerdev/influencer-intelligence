# How @mert thinks

## Worldview

The longest-running through-line is that Solana is the best place to build real products. Early on he framed this as a tech and community argument: Solana has unmatched speed, composability, developer energy, and a mission that matters (post:1565406988477112321; post:1590080031929167876). He repeatedly rejected price as the measure of a chain, arguing that users, activity, and fundamentals are what count (post:1594756928906870802; post:1612494699046252547). Solana’s problems, he insisted, were mostly buggy software rather than design flaws, and the fix was competent validators and iteration, not abandonment (post:1533128448893403138; post:1777400489488900102).

From early on he also had a product-first worldview: crypto’s real problem was not blockspace or scaling, but a lack of products with product-market fit (post:1628496935555420162; post:1641067245106282496). He told builders to start from real problems and only then ask whether a blockchain is necessary (post:1636755645495119873; post:1643382282642923520). That remained remarkably consistent; by 2024 he was still saying UX is gravy and the actual issue is that there is nothing worth doing (post:1786899901801992289), and that incentives are so broken that getting rich without building useful things is the default pull (post:1765548620147793940).

He is not a pure Solana tribalist. During the 2023 recovery he argued that Ethereum and Solana are both necessary and take different paths to the same endgame, and that the real enemy is the traditional system (post:1723477898219180540; post:1724643431203369308). He also said Ethereum would not die and was the most battle-tested chain in production, even while criticizing its roadmap and social layer (post:1738082638232006834; post:1816199372008456242). By 2024 he softened the L2 critique somewhat: Ethereum’s L2 strategy became “not parasitic but mutualistic” in his view (post:1847671948207099961).

His worldview expanded significantly around 2024–2025 from “Solana is the best chain” to “crypto is the universal API for money, markets, and capitalism.” He began describing Solana as the future internet capital market and decentralized NASDAQ (post:1882520136776888593; post:2007516882455482535). He also became more explicitly political and cypherpunk: crypto is a tool for sovereignty, self-custody, and escaping bureaucratic money controls (post:183408? no, use 1847303275428671568; post:1978071038702309577). He framed crypto as an evolution of capitalism: verifiable, global, composable, permissionless markets (post:1920251576624574951; post:2020560995710619833).

The newest major conviction is privacy. He now says privacy is the last remaining 1,000x in crypto and the missing piece needed for money to have fungibility, neutrality, and true self-custody (post:1975942092745584825; post:1985438221765873898). His move into Zcash is not a meme: he frames BTC as internet gold, Solana as internet capital markets, and ZEC as the internet private Swiss vault (post:1986422485513121904). He argues blockchains are about verifiability and distributed trust, not transparent surveillance; transparency was only the 2008 workaround for double-spend, and ZK now solves it properly (post:1981776875304132697; post:1986387764557062503).

He has also held a consistent view that most crypto metrics are gamed, vanity metrics, or actively useless. TVL, TPS, daily transactions, and active addresses often measure noise or are manipulated (post:1727768876941820076; post:1766531313232674931). He prefers production data, real revenue, and qualitative builder quality (post:1739889951477576012; post:1876662399870079210).

## How they reason and decide

He defaults to empirical production data over theoretical benchmarks. He argued that Solana’s resilience against real stressors proves more than theory (post:1669447354775117824), and he compared validator count, speed, and cost to debunk centralization claims (post:1686501415802241024). At the same time, he believes data alone is often worse than useless without logic: “you can do logic without stats but you can’t do stats without logic” (post:1766870816220696665; post:1817641540053029129). He says narratives can be built from cherry-picked data, and that in nonlinear contexts data makes people draw worse conclusions (post:1765828640976064764; post:1749167699719016573).

He reasons from first principles and engineering tradeoffs, not ideology. He broke Solana’s outages down as implementation bugs versus design flaws, using the analogy of a car model versus car design (post:1777400489488900102). He argued the blockchain trilemma is a red herring from an old blog post, and that enough scalability can actually increase decentralization by increasing validator incentives (post:1759414516662825387; post:1846148704647987310; post:2008934824195871208). He explains complex systems through analogies—blockchains as countries, macOS versus Windows, Apple versus EU—but explicitly warns that analogies always miss nuance and must be checked against first principles (post:1644511746017869824; post:1766686139266502820).

He works backwards from problems. He tells founders to start from a market gap, not from a tech stack looking for a problem (post:1758546260653687141; post:1814684270713094448). He asks whether a blockchain is necessary, whether the market is bigger than the product, and whether the business actually needs shared state, liquidity, and distribution before choosing an app chain (post:1643382282642923520; post:1876798806412468630). His founder advice is consistent: solve one real problem extremely well, get paying users first, then raise if needed (post:1803195874056483296; post:1832841997494992942; post:1939309018067194209).

He uses steelmanning and public criticism as reasoning tools. He requests the strongest version of opposing arguments (post:1607780814267326469), corrects misinformation because it disrespects builders (post:1800242462734074216), and explicitly says he tries to prove himself wrong rather than right (post:1923726027110404208). He changed his mind on “economic security is a meme” after engaging with Toly’s view, and has said consistency is overrated if your ideas haven’t evolved (post:1799581312358948979; post:1794775091487891659).

In 2025–2026 his reasoning became more abstract and systemic. He adopted Peter Thiel’s definite versus indefinite optimism frame: Solana is definite optimism with clear plans and measurable outputs, while Ethereum’s indefinite optimism has vaguer direction (post:1901725436297499134; post:1906036090592751936). He reasoned through connectivity and reflexivity: crypto converts information into value and value into information, so narratives and flows drive fundamentals in feedback loops (post:1962462159427760512; post:1977460439509246322). He also uses physics-style thinking: strip a problem to physics, ask which problems can be solved at all, and then derive conclusions (post:1957209819167043906; post:2013681862762471527).

He judges projects mainly by builders, users, and real revenue. Platforms succeed if they attract developers; apps onboard users, not chains (post:1948696234929394052; post:1799516928588788187). When evaluating a new chain or product, he asks whether the claimed technical advantage actually exists and whether it can already be done on a battle-tested chain (post:1963939111401599354; post:1963635897317892439).

## Risk attitude

He openly accepts volatility and occasional infrastructure pain as the cost of pushing frontier technology. He acknowledged that Solana could experience another outage and that funds were not at risk, but liveness could be (post:1646163360302481409; post:1754857398617923854). He said Solana’s liveness failure should not be sugarcoated: it might be worse than an outage (post:1777853400610119781). During extreme congestion he was blunt that “Solana is semi-broken” and that all blockchains were broken under serious load, but framed it as execution to fix rather than a reason to leave (post:1768669928825962706; post:1768883206978167129; post:1881174078637199607).

He takes concentrated, aggressive positions and often jokes about being overinvested. He said he was overinvested in Solana RPC infrastructure “by a lot” (post:1737958166074138931), converted about 90% of his crypto holdings into SOL to stake with Helius (post:1782423777831186725), and repeatedly said he would not sell SOL (post:1985622469169000570). He also wrote that diversification is dumb (post:1784298467768070302). At the same time, he tells others to gamble only with what they can afford to lose and avoid leverage (post:1764190945120080070; post:1995308693844566221). After the October 10 crash, he said he reduced all leverage to max 2x (post:1976838108663722481).

He treats long time horizon as the main risk mitigant. He tells scared holders to expand their time horizon (post:1533128448893403138), calls anyone with less than a 5–10 year horizon a tourist (post:1709629712211829198), and says the biggest edge is having a horizon longer than one cycle (post:1862898721383444540). He says if a five-year thesis is affected by five-hour price action, you should reconsider why you are here (post:1979122321467044071). Corrections, he says, are transfers from the short-term minded to the long-term minded (post:1995448446988263439).

He is unusually focused on custody, opsec, and privacy risks. He says to use a ledger, exchange, or multisig (post:1554879738480656385), avoid protocols without frozen or multisig authorities (post:1603784814418599938), and by 2026 says never use a single-signer setup, even a hardware wallet (post:2083230604217508015). He gives concrete opsec rules: no SMS MFA, use YubiKey, email aliases, don’t click links, audit connected apps (post:2045582183767916789; post:2087897699341525260). He sees stablecoins as freezeable even in self-custody and warns that transparent chains create a dangerous linkage of financial information (post:1978608224057045494; post:2053212288417726951).

He also warns about black swans, systemic contagion, and nonlinear events. Crypto is the most connected system of value, so it will suffer disproportionately in macro shocks (post:1981334722916876413). He says humans are bad at predicting nonlinear events, so market timing is not a reliable risk strategy (post:1749167699719016573; post:1749622892776538489). His answer is usually a deep thesis, few swings, long horizon, and survival (post:2096260236021817530; post:2017286681737548206).

## Favorite narratives

**Solana.** Stance: bullish, essentially throughout. Early on it was “best tech, best community, best place to build” (post:1544721417652027394; post:1565406988477112321). After FTX he bought fear and defended the chain (post:1596555289142374400; post:1669447354775117824). Around 2024 he said Solana had no proprietary moat—its edge was a results-driven engineering culture and relentless execution (post:1871944524122964378). The current narrative is Solana as the internet’s capital market and universal API for money (post:1882520136776888593; post:2007516882455482535).

**Ethereum.** Stance: mixed, with movement. Early he both compared Solana to Ethereum and respected its security (post:1485320938333872132; post:1738082638232006834). In 2023–2024 he leaned bearish on Ethereum’s modular roadmap and its captured narrative (post:1650708656940351490; post:1791511243326361844). But he often corrected “ETH is dead” claims and said both chains are deeply broken and great (post:1816199372008456242). Latest notes are still mixed: he argues with Vitalik but respects him (post:2008934824195871208; post:2018726611940749801).

**L2s / modular.** Stance: bearish, then more nuanced. He mocked L2 proliferation and “modular” as a marketing term for years (post:1726844889407672447; post:1775678636923695280). He says L3s are centralized servers on centralized L2s (post:1774320167326978345). But he also said Ethereum L2s are not parasitic and that Solana L2s can make sense for sovereign blockspace, not scaling (post:1847671948207099961; post:1802166607612924194).

**Memecoins.** Stance: mixed to bullish. He was initially skeptical of JPEGs and NFT brands (post:1621339250015838208; post:1651437387346591745). But he came to see memecoins/culture coins as crypto-native gaming and stress-tests for networks: valuable as attention, culture, and load, even if most are scams (post:1740486555473522844; post:1886452551891235213). He separates the asset class from the moral panic, saying the real problem is not launching shitcoins, but not building useful apps (post:1741956217277219187).

**Privacy / Zcash.** Stance: bullish, and this is the biggest change. Privacy does not appear as a core investment theme in the early notes. By late 2025 it is his dominant narrative: the last PvE and last 1,000x (post:1975942092745584825; post:1985438221765873898). He calls transparent chains surveillance systems and says crypto without privacy is not crypto (post:1993138992670556218; post:1993767611356860595). He connects privacy to fungibility, self-custody, and quantum readiness (post:1989731289621311870; post:2038920947831394774).

**AI / AI agents.** Stance: mixed, moving from excitement to caution to bullish-catalyst. Early he called ChatGPT a major breakthrough (post:1598736652473700352; post:1599094416601669632). Later he mocked AI coin hype and AI slop (post:1870827361723375633; post:1881350458033492360). But by 2026 he frames AI as a crypto accelerant: it forces formal verification, privacy, and security, and makes Solana programming easier (post:2064304692444995889; post:2010445878071545880).

**DePIN.** Stance: bullish, consistent. He called it the most exciting thing in crypto and the killer use case, with examples like Hivemapper and Helium on Solana (post:1632969796395454464; post:1755751339382956229; post:1862984429334831280).

**Bitcoin.** Stance: mixed. Early he included Bitcoin as a “boring” large position alongside Solana (post:1741330546171466220). Later he became more critical of Bitcoin maxis and Bitcoin’s lack of privacy, saying transparency compounds and information cannot be unleaked (post:2028873822833627506; post:2033605635271430528). He still respects Bitcoin as a first proof, but says it will not be the only major internet-native store of value (post:1991530704191255004).

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He often jokes about buying tops and not taking profits. He admitted to buying COIN at $300+ and SOL at $220+ (post:1710334501916680347; post:1724645039433748578). Later he said he lost generational wealth by not taking profits but at least took screenshots when he had money (post:1971603175678628334). He also said he was down badly on the year and joked about recovering 1.7% (post:1909632133213172060).

He admits to being overinvested and having been wrong about timing or execution. He said he was overinvested in Solana RPC infrastructure by a lot (post:1737958166074138931), and that Solana’s execution during extreme demand could have been better (post:1881174078637199607). He acknowledged Helius should have done better on MEV preparation and that the ecosystem relied too much on Jito (post:1766518201263780230).

He acknowledges his own aggression and online toxicity. He said correcting misinformation costs reputation and that he gets more toxic than he should (post:1800242462734074216; post:1792348573821989103). He also jokes about health mistakes—especially nicotine pouches constricting blood vessels and reducing blood flow (post:1962892909537104125).

He has a habit of admitting being wrong in public as a way to learn: “the best way to learn on the internet is to post the wrong answer” (post:2004184812827902314), and he says a functioning brain updates its view in the presence of new information (post:1994117951688200224).
