# How @mert thinks

## Worldview

Mert sees crypto not as a casino but as the universal API for money, finance, and capitalism at planetary scale. In 2026 he writes: "crypto is the universal API for money, finance, and capitalism" (2014753903271931980, 2022011213702389845, 2036041120497471741). The through-line from 2021 to 2026 is that crypto evolves capitalism: "crypto is capitalism 2.0 — it evolves capitalism by enabling decentralized information flow, frictionless capital flows, property rights enforcement" (1967261102645129593, 1978071038702309577).

Early on he framed Solana as the best chain to build on and the future of crypto, with unmatched tech, community, and developer ecosystem (1565406988477112321, 1544721417652027394). Later this sharpens into: "Solana is the most usable, scalable, and battle-tested blockchain; it is becoming the internet's capital market" (2103811340276056170, 2018039059223060593). He refuses victory laps: "Solana has not won anything; paranoia to the max is required because the second you think you've won is when decline starts" (1902297641620308437).

The biggest worldview shift is from Solana-first to privacy-first, without abandoning Solana. By late 2025 he makes privacy the cypherpunk center: "crypto without privacy is not crypto" (1975942092745584825, 2014718351818555624) and "privacy is the last remaining 1,000x in crypto" (2100565585453326843). He argues the point of blockchains is verifiability, not surveillance: transparency was only the 2008 workaround for the double-spend problem (1981776875304132697, 1986387764557062503). This becomes a dual thesis: "BTC is internet gold, SOL is internet capital markets, ZEC is the internet private Swiss vault" (1986422485513121904, 1986512082779709622).

He is a builder supremacist. Crypto's bottleneck is not blockspace but lack of useful products: "the core problem in crypto is a lack of useful products with product-market fit, not a lack of blockspace or scaling solutions" (1628496935555420162, 1635137097266003970). He says crypto's real problem "is not UX but that there is nothing worth doing; UX is the gravy, not the steak" (1786899901801992289). The killer use case is markets: "the killer use case of crypto is markets; markets rule everything" (1854666358157402580, 1915458595556147227).

He is anti-multichain and anti-modular by default. The future narrows to top 2-3 L1s, like Visa/Mastercard or iOS/Android (1638546467651563523, 1643129223941599236). L3s are "centralized servers settling on centralized L2s" (1774320167326978345). Decentralization is instrumental, not sacred: "decentralization doesn't matter until something bad happens, then it's all that matters" (1766857965049401614). Performance and decentralization are not a tradeoff but mutually reinforcing: "performance/scalability increases the upper bound of decentralization" (1901665150270124479, 1919547782853001721). He is deeply anti-surveillance: crypto's danger is "not crime but total loss of freedom and sovereignty" (1975451012870586656).

## How they reason and decide

He reasons from first principles and physics, not historical analogy. "The easiest way to get smarter is to try proving yourself wrong instead of trying to prove yourself right" (1923726027110404208). He applies physics as the master framework: ask which problems are "barred by the laws of physics" and whether a system uses physics to guide its future (1957209819167043906). He distrusts linear historical analogies in a nonlinear industry: "most correlations in crypto are non-linear; don't rely on historical analogies interpreted linearly" (1739001719835480433).

He works backwards from problems, not upwards from tech: "work backwards from problems, not upwards from a tech stack you feel obligated to sell" (1814684270713094448). Founders should "start from real problems, not from trying to apply crypto" (1636755645495119873). The market matters more than the product: "the market in product-market-fit is more important than the product" (1772423337009987679, 1772455125660950726).

He privileges logic over data: "you can do logic without stats but you can't do stats without logic — cherrypicked data is fraud" (1766870816220696665, 1817641540053029129). He corrects inflated metrics even when they favor Solana, and insists on median over average: "hello kind reminder to not use averages for skewed datasets — use median" (1741004640299409680, 1771995935964291214). He treats short-term price as noise: "stop obsessing over price and build some cool fucking things" (1595173795505049641).

He steelmans and seeks the strongest counter-arguments (1607780814267326469). He updates priors daily: "do not assume superiority, ever ... update your priors every single day" (1803851097737703580). A functioning brain updates when new information arrives; not changing your mind is not a flex (1994117951688200224). He concedes when convinced, as with Toly's "economic security is a meme" after about 200 tweets (1799581312358948979).

He reasons through incentives: "show me the incentives, I'll show you the outcome" (1824477062616277233, 1824126579418730798). He separates chain vs app vs infra failures, e.g., failed transactions vs dropped transactions, and insists on correct attribution (1771700602629730795, 1881421973579849872).

Since 2025 his dominant mental model is reflexivity/connectivity: "connectivity is the most important concept for investors: crypto turns information into value and value into information, so flows lead fundamentals in a reflexive loop" (1977460439509246322, 1977469313759244402, 1961406588519960978). This makes cycles compressed: "the idea of a cycle is dead ... the cycle happens every day" (1979143383286255995).

## Risk attitude

He is high-conviction, often over-invested, and accepts pain: "fear is at all-time highs just bought an unhealthy amount of SOL — ama" (1596555289142374400), "over invested, by a lot" (1737958166074138931), "irresponsibly overweight" (1997258213352505434). But he runs a long time horizon: "expand your time horizon" (1533128448893403138) and "if your 5y thesis is affected by 5h price action, reconsider why you're here" (1979122321467044071).

He believes outsized returns require being contrarian and right, and accepting being wrong often: "to be rich, you have to be contrarian and right" (1962276148986974409). He invert-frames drawdowns: "be greedy when everyone else is greedy and be greedy when everyone else is fearful" (1945468575348613458). Panic is a discount: either crypto is over forever or "coins are at a discount and you're emotional" (1989247768259596455).

He warns against euphoria: "do not get overly euphoric here" (1838082352515596751) and "there will be future issues like outages, hacks, or worse" (1723060879913009450). He is paranoid about custody and opsec: single-signer setups "must be eliminated", use multisigs, hardware keys, email aliases, never SMS (2087897699341525260, 2083230604217508015, 2053427805325545700).

His leverage arc is cautionary: he jokes "I trade with 10x the money I don't have and make 10x the money, right?" (1901449588986273831), then after October 10 writes "after seeing aster do well (barely functional for me), reduced all leverage to max 2x" (1976838108663722481) and "ok can you guys like stop using leverage lol" (1995308693844566221).

Privacy and self-custody are primary risk mitigations: "use a ledger, exchange, or multisig" (1554879738480656385); "stablecoins can be frozen at any time" (1984767763681067174); "if it can be seen, it can be seized" (1992352055932170490).

## Favorite narratives

**Solana — bullish, the base narrative.** Early: unmatched tech, community, dev ecosystem (1565406988477112321, 1590080031929167876). Mid: "only possible on Solana" (OPOS) and resilience through stress (1669447354775117824, 1710367452511568013). By 2024 he separates "implementation bugs, not design flaws" (1777400489488900102). Late: Solana as liquidity layer and everything market (2103811340276056170, 1994194795695108363). He never claims it has won (1902297641620308437).

**Ethereum — mixed, from rival to respect-and-critique.** Early mixed on ETH's tradeoffs (1485320938333872132, 1601634493483593728). Early 2024 he concedes ETH won't die and is "the most battle-tested thing in production" (1738082638232006834, 1744102476633022537). By mid-2024 he critiques the modular road map and mindshare capture (1791511243326361844). Late he mocks ETH maxis while respecting ETH core devs (1980220965654233236, 1920221656481489063).

**L2s / modular — bearish, with temporary nuance.** Early bearish (1627180917209825283, 1641067245106282496). He calls modular vs monolithic a marketing distinction (1758960683562193005, 1775678636923695280). L3s are the worst: "centralized servers settling on centralized L2s" (1774320167326978345). He briefly admits L2s may scale (1684020531316621312) but remains predominantly bearish.

**Privacy / Zcash — bullish, the defining late arc.** Not present early; emerges late 2025. "Zcash is the most obvious mispricing in crypto" (1976997313731088460). "Privacy is the last PvE" (2100565585453326843). "Private money is the 5th Amendment of the internet" (1977534889927811122). Zcash is "the purest expression of cryptographic money" (2065031553789444107).

**Memecoins — mixed.** Bullish as culture coins and network stress-tests (1740486555473522844, 1765232464031596896); also honest that "Memecoins are net bad for the industry's image but net good for getting crypto into more hands" (1783504046688440617).

**DePIN — bullish.** "DePIN is the killer use case of crypto" (1755751339382956229, 1632969796395454464).

**Firedancer — bullish.** "Firedancer is the biggest and most important project in crypto right now, and it is not priced in" (1763046521983938856).

**XRP / Cardano — bearish throughout.** Cardano mocked from 2021 (1571131111161606145) through 2026 (2062483970118353383); XRP bearish at least from 2024 onward (1769592724540256408, 1987106228615717180).

**AI — mixed to bullish on security.** Excited about ChatGPT early (1598736652473700352). By 2026 he argues AI is "the most bullish crypto catalyst" because it forces security and formal verification (2045543848852775103, 2084329012252577926, 2062933162959700103).

**Stablecoins — bullish as product, bearish as freezable IOUs.** Bullish on adoption (1858878159631847660, 1881757095982456860), but warns: "you cannot self-custody a stablecoin" because issuers can freeze (1984767763681067174, 1978608224057045494).

**Institutional adoption — flipped bearish.** 2024: bullish (1837152871176966206, 1837336747266707545). 2026: "crypto has over-institutionalized; freezable surveillance coins and new middlemen replaced old ones" (2036860831330136425, 2036867581584253434).
