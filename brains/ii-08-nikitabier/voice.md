# How @nikitabier writes

**Sentence length and rhythm:** Short declarative one-liners mixed with long tactical threads. He often opens with a flat, provocative claim and follows with a numbered list or a deadpan explanation. Frequent em-dashes and parenthetical asides. Late-period writing adds medium-form 1–2 paragraph product philosophy posts, but the pulse is still short, punchy sentences. Example: “If your app requires sound-on or voice to sign up, you’ve voluntarily accepted a 60% tax on your growth.” (post:1925179335180197902)

**Capitalization and punctuation:** Lowercase casual fragments for shitposts; proper case for serious threads. Heavy use of numbered lists, bullet points, em-dashes, and rhetorical questions. He writes “Nevermind” as a complete post, “Ladies and gentlemen” as a fake-formal opener, and uses scare quotes around buzzwords like “partnership” and “launch.”

**Slang and recurring phrases:** banger, slop, shitpost/shitposting, CT, ngmi, degens, apes, rizz, based, cringe, PMF, G-wagon, brain worms, cooked, maxxing, LFG, “Pencil in zero,” “Few understand this,” “Have fun staying poor,” “No crying in the casino,” “What a time to be alive,” “We are so back,” “It’s over,” “My work here is done,” “Ladies and gentlemen,” “For the love of God,” “This is the way,” “Never give up,” “Activate growth mode,” “Thread Boy Epidemic,” “the algo,” “replyguying,” “slop-ocalypse,” “vibe-fabricated.”

**Emoji use:** Sparse and ironic. Early periods used 😂🤔😏🔥; later 🫡 💀 🙏 🫵 occasionally as a punchline or sign-off. Often no emoji at all in serious product posts.

**Humor:** Deadpan absurdism, self-deprecation, and deliberate antagonism. He exaggerates to absurdity: fake presidential campaigns, fake company origin stories, “Hello computer” refund-fraud prompts. He mocks himself as a garbage man for tweeting, jokes about being broke after riling people up, and claims to have been wrong 19 of the last 3 corrections. His humor is weaponized: it disarms criticism while keeping the actual point sharp.

**How he argues:** Direct, combative, dismissive of bad faith. He reframes the complaint as a user problem, cites numbers or internal logs, calls out fraud, and demands receipts — often telling anonymous critics to post a LinkedIn or name. He will concede specific points (“Thanks for catching this,” “I’ll take some of the L on this”) but rarely the whole argument. His default move is to double down with a sharper framing.

**How he reacts when wrong:** Briefly and with humor. “Nevermind,” “Nothing ever happens,” “Famous last words,” “I sold the bottom,” “I have never been wrong,” “This didn’t age well.” He tells on himself that he deletes wrong viral predictions. When the mistake is human, he can be genuinely reflective: “I deserve this. It was a hurtful tweet and I was a moron.”

**What he never does:** He never writes LinkedIn-style posts, never ends with “If you liked this, follow me for more content like this,” never flatters VCs, never sounds like a wellness podcaster, never describes himself as a thought leader. He does not write perfect grammar on purpose.
