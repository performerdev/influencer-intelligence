# @nikitabier's rules

## Entry
- Target an embarrassingly small audience first. “My rule of thumb is that the target audience should be embarrassingly small, so much so that you’re uncomfortable telling your friends and family what you’re working on.” (post:1255291891647688705)
- Ignore the excitement around a space; find the best companies in a domain. “My heuristic going forward is to ignore the excitement around a space and instead find the best companies in a respective domain.” (post:1344396534398402562)
- Invest in founders who were running scams or hustles at 13, not the extracurricular kids. “My favorite Q to assess a founder is: what were you doing when you were 13? ... I never hesitate to invest in B.” (post:1344779790679564288)
- If a startup says they’re targeting Gen Z, pass. “It might be a bad heuristic but if a startup says they’re targeting Gen Z, I pass.” (post:1419860230205153285)
- Don’t “launch”; seed with a small group, see if it’s working, and then grow. “Never “launch”. Seed with a small group, see if it’s working, and then grow.” (post:1672764498858373122)
- Buy small amounts of shares of products or services that blow you away. “I usually buy a small amount of shares of products or services that blow me away—everything from Ubiquiti routers to household things like Dymonic Caulk to retail experiences like TJ Maxx.” (post:1869071297126482350)
- Be greedy when others are fearful. “Be greedy when others are fearful.” (post:1869596750711030085)
- On euphoric Robinhood-screenshot days, buy volatility and treasuries. “On days when your entire Twitter feed is euphoric screenshots of Robinhood accounts ↓ Buy $VXX and $TLT calls.” (post:1282875700416876544)
- Don’t buy equity without pricing in a possible 50% valuation discount. “go ahead and pencil in a 50% discount and see if you’d still do it.” (post:1819488281450692985)
- Pay whatever it takes to reach the first users; density matters more than CAC. “you should pay whatever it takes to reach that initial cluster—even $20 an install.” (post:1844050353387348081)
- Close seed investors by asking how much they usually invest, then ask if they’d do half. “The only time I’ve ever closed a seed investor is by asking how much they usually invest—and then ask if they would be OK with doing half of that.” (post:1781719280012386670)
- Set terms on your own round and take money immediately on a convertible note. “just set the terms on your own round and raise from many people. When anyone indicates interest, just immediately take their money on a convertible note.” (post:1870642819481706611) (stated once)

## Exit and taking profit
- Take life-changing money at seed stage rather than risk losing it all chasing a bigger outcome. “I’ll take the life-changing money, instead of potentially losing it all in an attempt to enrich VCs.” (post:1386073453669748737)
- Sell secondary at seed stage when the opportunity arises. “it’s still usually highest EV to sell at seed stage if the opportunity arises.” (post:1386076327036346370)
- Never sell anything if you live in California; sell calls instead of shares. “Never sell anything if you live in Taxifornia.” (post:1418634569352290305) (stated once)
- If an opportunity to exit would let you own a home, take it even if earlier than expected. “If an opportunity to exit presents itself where you would make enough to own a home, take it—even if it’s earlier than the expectations you set.” (post:1744428250976371044)
- If an investor says they’ll invest if you find a lead investor, that’s a no and you should move on. “If an investor ever tells you they’ll invest if you find a “lead investor”: that’s a no and you should move on.” (post:1870642819481706611)
- Never accept performance-based earn-outs; take cash. “We don’t do performance-based earn outs, because you don’t have control of the outcome.” (post:2112020622062821693)

## Sizing
- Keep mostly low-volatility boomer stocks with very little tech. “I’m in mostly low volatility boomer stocks. Very little tech. I feel confident holding.” (post:1331276810995175424) (stated once)
- Keep dry powder in the brokerage account for dips. “What percent of dry powder do you keep in your brokerage account for dips?” (post:1326978302339256320)
- Crypto position sizing as a share of net worth with a stated cost basis. “5.5% total net worth at $12,500/coin cost basis.” (post:1363226246411292672) (stated once)
- Put 30% of portfolio into long-duration munis, treasuries, mortgage REITs ahead of rate cuts. “Put 30% of my portfolio into long duration munis, treasuries, mortgage REITs in October last year.” (post:1834611214703542632) (stated once)
- Seed size should be 8% network adoption within 18 hours. “8% network adoption within 18 hours.” (post:1597810030535135232)

## Averaging down
- Double down when the selloff looks stupid relative to the numbers. “I doubled down on the 2nd dip after the earnings call. The sell off seemed stupid based on the numbers.” (post:1358170845038968832)
- Buy and hold and keep adding to positions over time. “Selling is for the birds. Buy and hold and keep adding to the positions over time!” (post:1326978302339256320)

## Hold time
- Leveraged index trades are 1-day holds. “I closed most of it out: TQQQ and /ES futures (1 day hold things). But I did bottom-tick a few long term holds (UI, HOOD, and VOO).” (post:1910314726124376454)

## Risk management
- Cut a startup’s runway in half to force efficiency. “Cut a startup’s runway in half and watch magic happen.” (post:1207422894944346112)
- Use interest-only loans and pledge stock portfolios as collateral to avoid down payments. “You can collateralize the down payment by pledging a stock portfolio ($0 down). And if you opt for interest-only, you can really juice your returns.” (post:1357549098694397952)
- Get a 10-year term so there’s a 3-year buffer if the market is down in year 7. “I got a 10y term so I’d have a 3 year buffer in case the market was down in Year 7 and I needed to wait it out.” (post:1357551431687966723)
- Avoid lifestyle changes based on investment returns above 8%; they are borrowed from future years. “Any investment returns in excess of 8% are borrowed from future years. Consider that before making any lifestyle changes.” (post:1361437339214487556)
- Don’t raise VC for paywall-ad arbitrage apps. “the absolute last thing you should is raise outside funding for Type B.” (post:1849291216166289772)
- Only invest in friends; everything else is a scam. “the only time I’ve made money in startups is when I invest in friends. Everything else is a scam.” (post:1814732228536009085)
- Stick to Coinbase for crypto custody because they can sell shares to cover a catastrophic loss. “Stick to Coinbase. At least they can sell shares to cover a catastrophic loss.” (post:1443972042521395208)
- Use virtual cards with preset limits for recurring charges, but never for SMS providers. “Do NOT do this for SMS providers. Learned the hard way.” (post:1843531483082010927)
- Go to cash when macro uncertainty spikes. “Went to 80% cash.” (post:1918678565840638354)
- Assume all systems will break every few days when scaling exponentially and have redundancy plans. “We had to assume all systems would break every few days & have redundancy plans. And everything did: user support, 2FA, databases.” (post:1637179063403008000)
