# @nikitabier's rules

## Entry
- Target an embarrassingly small audience first. “My rule of thumb is that the target audience should be embarrassingly small, so much so that you’re uncomfortable telling your friends and family what you’re working on.” (1255291891647688705)
- Ignore the excitement around a space; find the best companies in a domain. “My heuristic going forward is to ignore the excitement around a space and instead find the best companies in a respective domain.” (1344396534398402562)
- Invest in founders who were running scams or hustles at 13. “My favorite Q to assess a founder is: what were you doing when you were 13? ... I never hesitate to invest in B.” (1344779790679564288)
- On euphoric Robinhood days, buy VXX and TLT calls. “On days when your entire Twitter feed is euphoric screenshots of Robinhood accounts ↓ Buy $VXX and $TLT calls.” (1282875700416876544)
- Buy growth names trading under 10x sales when the market is broken. “Just did myself. Trading at 6x sales. Market is broken.” (1390335338774556678)
- Buy the dip when the selloff looks stupid relative to the numbers. “I doubled down on the 2nd dip after the earnings call. The sell off seemed stupid based on the numbers.” (1358170845038968832)
- Pass on startups that say they target Gen Z. “if a startup says they’re targeting Gen Z, I pass.” (1419860230205153285)
- Never build an app to meet up with friends. “Never build an app to \"meetup with friends.\"” (1481118418883399686)
- Throw out social apps without hourly recurring sessions. “If you’re building a social app and you’re not seeing recurring sessions on an hourly basis each day among some cohort of users, don’t waste another minute: throw everything out and go back to the drawing board.” (1610710405411926016)
- Don’t build sharing flows until users hack it themselves or demand is demonstrated. “If you can, don’t build any sharing flows until (1) you see users hacking it themselves with screenshots or (2) demand to share that way is already demonstrated in an existing in-market app.” (1607271350984658945)
- Avoid launching a real-friends social app Thursday to Sunday; seed Monday at 7pm or Sunday after dinner. “If you're launching a real-friends social app, be cautious releasing Thurs to Sunday—for 2 critical reasons: 1/ You need several sequential in-person days to get word-of-mouth going 2/ The quietest online days are Fri & Saturday” (1591555991447175169)
- Seed with a small group; never “launch.” “Never “launch”. Seed with a small group, see if it’s working, and then grow.” (1672764498858373122)
- Geofence California and New York out of the app and launch there last. “I always geofence California and New York out of the app and launch there last. Not worth the distraction when you’re scaling up.” (1729488294314917988)
- Test apps in the American South and Midwest before coastal audiences. “Every app I’ve built I’ve quietly tested in the American South and Midwest for many months.” (1660998272612220928)
- Use a single authentication method aligned with distribution and friending. “you should only allow one method—and have that method be on the same “protocol” as your distribution & friending system” (1662100378500866049)
- Close seed investors by asking how much they usually invest, then ask if they’d do half. “The only time I’ve ever closed a seed investor is by asking how much they usually invest—and then ask if they would be OK with doing half of that.” (1781719280012386670)
- Replace “launch” with “test” and control who is exposed. “You should remove the word “launch” from your vocabulary and replace it with “test”.” (1800197768981762396)
- If a product requires a partnership, run. “If your product requires a \"partnership\", run.” (1481118427129344009)
