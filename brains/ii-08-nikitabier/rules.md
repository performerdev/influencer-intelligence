# @nikitabier's rules

## Entry

- Target an embarrassingly small audience first; be uncomfortable telling friends and family. “My rule of thumb is that the target audience should be embarrassingly small, so much so that you’re uncomfortable telling your friends and family what you’re working on.” (1255291891647688705)
- Never “launch”; seed with a small group, see if it’s working, then grow. “Never ‘launch’. Seed with a small group, see if it’s working, and then grow.” (1672764498858373122)
- If a social app doesn’t produce recurring hourly sessions in some cohort, throw everything out. “If you’re building a social app and you’re not seeing recurring sessions on an hourly basis each day among some cohort of users, don’t waste another minute: throw everything out and go back to the drawing board.” (1610710405411926016)
- Buy growth names trading under 10x sales when the market seems broken. “Just did myself. Trading at 6x sales. Market is broken.” (1390335338774556678)
- Buy small amounts of shares in products or services that blow you away. “I usually buy a small amount of shares of products or services that blow me away—everything from Ubiquiti routers to household things like Dymonic Caulk to retail experiences like TJ Maxx.” (1869071297126482350)
- Be greedy when others are fearful. “Be greedy when others are fearful” (1869596750711030085)
- Make version 1.0 so verticalized that target users feel it was made for them and founders feel embarrassed. “Version 1.0 of any product should be so verticalized that: 1) when a target user lands on the page, their only reaction is ‘Well, shit. This was made for me. I better sign up.’ 2) when you tell your friends & family what you're working on, it is so narrowly focused that you feel embarrassed” (1889370882696683755)
- Test apps quietly in the American South and Midwest before coastal audiences. “Every app I’ve built I’ve quietly tested in the American South and Midwest for many months.” (1660998272612220928)

## Exit and taking profit

- Sell secondary at seed stage when the opportunity arises. “it’s still usually highest EV to sell at seed stage if the opportunity arises.” (1386076327036346370)
- Take life-changing seed-stage money instead of chasing a bigger outcome. “I’ll take the life-changing money, instead of potentially losing it all in an attempt to enrich VCs.” (1386073453669748737)
- Never sell anything if you live in California for tax reasons. “Never sell anything if you live in Taxifornia.” (1418634569352290305)
- If an investor says they’ll invest only with a lead, treat it as a no and move on. “If an investor ever tells you they’ll invest if you find a ‘lead investor’: that’s a no and you should move on.” (1870642819481706611)
- If an exit offers enough to own a home, take it even if earlier than planned. “If an opportunity to exit presents itself where you would make enough to own a home, take it—even if it’s earlier than the expectations you set.” (1744428250976371044)

## Sizing

- Keep mostly low-volatility boomer stocks with very little tech. “I’m in mostly low volatility boomer stocks. Very little tech. I feel confident holding.” (1331276810995175424)
- Keep dry powder in the brokerage account for dips. “What percent of dry powder do you keep in your brokerage account for dips?” (1326978302339256320)
- Crypto position sizing as a share of net worth with a stated cost basis. “5.5% total net worth at $12,500/coin cost basis” (1363226246411292672)
- Seed size should be 8% network adoption within 18 hours. “8% network adoption within 18 hours” (1597810030535135232)

## Averaging down

- Double down when a post-earnings selloff looks stupid relative to the numbers. “I doubled down on the 2nd dip after the earnings call. The sell off seemed stupid based on the numbers.” (1358170845038968832)
- Keep adding to value ETFs for months. “Kept adding to Value ETFs for two months and feels good about it.” (1326181408969093120)
- Buy and hold and keep adding to positions over time. “Buy and hold and keep adding to the positions over time!” (1326978302339256320)

## Hold time

- Buy and hold; selling is for the birds. “Selling is for the birds. Buy and hold and keep adding to the positions over time!” (1326978302339256320)
- Leveraged index trades are 1-day holds. “I closed most of it out: TQQQ and /ES futures (1 day hold things). But I did bottom-tick a few long term holds (UI, HOOD, and VOO).” (1910314726124376454)

## Risk management

- Cut a startup’s runway in half to force efficiency. “Cut a startup’s runway in half and watch magic happen” (1207422894944346112)
- Use interest-only loans and pledge stock portfolios as collateral. “You can collateralize the down payment by pledging a stock portfolio ($0 down). And if you opt for interest-only, you can really juice your returns.” (1357549098694397952)
- Get a 10-year term so there’s a 3-year buffer if the market is down in year 7. “I got a 10y term so I’d have a 3 year buffer in case the market was down in Year 7 and I needed to wait it out” (1357551431687966723)
- Stick to Coinbase for crypto custody because they can cover a catastrophic loss with shares. “Stick to Coinbase. At least they can sell shares to cover a catastrophic loss.” (1443972042521395208)
- Assume all systems will break every few days when scaling exponentially and have redundancy plans. “We had to assume all systems would break every few days & have redundancy plans. And everything did: user support, 2FA, databases.” (1637179063403008000)
- Go to cash when macro uncertainty spikes. “Went to 80% cash.” (1918678565840638354)
- Avoid lifestyle changes based on investment returns above 8%. “Any investment returns in excess of 8% are borrowed from future years. Consider that before making any lifestyle changes.” (1361437339214487556)
- No crying in the casino. “No crying in the casino.” (1899191398370508805)
