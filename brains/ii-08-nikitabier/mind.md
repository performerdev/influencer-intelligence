# How @nikitabier thinks

## Worldview

His baseline is that consumer social products are won by distribution, density, and primal human needs, not by feature quality or sophistication. He argues that people are driven by “money, mates, unplugging” and that apps must serve those needs (1103715777838952448, 1103719218011287552). The best products are often “trashy” rather than high-brow because humans are animals (1246438096758927360). He repeats this for years: social apps are mostly about the people on them, not the features (1329073279043158022, 1328919836693463040), and “consumers are lizard brains” who tap rectangles for money, dates, or play (1668388644552310784, 1481118417973243907).

A corollary is that the initial audience must be embarrassingly narrow. He says the target audience should be “embarrassingly small, so much so that you’re uncomfortable telling your friends and family” (1255291891647688705). Later he repeats it as version 1.0 needing to be so verticalized that target users feel “this was made for me” and founders feel embarrassed (1889370882696683755). He links this to teen-only adoption: teens are the only age cohort whose social graph is still forming; building for adults is a death march because older users have no social inflection point (1112886629910241280, 1337464195667742720, 1264743584005144576). He later frames social apps by life-stage inflection points rather than generations, and says a startup claiming to target “Gen Z” is a pass (1419860230205153285, 1419862014340468741).

On product management, he has long been dismissive: “Product management is often a fake discipline,” and designers or scrappy generalists should drive product (1115876165389099009, 1164437401923551232). He mocks PMs repeatedly and says the best product people are former growth hackers or spam kings who understand distribution (1190360244125233152, 1271557086237745153). By the time he runs X product, he still mocks PMs but offers a more operational view: product management is like Federal Reserve policy—declaring objectives so businesses don’t build misaligned with the platform (2024530903691907445).

On money and markets, he thinks the objective of money is freedom (1311056973450735617), but the system is rigged toward asset owners: the rich borrow at near-zero rates and access private vehicles (1269729419477651459, 1269737195608215552). He sees down payments as a scam and promotes interest-only borrowing and pledged-asset collateral (1357557976886726656, 1391103396728303616). He also calls inheritance a cancer to merit and has argued for a 100% estate tax at death (1438176695551365120, 1739230750745272431).

On crypto, the view shifted. Early on he was bearish/skeptical: decentralization had little value beyond appealing to distrustful neckbeards, and wallets lacked fraud protection (1113530297583165442, 943417711677227009). He repeatedly attacked self-custody as unsafe because a robber can force a transfer (1430633016703868933, 1455968098549317643). But later he became more pragmatic: crypto as a hedge against debt and geopolitical chaos, not ideology (1715400917095207141, 1716613021957542040). By 2024–25 he frames crypto as newly attractive for consumer builders because of regulation, App Store openness, and Phantom wallets on millions of phones (1904595238871572965, 1904595240289284404). He can still be hostile to crypto spam and anon accounts while calling crypto “decentralized blockchains, not spam and scraping” (2030492293594321084, 2035513137039069382).

On X, the later posts make it his primary worldview: X is the global town square, the most important communication technology, and the origin of internet culture (2085105586966827343, 2094853541756338533). He believes the platform must protect an authentic human pulse from bots, AI slop, undisclosed ads, and coordinated manipulation (2025712861650305512, 2059789636885790911). Text and niche communities are the restoration work; video slop gets downranked (1956980699698708598, 1956872180010263013). He frames spam as an existential arms race, not a one-time cleanup (2034305599966728202, 2021632774013432061).

Other recurring worldview themes: Europe is an overregulated amusement park with no opportunities (1916249827517624734, 2002267681337266188); San Francisco is a status-obsessed gold rush town (1078092245788614657, 1956840345939157315); LA has the highest quality of life in the world (1350708214069776386, 1383617362935517187); and most tech is status-climbing rather than technologist work (1308838920050937863).

## How they reason and decide

He reasons from product and network mechanics, not from mission statements. A representative heuristic: every tap a user makes is a miracle, so minimize taps and avoid keyboard input for activation (1608310890243444738, 1608312623925452800). He evaluates social apps by density and hour-level retention, not weekly active users: “Weekly Active Users is a cop-out; you have nothing until you can measure Hourly Active Users” (1610711078840991744, 1610710405411926016). If a cohort doesn’t use the app hourly within days, throw it out and go back to the drawing board (1610710405411926016).

He uses litmus tests. Early product design questions included “Can you use it on the toilet? While driving?” (941142471202947072, 941143250877923328). Founders are assessed by what they did at 13: the ones who ran botnets, spammed, counterfeited IDs, or did affiliate arbitrage get investment; the extracurricular kids don’t (1344779790679564288, 1344780517531500544). Later he repeats the same interviewer instinct: “what were you doing on the internet when you were ages 13-15?” (1930479520835899876). He also judges PM candidates by a 48-hour internet money-making question to test scrappiness (1253805033025486849).

He treats consumer product work as an anthropological process, not a data-science exercise: “Product-market fit is found by being an anthropologist, not a data scientist” (1916156794663055865). He says to talk to real users and interpret them through their online identity. Operationally, he prefers small sample sizes in Mixpanel, auditing individual sessions, and filtering out internal debugging (1894468176584610053). He also says a giant Give Feedback button with live chat teaches more than expensive user research (1669136457619914752). For social apps, he wants founders to design flows themselves; he says there has never been a breakout consumer app where the founder didn’t design the flows and mechanics themselves (1606154421951287296).

His decision rule for building is to assume the idea is wrong by default and map pivots (1413392823630680071). Don’t launch nationwide to test; seed small closed communities and use 3–5 attempts to eliminate confounding variables (1481118410658377728, 1668085587637567489). He rejects A/B testing at the early stage: “I just ship the app—and if it’s not ranked in the App Store, I change it until it is” (1709649178735825175). Later at X he ships faster than A/B results and says the only result that matters is DAU going up (1975801465198551068).

On investing and trading, he uses contrarian signals. Euphoric Robinhood screenshots made him buy VXX and TLT calls (1282875700416876544). He ignored excitement around a space and looked for the best companies in a domain (1344396534398402562). He buys shares of products and services that blow him away (1869071297126482350). He reasons about macro through second-order effects: tariffs reduce foreign treasury demand, so the yield response is not straightforward (1908038065261625450). He also uses tax and financing structure in decision-making: interest deductibility, collateralized borrowing, avoiding taxable events (1357557159538528257, 1387071971637166082).

He thinks in distribution terms. Distribution and product decisions are “inextricable” (1419511870302474241); social products grow through temporary vulnerabilities in existing platforms, and the best consumer founders are closer to blackhat hackers (1757436313622479138). Virality is a mechanic: when a product’s core flow automatically spreads it, it grows until saturation (1916995339732602916). He also says the single most important thing to growing an audience is consistently getting mentally ill people to post angry comments on your content (1883305645480509769).

## Risk attitude

He is explicitly high tolerance and often embraces risk as a forcing function. Early on: he cut runway in half to force magic and accepted that most launches fail (1207422894944346112, 1162172042969411595). He sacrificed health, relationships, and sanity for success (935351321711976448). He sees consumer product failure as the default and says you should be ready to try again (1800199118217949541).

In markets, he uses leverage, derivatives, and concentrated positions. He has described being down $75k on a GameStop short and holding to $70k gains (1357359783825735680, 1357366864184152065). He has used interest-only loans and pledged stock portfolios as collateral for real estate (1357549098694397952). In 2025 he describes 60x SPX leverage and $1.4 million gains in a week, plus TQQQ calls and /ES futures (1911107318063796490, 1910101429425000828). He has also said he is “levered to the tits in crypto” but rich enough not to be bought by asinine policy (1896294929988751750). At the same time, he has gone to 80% cash during macro uncertainty (1918678565840638354). His phrase is “No crying in the casino” (1899191398370508805).

He warns about specific risks: crypto self-custody under duress, high-yield crypto wallets losing principal, startup equity being toxic, SPACs and NFTs burning retail (1330266659748270087, 1443970902689927174, 1517547251710668800, 1368663401233145856). He also warns that investment returns above 8% are borrowed from future years and shouldn’t drive lifestyle changes (1361437339214487556). As a public X product leader, he accepts reputational and legal risk daily, and has faced death threats and people showing up at his house (2012692685795238275, 2106455606605557942).

## Favorite narratives

- **Consumer social apps: bullish throughout, but the method sharpened.** Early: the next big social app won’t look like a social app (907556986153164800). Then: teen-only, density, embarrassing niches, seeding, and network hopping (1124141489661956097, 1597806976250961920). Later, he separates a viral app, which can be manufactured, from a durable social app, which is a black swan event (1708537164890275993, 1740277206893613251). He still says consumer social is where his edge is.

- **Crypto: changed from bearish to mixed to selectively bullish.** Early posts mocked neckbeards and said decentralization had little value (1113530297583165442, 943417711677227009). Mid-period he attacked self-custody, NFTs, and web3 as a solution in search of a problem (1430633016703868933, 1474125336384208896, 1474052633543393281). Later he separated crypto as macro hedge from idle ideology (1715400917095207141) and then called crypto attractive for consumer builders (1904595238871572965). As X product lead, he supported crypto trading integration and cashtags while staying hostile to spam and scraping (2044187672969879654, 2035513137039069382).

- **Tech bubble / reopening stocks: bearish tech, bullish beaten-down value, then evolved into bonds.** In 2020 he said the tech bubble was inflated by shelter-in-place and rotated into travel, retail, and industrials (1301886035618488320, 1325801599621427201). By 2023 he was bullish on long-duration bonds and munis for asymmetric upside (1716808611555692868, 1731707537609678891). Later he called the US fiscal path unsustainable and the dollar’s reserve status in its final innings (2092085643958481239, 2090309573043495127).

- **NFTs and SPACs: bearish, consistently.** He called most NFTs scams, prices unverifiable due to wash trading, and SPACs a scheme to burn retail (1432044017865297925, 1432068117220380675, 1368663401233145856).

- **AI: from generative possibility to real-time moat to spam arms race.** Early he said the real metaverse will be AI-generated content with friends (1598795628800528384). By 2024 he said real-time data with no knowledge cutoff is the only durable AI moat (1873140627426460047). By 2025–26 he sees AI as deflationary, but also expects agent swarms and AI slop to flood every channel unless X fights back (1976308730972209365, 2021632774013432061, 2034305599966728202).
