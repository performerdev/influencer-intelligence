# How @nikitabier thinks

## Worldview
The through-line is that consumer tech is a distribution and psychology problem, not a feature problem. The early posts insist social apps win through distribution, seeding, friendfinder/invite systems, and network effects rather than design (post:907556986153164800, post:1001666968917757952, post:1124141489661956097, post:1329073279043158022, post:1352749766204002304, post:1403498766737444865, post:1481118414160613376). People are primal and predictable: they download apps for love, money, or play; they are lazy, selfish, and have short attention spans (post:1103715777838952448, post:1246438096758927360, post:1273437328866832384, post:1273447162320261120, post:1481118417973243907).

That sharpens over time. By 2022 he says consumer social apps are won by insight about human psychology and execution, not by concept alone; the difference between exponential decline and growth can be a single invite button or ranking tweak (post:1557132295714222080, post:1558712991700246528, post:1558714101710200835, post:1702335787688427826). The best consumer founders are willing to acknowledge shameful truths about people and attach their name to the product (post:1675526162318065668, post:1750225847595016491). Density matters more than global reach; a real flex is ranking on the App Store while only available in one state (post:1561046570644099072, post:1579933823415951361, post:1660998272612220928). By 2024–25 he pushes narrowing the audience and distilling the product to a simple marketable sentence (post:1770088599687504227, post:1777712633082212411). Launch and then iterate is losing; first impressions are all that matter (post:1800197768981762396). By 2025–26 the only thing that matters in consumer software is velocity of shipping, first-mover advantage is a myth, and the big wins in a mature product are old neglected flows where one-line changes can move metrics +10% overnight (post:1990479124536905866, post:1943196244156776696, post:1947913957127102655). Every tap is a miracle, so never waste a user’s time (post:1958799724934570401).

On startups and money, early he says startups take a decade and founders should take life-changing money rather than gamble for world domination (post:1307698958341349376, post:1386073453663748737, post:1449451256263499779). By 2022–24 this becomes harsher: startup equity is toxic, raising money only increases the probability of failure, and most employees with 90-day exercise windows will end up with nothing (post:1517547251710668800, post:1517970411295690752, post:1730994642991173886). The venture path is often a trap: founders spend their youth in meetings waiting for a liquidity event, and VCs mistreat founders so they go scorched earth (post:1739694564104163809, post:1768288421967974891). Most consumer businesses younger than five years can die overnight from a data breach, bad article, regulation, API shutdown, or key engineer leaving (post:1769882609591447897). The Lina Khan FTC environment discourages big swings and acquisitions, pushing founders toward fleeting cashflowing apps (post:1781191787123781713, post:1781327539627319336, post:1849635021339992482).

On macro, early 2020–2022 he thought tech was in a bubble and beaten-down travel/retail “Shit Stocks” would resurge as the economy reopened (post:1301886035618488320, post:1325801599621427201, post:1325874502207639552). He used public market signals: calling hotels for occupancy, App Store charts, airport crowds (post:1275971195817521153, post:1275975083094208512, post:1337930624175198208). By 2024–25 the best real-time tracker of stock market movements is Trump’s TruthSocial notifications (post:1899485768198050139). He went to 80% cash at one point (post:1918678565840638354). AI is deflationary even as gold and bitcoin act like hyperinflation is coming (post:1976308730972209365).

On platforms, Apple is more powerful than the government at destroying ad-based businesses and shaping the economy; the entire economy is on a conveyor belt waiting to be swallowed by Apple (post:1550496291108429825, post:1647985187005890561, post:1701569732061606262). Big Tech culture is data-driven and ruthless, while Apple’s culture lets designers double scope based on feelings; both have tradeoffs (post:1666057507133878276, post:1666059419577204737). Facebook/Meta is mixed: early he calls it one of the better BigTech acquirers whose decline is bad for American innovation and benefits China (post:1461064859148177411, post:1489007202056634370, post:1489023058090225670); later he mocks Meta but still respects Zuck dogfooding it (post:1676825994307706882, post:1707740224095146087, post:1762482982437126231). X becomes central after he joins: the most important social network, the global town square where internet culture originates and the world’s most influential people convene (post:1939723101723574703, post:1966028654267568607, post:1979728637751492732, post:2094853541756338533).

On crypto, the view changed. Early: crypto is mostly a scam/gambling ecosystem; NFTs are wash-traded; web3 is overhyped with no real products (post:1370805302812024831, post:1432068117220380675, post:1437104153713467394, post:1438617943206596616, post:1473404180589015040, post:1474052633543393281). Decentralization is fundamentally broken until reversibility or insurance exists for transfers made under duress (post:1330266659748270087, post:1455968098549317643, post:1456135214988595204, post:1478255235218821120). In 2022–23 he says crypto is mostly speculation and scams, but the underlying technology may eventually matter; he doesn’t believe in crypto but isn’t sure what else to believe in given debt levels and war (post:1535688312156848128, post:1715400917095207141, post:1716659277320896702). By 2025 crypto has become more attractive for consumer builders because of the regulatory environment, the App Store welcoming crypto apps, and meme coin mania putting wallets on millions of phones (post:1904595238871572965). Solana is bullish; meme coins stay bearish (post:1885374826904158230, post:1904595238871572965, post:1904595240289284404). In 2025–26 crypto remains mixed (post:1932236714153513214, post:2043893779552448735, post:2049704418669723729).

On people and society, products that resonate often violate traditional Anglo values, and founders should be conscious of their bias (post:1780967619199467685). Women account for most time spent online and retail spending; men need not exist online (post:1792524799090237578, post:1792546215743361397). Most people never hit their potential because they feel too much shame to verbalize precisely what they’re good at (post:1794953795803414993). Community is mostly a fake term; most cited communities are just groups of people shilling things (post:1773331696235995625). A child’s future impact is mainly affected by how early they act independently; unwalkable suburbs delay agency (post:1817376762785095945). The greatest threat to a free-speech internet is militant political groups coordinating to doxx and silence dissent (post:2093765112343806292). People engaging in fraud are always the first and loudest to complain (post:2048074444132995517). The best posters were not famous before joining X; they were forged in fire by replyguying in obscurity (post:1950797871785423203).

## How they reason and decide
He uses blunt heuristics and litmus tests: the toilet/driving test, 25% retention, 70% contacts access, 7 new users per session (post:941142471202947072, post:951506360159498240, post:1403498766737444865, post:1481118424503775233). He inverts problems and interactions to find explosive engagement loops (post:1262997926311149569). He tests ideas in small communities before scaling; narrow wedges beat broad launches (post:1481118409785970692, post:1481118410658377728, post:1481118412122165248). He backs founders who did dirty work young—botnets, spam, piracy—over those with conventional resumes (post:1344779790679564288). He ignores hype around a space and instead finds the best companies in a domain (post:1344396137105555456, post:1344396534398402562). He uses “why it won’t work” slides in pitches to build credibility (post:1393275250230530048). He treats App Review like crossing a border: keep your mouth shut, do the minimum, then party (post:1273094824418095107).

For consumer products, he says look at where users’ eyes are already fixated with high attention, then work backwards to appear in that surface (post:1558532557687296000). Test in Middle America and the South first; if it works there, it will work everywhere (post:1660998272612220928, post:1661098399460999169). Study old screenshots and marketing materials from incumbent apps at their inflection point to understand why they did what they did (post:1742985773144744017). Draft a murderboard of worst headlines before launching, then reposition the product to be bulletproof against criticism (post:1744091716423176433). Product-market fit will be abundantly obvious; data will often deceive you, especially with instrumentation (post:1716470985103184196). Measure Hourly Active Users, not Weekly Active Users; recurring hourly sessions are the real bar for social apps (post:1610710405411926016, post:1610711078840991744). Consider every tap a miracle; get signup and profile completion down to minimal taps with no keyboard required (post:1608310890243444738, post:1608312623925452800). Build an academic culture of intellectual honesty so being wrong is met with quick stoic acceptance and teams don’t chase mirages (post:1753915234794774978). Set up redundancy for commodity service providers and negotiate aggressively when they try to extort you after going viral (post:1738720690772258841). The ideal user experience is a moving target based on network density; sacrifice UX until network value reaches local maxima (post:1754652959231619467).

Later he adds: sequencing what needs to be validated is the most valuable skill (post:1798372438960730423, post:1798374551883280520); do things that don’t scale to avoid confounding results from poor execution (post:1820660351366738276); use a simple table of users sorted by last active time and interpret behavior through their online identity—be less of a data scientist and more of an anthropologist (post:1916156794663055865). When testing share features, use connected groups to measure Peer-validated Share Rate; otherwise share rate is understated (post:1810346603141885998). For network-based products, run experiments in small closed communities when possible; mega launches are harder to iterate (post:1917931582670733394). Invert time to value so users experience the Aha Moment in seconds (post:1813735668276928682). Use Mixpanel with small sample sizes and audit individual sessions to avoid delusions (post:1894468176584610053). If a product’s core flow automatically spreads it to new users, it usually keeps growing until market saturation (post:1916995339732602916).

At X, he uses X itself as a focus group: suggest a feature and get every perspective and edge-case in 60 minutes, taking half the risk off the table before engineering (post:1981038797665292655). He sits down with 2–5 diverse users per day to go over their timeline and ask why they see what they see; every decision must have an answer (post:1976512694485463043). To catalyze a new behavior on a social network you have to overcorrect for a short period, especially for a long-tenured userbase (post:2012928978940834118). The first step to eliminating spam is eliminating the incentive; he joins scam WhatsApp groups to understand ecosystems (post:1961869502569959816, post:1967958967797318095). He refuses A/B tests: if it’s a good feature it goes into production; the only result is DAU going up (post:1975801465198551068). Product management is the art of writing the least amount of code for the greatest benefit to users (post:2017790464884457863). A lot of product management for a social network is declaring policy objectives so businesses and apps don’t build misaligned with the platform—like the Federal Reserve (post:2024530903691907445). Build-in-public is probably wrong for startups, but good for established companies to pre-announce, get feedback, and launch with precision (post:2016186182628249791). The best policy enforcement tool for a free-speech network is the community shaming you in the replies (post:2028239165746454757). When running a growing consumer product, the only time to sleep is Friday night to Sunday mid-afternoon, when the internet turns off (post:1969534048864518208).

## Risk attitude
Early he makes big contrarian bets and jokes about ruin: travel stocks, GameStop short, Snap calls, catastrophic outcomes (post:1325801599621427201, post:1357359783825735680, post:1489323431904481282, post:1489346665290948612). He warns that crypto transfers under duress have no recourse; robbers can force transfers (post:1330266659748270087, post:1455968098549317643, post:1456135214988595204). He notes investment returns above 8% are borrowed from future years (post:1361437339214487556). He acknowledges that most startups fail and that he had 14 duds before a winner (post:1380599922857144321). He jokes about losing everything on trades and being margin called (post:1484669429971439616, post:1485679049011712002).

From 2022–24 risk language becomes operational: raising money only increases your probability of failure, defined as founders not getting rich (post:1730994642991173886); most seed deals where founders got drunk on fundraising and raised massive rounds are now zero, while quiet founders who obsessed about users for 4 years are clean 10x (post:1631699586988531713); most consumer businesses younger than 5 years can die overnight, so mitigate risk relentlessly once product is resonating (post:1769882609591447897); if an opportunity to exit presents itself where you’d make enough to own a home, take it even if earlier than expected (post:1744428250976371044); if a founder you invested in went to Art Basel, pencil in $0 (post:1735110553339588749).

In 2024–25 risk sharpens around product validation and capital: the most insidious risk is running inconclusive tests due to undercommitting (post:1770477694968270895); a failed launch is far more expensive than paying up to $20 per install for initial users (post:1844050353387348081); if you raise outside funding for a paywall-ad arbitrage app, the only people dumber than you are your investors (post:1849291216166289772); if a company you invested in has a logo on the door before 1 million DAU, pencil in zero (post:1923222477817237796); if you’re about to buy equity, pencil in a 50% discount and see if you’d still do it (post:1819488281450692985). He admits losing several million dollars while calling it an incredible week for content (post:1909811364329103832). His G Wagon was repossessed from NVDA call options, so he downgraded to the Mini G Wagon (post:1829939603727663392).

In 2025–26 risk is about platform survival and agents: every wasted tap compounds until the network evaporates (post:1958799724934570401); agent swarms will suffocate every website and form, with small companies and government sites most vulnerable (post:2102432368158245252, post:2107157904168239416); all channels thought safe from spam will be flooded and unusable within 90 days (post:2021632774013432061); it would be negligent not to have every tool ready to protect the platform against the AI slop apocalypse (post:2034305599966728202). The job is physically taxing—cortisol levels and biological age rising—and building impactful products is not for everyone (post:2071985808991789204, post:1969534048864518208).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He is explicit that most startups failed for him: he had 14 duds before a winner (post:1380599922857144321). Being wrong about what users want is normal; he says you will regularly be wrong, which is why velocity matters (post:1990479124536905866). His preferred fix is intellectual honesty: build a culture where being wrong is met with quick stoic acceptance rather than chasing mirages (post:1753915234794774978). He admits losing several million dollars on trades while still calling it an incredible week for content (post:1909811364329103832). He jokes about the repo: “My G Wagon was repossessed this weekend (NVDA call options) so I had to downgrade to the Mini G Wagon” (post:1829939603727663392). The running “Nevermind” and post-deletion habit appears as a correction mechanism rather than a long apology; he treats wrongness as the cost of shipping (post:1975801465198551068).
