# How @nikitabier thinks

## Worldview

He is a consumer-product builder who repeatedly insists the product is the distribution, not the feature set. Early on: "Consumer social products win through seeding, marketing, and growth innovation, not better features; social is fundamentally text messaging and photo posting" (post:1124141489661956097). He says the next big social app "won't look like a social app; it will emerge from a new behavior or platform" (post:907556986153164800), and that teens are the only viable initial audience because their social graph is still forming (post:1112886629910241280, post:1264743584005144576). By 2022 he refined this from "teens" to life-stage inflection points, declaring "If a startup says they're targeting Gen Z, I pass" (post:1419860230205153285) and "People download apps to solve core human needs: finding love, making or saving money, and play" (post:1481118417973243907). He thinks in lizard-brain terms: "Consumers are lizard brains who tap rectangles for basic needs" (post:1668388644552310784), and "High-brow products flop; trashy products grow because humans are animals" (post:1246438096758927360).

His view of the industry is anti-credential and anti-prestige. "99% of the tech sector is status-climbers obsessed with brand, not technologists" (post:1308838920050937863). Product management is "often a fake discipline" (post:1115876165389099009); the best product people are "former growth hackers or spam kings who understand distribution" (post:1190360244125233152). Venture-backed startups are a bad way to make money; scrappy IAP/ad-arbitrage apps can clear millions with better risk/reward (post:1265830766665060352, post:1279826442830807041, post:1280287813791961089). He later sharpened this into "The greatest trick VCs played is making it glamorous to spend your youth in meetings waiting for a liquidity event" (post:1739694564104163809), and "Raising money only increases the probability of failure for founders" (post:1730994642991173886). He respects people who build distribution, not resumes.

He believes society runs on money, mates, and unplugging (post:1103715777838952448), the tax code enriches real estate holders (post:1310620905777713153), and inheritance is "a cancer to capitalism and merit" (post:1438176695551365120); later he went further: "Inheritance/estate tax should be 100% at death" (post:1739230750745272431). LA is the highest quality of life in the world (post:1350708214069776386, post:1383540274958200837), Europe is an "amusement park with no opportunities" (post:1916249827517624734), and its decline is terminal, overregulated, and brain-draining (post:2002267681337266188). America is a meritocracy but with systemic inequities (post:1700925103544885570).

On the internet, he sees Twitter/X as a game of stirring the pot: outrage and shitposts are the point (post:1358515393493520385). Shitposting signals intellectual honesty in a world of grift (post:1432378353936338951); "the richest and best-performing people online are shitposting meme kings" (post:1357198839359303680). As X product leadership later, he held that X is the global town square and "must resist anything that misrepresents or adulterates the human pulse" (post:2025712861650305512). Bots, spam, and AI slop are the greatest risk (post:2032279113743155634, post:2034305599966728202), and social surfaces "decay into slop by default" (post:2110049188142813692). He thinks AI is deflationary even as gold and bitcoin act like hyperinflation is coming (post:1976308730972209365), and AI's value shift is "from deterministic outputs to probabilistic ones; young builders wired this way have an advantage" (post:2101763620954894647).

## How they reason and decide

Product decisions come from a small set of heuristics, not broad strategy. He uses retention as a filter: "I would regularly shut down apps that didn't retain 25% of users" (post:951506360159498240), and "Weekly Active Users is a cop-out; you have nothing until you can measure Hourly Active Users" (post:1610711078840991744). Every tap is a miracle: "When designing a consumer product, you should consider every tap by a user to be a miracle" (post:1608310890243444738). He assumes an idea is wrong by default: "If you're building a social app, you should assume by default that your idea is wrong and map out the possible pivots" (post:1413392823630680071). He refuses big launches: "Never 'launch'. Seed with a small group, see if it's working, and then grow" (post:1672764498858373122); later: replace "launch" with "test" (post:1800197768981762396). He runs 3–5 launch attempts to eliminate confounders (post:1668085587637567489), tests in the South and Midwest (post:1660998272612220928), and geofences California and New York out (post:1729488294314917988). Density is the bar: "8% network adoption within 18 hours" (post:1597810030535135232), and "If you're building a social app and you're not seeing recurring sessions on an hourly basis each day among some cohort of users, don't waste another minute: throw everything out and go back to the drawing board" (post:1610710405411926016).

He reasons about product-market fit like an operator, not a data scientist. Early on he inverts interactions to find explosive engagement loops (post:1262997926311149569) and uses "Speed to the Aha Moment" as a metric (post:1227765328651812864). He says "Most invites/referrals/shares go out in the first 48 hours; retention is a guarantee of acquisition growth but growth can happen without it via intense short-term usage" (post:1577726695141912576). Later he distilled virality into "four reasons people post: to sound smart, be funny, look hot, look rich" (post:1841144495808696733). He validates by simulating the populated experience before building (post:1804214914472644975), and says "Don't A/B test; ship the app and change it until it ranks in the App Store" (post:1709649178735825175). As X leader he relied on direct user observation: "Sits down with 2-5 diverse users per day to watch their timeline and ask 'why am I seeing this'" (post:1976512694485463043), and "Treats X itself as the best focus group: suggest a feature and get every perspective and edge-case in 60 minutes" (post:1981038797665292655).

Investing decisions are contrarian and field-research based. He called hotel front desks to check occupancy as real-time demand signal (post:1275971195817521153). He used euphoric Robinhood screenshots as a signal to buy VXX and TLT calls (post:1282875700416876544). He ignores excitement and finds best companies in a domain (post:1344396534398402562). He buys shares of products that blow him away (post:1869071297126482350). He assesses founders by "what were you doing when you were 13?"—preferring botnets, spam, counterfeit IDs, affiliate arbitrage over extracurriculars (post:1344779790679564288). He later added: "The best interview question is: what were you doing on the internet when you were ages 13-15?" (post:1930479520835899876).

He thinks from second-order effects. On tariffs: "Tariffs reduce foreign treasury demand, which counteracts the yield drop" (post:1908038065261625450). On banks: he moved money to money market funds because banks invested deposits in long-duration bonds, creating a silent run (post:1634660590307389441). On market cycles: "Market cycles keep getting faster in the internet age; each successive one is half the duration of the previous" (post:1884060441149858289).

## Risk attitude

He is high-risk and often leverage-friendly, but he has learned to hedge. Early on he accepted that success required sacrificing health, relationships, and sanity (post:935351321711976448). He advocates cutting runway to force efficiency (post:1207422894944346112). He shorted GameStop, was down $75k, held, and closed for $70k gains (post:1357359783825735680). He uses interest-only loans and pledges stock portfolios as collateral (post:1357549098694397952). He negotiated margin to 0.9% (post:1449439667930103814). In 2025 he traded 60x leverage on SPX and closed $1.4 million in gains in a week, with "zero insider knowledge" (post:1911107318063796490); he also bought 450 TQQQ calls and 30 /ES futures, closing both for ~$400k (post:1910101429425000828). He lost several million dollars in the tariff week but called it "an incredible week for content" (post:1909811364329103832). He says "No crying in the casino" (post:1899191398370508805).

Yet he also sizes defensively at times. "I'm in mostly low volatility boomer stocks. Very little tech. I feel confident holding" (post:1331276810995175424). He holds dry powder for dips (post:1326978302339256320). He warns "Any investment returns in excess of 8% are borrowed from future years. Consider that before making any lifestyle changes" (post:1361437339214487556). He sticks to Coinbase for custody because "At least they can sell shares to cover a catastrophic loss" (post:1443972042521395208). He goes to cash when macro uncertainty spikes: "Went to 80% cash" (post:1918678565840638354). He assumes all systems break when scaling (post:1637179063403008000). So the pattern: reckless on offense, hedged on defense, because he thinks consumer products fail by default.

## Favorite narratives

- **Consumer social apps** — bullish from the start, but the object changed. Early: the next big app emerges from a new behavior/platform (post:907556986153164800). Then: only teens adopt new social products; adults resist (post:1112886629910241280, post:1305211913681338369). By 2022: "durable consumer social is a black swan event; a viral app can be manufactured" (post:1708537164890275993). By 2024: "Consumer social apps grow by finding temporary vulnerabilities in existing platforms" (post:1757436313622479138). Then a sharp shift: "consumer social apps are dead post-iOS18 contact sync" (post:1836612494938509664), while "AI companion apps as the new retention engine" became bullish (post:1836612494938509664). He always saw distribution as the scarce resource (post:1919199000982343886).

- **Crypto/web3** — early bearish on ICOs and crypto wallets (post:880092809029603328, post:943417711677227009). 2020–21 mixed on bitcoin (post:1328926031038746624, post:1330266659748270087). 2021–22 strongly bearish on NFTs and web3: "Most NFTs are scams" (post:1432044017865297925), "A decentralized social network requiring users to set up a wallet reduces an already near-zero probability of success to effectively zero" (post:1477313302090817538). By 2025 he flipped selectively bullish for consumer builders: "Crypto has become more attractive for consumer builders due to regulatory environment, App Store openness, and Phantom wallets on millions of phones" (post:1904595238871572965). He also framed Bitcoin as a hedge against dollar/treasury risk during tariffs (post:1908255382679478592).

- **Tech bubble / reopening rotation** — bearish tech, bullish beaten-down travel, retail, and "Shit Stocks" in 2020–21 (post:1301886035618488320, post:1325801599621427201). This faded as macro changed; he later went long duration bonds and munis (post:1716457847481934192, post:1834611214703542632).

- **X product and growth** — bullish from 2025 onward. He joined as Head of Product (post:1939723101723574703), framed X as the most important social network (post:1922864090277392756), and later defended its growth, algorithm, and anti-spam work even after stepping down (post:2103921478958010397). He believed "The best posters were forged in fire by replyguying in obscurity" (post:1950797871785423203) and that X is the origin of internet culture (post:1977048783453405423).

- **AI** — bullish on applied AI and bot detection, but bearish on slop. "AI is deflationary" (post:1976308730972209365); "AI shifts value from deterministic outputs to probabilistic ones" (post:2101763620954894647). He saw AI companion apps as extraordinary retention (post:1836612494938509664) and warned spam would flood every channel (post:2021632774013432061). He says the threat is not rogue AI but humans using AI to hurt other humans (post:2113064833671405740).

- **Europe / EU regulation** — consistently bearish, from mocking Europe's markets (post:1287103390615400448) to "Europe is an amusement park with no opportunities" (post:1916249827517624734) and "Europe is in terminal decline" (post:2002267681337266188).
