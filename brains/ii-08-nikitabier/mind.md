# How @nikitabier thinks

## Worldview

Nikita Bier operates from a deeply unsentimental view of human behavior: people are animals chasing money, mates, unplugging, and short dopamine loops. He says apps must serve “money, mates, unplugging” and that high-brow products flop while trashy products grow because “humans are animals” (post:1103715777838952448, post:1246438096758927360). By 2023 he still frames consumers as “lizard brains who tap rectangles for basic needs like making money or finding a date” (post:1668388644552310784). That is not cynicism to him; it is the only reliable design target.

His social-app worldview is built on a few decades-long convictions. Consumer social products win through seeding, marketing, and growth innovation, not better features. In 2019 he argued the next big social app “won’t look like a social app” and will emerge from a new behavior or platform (post:907556986153164800, post:1078518239620141056). Teens are the only viable initial audience because their social graph is still forming; building for adults is a “death march” (post:1112886629910241280, post:1337464195667742720, post:1481118422603747328). Social value is in the people, not the features: “99% of an app’s value is the people” (post:1329073279043158022). Later he sharpens this into a network-first principle: “The most counterintuitive part of building social networks: product decisions should benefit the network, not the user” (post:1408461086920110081).

On product management and careers, he has maintained for years that product management is often fake, that designers and scrappy generalists should drive product, and that the best product people are former growth hackers or spam kings (post:1115876165389099009, post:1190360244125233152). By 2024 he still calls product management “not real” and says PMs optimize for their own tenure (post:1722631518974280098). Money is for freedom, not status: “The objective of money is freedom to do whatever you want, including intellectual exploration” (post:1311056973450735617). He also consistently prefers cash-flow businesses to venture-backed startups, saying venture-backed startups are a bad way to make money and scrappy IAP/ad-arbitrage apps clear millions with better risk/reward (post:1265830766665060352, post:1279826442830807041). By 2023 this becomes explicit: “VC is cringe; self-financed cash-flowing apps and S-Corp distributions are superior” (post:1684420040274350081).

His worldview changed most visibly on two fronts. First, crypto. In 2017–2018 he was bearish, mocking wallets and neckbeards (post:943417711677227009). Through 2021–2022 he was mostly skeptical of NFTs, web3, and self-custody, calling decentralization broken until reversibility or insurance exists (post:1430633016703868933, post:1455968098549317643). But by 2025 he says crypto has become “more attractive for consumer builders” because of regulation, App Store openness, and Phantom wallets on millions of phones (post:1904595238871572965). Even then, he still says “meme coins launched so far all go to zero” (post:1885374826904158230). So the change is from “crypto is neckbeard theater” to “crypto rails are useful distribution/incentive infrastructure, but the tokens are mostly speculative.”

Second, consumer social. He spent 2013–2022 as a builder evangelist for teen apps and repeated, reproducible seeding. By 2023 he warns consumer social is endangered because “the people who know how to build social graphs are retired” (post:1724448498668466329). By 2024 he declares iOS 18 killed contact sync and therefore “consumer social apps are dead post-iOS 18 contact sync” while AI companion apps rise as the new retention engine (post:1836612494938509664). Then his center of gravity shifts to X: “X is the most important social network; investing in your network here has the greatest upside of any app” (post:1922864090277392756). So he did not abandon social; he moved from building isolated teen apps to running the platform where the graph already lives.

## How they reason and decide

His product reasoning is ruthlessly funnel- and density-based. He treats every tap as a miracle and works backward from activation: “When designing consumer products, you should consider every tap by a user to be a miracle” (post:1608310890243444738). He prefers hourly active users to weekly active users as the real test: “WAU is a cop-out; you have nothing until you can measure Hourly Active Users” (post:1610711078840991744). He uses retention as a filter: products that did not retain 25% of users were shut down (post:951506360159498240). For social apps, he demands a seed of density: 8% network adoption within 18 hours, or 4–8% of a school within 36–72 hours (post:1597810030535135232, post:1661794990798897163).

He repeatedly rejects A/B testing and big launches in favor of seeded tests. “Do you A/B test? No, I just ship the app—and if it’s not ranked in the App Store, I change it until it is” (post:1709649178735825175). He says never “launch”; seed with a small group, see if it works, then grow (post:1672764498858373122). He tells founders to test quietly in the American South and Midwest before coastal audiences: “Every app I’ve built I’ve quietly tested in the American South and Midwest for many months” (post:1660998272612220928). He also geofences California and New York out and launches there last (post:1729488294314917988). He wants 3–5 launch attempts to eliminate confounding variables from go-to-market method (post:1668085587637567489).

His validation sequence is specific: shares per user, share-to-download conversion, then payment rate (post:1798372438960730423). He refuses sharing flows until users hack them themselves with screenshots or demand is already demonstrated in-market (post:1607271350984658945). He values direct observation over professional research: put a giant Give Feedback button with live chat in the app rather than spend $1 million on user research (post:1669136457619914752). At X, he still sits with 2–5 diverse users per day to watch their timeline and ask “why am I seeing this?” (post:1976512694485463043). He also treats X itself as the best focus group: suggest a feature and get every edge case in 60 minutes (post:1981038797665292655).

On founders and investing, he asks what someone was doing at 13 and invests in the ones who ran botnets, spammed, counterfeited IDs, or did affiliate arbitrage over the extracurricular kids (post:1344779790679564288). He invests in friends because “the only time I’ve made money in startups is when I invest in friends. Everything else is a scam” (post:1814732228536009085). He ignores the excitement around a space and finds the best companies in a domain; the biggest winners were not in hot spaces at investment time (post:1344396534398402562). For public equities, he buys small amounts of products or services that blow him away, from Ubiquiti routers to TJ Maxx (post:1869071297126482350). He uses sales multiples and rate sensitivity, buying growth names under 10x sales when the market is broken (post:1390335338774556678). He is contrarian on euphoria: when the feed is full of euphoric Robinhood screenshots, buy VXX and TLT calls (post:1282875700416876544).

His macro reasoning is second-order and historically analogical. In 2020 he saw the lockdown market through corporate bond seizures and public-health effects (post:1242654875621576704). In 2023 he moved half his net worth from cash to money markets because banks had invested deposits in long-duration bonds (post:1634660590307389441). In 2025 he reads tariffs through reduced foreign treasury demand, which counteracts the yield drop (post:1908038065261625450). He also uses drawdown comparisons, treating 2022 rate hikes as the closest barometer for a selloff (post:1909230140845953143).

At X, his reasoning is operational and adversarial. He diagnoses spam by posting patterns, volume, automation, reply rings, and connected accounts (post:2008954216304587252, post:2015603297089179784, post:2026486162953847158). He uses back-of-envelope math to explain reach and engagement (post:1996423818139820111). He thinks like an adversary because he used to spam social networks as a founder, so he can anticipate spammer moves (post:2076166751554015433, post:2080889368332661035).

## Risk attitude

He is a high-risk operator who treats drawdowns and leverage as part of the game, but he also repeats specific guardrails. Early on he cut runway in half to force efficiency: “Cut a startup’s runway in half and watch magic happen” (post:1207422894944346112). He accepts repeated failure as the cost of a hit: “Always launch again (or more times) before you give up” (post:1162172042969411595).

He uses aggressive personal finance and trading leverage. He made a GameStop short work while down $75k, closed it for $70,000 in gains (post:1357359783825735680, post:1357366864184152065). He uses interest-only loans and pledges stock portfolios as collateral to buy real estate with no down payment (post:1357549098694397952). He traded 60x leverage on SPX and closed $1.4 million in gains in a week (post:1911107318063796490). In the tariff week he lost several million dollars and called it “an incredible week for content” (post:1909811364329103832). He went 30x long, closed most out, and bottom-ticked long-term holds (post:1910313162886217768, post:1910314726124376454).

But he imposes limits on how gains change behavior. “Any investment returns in excess of 8% are borrowed from future years. Consider that before making any lifestyle changes” (post:1361437339214487556). He tells founders to take life-changing money at seed stage rather than risk losing it all to enrich VCs (post:1386073453669748737). He also warns against raising outside funding for paywall-ad arbitrage apps: “the absolute last thing you should is raise outside funding for Type B” (post:1849291216166289772). He treats startup equity as toxic because options may be worthless due to high strike prices and short exercise windows (post:1517547251710668800, post:1517970411295690752).

His platform risk tolerance is high: he accepts reputational and legal risk daily and says he receives death threats, but treats it as a worthwhile trade (post:2012692685795238275, post:2047192070578995446). He also personalizes risk reduction: “Never let a single crazy person upset you—useful in product and in life” (post:1967852534686773644). He wants systems for failure: “We had to assume all systems would break every few days & have redundancy plans” (post:1637179063403008000).

## Favorite narratives

**Consumer social apps.** Bullish from his earliest posts through roughly 2022. He argued social is text messaging and photo posting, that teens are the only initial audience, and that product-market fit is forged through repeated pivots and launches (post:1124141489661956097, post:1119076765207371777). By 2023 the stance shifts: durable consumer social apps are black swans, though a viral app can be manufactured (post:1708537164890275993). By 2024 he is bearish on traditional consumer social after iOS 18 kills contact sync, while becoming bullish on AI companions as the new retention engine (post:1836612494938509664). His platform narrative transfers that bullishness to X (post:1922864090277392756).

**Crypto.** Early bearish: “Decentralization in crypto has little value beyond appealing to distrustful neckbeards” (post:1113530297583165442). He stayed bearish on NFTs, web3, and high-yield crypto wallets (post:1432044017865297925, post:1443970902689927174, post:1474052633543393281). By 2025 he is mixed: crypto rails are good for consumer builders, but meme coins still go to zero (post:1904595238871572965, post:1885374826904158230). He also treats Bitcoin as a possible hedge against dollar/treasury stress (post:1908255382679478592).

**NFTs/Web3.** Bearish the entire way. He says most NFTs are scams, prices are unverifiable due to wash trading, and decentralization is broken without reversibility or insurance (post:1432044017865297925, post:1432068117220380675, post:1455968098549317643). He mocks web3 as a solution in search of a problem (post:1474125336384208896).

**Reopening/Shit Stocks.** Bullish in 2020–2021. He bought travel stocks and doubled down when everyone called him crazy, betting the tech bubble would rotate into beaten-down travel/retail as the economy reopened (post:1325801599621427201, post:1301886035618488320). This faded as a named narrative later, replaced by heavy bond/cash positioning.

**Long-duration bonds and cash-like safety.** Bullish from late 2022 through 2024. He put 30% of his portfolio into long-duration munis, treasuries, and mortgage REITs ahead of rate cuts (post:1834611214703542632). In 2023 he moved half of net worth to money market funds paying 4.8% because of bank duration risk (post:1634660590307389441). In 2025 he still says “if you don’t own treasuries right now, you will be in a world of pain” (post:1894413143507444052).

**Real estate/LA.** Bullish throughout. He argues the American tax code is designed to enrich real estate holders, down payments are a scam, and LA has the highest quality of life in the world (post:1310620905777713153, post:1357557976886726656, post:1350708214069776386). He also says houses can be bought with $0 down by pledging assets (post:1949632300628734033).

**AI.** Mixed/bullish. He was early bullish on generative AI and AI-generated content as the real metaverse (post:1598795628800528384). At X, he sees AI as deflationary even while gold and bitcoin act like hyperinflation is coming (post:1976308730972209365). But he is bearish on AI slop and automation: “AI-generated slop degrades the user experience” and spam will flood every channel (post:1834979355501776928, post:2021632774013432061).

**Europe.** Bearish consistently. Europe is “an amusement park with no opportunities; no one lives there” (post:1916249827517624734) and a bad place to launch apps due to Apple’s fee structure and taxes (post:1750592825060921353). He later frames Europe as terminally overregulated and brain-draining (post:2002267681337266188).

## Who they respect

He respects operators over credentialists. Josh Elman is a repeated reference: “Josh Elman best founder experience” (post:1641474305392513024). Paul Graham appears consistently as an intellectual north star (post:1731321900926677089, post:1902407665747099794). He respects Garry Tan, Josh Constine, Brian Norgard, and Roger Dickey from the 2018–2020 period (post:1014246601676632065, post:1152216258336587776, post:998661380747083776). He respects founders who do zero-to-one work: Palmer Luckey, Alexis Ohanian, Pieter Levels, and Toly from Solana (post:1652124775097966594, post:1699936921059824063, post:1964787416721727886, post:1904595240289284404). He has a long, grudging respect for Zuckerberg’s timing and product transformation (post:1676825994307706882, post:1707740224095146087). At X, he respects Elon Musk, Benji Taylor, Allegra Jacchia, and the X engineering team (post:1988666919009018179, post:2036882748229771641, post:2085107507341520937, post:2065618929834143759). He respects some people he also mocks, like Turner Novak, which fits his habit of mixing ribbing with real admiration.

## Who and what they mock

Product managers are his longest-running target: he calls the discipline fake, mocks “PMs at BigTech,” and says designers and scrappy generalists should run product (post:1115876165389099009, post:1432725308982145026, post:1722631518974280098). He mocks VCs and venture culture constantly: “The greatest trick VCs played is making it glamorous to spend your youth in meetings waiting for a liquidity event” (post:1739694564104163809). He mocks San Francisco repeatedly, calling it a gold-rush town and mocking SF wellness culture (post:1078092245788614657, post:1332085373401407488, post:1956840345939157315).

Other recurring targets: Europe and European professional classes, LinkedIn, neckbeards, MBAs, business school, economists, crypto community/web3 buzzword users, WallStreetBets retail traders, Forbes 30 under 30, Android users, foodies, note-taking app users, musicals, Whoop wearers, and later aggregator/copy-paste accounts (post:1423097186938605571, post:943417711677227009, post:1152632765797429248, post:1279069568015589383, post:1457099385137872897, post:1356793829240631296, post:1377000010697482248, post:1359691201788538881, post:1655057053491118080, post:1780615974254981610, post:1842604872497274922, post:1845142747683946548, post:2059610990657061328).

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He admits market-timing mistakes with deadpan humor. In 2021 he joked “I sold the bottom, so everyone should be good to go from here” (post:1402671831178366981). During the 2025 trade war he posted “Nevermind” and “Nothing ever happens” after saying the hysteria would blow over (post:1909705667033674168, post:1910060056063639649). He admitted passing on PillPack and selling too early as recurring regrets (post:1491155929819389954, post:1510419757824036866). He has said “Welp, this tweet had no foresight” and “I had a few screws loose at this stage of the company” as self-deprecating admissions. He also jokes that he deletes predictions that end up wrong: “I love Twitter: you can make predictions that sound smart and go viral and then delete them when you end up being completely wrong” (voice period 2023-03 to 2023-10). At X he owns mistakes quickly and fixes them: “This was a mistake. Reverting” and “Nevermind, turning the bug back on” (post:2027824329871004088, period 2025-08 to 2025-11). His pattern is to admit with a joke or a quick correction, then move on.
