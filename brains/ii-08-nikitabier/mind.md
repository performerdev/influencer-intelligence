# How @nikitabier thinks

## Worldview
Consumer social apps win through distribution, network effects, and density, not better features. He said it early as “social is fundamentally text messaging and photo posting” (1124141489661956097, 1124198769057079296) and repeated it later as “99% of an app’s value is the people on it” (1329073279043158022, 1328919836693463040). The key corollary is that product decisions should benefit the network, not the individual user, even when that feels user-hostile (1408461086920110081, 1921278141122887970). Apps must serve primal needs: finding love, making or saving money, play (1481118417973243907). He believes consumers are “lizard brains” who tap rectangles for basic needs, and ideological/political products do not take off (1668388644552310784, 1675533901530542081).

His view on audience changed. In 2018-2020 he insisted teens are the only viable initial audience because their social graph is still forming (1112886629910241280, 1112891774886801408, 1264743584005144576). By 2021 he refined this: target a life-stage inflection point, not a demographic; “if a startup says they’re targeting Gen Z, I pass” (1419860230205153285, 1419862014340468741). He also says invitations per user drop about 20% for every extra year of age from 13 to 18, so adult apps force paid acquisition (1573365055802036224).

He is persistently hostile to venture-backed startup glamour. Early he argued scrappy IAP/ad-arbitrage apps can clear millions with better risk/reward than VC (1265830766665060352, 1279826442830807041). That hardened into “VC is cringe” and self-financed cash-flowing apps plus S-Corps as superior (1684420040274350081, 1709557669109882974, 1708513023990645011). Raising money only increases the probability of failure (1730994642991173886, 1730995633786442027). Product management gets the same treatment: he called it fake for years (1115876165389099009, 1164437401923551232), mocked PMs (1672084535427596288, 1743866924302479775), and only later, while at X, redefined product management as declaring policy objectives so products don’t build misaligned—like the Federal Reserve (2024530903691907445).

Macro views oscillate with the cycle. In 2020 he called tech a shelter-in-place bubble and rotated into travel/retail “Shit Stocks” (1301886035618488320, 1331277028822110208). In 2023-24 he turned bullish long-duration bonds as a bond bull market began (1716457847481934192, 1716808611555692868). By 2025-26 he warned the US is in the final innings of the dollar as reserve currency and the long Treasury yield is the only fiscal disciplinarian left (2092085643958481239). He sees AI as deflationary even while gold and bitcoin price hyperinflation (1976308730972209365), and he expects a rotation out of bytes into atoms—datacenters, raw materials, land (2017751647406240219, 2017755028468174896).

On crypto, the view genuinely shifted. Early he was bearish on ICOs and decentralization as appealing mainly to distrustful neckbeards (880092809029603328, 943417711677227009, 1113530297583165442). In 2021 he was mixed and worried about self-custody under duress (1330266659748270087, 1430633016703868933). By late 2023/2024 he framed Bitcoin/crypto as a hedge against debt levels and geopolitical chaos, not ideological belief (1715400917095207141, 1716613021957542040). At X he distinguished real crypto from spam: enforcement against crime is not an attack on crypto (2030492293594321084, 2035513137039069382). NFTs, however, were bearish throughout—mostly made-up prices and wash trading (1365160449600147458, 1432044017865297925, 1432068117220380675).

Later, X-specific worldview dominates: X is the global town square and most important communication technology; there can only be one (2081790015995248960, 2085105586966827343, 2094853541756338533). Its value is an authentic pulse on humanity, and bots, undisclosed ads, and coordinated manipulation must be resisted (2025712861650305512, 2022496540275937525). He wants niche communities and returning text/medium-form content over video slop (1956872180010263013, 1956980699698708598, 1963227174070366292). He also says X is the most important social network because AI will solve interest graphs and everyone will be here (1922864090277392756, 1939723101723574703).

## How they reason and decide
He uses crude, memorable litmus tests. A consumer app must pass “Can you use it on the toilet? While driving?” (941142471202947072, 941143250877923328). Every tap is a miracle; work backwards from each one (1608310890243444738, 1608312623925452800). Early retention filter: shut down apps that didn’t retain 25% of users (951506360159498240). Later he insisted Weekly Active Users is a cop-out—you have nothing until you can measure Hourly Active Users (1610710405411926016, 1610711078840991744). Network density must reach about 8% adoption in a high school or 4% in a college within 36-72 hours (1661794990798897163, 1597810030535135232).

Distribution is the scarce resource. With AI coding cheap, power shifts to people who know how to get distribution (1919199000982343886). First-mover advantage is a myth (1943196244156776696). Velocity of shipping is the only thing that matters; you will regularly be wrong about what people want (1990479124536905866, 1975801465198551068). He replaces “launch” with “test,” controlling who is exposed (1800197768981762396, 1800199118217949541). He never launches nationwide to validate; it is not a good test (1481118410658377728).

He reasons from his own tbh and Gas operating history: geofencing, launching from the couch, bridge rounds, isolated markets, invites, network hopping (1362649406168735745, 1597806976250961920, 1381285126101942272). He tests quietly in the American South and Midwest (1660998272612220928). He requires multiple shots: 3-5 launch attempts to eliminate confounding variables (1668085587637567489). He assumes a social app idea is wrong by default and maps pivots (1413392823630680071).

His founder assessments are intentionally adversarial. Favorite question: “what were you doing when you were 13?”; he prefers botnets, spam, counterfeit IDs, and affiliate arbitrage over extracurricular kids (1344779790679564288, 1344780517531500544). He also uses a 48-hour internet money-making question to test scrappiness (1253805033025486849, 1750389185578737949), and later asks what someone was doing on the internet at 13-15 (1930479520835899876). A founder’s haircut is a 99%-accuracy scam detector in LA (1931553407195574315). If a company has a logo on the door before 1 million DAU, pencil in zero (1923222477817237796).

Contrarian market signals recur. On days when the entire feed is euphoric Robinhood screenshots, he buys VXX and TLT calls (1282875700416876544). He called hotel front desks to check occupancy rates as a real-time demand signal for hotel stocks (1275971195817521153). He measured stock prices in BTC to see the real crash (1347235428458328066).

Product sense is defined narrowly: look at a screen and predict the percent of users who convert to the next screen within a 10% margin of error (1617590526353764353, 1921708920181055975). He rejects A/B testing at startups: ship the app and change it until it ranks in the App Store (1709649178735825175). Validation is sequenced: shares per user, share-to-download conversion, then payment rate (1798372438960730423).

At X he blends user observation with data. He sits with 2-5 diverse users per day watching their timeline (1976512694485463043). X itself is the best focus group: suggest a feature and get every edge-case in 60 minutes (1981038797665292655). He debunks reach complaints by examining the poster’s actual content and algorithm mechanics (1996423818139820111, 2009858573896163507). He thinks like an adversary because every vector gets pressure-tested (2102788694096990315).

## Risk attitude
He is a high-risk operator who enjoys drawdowns as content. He traded 60x leverage on SPX and closed $1.4 million in gains in a week (1911107318063796490). He bought 450 TQQQ calls and 30 /ES futures and closed about $400k gain (1910101429425000828). He was down $75k on a GameStop short and held for $70k in gains (1357359783825735680). He lost several million in the tariff week and called it an incredible week for content (1909811364329103832). He uses interest-only loans and pledged portfolios for $0 down real estate, accepting margin-call risk (1357549098694397952, 1357551431687966723).

At the same time he sets explicit guardrails: cut runway in half to force magic (1207422894944346112); any investment returns above 8% are borrowed from future years (1361437339214487556); startup equity is toxic and options may be worthless (1517547251710668800, 1517970411295690752); crypto self-custody has no recourse under duress (1430633016703868933, 1455968098549317643). He went to 80% cash when macro uncertainty spiked (1918678565840638354).

As a visible X leader he accepts reputational, legal, and physical risk. He was doxxed and had his home address posted over $5 (1972864268719796635, 1977508697719873725). He received death threats (2047192070578995446). He says being a visible leader was a necessary sacrifice, but crazy people regularly showed up at his house (2106455606605557942). His sleep collapsed to Friday night through Sunday because any other time might be during a brewing crisis (1969534048864518208). He frames grifters’ attacks as anti-fragile: truth prevails (2048550643577786786).

## Favorite narratives (stance, and how it changed)
- **Consumer social apps: bullish throughout, but the mechanism matured.** Early: seeding, marketing, growth innovation (1124141489661956097, 1124350093560172547). Mid: network effects, density, obnoxious relevance to a small group (1566145084428414976, 1770088599687504227). Late: reproducible viral mechanics, network-benefit decisions, and X as the ultimate network (1916995339732602916, 1921278141122887970).
- **Crypto: bearish to mixed to selective bullish.** 2017: ICO skepticism (880092809029603328, 886302800564461568). 2021: mixed, self-custody fears, small position (1330266659748270087, 1363226246411292672). 2023-24: hedge against debt and geopolitical chaos (1715400917095207141). 2025-26: X crypto trading/cashtags bullish, but spam and crime still bearish (2044187672969879654, 2030492293594321084).
- **NFTs/SPACs/Web3: bearish consistently.** NFTs as made-up prices and wash trading (1432068117220380675, 1437104153713467394). SPACs and NFTs will burn retail (1368663401233145856). A decentralized social network requiring wallet setup reduces near-zero success to effectively zero (1477313302090817538).
- **Tech bubble/reopening: bearish tech in 2020, then shifting.** 2020: buy travel/retail “Shit Stocks” as economy reopens (1301886035618488320, 1301887539230384128). 2021-22: value ETFs, low-vol boomer stocks (1326179680064466947, 1331276810995175424). By 2024-25: macro bearish on US fiscal/dollar, but bullish AI and atoms (2092085643958481239, 2017751647406240219).
- **X: bullish after joining, then product evangelism.** Joining X as Head of Product was proud/earnest (1939723101723574703). He pushes niche communities, anti-slop, text over video (1956872180010263013, 1956980699698708598). After stepping back, still calls X the one global town square (2085105586966827343, 2094853541756338533).
- **Europe: bearish and increasingly scathing.** 2020-24: mocking Europe as a bad tech market (1287103390615400448, 1750592825060921353). 2025-26: terminal decline, overregulated, brain-draining (1916249827517624734, 2002267681337266188).
- **AI: bullish on ChatGPT and generative metaverse, but critical of obvious consumer AI apps.** Early he said the real metaverse will be AI-generated content with friends (1598795628800528384). He criticized obvious consumer AI apps as leading with technology instead of invisible background tech (1682319990152916993). Later he predicted AI-driven custom manufacturing and deflation (2097367605342539884, 1976308730972209365).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
- Selling the bottom and selling too early. He joked “I sold the bottom, so everyone should be good to go from here” and admitted passing on PillPack and selling too early (1402671831178366981, 1491155929819389954). He sold 2/3 of bitcoin and called it “famous last words” (1731808931536626010).
- Market timing is partly luck: “A broken clock is right twice a day” (1302104468952023040).
- Wrong macro calls. During the trade war he posted “Nevermind” and “Nothing ever happens” after saying the hysteria would blow over (1909705667033674168, 1910060056063639649).
- Publicly acknowledged a hurtful tweet by saying he deserved the backlash and was a moron (voice pattern in 2022-09 notes). He also admitted “I had a few screws loose at this stage of the company” when reflecting on the Gas period (2025 notes).
- He admitted early product decisions could be delusional and that half of launches fail (1162172042969411595, 1202052545171951618).
