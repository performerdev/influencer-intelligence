# How @nikitabier thinks

## Worldview

The through-line from the tbh days to X is that consumer products win on distribution, not better features. Early on he wrote that social is fundamentally text messaging and photo posting, and that “the next big social app won’t look like a social app” (post:907556986153164800, post:1124141489661956097). By 2025 he was still saying the same thing in a harder form: for consumer products “it has always been about distribution, not building software” (post:2092640752958615961, post:2090463089225306481).

He sees humans as animals motivated by money, mates, and unplugging; apps must serve those primal needs (post:1103715777838952448, post:1481118417973243907). That produces a deliberately unfashionable product taste: “High-brow products flop; trashy products grow because humans are animals” (post:1246438096758927360). He targets an embarrassingly small audience, not a broad market, because the first users need to feel the product was made for them (post:1255291891647688705, post:1889370882696683755). He also argues the product decision should benefit the network, not the individual user, even when that feels user-hostile (post:1408461086920110081, post:1921278141122887970).

His macro worldview flipped hard from the 2020-2021 era. He called tech a bubble inflated by shelter-in-place and rotated into beaten-down travel, retail, and industrial “Shit Stocks” (post:1301886035618488320, post:1325801599621427201). He viewed wealth inequality as a product of cheap credit for the rich and private investment vehicles unavailable to everyone else (post:1269729419477651459). Later, as X’s product leader, the macro view receded and the platform view took over: X is the global town square, and spam, bots, and undisclosed paid shilling are the greatest existential threats to that commons (post:2025712861650305512, post:2080747924380856519). A newer, consistent view: every social surface decays into slop by default unless you actively fight it (post:2110049188142813692).

## How they reason and decide

He uses sharp, reductive filters. For social apps, he asked: “Can you use it on the toilet? While driving?” (post:941142471202947072, post:941143250877923328). For retention, he regularly shut down apps that did not retain 25% of users (post:951506360159498240). Later he called WAU a cop-out and demanded hourly active users: if you don’t see recurring hourly sessions in some cohort, throw everything out (post:1610710405411926016, post:1610711078840991744).

He reasons from density and cold-start mechanics, not aggregate vanity metrics. A social product needs concentrated adoption like 8% of a high school or 4% of a college within 18-36 hours, and you should run 3-5 launch attempts to eliminate confounds (post:1597810030535135232, post:1661794990798897163, post:1668085587637567489). He also thinks the first 72 hours are decisive: if Day 3 usage is smaller than Day 2, it is probably only going down (post:1899595483699621992, post:1682321838867910657).

He is anti-data when data is half-baked, but uses data when it is precise. He says true product-market fit is self-evident and data often deceives (post:1716470985103184196). He does not A/B test; he ships and changes the app until it ranks in the App Store (post:1709649178735825175). But he audits Mixpanel sessions one by one with small sample sizes (post:1894468176584610053), and he looks at individual user stories in the first hour rather than aggregate charts (post:1519109397120647172). He tests quietly in the American South and Midwest before coastal audiences (post:1660998272612220928), and geofences California and New York out (post:1729488294314917988).

He thinks in mechanisms, not ideology. For investing, he ignored excitement around a space and looked for the best companies in a domain (post:1344396534398402562). He used euphoric Robinhood screenshots as a contrarian signal to buy VXX and TLT calls (post:1282875700416876544). He called hotel front desks to check real-time occupancy as a demand signal for hotel stocks (post:1275971195817521153, post:1275975083094208512). He evaluates founders by what they were doing at 13: the botnets, spam, counterfeit IDs, or affiliate hustles beat the extracurricular kids (post:1344779790679564288, post:1344780517531500544).

As X product lead, he still reasons like an adversary. He says he used to spam social networks as a founder, so he can anticipate spammer moves (post:2076166751554015433, post:2080889368332661035). He explains decisions with back-of-envelope math, explicit assumptions, mechanism, and trade-offs (post:1996423818139820111, post:2006851954811757021). He also uses product decisions as policy: product management is like the Federal Reserve, declaring objectives so others don’t build misaligned with the platform (post:2024530903691907445).

## Risk attitude

He is comfortable with aggressive leverage and concentrated macro bets. He used interest-only loans and pledged stock portfolios as collateral for real estate, with a 10-year term as a buffer against a down market in year seven (post:1357549098694397952, post:1357551431687966723). He borrowed against stock at 0.9% and said that beats crypto exchange rates of 10% (post:1449396267394625537, post:1449439667930103814). In 2025 he traded 60x leverage on SPX and closed $1.4 million in gains in a week, and on another day bought TQQQ calls and /ES futures and closed both for around $400k (post:1911107318063796490, post:1910101429425000828).

But he is not a pure degen. He talks about drawdowns casually and with humor: “A broken clock is right twice a day” (post:1302104468952023040). He warns that returns above 8% are borrowed from future years and should not cause lifestyle changes (post:1361437339214487556). He went to 80% cash when macro uncertainty spiked (post:1918678565840638354), and he kept a mostly low-volatility boomer-stock book earlier when he wanted to hold through noise (post:1331276810995175424). He also emphasizes existential risks to a startup more than upside; PR takedown, data corruption, or losing a key teammate are the risks he worries about (post:1386074108899692549).

He accepts that most consumer products fail, so the default is failure and readiness to try again (post:1800199118217949541, post:1162172042969411595). At the same time, he says a failed launch is more expensive than paying $20 an install for the first 50 users, because density matters more than CAC (post:1844050353387348081).

## Favorite narratives (stance, and how it changed)

### Consumer social apps
Bullish long-term, but the framework evolved. Early: the next big social app will emerge from a new behavior or platform, and teens are the only viable initial cohort (post:907556986153164800, post:1112886629910241280). Mid-period: social apps are mostly about the people on them; 99% of an app’s value is the people (post:1329073279043158022). Later, more hard-nosed: virality is a science, retention is a black swan, and a viral app can be manufactured (post:1824491565622104552, post:1708537164890275993). He also warned consumer social was endangered because the people who know how to build social graphs are retired (post:1724448498668466329). By the X era, he still believed in social, but the mission was protecting the network itself rather than launching another app.

### Crypto
Changed several times. Early he was bearish: crypto wallets lacked fraud protection, and decentralization appealed mostly to distrustful neckbeards (post:943417711677227009, post:1113530297583165442). By 2021 he was long some BTC with a stated cost basis, and bullish Ethereum while bearish NFTs and SPACs (post:1363226246411292672, post:1391603467538419712, post:1365160449600147458). In 2021-2022 he turned sharply bearish on Web3 and decentralization: self-custody is unsafe under duress, NFT prices are wash-traded, and decentralized social requires wallets so its success probability is effectively zero (post:1430633016703868933, post:1432068117220380675, post:1477313302090817538). By 2025 he became more bullish on crypto for consumer builders because of the regulatory environment, App Store openness, and Phantom wallets on millions of phones (post:1904595238871572965). At X he distinguishes decentralized blockchains from spam and scraping: enforcement against crime is not an attack on crypto (post:2030492293594321084).

### AI
Early he was bullish on AI-generated content as the real metaverse, because everyone’s life is boring and we want daydreams (post:1598795628800528384). He later warned AI-generated slop degrades the internet and creates an opening for authenticity (post:1834979355501776928). As X product lead, he treated AI bots as a coming tsunami and said human verification will be the most urgent business demand (post:2102432368158245252, post:2107157904168239416). He also argued the real AI threat is humans using AI to hurt other humans, not rogue agents (post:2113064833671405740).

### Product management and VCs
Consistently hostile. Product management is often a fake discipline; designers and scrappy generalists should drive product (post:1115876165389099009, post:1164437401923551232). VCs are mocked and treated as a bad path for founders: raising money only increases the probability of failure (post:1730994642991173886). He still angel-invests, but he said the only time he made money in startups was investing in friends; everything else is a scam (post:1814732228536009085).

### Europe and San Francisco
Consistently bearish and mocking. Europe is an amusement park with no opportunities; no one lives there (post:1916249827517624734). San Francisco culture is repeatedly mocked, especially wellness and status-climbing (post:1332085373401407488, post:1078092245788614657).

People who built, shipped, or understood distribution. Early: Brian Norgard, Josh Constine, Roger Dickey, Garry Tan, Elon Musk, Jack Ma (post:1014246601676632065, post:1152216258336587776, post:1087439318275117056, post:998661380747083776, post:1220740223056797696). Later: Josh Elman, Paul Graham, Keith Wasserman, Moses Kagan, Turner Novak, Suhail, Palmer Luckey, Alexis Ohanian, Toly, Rahul, and Zuck for timing/product instincts (post:1306982057764032514, post:1309749943440044032, post:1326978302339256320, post:1329445692104978433, post:1329810197875834881, post:1336155491223781378, post:1652124775097966594, post:1699936921059824063, post:1735795035960901904, post:1816151097922445709, post:1676825994307706882). At X he respected the algorithm engineers and people like Benji Taylor, Allegra Jacchia, Josh Elman, and TJ Parker (post:2090411385171685623, post:2082516416100790397, post:2085107507341520937, post:2107477894008369178).

## Who and what they mock

Product managers, VCs, LinkedIn influencers, San Francisco, Europeans, Android users, crypto neckbeards, Forbes 30 under 30, aggregators and repost farmers, and people who complain about reach. Product managers are a recurring target (post:1115876165389099009, post:1333643966391091202, post:1672084535427596288). LinkedIn is “cancer” (post:1423097186938605571). Aggregators are “a cancer to social networks” and should be demonetized (post:2062144369134834018, post:2097047490998808874). He mocks Threads/Bluesky as also-rans and Spain? not. He also mocks biohacking, wellness podcasters, and people with emoji-heavy bios (post:1644066273557630976, post:1748867191762821337, post:1377385379066572800).

- Bad timing/luck claims: “A broken clock is right twice a day” (post:1302104468952023040).
- Selling the bottom, joked: “I sold the bottom, so everyone should be good to go from here” (post:1402671831178366981).
- Passing on PillPack and selling too early (post:1491155929819389954, post:1510419757824036866).
- Trade-war call: he said the hysteria would blow over, then posted “Nevermind” and “Nothing ever happens” when wrong (post:1909705667033674168, post:1910060056063639649).
- Exit underprice: tax accountant laughed at exit price (post:1860012539092041812).
- Startup legacy mistakes: he warns startup equity is toxic, and he was extorted by Boston investors during the Facebook deal; SF VCs signed blindly (post:1517547251710668800, post:1839510474699788507, post:1832875079027634637).
