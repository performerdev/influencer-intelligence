# How @MEADGod thinks

## Worldview

Early on, @MEADGod treats crypto as mostly fake, lazy, and incentive-corrupted. He sees DeFi as fake TVL and incentive-chasing where only a few venues are legit (1969059963013071208, 1963860768929063302). He is early bearish on stablecoin stacks: stablecoins backed by other stablecoins backed by off-chain entities will cascade into the next bear market (1965327296056996015, 1965490929625956439, 1965491845087330477). He repeatedly rejects vanity metrics: wallet counts are not users; real user numbers are far smaller (1965834271916175582). He believes founders must stick to what they built instead of pivoting, and grant-hoppers and flip-floppers are losers (1967149024278143387, 1973781980002337096). His building philosophy is to be your own category rather than chase trends, because chasing trends always loses and you should make others chase you (1982395623220662753, 1982395907904880904).

That worldview does not soften over time; it sharpens. By mid-2026 he says crypto tokens are essentially suicide for projects and altcoins are uninvestable because few succeed (2031850768136093962). He later declares, “there is no such thing as DeFi, only MultisigFi,” blaming circular leverage and yield-chasing (2035807710428451140, 2046584860119072962, 2048116295149748706). He still believes crypto is bloated with grifters, little innovation, and no progress, and that most will return to 9-5s (1975443367812288834, 2018249252598682003, 2018499924464222711). Yet he does not abandon crypto; he repositions into building a product he views as real: Pons, a launchpad on Robinhood Chain. He frames it as the people’s launchpad, community-first, and against bundled/coordinated dogshit (2081860851250671905, 2097084277036839287, 2097091081087172767). This is not a flip on DeFi; it is a continuation of his view that only shipping real revenue-generating products beats chasing shitcoins (2068271828791894245, 2068271923302113780, 2062810617719701592).

He has a strong anti-Europe/Germany stance. Germany is mismanaged, over-taxed, over-regulated, and leaving is the move (1982191593873412546, 2023727594768658483, 2023870438129234236). He later adds: if you are smart and in the EU, leave (2047062662698201200, 2040112263760404592, 2039638109059739745).

On technology and work, his view moves from “AI increases workload and requires heavy human cleanup” to a harder economic take. He thinks AI companies are digging their own grave because cheaper models commoditize the space and the AI bubble will burst (2064989599487651963, 2065041800943792255, 2055404792563057128, 2048904041019650180). He also says AI taking jobs is good, not something to fear (2033112821752623130). By late 2026, he argues that in the AI era the best thing you can do in crypto is build a solid reputation, because nobody cares about your product without people backing who you are (2106846254122647750). A successful reputation layer will never be code-based; it will be P2P in the real world, with a human oracle crucial as AI and robots rise (2106905263080923407, 2106905400650162356).

He consistently pairs market cynicism with metaphysical humility. He says God giveth and God taketh away; never get comfortable because forces bigger than you exist (2033725422551371892, 2062810617719701592). Later, he says he is all in on God, family, health, and work, not numbers on a screen (2081068081585992049, 2081523127163744328). Everything happens for a reason; trust God and struggles become lessons (2089010715101221195, 2081067828833046807).

By late 2026 he has gone more bullish without abandoning the cycle frame. He thinks the market bottom is in and a nuke to new lows would need a black swan, but still calls it technically a bear because the 4-year cycle disagrees; he sees an “echo bubble” in alts (2104975101829476377, 2104975964144828704). He is bullish on privacy because full transparency everywhere will make people appreciate privacy in finance (2106214629454094469, 2106216341745148127). He is bullish on Robinhood Chain as the start of a new onchain frontier and tradfi coming onchain (2112975322475835650, 2108640342497280273, 2108623012899770476). He is very bullish on pairing memes against ETFs, saying ETFs have a great future onchain (2108308819520503839). He thinks owning one whole Bitcoin will be a sign of wealth later (2108404333561930170). And he believes VCs and onchain trenchers should buy in the same market with no special treatment (2112936531967267105).

## How they reason and decide

He reasons from incentives first. Early, he reads DeFi incentives and validator emissions to judge whether a product has a sustainable future, not just a chart (1965482203317764572, 1965515870836589000, 1965519189659316732). He judges projects by whether they have their own infrastructure or edge rather than being a wrapper, and he later applies that to neobanks that just wrap Aave/Morpho: they are cancerous and add no innovation (1984053107601633353, 1984919233466102060). His founder filter is whether people ship and stick around, not talk (1967149024278143387, 2022331892822053297).

As a trader, he uses pattern recognition and contrarian read of other traders’ hit rates (1978326822719725791, 1982036053297934672). He thinks in cycles: bear market timing, bottom targets, and when to bid (1996980501064646853, 2001662494092280314, 2019526140688306463). In the 2026 batch, he combines gut feel with explicit price paths: “I don’t like to fade my gut,” specific levels like 57K by Friday, 50K by end of July, and bounce conditions (2052596146749542451, 2069836559948386506, 2069334649134862522, 2071600546356564409). He is contrarian: when everyone grasps for bottom signals, he expects more pain; when grifters leave, he expects a relief rally (2062949976821403963, 2032512392144498905).

He also reasons like an operator. He weighs counterparty risk and whether a chain’s native apps can survive without mercenary incentives (1965527217326244147, 1986035133908979972). When accused of wrongdoing on Pons, he explains mechanics with numbered points and receipts—taxes, routing, mcap (2097602877925609720, 2080795196573229358). He uses community dashboards to back revenue and volume claims (2096062642708271463, 2095038780340093102). He distinguishes burn from sell when accused of dumping: “I never sold, I burned - big difference” (2085152772517695589, 2102057097949049194). He reasons from alignment: he only supports assets issued by Robinhood/Bitstamp, keeping it simple “like lego blocks” (2098571001084748093, 2084761983652417903).

His latest reasoning is heavily anchored by the 4-year cycle as the primary technical frame, even against his own bottom thesis (2104975101829476377, 2104975964144828704). He also reasons by analogy to past price events: today felt like the 2023 spike down after the FTX bottom (2108404919375946163). When explaining token mechanics, he gives concrete numbers—splitter balances, ETH/hour burn rates, 5-day claim cycles, TWAP (2106778812197314652, 2106783384722715056, 2106224161739657382).

He is not rigid. He admits error and corrects himself publicly when shown he misread a critic’s argument (2106220420659614105, 2106224161739657382, 2106224471824539942). He also uses community polls and questions to decide product direction (2084753264591937918, 2085474118065938629).

## Risk attitude

His personal risk tolerance is high, but his stated risk rules are conservative. He admits gambling on high leverage LTF and getting liquidated on multiple accounts (1979129462013718880). He says the honest lesson is sometimes “I should listen to myself instead of gambling LTF on high leverage.” He warns that hedging free airdrops with illiquid premarket perps blows people up (1985828294206296391). He expects a big player to get nuked off the market before the final bottom (2019526140688306463). He warns stablecoins will depeg more in summer 2026 (2051386212628246795).

At the same time, he tells people to pay off their mortgage before gambling in crypto, because psychology will mess with them (1970070095662850173). He warns that no airdrop is worth farming if you don’t believe in the product (2067919596506468497). He says “greed will kill you” (2082153065465934067). He warns followers not to tail his wallet; any action on the wallet is testing only, all NFA DYOR (2102057097949049194, 2102056017492095227). He also stays legally careful: he won’t discuss his own token’s price because of compliance and treasury holdings (2108578832139854011).

He has also been explicit that the health cost of building and trading is a risk. By 2026, he reports 3-5 hours sleep, panic attacks, dangerously high blood pressure, and vitamin D deficiency (2100465301045313925, 2103299031019762126, 2103263318635168187). He frames that as the cost of success, but it is clearly a live risk he tracks.

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He is willing to admit being wrong, especially about his own trading and health.

- He admits gambling on high-leverage LTF and getting liquidated on multiple accounts, and says sometimes he should listen to himself instead of gambling LTF on high leverage (1979129462013718880).
- He warns people not to hedge free airdrops with illiquid premarket perps after watching those blow up (1985828294206296391).
- He publicly admits the cost of building on his health: 3-5 hours sleep, panic attacks, dangerously high blood pressure, vitamin D deficiency (2100465301045313925, 2103299031019762126, 2103263318635168187).
- He says he has learned lessons about giving ETAs and talking about the future too much (2084755397026684990, 2084758195881284025).
- He admits when he misread a critic and apologizes, saying he does not mind being wrong if he is wrong (2106220420659614105, 2106224161739657382, 2106224471824539942).
- He frames “not believing in something can be expensive” as a costly lesson (2083042549376204845).
- He says he got numb to gains and losses after cycles: $10,000 no longer feels like $10,000 (2062810617719701592).

His general pattern: he may double down in the moment, but when shown a concrete error, he apologizes plainly and corrects the record.

## Conclusion

@MEADGod’s brain runs on two tracks. The trader track is bearish at euphoric tops, contrarian at bottoms, and always anchored to the 4-year cycle. The builder track is skeptical of fake DeFi and KOL narratives, but willing to go all in on a product he controls—first Roots, then Pons—because he respects shipping over talk. He is crude, funny, religious in his own way, and deeply aware that both leverage and building can wreck a person.
