# How @MEADGod thinks

## Worldview

The early record, from late 2025 into early 2026, is cynical about most of crypto. He says DeFi is mostly fake TVL and incentive-chasing; only a few venues are legit (1963860768929063302, 1969059963013071208). Wallet counts are not users, and real user numbers are far smaller than the dashboards claim (1965834271916175582). The stablecoin setup looks like a house of cards: stablecoins backed by other stablecoins backed by off-chain entities will cascade into the next bear market (1965327296056996015, 1965490929625956439, 1965491845087330477). At that point he was bullish on on-chain insurance and reinsurance as the next big narrative, covering liquidations, hacks and theft (1965491845087330477, 1965492880866508990).

He hates mercenary capital and trend-chasing. Founders should stick to what they built instead of pivoting; grant-hoppers and flip-floppers are losers (1967149024278143387, 1973781980002337096). Chasing trends always loses; the move is to be your own category and make others chase you (1982395623220662753, 1982395907904880904). Neobanks that just wrap Aave or Morpho for +5% are cancerous and add no innovation (1984053107601633353, 1984919233466102060). InfoFi and airdrop farming are, in his words, the worst things to happen to the space (1998398078802399535, 1994405492517486596). Token buybacks are a psyop because teams have no feel for the cycle (2007401338372526381).

Privacy has been one of his most consistent convictions. He wrote early that privacy is a human right and on-chain payments should be private by default (1980749499074003227, 1981316578135208422). It stayed: even in late 2026 he argued that full transparency everywhere will make people appreciate privacy in finance (2106214629454094469, 2106216341745148127).

Europe and Germany are a recurring negative. He sees Germany as badly mismanaged, over-taxed and over-regulated, and leaving is the move (1982191593873412546, 2023727594768658483, 2023870438129234236). By mid-2026 he was relieved to leave Germany (2040112263760404592), and later turned it into a blunt rule: if you are smart and in the EU, leave (2047062662698201200, 2039638109059739745).

His AI view changed in an important way. Early on he said AI is not replacing workers; it increases workload and requires heavy human cleanup and monitoring (2021206344380920248). He also said LLM subscription prices are subsidized by VC money and will rise, so hardware owners win the next five years (2025851761731281035, 2025852190460395874). By mid-2026 he was fully bearish on the AI bubble: AI companies are digging their own grave, cheaper models commoditize the space, and the bubble will burst (2064989599487651963, 2065041800943792255, 2055404792563057128, 2048904041019650180). But he flipped the labor question: AI taking jobs is good, not something to fear (2033112821752623130). So the AI bubble stayed bearish, while the earlier “AI means more human grind” worry was dropped.

His DeFi view hardened over time. Early he called most of it fake TVL. By March–July 2026 he said crypto tokens are essentially suicide for projects, and altcoins are uninvestable because few succeed (2031850768136093962). DeFi is broken by circular leverage and yield-chasing: “there is no such thing as DeFi, only MultisigFi” (2035807710428451140, 2046584860119072962, 2048116295149748706). But by mid-2026 he also started saying building real revenue-generating products beats chasing shitcoins, and a token utility shift is coming (2068271828791894245, 2068271923302113780, 2062810617719701592).

Solana became a hard no. In mid-2026 he said flatly: “I hate the solana team, the ecosystem and everything it stands for” (2072345596342460787, 2074860361937735842). He kept mocking Solana manlets later (2102569196018143718).

Robinhood Chain is his late-cycle conviction. He called it purpose-built for speculating on all assets, especially RWAs and memes (2078221139285795194). By late 2026 he was calling it the home of memestocks and the main battlefield of the cycle, saying people are still sleeping on the green chain (2086556282131996676, 2092329203278413832, 2098913311865606195). He is also convinced the upcoming cycle will be all about EVM (2081959280391958920).

Late 2026 brings a more constructive macro view. He says the market bottom is in and a nuke to new lows would require a black swan, but he still calls it technically a bear because the 4-year cycle disagrees (2104975101829476377, 2104975964144828704). He thinks we are in an echo bubble, especially with some alts moving crazy (2104975101829476377). Tradfi is coming onchain, and Robinhood Chain is the start of a new frontier (2112975322475835650, 2108640342497280273, 2108623012899770476). He is bullish on pairing memes against ETFs and on ETFs having a great future onchain (2108308819520503839). He is very bullish on indie devs, and thinks gaming could have an insane run in crypto (2108421151949300154). Owning one Bitcoin will be a sign of wealth in the future (2108404333561930170). VCs and onchain trenchers should buy in the same market with no special treatment (2112936531967267105).

A newer worldview shift is reputation. In the AI era, reputation is the most important thing to build in crypto because nobody cares about your product without people backing who you are (2106846254122647750). A successful reputation layer will not be code-based but P2P in the real world; a human oracle will be crucial as AI and bots rise (2106905263080923407, 2106905400650162356).

## How they reason and decide

He reads incentives and emissions. He judges whether a product has a sustainable future by looking at validator emissions and DeFi incentives, as with Berachain BEX (1965482203317764572, 1965515870836589000, 1965519189659316732). He also weighs counterparty risk and whether a chain’s native apps can survive without mercenary incentives (1965527217326244147, 1986035133908979972).

He reasons by pattern recognition and contrarian observation of other traders’ hit rates (1978326822719725791, 1982036053297934672). He judges projects by whether they have their own infrastructure or edge rather than being wrappers (1984053107601633353, 1982395907904880904). He thinks in cycles: bear market timing, bottom targets, and when to bid (1996980501064646853, 2001662494092280314, 2019526140688306463). He evaluates people by whether they ship and stick around, not by talk (1967149024278143387, 2022331892822053297).

He also uses gut feel. He said he does not like to fade his gut, and that past cycle experience drives top and bottom calls (2052596146749542451, 2062810617719701592). He reads market structure through specific price levels and timelines, like 57K by Friday or 50K by end of July (2069836559948386506, 2069334649134862522, 2071600546356564409). He is contrarian: when everyone grasps for bottom signals, he expects more pain; when grifters leave, he expects a relief rally (2062949976821403963, 2032512392144498905). He evaluates projects by whether they ship and treat builders well, not hype (2064800437551718639, 2076838646934577418).

He applies body data to life and markets. He uses personal health metrics to diagnose stress, then treats markets with the same data-driven lens (2059864376979362121, 2069274056092729826).

Once he is building, his reasoning becomes more mechanical and evidence-based. He explains mechanics in detail with numbered points when accused of wrongdoing, whether taxes, routing or market cap (2097602877925609720, 2080795196573229358). He uses community dashboards and data to back revenue and volume claims (2096062642708271463, 2095038780340093102). He distinguishes burn from sell when accused of dumping (2085152772517695589, 2102057097949049194). He reasons from alignment: he only supports assets issued by Robinhood or Bitstamp, keeping it simple like lego blocks (2098571001084748093, 2084761983652417903). He reads market structure: market makers mint stock tokens to re-peg, so squeezes on thin liquidity get arbitraged away (2094220402683793550, 2094221025433022493). He uses polls and community questions to decide product direction (2084753264591937918, 2085474118065938629).

He is also willing to update. He admitted lessons about giving ETAs and talking too much about the future (2084755397026684990, 2084758195881284025). He anchors regime calls on the 4-year cycle as the best indicator since inception, even when it clashes with his own bottom thesis (2104975101829476377, 2104975964144828704). He compares current price action to historical moments, like the 2023 spike down after the FTX bottom, to frame entries (2108404919375946163). He reads news headlines as confirmation of the tradfi-onchain thesis: Bloomberg on Robinhood Chain, the Lighter license, and the WSJ ad against tokenized equities (2106021155387658608, 2112975322475835650, 2114452408286011526). He engages critics with detailed mechanism explanations, such as the buyback splitter, claim cycles and TWAP (2106778812197314652, 2106783384722715056, 2106224161739657382). When he misreads someone, he apologizes and corrects the record (2106220420659614105, 2106224471824539942).

## Risk attitude

He is a high-risk trader who knows it. Early on he admitted to gambling on high leverage LTF and getting liquidated on multiple accounts (1979129462013718880). But he is also explicit about psychology: pay off your mortgage before gambling in crypto because psychology will mess with you (1970070095662850173). He warned that hedging free airdrops with illiquid premarket perps blows people up (1985828294206296391). He expected a big player to get nuked off the market before the final bottom (2019526140688306463).

By mid-2026 he described being numb to gains and losses after cycles: $10,000 no longer feels like $10,000 (2062810617719701592). He warned of more pain: “You think 59K is bad? Wait till you see 49K” (2062922857089298895, 2062949976821403963). At his most bearish he told people to exit the market: “EXIT THE FUCKING MARKET MATEY” (2054592566805360829). He also warned no airdrop is worth farming if you don’t believe in the product (2067919596506468497). He said stablecoins will depeg more in summer (2051386212628246795).

He puts limits on his own trading when the tape is bad. He said not to trade on ugly days; wait for the bounce (2062418191242932399). He was only comfortable being in a juicy long on Hyperliquid and short everything else (2069999384960360658).

By late 2026 he had a builder’s risk frame. He reframed downside as needing a black swan but conceded the cycle indicator still says bear (2104975101829476377, 2104975964144828704). He refused to discuss PONS price or price action because of legal compliance and treasury holdings (2108578832139854011). He warned followers about impersonators scamming in his name (2108600230942056687). He also acknowledged the health toll: 3–5 hours sleep, panic attacks, dangerously high blood pressure, and vitamin D deficiency (2100465301045313925, 2103299031019762126, 2103263318635168187). His risk aphorisms became simpler: greed will kill you (2082153065465934067), and not believing in something can be expensive (2083042549376204845).
