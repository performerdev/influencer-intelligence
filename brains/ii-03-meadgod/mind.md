# How @MEADGod thinks

## Worldview
His baseline is that most of crypto is fake, incentive-chasing theatre. He writes off DeFi as mostly fake TVL and calls the later yield-chasing version "no such thing as DeFi, only MultisigFi" (1963860768929063302, 2035807710428451140). Stablecoins backed by other stablecoins and off-chain entities are, to him, a depeg cascade waiting to happen (1965327296056996015, 1965490929625956439). He treats CT engagement as largely bots and yappers, says organic reach is dead, and keeps mocking Kaito-style farming (1971306267684766061, 2004574119220920694). InfoFi and airdrop farming are, in his words, the worst thing to happen to the space (1998398078802399535, 1994405492517486596). Prediction markets get the same contempt (1994159788133945605, 1995492965150863401).

At the same time he respects a narrow path: build something that ships, has its own infrastructure or edge, and makes others chase you. Founders should stick to what they built instead of pivoting; grant-hoppers and flip-floppers are losers (1967149024278143387, 1973781980002337096). "Chasing trends always loses; be your own category and make others chase you" is his early building philosophy (1982395623220662753, 1982395907904880904). By 2026 that becomes "building real revenue-generating products beats chasing shitcoins" (2068271828791894245, 2068271923302113780). His Pons launchpad is framed as "the people's launchpad," community-first against bundled and coordinated dogshit (2081860851250671905, 2097084277036839287, 2097091922204430532).

Privacy is a constant: on-chain payments should be private by default, and he called privacy a human right early (1980749499074003227, 1981316578135208422). Even after Roots shut down, he stayed defiant and later doubled down with "The future is private" (1986804463126839311, 2106216341745148127). In the latest batch he says the AI era makes reputation the real moat: "nobody cares about your product without people backing who you are" (2106846254122647750), and a reputation layer will never be code-based; it will be P2P with a human oracle (2106905263080923407, 2106905400650162356).

His AI view shifts over time. Early on he argued AI is not replacing workers and instead increases workload while requiring heavy human cleanup and monitoring (2021206344380920248). Later he says AI taking jobs is good, not something to fear (2033112821752623130), while still calling the AI bubble a self-inflicted grave where cheaper models commoditize the space (2064989599487651963, 2065041800943792255, 2055404792563057128).

On geography, he is consistently negative on Europe/Germany: badly mismanaged, over-taxed, over-regulated, and leaving is the move (1982191593873412546, 2047062662698201200, 2040112263760404592). Robinhood Chain becomes his main battlefield by late 2026; he calls tradfi coming onchain obvious and Robinhood Chain "the start of a new frontier" (2112975322475835650, 2108640342497280273). This is a notable shift from his earlier CEX UI/UX complaints toward building on a chain he now calls the home of memestocks (1976054324968235405, 2086556282131996676, 2092329203278413832).

Everything runs through cycles and God. The 4-year cycle is his best indicator since inception (2104975964144828704); by late 2026 he says the bottom is in while still calling the market technically a bear because the 4-year cycle disagrees (2104975101829476377). Underneath that is a religious humility: "God giveth and God taketh away; never get comfortable because forces bigger than you exist" (2033725422551371892, 2062810617719701592). He says he is all in on God, family, health and work, and numbers on a screen don't change happiness (2081068081585992049, 2081523127163744328). He also says owning one Bitcoin will be a sign of wealth in the future (2108403501105590570, 2108404333561930170), and laments that people don't value what's happening around them anymore (2106246752542019585).

## How they reason and decide
He reads market structure through specific price levels and timelines: 57K by Friday, 50K by end of July, dips below 65K as a long before 85 (2069836559948386506, 2069334649134862522, 2071600546356564409, 2035884265175502918). He says he does not like to fade his gut (2052596146749542451), and he compares current price action to historical moments like the 2023 spike down after the FTX bottom (2108404919375946163).

He is contrarian with the crowd. When everyone grasps for bottom signals, he expects more pain; when grifters leave, he expects a relief rally (2062949976821403963, 2032512392144498905). Earlier he judged DeFi by reading incentives, validator emissions, and whether a product has its own infrastructure rather than being a wrapper (1965482203317764572, 1965515870836589000, 1984053107601633353). Later he judges projects by revenue, mechanisms, extraction, and community-first design rather than hype (2106020226458370443, 2076838646934577418). He evaluates people by whether they ship and stick around, not by talk (1967149024278143387, 2022331892822053297).

When he is accused or challenged, he shifts into detailed mechanical explanation with receipts. He explains taxes, routing, and mcap point by point; he uses community dashboards and data to back revenue and volume claims; he reasons through tokenomics such as claim cycles, TWAP buybacks, and budget per period (2097602877925609720, 2096062642708271463, 2106220420659614105, 2106778812197314652, 2106783384722715056). He has also used community polls to decide product direction (2084753264591937918, 2085474118065938629).

He applies a data-driven approach to his own body, tracking health and stress patterns, then importing that same posture into markets (2059864376979362121, 2069274056092729826). He treats reputation and relationships as the real moat over product features, and reads institutional news such as ETF tokens, licenses, and stock tokens as confirmation of the onchain tradfi thesis (2106846254122647750, 2108308819520503839, 2112975322475835650, 2117675883255472215).

## Risk attitude
His personal trading can be degen: he admits gambling LTF on high leverage and getting liquidated on multiple accounts (1979129462013718880). After many cycles he says he is numb to gains and losses; $10,000 no longer feels like $10,000 (2062810617719701592). Yet his public risk warnings are loud and concrete: "You think 59K is bad? Wait till you see 49K," "EXIT THE FUCKING MARKET MATEY," and "Don't trade today, it will get ugly" (2062922857089298895, 2054592566805360829, 2062418191242932399). He warns that hedging free airdrops with illiquid premarket perps blows people out (1985828294206296391).

He thinks in counterparty disaster terms: counterparty risk is being butchered to almost zero as a design goal, and he expects a big player to get nuked off the market before the final bottom (1986035133908979972, 2019526140688306463). But he also preaches defusing personal psychology before taking crypto risk: pay off your mortgage first because psychology will mess with you (1970070095662850173). "Greed will kill you" is a one-line risk rule (2082153065465934067). By late 2026 he frames the downside as needing a black swan while still calling the market technically a bear (2104975101829476377, 2104975964144828704).

He refuses to discuss his own token's price or price action for legal compliance reasons (2108578832139854011), and he amplifies wallet-drainer and impersonator warnings (2116371410150936633, 2095184414053446012). His most candid risk admission is health: 3–5 hours sleep, panic attacks, dangerously high blood pressure, and vitamin D deficiency while building (2100465301045313925, 2103299031019762126, 2103263318635168187).
