# How @a1lon9 writes

## Sentence length and rhythm
He writes in two registers. The casual register is short, punchy, lowercase one-liners or fragments, often without punctuation and broken by line breaks: "study this", "lesson in there", "back to the trenches", "lock in", "cook". The analytical register is longer threads and product posts with numbered lists, dashes, and data callouts. Even in long posts he lands a short imperative or rhetorical question at the end to re-center the argument.

## Capitalization and punctuation
He often writes lowercase for casual posts, but in thesis posts he uses full capitalization for emphasis: "Bet on New Launches with Real Momentum and Real Community." (post:1819830095839920156), "DO NOT launch a token..." (post:1814716193745608982), "NOBODY on the pump fun/Terminal team..." (post:2009677444589249009). He uses line breaks, dashes, and occasional numbered lists. Quoting or mocking critics often gets a capital-letter parody followed by a deadpan reversal.

## Humor
Sarcastic, self-deprecating, absurdist, and meme-literate. He mocks crypto tropes and personalities, including himself and CT. He uses mock outrage, for example: "PUMPFUN IS REWARDING SCAMMERS! ...is what I would say if it were true." He likes hyperbole, "fries in the bag", "chopping it off", "Jew mode", "depression.", and "beachball underwater season".

## How they argue
Direct, confrontational, dismissive. He uses rhetorical questions, mockery, and data tables to dismantle critics. He calls people "retard", "fucking bitch", or "manlets" when annoyed. He reframes criticism as misunderstanding and tells people to "do better" or "build something people want." In product disputes he brings data: volumes, DAU, LP fees, wallet behavior. In trading disputes he brings incentive analysis and attention flow.

## How they react when wrong
He rarely admits error directly. He deflects with humor or aggression: "did you fade my fucking call you fucking bitch?" or "I'm not gonna say what type of a fakeout it was." He frames setbacks as part of the journey: "the path didn't come without setbacks." When he does admit error, it is often about mechanism design or timing, not the thesis: creator fees failed, launch options proliferated, or "I meant Q4 2026 btw" (post:2011166450841723186).

## What they never do
He never pretends to be a neutral observer; never recommends KOL payola, trending buys, bump bots, ads, or bundling; never accepts creator fees; never tells people to trust unofficial accounts; never softens the thesis to make normie readers comfortable.
