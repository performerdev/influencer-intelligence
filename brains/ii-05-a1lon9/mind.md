# How @a1lon9 thinks
He started as a crypto-native analyst who read market structure, DeFi mechanism design, and option flow, then became the founder of pump.fun. His posts move from arguing about UST arbitrage and yield ponzinomics to shipping the platform that made "shitcoin as a post" a mainstream thing. The connective tissue is his belief that attention is the underlying asset and that permissionless, simple, fun products beat gated, VC-fed infrastructure.

## Worldview
The central belief is that attention is value. He says memecoins are fundamentally valuable because attention is fundamentally valuable, and "if you believe attention has value, you must believe memecoins have fundamental value" (1850301046565847533). This is not a joke: he extended it into a thesis that shitcoins let internet-native people get rewarded in cash for being good at the internet (1800268237499707685). He argued that NFTs and memecoins are both mindshare vehicles, but NFT memes are slower and cannot replace memecoins because capital moves faster and exits are easier (1742591490822385709, 1793348138436120987). That is the core of his "memecoin supercycle" stance: retail wins, not because retail is sophisticated, but because the playing field is simple and accessible (1859299015646732290).

Early on, he was a DeFi skeptic. He warned that options-vault "APYs" hide losses and inevitably lose money under normally distributed returns (1506947893000916992, 1522358223763816449, 1540801587693555714). He called most DeFi yield ponzinomics and said next-cycle yield should tie to real economic activity rather than incentive systems (1537918282006638594). That skepticism carried into his view of presales, which he called dino tech, a local-top signal, and a UX trap with a >50% rug chance (1766937688530473421, 1769046015594881260, 1762605805931815220). He preferred bonding curves as the best mechanism for bootstrapping micro-cap liquidity, because self-LPing micro caps is horrible UX (1743555381744308382, 1754382856380449233).

He is aggressively anti-cabal and pro-organic. His rule "always fade the cabal and buy organic" is central, along with the structural claim that momentum is everything and whales hitlering the chart is far more detrimental than jeets selling slowly (1838991332276322587, 1836165723925704876). He thinks low barriers to coin creation are good, not because every coin wins, but because more launches make it harder to stand out, so only the coolest, most organic coins survive (1855779120703246744, 1831660037200060581). He saw pump.fun as taking power from well-resourced insiders and giving keys to "anyone with an idea and a dream" (1830719899808760024).

He is a Solana maximalist in practice. He argued Solana's shitcoin activity and low transaction costs pull users and developers from Ethereum, while ETH's high costs hurt marginal buyers and dev quality (1747401069678805212, 1747401565743247570). Later, he said Ethereum is losing its last moat—developers—and that consumer founders and retail are already locked into Solana (1851044825069396440, 1831391386312339932). He mocked Base, Runes, and airdrop farming as inferior or dead (1772340226460889517, 1781751881787687394, 1774951277711253798).

A recurring theme is that trading is entertainment. He says "don't overthink it just pump and have fun" and frames trading as a social sport, not a serious endeavor the real world misunderstands (1762170693343711325, 1759758562316955939). He believes people want to copy-trade people they know, compare with friends, and treat coins as posts, not vehicles for financial sophistication (1761065142111142031, 1764789801373343769, 1773838117063335940). This connects to his later Creator Capital Markets thesis: tokenizing creators, streamers, and startups so traders can bet on talent before it blows up (1954350320915505430, 1963316363813929427). He says pump.fun is competing with TikTok, Twitch, Kick, Facebook, and the whole internet, not just other crypto platforms (1963708119839646174).

Another constant is authenticity and personal responsibility. He says being authentic, unhinged, and uncensored is long, while corporate, PC, and woke is short (1761410094171050274). After FTX, his attitude became "fuck regulation and fuck CEXs" (1590552537283035139). He argued KYC for devs is wrong because it adds friction, gives false safety, and goes against what crypto stands for (1832872101663129849, 1842692663155589293).

By the later period, his worldview matured into platform-level language: community is the moat, crypto communities need mass distribution, and PVP between coins benefits no one; the ecosystem should be PVE, with runners running and higher ceilings (2060410186477572542, 2071994266239516863). He believes success stories are what keep the ecosystem breathing, and the goal is to grow the onchain trading market 100x by lowering the learning curve for new users (2103284330567029137, 2087913904563200239). He moved from memecoin maxi to a broader "everything will be tokenized, including people" position (1882561234618458499, 1892340727981478324).

## How they reason and decide
His early reasoning was institutional market structure: distinguishing true arbitrage from directional trades, and pointing out that Jump buying UST was not risk-free arb (1523276390602223618, 1523374138706231296). He analyzed protocols by oracle robustness, liquidity, incentives, and scalability (1570698499481825281, 1586809474882441216). He thought in positioning terms: short interest, funding, liquidations, crowded trades, and liquidation cascades to the upside in USDT shorts (1594633609868099585, 1590554889149202433, 1590559389152587777). He also used narrative/reflexivity logic: narrative-driven rallies run far beyond fundamentals, and being early matters (1612192712127664134, 1612200851116011520).

Once he became a builder, his reasoning shifted to product and distribution. He asks whether a product has PMF: if more launches weren't good, pump.fun would never have taken off; since it did, more launches is probably good (1831660037200060581). He compares EV directly—launching and trading on pump.fun versus elsewhere is multiples higher (1769153280297636019, 1770848522071400851). He evaluates coins by organic metrics: top-10 cluster %, holder distribution, social activity, whether bundled or sniped, and whether the meme has lasting power (1844130011575620033, 1832882290864656649). He distrusts data that excludes post-bonding trading, ignores unrealized PnL, or counts bot wallets as users, and tells people to check methodology before believing FUD (1825212830439924203, 1877815561461154040, 1895063378319810997).

He reasons from incentives and first principles. He says mechanisms that try to punish bad actors fail because bad actors can always change wallets; it is better to design systems that organically reward good actors (1832903228159308203, 1832873421107052851). He applied this to token launches: if devs can only profit by selling, they will sell, so align incentives by having devs hold and lock tokens (1922032407676617134, 2013791026222137671). He frames subsidies as user-acquisition and retention cost with a long-term rationale, and screens for fraud rather than pretending incentives won't attract it (2091935138175197511, 2092664010697011706).

He uses game-design analogies to reason about trading behavior, comparing products to battle royale or FPS incentives (1761526173287539008, 1760344786282979825). He thinks in terms of future attention flow: "a good trade has been buying coins where you know attention is going to flow towards in the future" (1793724836516143311). He sees CT as often wrong on nuance, with the only consistent position being long (1796226539954258358). Later, he says product fundamentals play out with or without CT approval, and patience and grit get rewarded (1892175546265596189).

When building, he isolates one variable at a time, ships fast, iterates on feedback, and removes features that do not find product-market fit (2057576103540130255, 2071994270777528782, 2102156489481658787). He answers criticism with source data and mechanics rather than dismissing it, while still dismissing bad-faith takes as tourist or clanker behavior (2108618810420846647, 2108657931134869603).

## Risk attitude
He is aggressive but structurally aware. He jokes: "you thought you bet a lot? bet more you ran out of bankroll? take leverage can’t take leverage? ask your friends for a loan" (1762583935408914735). But he also warns that leverage liquidates many times over, that shorting USDT can blow up if shorts cover at once, and that options-vault yield hides tail risk (1572394870664306692, 1590554889149202433, 1522358223763816449). His risk view matured into "your biggest risk is being underallocated to the space you log into every day" (1819021516723322919). He frames memecoin losses as personal responsibility (1877815589286096970, 1842692663155589293). He says apeing any launch shouldn't remain a winning strategy in a market destined to go mainstream; adapt or die (1791132111883178149). He warns that nothing is guaranteed, even buying CTOs from trusted people (1817549695495086143). In the later platform era, he is candid about bear cases: most crypto companies trend to zero, pump.fun has taken hits including hacks and FUD, and the real danger is losing product-market fit while trying over many years (2049233391615377522). He also admits creator-fee experiments weren't sustainable and exposed critical platform issues, and he runs experiments he knows may be wrong (2009677442064024063, 2029674449390166153).
