# How @a1lon9 thinks

## Worldview

The throughline is that attention is the real asset and memecoins are the cleanest market for attention. Early on he calls shitcoins "the most efficient way to price ideas and news events" because holders are financially incentivized to spread them faster than content (post:1776370487410692476, post:1779932740776759383, post:1781751881787687394). By mid-2024 he pushes the same idea harder: memecoins are not a fad, retail interest in DeFi/RWA/restaking/modularity is "programmatically impossible" in the new paradigm (post:1817243723266400256), and if attention is fundamentally valuable then memecoins have fundamental value (post:1850301046565847533). By 2026 that has broadened rather than reversed: attention and belief resolve faster and at higher magnitudes than outcomes, and 100x moves happen multiple times a week (post:2052510697943130494). The change is from "shitcoins price news" to "the same primitive carries community coins, project coins, attention coins, and startup coins" (post:2071683173453332584, post:2049233389991911832, post:2105386543602905217).

He is a fair-launch maximalist who became the leading voice for the thing he thought should exist. Before pump.fun was clearly dominant, he argued bonding curves are the best way to bootstrap tiny tokens and that combining them with AMMs solves scale (post:1743555381744308382, post:1745225801543434500, post:1760402439398899887). He called presales predatory, slow, and a local top signal (post:1743058264294617345, post:1762605805931815220, post:1769378176613134545, post:1776701413089054909, post:1802021236911882681). Later he frames pump.fun as the radical reduction of creation cost: $2 instead of $2,000+, one minute instead of sixty, three steps instead of thirty (post:1823072046500520361). It took power from the well-resourced and gave keys to anyone with an idea (post:1830719899808760024). But his view matures: by 2026 he separates community tokens from project tokens and says they need different design and incentives (post:2023782348874289301). He also admits creator fees worked as a tool for high-quality project tokens but failed to change the average memecoin deployer and skewed incentives toward low-risk coin creation (post:2009677442064024063). So the belief in open creation stayed, but the mechanism design became more pessimistic about most creators.

His view of what matters in crypto is anti-protocol, pro-product, pro-attention. He declared the fat protocol thesis dead: consumer products and attention matter more than protocol value (post:1764121833429721334, post:1761888555071386044, post:1777063798350004727). Trading is entertainment and a social sport more than a serious endeavor (post:1759758562316955939, post:1761481135811666166, post:1767913658942947586, post:1777003399814197378). He repeats later that most people misunderstand why retail users come to crypto: they are also driven by personal fulfilment, fun, and friends (post:1886512815361683518). By 2026 he is emphatic that traders are the lifeblood of the platform and that an environment must incentivize trading, liquidity, volume, and risk-taking, not just creation (post:2009677442064024063). This is a continuity: fun was the product, then traders were the customer.

On chains, he is a Solana bull first and an Ethereum bear second. Early: Solana is the best chain for shitcoins because of UX, fees, and culture, while Base and ETH L2s are inferior (post:1766879539404439824, post:1770464350626803835, post:1781751881787687394, post:1805691373640700001). Mid-cycle he says innovation "always has and always will be on Solana" (post:1974239667248562452). But his latest connective-tissue view is less tribal: pumpfun should not wall off access or disadvantage other ecosystems, and the app only succeeds if traders can win on any onchain opportunity (post:2105051255416705524), with crosschain trading bullish (post:2074864951185801486, post:2089465838734602355, post:2092613725618356659). The shift is from Solana-exceptionalism for memecoins to a Solana-centric platform that wants to swallow all onchain action.

He consistently hates cabals, KOL payola, VCs, and manufactured corporatism. Culture matters because permissive and truly open communities beat manufactured corporate shills (post:1834995702751273420). Buying cabal coins puts you at a huge disadvantage, while grassroots coins let you assess narratives objectively (post:1836165723925704876). He mocks VCs and VC-infra as a cabal (post:1843087071176745391, post:1845875128393257406, post:1849127282419421224). The later posts show the same instinct expressed as product principle: reduce information asymmetries, lower barriers, and make the game fairer (post:2023782348874289301, post:2057489304947970136).

## How they reason and decide

His first move is incentive analysis. He thinks through how people will actually behave given the payoff structure, then predicts or designs around that. Early examples include distinguishing arbitrage from directional bets and dissecting flawed logic (post:1523276730462466049, post:1523374138706231296, post:1523374679997566978). He uses game theory to predict jeeting and fair-launch behavior (post:1761829903496892843, post:1761829906072183198, post:1769453529243291815). Later he presses the same lever on creators: devs can only benefit by selling and are the first buyers at the lowest price, so their incentives are misaligned with holders; rewarding creators with a portion of trading fees lowers the incentive to dump (post:1922032407676617134, post:1922032410579108275). After creator fees fail, he returns to that exact framework to explain why they skewed behavior (post:2009677442064024063).

He is data-literate and responds to criticism by contesting the data. He brands a pump.fun profitability study's method so bad the results are meaningless and says to look through the methods yourself (post:1825212830439924203). He argues true profitable wallets are far larger because post-bond buys are excluded, unrealized profit is excluded, and about 30% of wallets only ever place a single sell order—certainly not human activity (post:1877815561461154040, post:1877815569988166039, post:1877815574060749168, post:1877815581912519076). He compares PumpSwap LPs only a few days old to Raydium LPs that have had months to compound (post:1907524891156328559, post:1907524893648056502). That is his style: he picks apart the counter-evidence instead of just asserting the thesis.

He reasons by analogy to traditional finance: memestocks and SPACs (post:1790443459637096540, post:1793348138436120987); ETF inflows that cannot move BTC up will move ETH down (post:1815786954694476227); onchain trading's age and growth compared to older crypto sectors means it is early (post:2049233389991911832). He is comfortable turning broad historical patterns into memecoin conclusions. He also reasons from first principles about markets: fair launch is only as good as the number of people able to get in early (post:1827823318533374261); lower barriers to creation make standing out harder, which forces effort, so only the coolest organic stuff wins (post:1855779120703246744); if small ports chase 1000x, utility is vaporware, and the bull market lives, then you are necessarily bullish on memecoins (post:1831027455408381988).

He evaluates products through accessibility and UX, not just tech. He asks whether memecoin UX can onboard retail (post:1783962900094202215), compares normies to crypto natives (post:1814346139879256557), and notes that although over 60% of onchain traders use mobile devices, most tools are desktop-first and past mobile apps fell short on UX or depth (post:1938617757970444355, post:1938617757970444355). The later product decisions follow from this: simplicity is a core design principle, too many launch options create needless PVP and make onboarding harder, and the next wave of mass retail onboarding will come from mobile apps (post:2023782348874289301, post:2057489304947970136, post:2071994266239516863, post:2049886778622967824).

His decision-making is also meta-anticipatory. He says a good trade is buying where attention will flow in the future (post:1793724836516143311). He accepts being early rather than wrong (post:2014832138827247983). He likes "do something different" as a selection filter in a world fighting for attention (post:1828552524518789332). He buys leaders, not derivative plays (post:1805691373640700001). The newer posts show a more operational loop: test new features in the real market, study usage and feedback, then remove what lacks product-market fit (post:2057489303106687165, post:2057489304947970136, post:2071994268437082155). That is a meaningful evolution from mostly trading calls to mechanism design.

## Risk attitude

He has a trader's taste for asymmetric upside. He acknowledges impermanent loss and volatility but still hunts the big payout (post:1489637264628068353, post:1788599812209745990). His most characteristic risk statement is that the biggest risk is being underallocated to the space you log into every day (post:1819021516723322919). That is not "risk off"; it is FOMO as portfolio policy.

At the same time, he is blunt about the trenches being dangerous. He warns that apeing fresh launches is far too risky for most people (post:1823364292026237117), that if you choose to trade coins you should consider what you put in will be lost (post:1877815589286096970), and that people should verify custom pair contract addresses rather than trusting image, ticker, or name (post:2108266109224751409). He also warns about centralization risk: do not take freedoms for granted because centralized L2s can blacklist memecoins and nuke positions to zero (post:1879038646231429332). He passes along a systemic-risk warning about the meme economy from a trusted ten-year friend (post:1819095263622930775), which shows he does not fully suppress the bear case.

As a builder he becomes more explicit about strategic risk: the bear case is losing product-market fit while grinding over many years and failing the vision; the platform has taken endless FUD, a hack, and market pullbacks, and survival is the prerequisite (post:2049233391615377522). He also admits that callout reward algorithm changes lag by weeks and historical data cannot be reversed even if rewards were not justified (post:2108657931134869603). So his risk view is layered: high personal appetite, sharp warnings for normies, and growing awareness that platform risk is existential.

## Favorite narratives

His stances changed in a few places, but the big memecoin thesis was stable.

- **Fair launches / bonding curves / pump.fun**: bullish from the first batch, and it became his identity. Early: bonding curves are the best mechanism (post:1743555381744308382). Later: pump.fun is the most used interface for trading onchain, not an aggregator, bot, or dex (post:1885459341160874149), and its liquidity compounds like Raydium/Uniswap (post:1907524891156328559). The change is not a flip but a widening: from launchpad for tiny tokens to the primitive for all tokenized attention (post:2071683173453332584).
- **Creator fees / dynamic fees**: this is the clearest change. In 2025 he saw creator revenue sharing as solving dev/holder misalignment (post:1922032407676617134, post:1922032410579108275). By 2026 he says creator fees failed to change the average deployer and are better suited for high-quality project tokens (post:2009677442064024063). So: bullish on the general tool, bearish on its effect on average memecoin creation.
- **Memecoins / attention coins**: bullish throughout. Early as idea-pricing instruments (post:1776370487410692476); mid as the cycle's biggest innovation and supercycle (post:1839371020094083549, post:1859299015646732290); late as permanent and returning stronger (post:2071683173453332584, post:2105386543602905217). He also says "XRP is the perfect example that good memes never die" (post:1863715361117368783).
- **Solana**: bullish throughout, with the late nuance that the platform should not be a walled garden and crosschain trading is part of the future (post:1974239667248562452, post:2105051255416705524, post:2074864951185801486).
- **Ethereum, Base, ETH L2s**: bearish and mocked across batches. ETH builders are mocked (post:1811067993901912386), Ethereum maxis and the ETH cabal are targets (post:1878128265149219261, post:1884288223301754894, post:1831391386312339932). No recant appears.
- **Presales**: bearish from the first batch (post:1762605805931815220). No recant appears.
- **AI memecoins / onchain agents**: bullish in 2025 on AI memecoins (post:1847373989574037935, post:1848454166428926353, post:1856362582703034613). By 2026 this becomes the larger thesis: onchain agents will be core to a trillion-dollar agentic economy, and tokens are a distribution layer for agents (post:2032472708387160561).
- **Creator economy / streaming**: bullish from the mid batch (post:1825939566827811126, post:1925285196351345009, post:1963316363813929427, post:1967245564506251509). Later it merges with mobile/social trading and coin communities (post:2049886778622967824, post:2060410823491641645).
- **Recent mechanisms**: cashback coins, holder rewards, callout rewards, custom pairs / meme:memes, charity coins, USDC pairs, Coin Communities, HomeDAO, Build in Public Hackathon / Pump Fund are all bullish in the 2026 batch (post:2023782348874289301, post:2098819322936177080, post:2095201561186111663, post:2103576976363528261, post:2049546970646081829, post:2057489300598710321, post:2060410823491641645, post:2080682367165841595, post:2013387597100282149). His late meta is that PVP between coins benefits no one and the goal is to funnel holders to established runners—PVE (post:2071994266239516863, post:2108266106481717271).
- **Cults and CTO meta**: mixed; he sees value and risk in both (post:1821289260491886916, post:1837980394957762931, post:1842628629466886288, post:1820903870803145212, post:1836799901092479097, post:1946290531929100598).

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He rarely says "I was wrong" in a groveling way, but he does admit product mistakes after data. The clearest is creator fees: he admits they failed to change average deployer behavior and skewed incentives toward low-risk creation (post:2009677442064024063). He also admits the platform over-proliferated launch options, which created needless PVP and made onboarding harder (post:2057489304947970136, post:2071994266239516863). He corrects a timing call with "I meant Q4 2026 btw" (post:2011166450841723186), and his "not wrong, just early" is the usual deflection (post:2014832138827247983). His pattern is to treat mistakes as iteration inputs: test, observe, remove, then ship again. He is much more eager to admit the product's past mechanism was wrong than to admit his market thesis was wrong.
