# How @a1lon9 thinks

## Worldview
The throughline is that attention is the real asset. Early on, @a1lon9 wrote like an onchain derivatives and DeFi market-structure guy: UST arbitrage debates, options vault yields, leverage, liquidations, funding. He was already hostile to fake yield and fake safety. By early 2024 he had moved fully into the Solana shitcoin/launchpad world and became a memecoin maximalist. The shift is not a contradiction: he kept the mechanism-design skepticism but concluded that the best way to win is to make coin creation cheap, fair, fun, and social.

His core belief: attention has fundamental value, so memecoins have fundamental value. He says it directly: "if you believe attention has value, you must believe memecoins have fundamental value" (1850301046565847533). Shitcoins are the most efficient way to price ideas and events (1779932740776759383); capital and attention move faster in coins than in NFTs (1793348138436120987). That explains why he thinks the fat protocol thesis is dead and value accrues to apps, frontends, and consumer products instead of protocols (1764121833429721334).

He believes lowering the barrier to launch is not a bug but the product. More coins create brutal competition at low market caps, and the coolest, most organic, most battle-tested communities win (1831660037200060581, 1855779120703246744). He calls pump.fun a social platform and "shitcoins as posts" (1773838117063335940). The launchpad, in his telling, took power from well-resourced insiders and gave it to anyone with an idea (1830719899808760024).

His view of retail is not patronizing: retail wants fun, friends, dopamine, and belonging, not financial sophistication (1764421594049565071). Trading is entertainment and a social sport; the real world misunderstands this (1759758562316955939). That leads him to favor simple, mobile-first, consumer-grade interfaces over pro/terminal software (1938617757970444355, 1764421594049565071).

On chains, he is loudly Solana-maximal and Ethereum-bearish. Solana won through better UX, sticky liquidity, and a native asset that pumped with adoption (1885388456823841063). Ethereum lost its developer moat and is, in his framing, high-cost, slow, and not where retail or consumer founders are (1851044825069396440, 1831391386312339932). He also repeatedly attacks Base, Runes, Friendtech, presales, and airdrop farming as grifts or dead ends.

He has a strong anti-cabal, anti-KOL, anti-fake-metrics worldview. Organic communities, grassroots coins, and real social activity beat paid promotion, bump bots, trending, and bundled supply (1836841214022406181, 1838991332276322587). He says the solution to too many coins is better curation, not limiting freedom (1823364292026237117). He is against KYC for devs because bad actors can always change wallets and because protectionism goes against crypto’s core values (1832872101663129849, 1842692663155589293).

His later worldview expands from memecoins to creator capital markets. He wants to tokenize creators, streamers, and startups so traders can bet on talent before it blows up (1963316363813929427, 1942948099854328150). He sees pump.fun competing with TikTok, Twitch, Kick, Facebook, and the whole internet, not just other crypto platforms (1963708119839646174, 1966561509930094792). He also says traders are the lifeblood of the platform, not creators/devs (1914421121350570333). By 2026 he was pushing Coin Communities, Callout Rewards, Holder Rewards, Custom Pairs, and crosschain trading, with the view that quality information and community are the moat (2060410823491641645, 2087913904563200239).

He also holds a cyclical view: the four-year cycle plays out almost identically, give or take ten days (1831349897351606753). Memecoins are not a one-cycle affair; good memes with strong communities can live forever, like XRP (1863715361117368783). Even in a bear market, he argues memecoins will come back stronger because humans are greedy and the group chat persists (2071683173453332584, 2105386543602905217).

## How they reason and decide
He reasons from mechanism design and incentives. Bad actors can change wallets, so punishing them by identity or vesting creates an illusion of safety while the game theory hasn’t changed. Better to design systems that reward good actors through transparent information and organic social proof (1832903228159308203, 1891599556724273423). This is why he rejects max-buy limits, dev locks, sell taxes, KYC, and other patches as false protection (1769453529243291815, 1832872101663129849).

He also thinks in market-structure and positioning terms from his earlier trading days: short interest, funding, liquidations, and crowded trades shape risk/reward (1594633609868099585, 1590554889149202433). He distinguishes true arbitrage from directional trades that require a view; that distinction drove his UST/Terra analysis (1523374138706231296).

His most repeated trading framework is attention flow. A good trade buys coins where future attention will flow: elections, countries, people, memes, events (1793724836516143311). He wants price-insensitive future buyers, not niches. A “cringey” first reaction is a buying signal because mass appeal is the point (1794404584128917858). He judges coins by whether they can retain attention and liquidity, not by roadmap or fundamentals (1793348138436120987).

He is data-literate and distrusts bad data. He attacks PnL analyses that ignore unrealized PnL, post-bonding Raydium trading, bot wallets, or wash trading, and tells people to look through the methodology (1825212830439924203, 1877815561461154040). He uses onchain volumes, holder distribution, top-10 cluster percentages, and social activity rather than vibes (1844130011575620033).

He uses product-market fit as the test. If pump.fun took off, the market approved it; proposed restrictions that only benefit insiders are strictly worse (1823079617277861986, 1823364292026237117). When changing product parameters, he isolates one variable at a time and starts from what already worked before (2057576103540130255, 2057578034111164430).

He thinks in expected value. For small stacks, trenching and coin creation have higher EV than anything else; for larger stacks, you must become a conviction trader (1829934913627267514, 1851002063377088673). He also uses gaming analogies: battle royale, FPS, dopamine loops (1761526173287539008). Later, as a builder, he emphasizes shipping fast, working in a problem space you understand, developing relationships with target users first, and iterating on feedback (1882558167722393608, 1882570288250151234).

## Risk attitude
He is a self-aware degenerate, but not a naive one. He can joke about max leverage, "bet more, take leverage, ask friends for a loan" (1762583935408914735), and he treats being underallocated as the biggest risk (1819021516723322919). But his early posts show respect for tail risk: leverage can liquidate you many times over regardless of funding costs (1572394870664306692); shorting USDT can cascade to the upside (1590554889149202433); options vault “yields” are just hidden downside and inevitably lose money (1522358223763816449).

His current risk rules are mostly about avoiding fake safety and stacked decks. Presales are a rug trap with bad UX (1762605805931815220). Cabal coins put you at a disadvantage because you don’t know why they go up or when to exit (1836165723925704876). Whales hitlering a chart are worse than jeets selling slowly, so he says fade the cabal and buy organic (1838991332276322587). Centralized L2s can blacklist memecoins and nuke positions to zero (1879038646231429332).

He also frames losses as part of the game: people buy the riskiest asset class and then wonder when there is a flush; generational wealth is not handed out (1804998365652091036). Memecoins can go to zero, and he says that is personal responsibility, not a reason for paternalistic safeguards (1877815589286096970, 1842692663155589293). He is willing to run experiments that may be wrong and iterate based on data (2029674449390166153).

## Favorite narratives
- **Memecoin supercycle**: bullish and increasingly central. Early on he called memecoins the product of the cycle (1764421594049565071); by 2024–2025 he called it a paradigm shift and retail’s best opportunity (1791132111883178149, 1859299015646732290). In 2026 he still says memecoins are permanent and will come back stronger (2071683173453332584, 2105386543602905217).
- **Pump.fun / fair launches**: bullish. He went from “launchpad for shitcoins on Solana” (1740484955828855262) to “pump fun took power from well-resourced” (1830719899808760024) to defending it as the best onchain trading app with 0% fees and crosschain (2105051255416705524).
- **Solana vs Ethereum**: Solana bullish, Ethereum bearish. He mocked ETH maxis early, but the view hardened after he built on Solana. He says Solana is past, present, and future; the user will not switch without a 10x improvement that doesn’t exist (1770464350626803835, 1805691373640700001, 1851044825069396440).
- **Onchain options**: this is a clear reversal. He was bullish on NFT-Fi / NFT derivatives early (1556822283607576578), then later dismissed onchain options as “the most midcurve narrative for years” (1761446208168763768). The change happened as he moved away from DeFi infrastructure toward consumer shitcoin rails.
- **Presales**: consistently bearish. He called them predatory, slow, and a local-top signal, with >50% rug chance (1762605805931815220, 1769378176613134545, 1802021236911882681).
- **Airdrops / points / farming**: bearish. “Farming as a whole is dying”; airdrop farming was a previous cycle, and now the hustle is trenching and coin creation (1805727735287103731, 1851002063377088673).
- **AI memecoins / onchain agents**: mixed, then bullish, then more cautious. He was originally mixed (1766962991722115434). By 2024–2025 he called AI memecoins a ChatGPT moment for crypto (1848454166428926353). Later he still believed in onchain agents as a trillion-dollar agentic economy layer (2032472708387160561), but removed the Tokenized Agents launch option because too many launch modes created PvP and slowed narratives (2071994266239516863).
- **Creator capital markets / streaming**: bullish from 2025 onward. He sees tokenizing creators as the fix for a broken creator economy (1921942671788064891, 1963316363813929427).
- **Callout Rewards / Holder Rewards / Custom Pairs / Coin Communities**: bullish in 2026 as ways to reward quality, reduce PvP, and give communities mass distribution (2060410823491641645, 2087913904563200239, 2098819322936177080, 2103576976363528261).
- **Fartcoin**: bullish, repeatedly cited as an example of a real community runner (1952866820748144795, 1973141501178946019).
