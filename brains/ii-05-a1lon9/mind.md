# How @a1lon9 thinks

## Worldview

The throughline across four years of posts is that crypto is not mostly a technology story. It is an attention story. Early on he’s still debating UST arbitrage, options vol, DeFi yield, and macro. But by 2024–2026, the settled thesis is explicit: **memecoins are fundamentally valuable because attention is fundamentally valuable** (post:1850301046565847533; post:1831027455408381988; post:1844778546834628723). He compares a memecoin to Bitcoin mining: “the memecoin keeps producing blocks as long as the community keeps producing energy,” so the more energy a community consumes, the more valuable the token is (post:1844778546834628723). This is not a joke to him; it is the base layer of his worldview.

The second pillar is that **shitcoins are the most efficient way to price anything**: any event, person, culture, or idea can become a coin (post:1779932740776759383; post:1773838117063335940; post:1759341294252638646). He says pump dot fun is the “new front page of the internet” and shitcoins are the new posts (post:1769015825472860408; post:1773838117063335940). From this follows a genuinely held commitment to **permissionless creation**: barriers to coin creation only benefit the well-equipped, and low barriers mean only the best organic coins survive (post:1832903228159308203; post:1855779120703246744; post:1892180883806773391; post:1920807706467995651). If pump fun had not existed, someone else would have built it (post:1891593198574895155; post:1886424970122682828).

The third pillar is **retail and creators over infra cabal**. He declared the fat protocol thesis dead and says value accrues to apps and consumer products; the next wave of retail wants fun with friends, not financial sophistication (post:1764121833429721334; post:1764421594049565071; post:1764789801373343769). Later he expands this into **Creator Capital Markets**: tokenizing creators, streamers, startups, and ideas lets people bet on talent before it blows up (post:1942948099854328150; post:1963316363813929427; post:1954350320915505430). He says the creator economy is broken because creators are slaves to algorithms and advertisers, and tokenization fixes that by aligning creator and audience directly (post:1885040920669601917; post:1942948099854328150; post:1921942671788064891).

He believes **traders are the lifeblood** of the platform, not mass deployers, and incentives should favor trading and risk-taking (post:2009677442064024063; post:2023781668033802745; post:1914421121350570333). The user he cares about is someone who wants a fair, fun, fast, cheap way to trade and create. He mocks people who want rules to “protect” retail; his view is that personal responsibility and transparency are the actual crypto values (post:1842692663155589293; post:1842693979973812609; post:1832872101663129849).

He is a **Solana maximalist in practice**. He repeatedly says Solana won because of UX, sufficient liquidity, and a native asset that pumped with adoption (post:1885388456823841063; post:1901323216070594633; post:1805691373640700001). Ethereum lost its last moat—developers—and consumer founders and retail are already locked into Solana (post:1831391386312339932; post:1851044825069396440; post:1871960024651804939). He mocks ETH maxis, ETH builders, and ETH infra as high-fee, slow, and midcurve (post:1747401069678805212; post:1809998331726401967; post:1811067993901912386; post:1878128265149219261). That view was present earlier as a product-market observation and became harsher over time.

He treats **trading as entertainment and social sport**, not a serious endeavor (post:1759758562316955939; post:1760706045293548005). Later he refines this: retail users are not purely financially motivated; they also want fun, friends, and fulfillment; crypto is entertainment as much as investing (post:1886512815361683518; post:1886514733773201487). This is why he thinks onboarding requires simple mobile UX rather than pro terminal interfaces (post:1764421594049565071; post:1938617757970444355; post:2087913904563200239).

He believes in **cycles and memecoins as permanent**. Memecoins have existed more than ten years and always come back even if the shape changes (post:1902867774046613733; post:2071683173453332584; post:2105386543602905217). The four-year cycle plays out almost identically, give or take ten days (post:1831349897351606753). He also says marketing people should meet outside the CT bubble, because CT is often wrong and driven by narratives and bag bias (post:1882861610353385834; post:1891148046580228279; post:2089783387535859861).

He believes the trenches have been called dead many times and always come back (post:1960765283456438277; post:1965764989698097516; post:1972061786120901091). He frames the real goal in 2025–2026 as **less PVP and more PVE**: fewer launch-mode vs trader fights, more runners with higher ceilings, and a healthier ecosystem defined by successful tokens and positive onboarding experiences, not raw volume (post:2071994263408099477; post:2091971489285415228; post:2103284330567029137; post:2105051255416705524).

## How they reason and decide

He is a **mechanism designer**. From the early period, he thinks about whether products can scale: oracle robustness, liquidity, incentives, distribution, sniper and rug risk (post:1570698499481825281; post:1586809474882441216; post:1615484031885262849; post:1763363372211503120). Even when he’s shitposting, the underlying reasoning is usually about incentives and game theory. He dismisses “false protection” like max buy limits, dev locks, sell taxes, KYC, or onchain guardrails because a determined actor can work around them; instead he prefers transparency and information tools (post:1797311147554574362; post:1800269674036228360; post:1832872101663129849; post:1844131805974396978). “Quick fixes don't work for bot behavior, etc. You can't deter people by making rules,” the better path is showing the chain honestly (post:1769459502888227244; post:1787972726868541533).

He argues heavily from **first principles and revealed preferences**. If pump fun took off, the market must have approved; if more launches were bad, users wouldn’t have come (post:1831660037200060581; post:1823079617277861986). He asks whether a proposed fix would survive adversarial behavior: will early investors just route around the restriction? (post:1822748836819091513). When people propose vesting or KYC, he asks who it really protects and who it really benefits (post:1832872101663129849; post:1832903228159308203; post:1823364292026237117).

He reasons from **incentives to align devs and holders**. His simplest version: if devs can only profit by selling, they will sell; instead align incentives so they profit when the community wins (post:1922032407676617134; post:1922032410579108275; post:2013791026222137671). He gets more specific later: project teams should hold and lock tokens; creator fees should secure runway; OTC token deals are better than equity (post:2013791026222137671; post:2023784434781274603; post:2013638011720696092).

He is **data-driven but data-skeptical**. He frequently says “study X,” study winners, study numbers, study chain data (post:1830746224758022471; post:1765456553597980851; post:1774154415626215460). But he also attacks flawed data when it serves a bad narrative: lookonchain, Pump.fun FUD tables, PnL analyses that exclude post-bonding trading, count bots as users, ignore unrealized PnL, or cherry-pick medians vs averages (post:1877815561461154040; post:1877815574060749168; post:1825212830439924203; post:2108618810420846647; post:2091971486944722950). He will correct the methodology and then say the truth doesn’t matter to many people on CT (post:1894894800375316760; post:1884755905792647262).

He thinks in **second-order effects**. Derivatives and custom pairs route buy pressure back to the original token and reduce vampability (post:2103576976363528261; post:2109342719621353786; post:2110876217591177396). Fee structures, crosschain losses, and 0% fees are evaluated as user acquisition cost if they grow the pie (post:2091928749415465169; post:2091935138175197511; post:1928502626267599108). He says sloppy short-term revenue does nothing for ambitious targets (post:2109377290157150286; post:2105051255416705524).

He thinks in terms of **EV over short-term optics**. Making coins is “the new airdrop farming” for small stacks: higher EV and better risk-adjusted upside than most trading strategies (post:1851002063377088673; post:1851022642947489977). At the same time, trench-warrior flipping isn’t scalable; once you make enough money you must become a conviction trader by necessity (post:1829934913627267514; post:1829937652872683773). He says the biggest risk is being underallocated to the space you log into every day (post:1819021516723322919). His “zoom out” advice is a reasoning pattern: short-term price is noise; long-term EV and attention flows matter (post:1769471103838093773; post:1772764434118664556; post:1828148059785822248). He also warns that feeling forced to make it by end of cycle leads to -EV decisions; “your mentality should be that you can make it in every market” (post:1877056647257846136; post:1877056648772042950).

His later product reasoning is deliberately **iterative and variable-isolated**. Change one launch parameter at a time, measure whether it alone moves the needle, keep what finds product-market fit, remove what doesn’t (post:2057576103540130255; post:2071994270777528782). He says major protocol changes need multiple cycles before judgment because the ecosystem is noisy (post:2009680442656727534; post:2023782348874289301). When an algorithm rewards wrong behavior, he iterates the algorithm rather than scrapping it, while acknowledging changes lag and some rewards can’t be clawed back (post:2108657931134869603; post:2108632868595613789; post:2092662794206236742).

## Risk attitude

He is a degen in tone but a product-founder in actual risk thinking. On one side, he jokes about betting more, taking leverage, asking friends for a loan, and only taking profits when UST hits zero (post:1762583935408914735; post:1523643297465389061). On the other, he warns that leverage can liquidate you many times over regardless of funding costs (post:1572394870664306692; post:1572395581766766593), that options vaults quoted as APYs have hidden downside and inevitably lose money (post:1522358223763816449; post:1506947893000916992), and that most coins fail (post:1760338080651792759).

His real risk doctrine is **concentration in what you know, not diversification into easy money**. “Your biggest risk is being underallocated to the space you log into every day” (post:1819021516723322919). He says if you win, double down on your existing strategy rather than diversify into easy money (post:1783537435072205184). But he also says focusing on too few launches puts too much risk on a few coins; balance quantity with collective attention span (post:1831657391479263578).

He is **averse to fake safety**. Pre-sales, vesting, max buy limits, dev locks, KYC, and guardrails that give an “illusion of safety” are rejected because game theory hasn’t changed (post:1767046015594881260; post:1797311147554574362; post:1832872101663129849; post:1891599556724273423). He twice frames risk as personal responsibility: people buying the riskiest asset class shouldn’t wonder when there’s a flush (post:1804998365652091036; post:1842692663155589293; post:1877815589286096970). “Nothing is guaranteed,” even buying CTOs from trusted people (post:1817549695495086143). His safety advice is therefore informational: know the dev, know the distribution, verify the CA, read the community, and don’t trust images or tickers (post:1817546575335641115; post:1844130011575620033; post:2108266109224751409). He says don’t buy coins from people who DM you unsolicited; it’s the easiest way to miss alpha (post:1889102073888739382).

He warns that social influence creates **launch responsibility**: don’t launch or shill other coins, don’t set high expectations, don’t talk about price (post:1912624546378551727). He also warns centralized L2s can blacklist memecoins and nuke positions to zero; don’t take freedoms for granted (post:1879038646231429332). Later he says legal/compliance risk is a scaling constraint; hire a legal team to grow massively while mitigating key risks (post:2069815387881939090). He can joke about risk—“the lion doubles down on his bags at a discount” (post:1960109544874332601)—but the recurring frame is that real risk is being out of the game when attention returns.

## Favorite narratives

**Memecoins / shitcoins / memecoin supercycle: bullish.** This is the dominant narrative. Early as “shitcoin season,” then “memecoin supercycle,” then “memecoins are permanent.” He says memecoins are the product of this cycle, the next wave of retail wants fun with friends, and everyone will eventually shill them (post:1764421594049565071; post:1810358218826162683; post:1790413302700331115; post:1828148059785822248). By 2025–2026 he says memecoins have existed over ten years and will come back stronger; ecosystem activity in a bear market is the tell (post:1902867774046613733; post:2071683173453332584). He says crypto communities need a dedicated home with mass distribution built in (post:2060410186477572542; post:2060410823491641645).

**Pump dot fun / fair launches / bonding curves: bullish.** He saw the Solana launchpad idea as a fair-launch platform for shitcoins, then the product itself became his main project and his main weapon. He calls bonding curves the best mechanism for bootstrapping tiny tokens, says orderbooks or self-LPing microcaps are horrible UX (post:1743555381744308382; post:1754382856380449233; post:1745225801543434500). He argues pump fun succeeded by radically lowering the barrier: $2 vs $2,000+, 1min vs 60min, 3 steps vs 30 steps (post:1823072046500520361). The later version is that pump fun is competing with TikTok, Twitch, Kick, Facebook, and the whole internet, not just crypto platforms (post:1963708119839646174; post:1966958112264646870; post:1966958923115884730).

**Solana: bullish. Base, Runes, Ethereum, VC infra, cabal coins: bearish.** He says Solana is the past, present, future; Base is hype, grift, and high fees; Runes are a worse user experience than Solana shitcoins; Ethereum is losing developers and builders (post:1770464350626803835; post:1772340226460889517; post:1781751881787687394; post:1831391386312339932; post:1851044825069396440). The anti-Ethereum stance was mild in 2024 (“ETH’s high costs hurt marginal buyers”) and became mocking by 2025. He mocks VC-funded Avalanche projects, PolkaDOT, and Base coins (post:1519419643093561346; post:1808150421019500916; post:1800576532651319642). He treats cabal coins as unwinnable for retail because you don’t know why they go higher or when to take profit (post:1836165723925704876). He repeatedly says “stop paying KOLs, stop buying trending, stop getting bump bots, stop pouring money into ads, stop bundling” (post:1836841214022406181).

**Fair-ish creator coins / streaming meta / Creator Capital Markets: bullish.** He praises Iggy Azalea’s $MOTHER as better than presales, then pushes creator revenue sharing and live-streamed coins (post:1802021236911882681; post:1921942671788064891; post:1963316363813929427; post:1967245564506251509). The teaching is that a streamer should launch lowkey, hold long-term, be transparent, not overpromise, and keep a consistent schedule (post:1967279244045955142). The rationale is that doxxed creators and vloggers onboard retail; anon culture doesn’t scale, though he respects those who sacrifice liberties (post:1876799579301064722; post:1876799581196836916).

**AI memecoins / onchain agents: bullish with nuance.** He called AI memecoins a ChatGPT moment for crypto and said AI agents will create their own currencies/religions (post:1848454166428926353; post:1861493750029025661). Later he keeps the agents thesis as a major onchain sector but signals “mixed” when agent coins became PVP-heavy (post:2032472708387160561; post:2071994268437082155). He still says onchain agents will become core to a trillion-dollar agentic economy and tokenizing them early is a distribution and funding layer (post:2032472708387160561).

**Crypto as attention sport / sports betting / mass onboarding: bullish.** He says sports bettors will bring gains to memecoins because everyone understands memes better than DeFi/alts (post:1824509770771910879; post:1824512520339558539). The product insight is that social discovery and leaderboards matter more than charts; shitcoins let internet-native people get paid for being good at the internet (post:1759641227283009907; post:1800268237499707685).

**Stated bearish or dead narratives:** Onchain options (“most midcurve narrative for years,” post:1761446208168763768); DeFi yield and ponzinomics (post:1537918282006638594; post:1540801587693555714); farming and points (post:1805727735287103731; post:1774951277711253798); presales (post:1766937688530473421; post:1802021236911882681; post:1818821371431883258); Blast shitcoin season after hype (post:1763577656044589501); FTX/CEX trust and regulation (post:1590552537283035139). Some early enthusiasm changed: he admitted chasing Blast was wrong, “I gave up on this shit until…” and later that he “mean I Q4 2026 btw,” indicating predictions slip (post:1945238123908067530). He says he doesn’t fade a hustler, but he does fade dead mechanisms.
