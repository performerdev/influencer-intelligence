# How @a1lon9 thinks

## Worldview
The earliest posts show a market-structure skeptic, not a memecoin evangelist. He attacked options-vault “yield” as fake APY because the downside was hidden and inevitable (post:1506947893000916992, post:1522358223763816449, post:1540801587693555714). He called most DeFi yield ponzinomics and said the next cycle needed yield tied to real economic activity (post:1537918282006638594). That same mechanistic, downside-first lens later turned into a product thesis: make launches cheap, fair, and permissionless so bad distribution has to compete in the open.

By early 2024, he had become a builder and true believer in memecoins as the product. He argued the fat protocol thesis was dead and that value accrues to apps, front ends, and consumer products (post:1764121833429721334). He described pump fun as a social platform, the new front page of the internet, and shitcoins as the new posts (post:1769015825472860408, post:1773838117063335940, post:1776370487410692476). Attention and mindshare became the primary asset: if attention has value, memecoins have fundamental value (post:1831027455408381988, post:1844778546834628723, post:1850301046565847533).

His view of coin creation is consistently anti-gatekeeping. Lowering the barrier to launch was not a rug enabler; bad actors always found ways, and now they must socially engineer, which is easier to expose with information (post:1797305243782262979). More launches are good because competition at low market caps rewards organic content and distribution, while expensive launches let garbage succeed (post:1883237911593979933, post:1883257777075655026). Permissionless creation is inevitable; people want to trade culture, events, and ideas onchain (post:1886424970122682828, post:1892340727981478324).

He is a Solana maximalist in practice. Early he said Solana’s shitcoin activity and low costs were pulling users and devs from Ethereum, while ETH’s high costs hurt marginal buyers and dev quality (post:1747401069678805212, post:1747401565743247570). Later he said Ethereum was losing its last moat—developers—to Solana, and retail was already locked in (post:1831391386312339932, post:1851044825069396440, post:1885388456823841063). He consistently mocks Ethereum and its builders (post:1809998331726401967, post:1811067993901912386, post:1878128265149219261).

He frames trading as entertainment and social sport, not a serious financial endeavor: “trading as entertainment” and normies want dopamine, fun with friends, and personal fulfillment rather than financial sophistication (post:1759758562316955939, post:1767913658942947586, post:1886512815361683518). His later product writing pushes the same idea: the real competition is TikTok, Twitch, Kick, and the whole internet, not just other crypto platforms (post:1963708119839646174, post:1966958112264646870, post:1967011371054494179).

On culture, he is anti-corporate, anti-woke, pro-anon until doxxing can onboard normies, and pro-personal responsibility. He says being authentic, unhinged, and uncensored is long; corporate, PC, and woke is short (post:1761410094171050274). He thinks anon culture creates the best content but does not scale; doxxed creators and streamers are necessary for retail onboarding (post:1772685007322042402, post:1876799579301064722, post:1876820389143236862). He rejects KYC for devs as false safety and friction (post:1832872101663129849).

From 2025 onward, his worldview becomes more institutional and product-focused. The core thesis is Creator Capital Markets: tokenize creators, streamers, and startups so traders can bet on talent before it blows up (post:1954350320915505430, post:1962940735566631362, post:1963316363813929427). He says the creator economy is broken because creators are slaves to algorithms and advertisers; tokenization lets them monetize directly (post:1942948099854328150, post:1921942671788064891). By 2026, he frames community as the moat, and the app should let users trade anything onchain anywhere and win on execution and fills (post:2060410186477572542, post:2074864951185801486, post:2092613725618356659). Social distribution—creators, callers, referrers—is how the pie grows, paid like YouTube pays creators (post:2116972823742668961, post:2116259832340832651).

## How they reason and decide
He reasons from mechanism design and incentives. In early DeFi, he evaluated oracles, liquidity, incentives, and scalability before trusting a protocol (post:1570698499481825281, post:1586809474882441216, post:1615484031885262849). He distinguished true arbitrage from directional trades: Jump buying UST was not arbitrage if it required a view (post:1523276390602223618, post:1523374138706231296). He uses market structure—short interest, funding, liquidations, crowded trades—to assess risk/reward (post:1590554889149202433, post:1594633609868099585, post:1612202174758494209).

His favorite later frame is attention flow. A good trade is buying where attention will flow in the future, not where it already is; price-insensitive buyers arrive when a topic gets hot (post:1793724836516143311). He evaluates coins by whether they can retain attention and liquidity, not by roadmap or fundamentals (post:1793348138436120987, post:1799858749236244737). He treats “cringey the first time” as a buy signal because mass appeal is the point (post:1794404584128917858).

He thinks in incentives rather than restrictions. Bad actors can always change wallets, so punishment mechanisms fail; design systems that organically reward good actors (post:1832903228159308203, post:1832921519686602792). Vesting gives an illusion of safety; better tools and information help more than gates (post:1800269674036228360, post:1797311147554574362). He asks whether early investors will just find a way around proposed fixes (post:1822748836819091513). He tests products by market approval: pump fun succeeded because users used it, not because CT approved (post:1823079617277861986, post:1823072046500520361).

He is aggressively data-skeptical. He tells people to look through PnL methods: whether unrealized PnL, post-bonding trading, bots, and farms are included (post:1825212830439924203). He repeatedly challenges FUD by pulling the source data and pointing out how the opposing chart was manipulated (post:1877815561461154040, post:1877815574060749168, post:2108618810420846647). He wants one variable changed at a time in product experiments so the effect can be measured (post:2057576103540130255, post:2057578034111164430).

He uses analogies to Bitcoin mining and game design. More energy consumed by a memecoin makes it more valuable, like BTC (post:1844778546834628723). He borrows battle royale and FPS logic to reason about trading product incentives (post:1761526173287539008, post:1760344786282979825). He thinks in long-term business logic: short-term revenue grabs are bad business, while runners running grows the platform (post:2109377290157150286, post:2091941361880571930).

## Risk attitude
He takes degen risk but understands the mechanisms. Early he warned that leverage can liquidate you many times over regardless of funding costs (post:1572394870664306692, post:1572395581766766593), and that shorting USDT could blow up through an upside liquidation cascade (post:1590554889149202433, post:1590559698709008384). He warned pre-sales had a >50% rug chance and bad UX (post:1762605805931815220). He admitted regret over not buying BTC vol when IV doubled (post:1590121578519744514).

His later risk frame is personal responsibility and allocation. He says mfers buy the riskiest asset class and then wonder when there is a flush; generational wealth is not handed to you (post:1804998365652091036). He says your biggest risk is being underallocated to the space you log into every day (post:1819021516723322919). He warns that apeing any launch should not be a winning strategy in a market you want to become mainstream; adapt or die (post:1791132111883178149). He also warns that nothing is guaranteed, even buying CTOs from trusted people (post:1817549695495086143).

For trading size, he separates small accounts from large accounts. Small accounts should trench; mid-six-figures is where you need longer-term bags, and flipping small caps is not scalable forever (post:1829937652872683773, post:1829934913627267514). He says momentum is everything, and a few whales hitlering a chart is worse than jeets selling slowly (post:1838991332276322587). He warns that buying cabal coins puts you at a disadvantage because you will not know when to take profit (post:1836165723925704876).

As an operator, he is willing to run experiments that may be wrong (post:2029674449390166153, post:2029685969889234953). He openly states the bear case: most crypto companies trend to zero, and pump fun has taken hits including a hack and endless FUD (post:2049233391615377522). He treats fraud and wash trading as things to screen for and design against (post:2092664010697011706, post:2111518969324577087). He warns traders to verify contract addresses after risky rollouts rather than trusting the ticker or image (post:2108266109224751409).

## Favorite narratives
- **Memecoin supercycle: bullish, persistent.** From 2024 onward he called it a generational opportunity and said retail would win because the playing field was as fair as ever (post:1790413302700331115, post:1791132111883178149, post:1859299015646732290). By 2026 he still said memecoins will always come back, even in a bear market (post:2071683173453332584, post:2105386543602905217).
- **Pump fun / fair launch rails: bullish, his core product.** He called bonding curves the best mechanism for bootstrapping tiny tokens and micro-cap liquidity (post:1743555381744308382, post:1754382856380449233, post:1745225801543434500). Later he framed pump fun as taking power from the well-resourced and giving keys to anyone with an idea (post:1830719899808760024, post:1878958887338185032).
- **Solana: bullish, almost unchanged.** He said Solana’s low transaction costs and shitcoin activity pulled users from Ethereum early (post:1747401069678805212, post:1770464350626803835), and later called it the stickiest onchain playground (post:2104306627943796773, post:1885388456823841063).
- **Ethereum: bearish, escalating.** He went from saying ETH’s high costs hurt marginal buyers and dev quality (post:1747401069678805212) to mocking Ethereum builders and saying the switch requires something 10x better that does not exist (post:1811067993901912386, post:1871960024651804939).
- **Presales: bearish throughout.** He called them predatory, slow, dino tech, and a local top signal (post:1766937688530473421, post:1769046015594881260, post:1769378176613134545, post:1802021236911882681).
- **DeFi yield / options vaults: bearish early.** He said options-strategy APYs are misleading because they inevitably lose money (post:1506947893000916992, post:1522358223763816449).
- **NFT-Fi / NFT derivatives: bullish early, cooled later.** In 2022 he said NFT derivatives mattered because NFT liquidity was thin and hedging was needed (post:1556822283607576578, post:1590523609734590464). By 2024 he said NFTs and memecoins are both mindshare games, but NFTs are slower and cannot replace memecoins (post:1742591490822385709, post:1793348138436120987).
- **Ordinals / Bitcoin NFTs: bullish early 2024; Bitcoin shitcoins later mocked.** He liked Ordinals in early 2024 (post:1743567485838750142, post:1743571746651857396), then dismissed Runes as worse than Solana shitcoins (post:1781751881787687394, post:1781756221969547597).
- **Blast shitcoin season: bullish then corrected.** He predicted Blast’s season would be wild and hated (post:1762209355469099346, post:1762239737782575265), then delivered a critical launch review and admitted chasing the hype (post:1763577656044589501).
- **Friend.tech / bonding curves: mixed.** He was bullish on bonding curves but bearish on Friendtech’s execution later (post:1743555381744308382, post:1786266047613419585, post:1786425490254749905).
- **Onchain options: bearish, dismissive.** He called it the most midcurve narrative for years (post:1761446208168763768).
- **AI memecoins / agents: mixed.** He was early bullish on AI memecoins and AI agents creating currencies and religions (post:1766962991722115434, post:1848454166428926353, post:1861493750029025661). Later he removed Tokenized Agents as a launch option but still found the sector interesting (post:2071995373086806020).
- **Creator Capital Markets / streaming: bullish.** From 2025 he pushed tokenizing creators and streamers as the next unlock (post:1963316363813929427, post:1966877156904251546, post:1967245564506251509).
- **CEX listings: bearish.** He said CEX listings are dead (post:1905773051830239474).
- **Hyperliquid: bullish.** He treated Hyperliquid as a blueprint and later integrated perps (post:1889567334869750260, post:2115447899442045268).
- **Charity coins: bullish.** He called charity coins and donation rails a legit path to institutional onboarding (post:1872306993144443376, post:2049546970646081829, post:2049616841987756265).
