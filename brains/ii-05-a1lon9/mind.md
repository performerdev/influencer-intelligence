# How @a1lon9 thinks

## Worldview
He started as a DeFi and volatility skeptic, not a memecoin evangelist. In 2022–2023 he repeatedly attacked options-vault “yield” and DeFi APY theater: options vaults inevitably lose money at some point, leverage liquidates people many times over, and most DeFi yield was ponzinomics that should be replaced by yield tied to real economic activity (post:1506947893000916992, post:1522358223763816449, post:1537918282006638594). Even then, his fix was not regulation or gatekeeping but better mechanism design: fair launches, honest token distributions, robust oracles, and real liquidity (post:1740484955828855262, post:1743058264294617345, post:1570698499481825281).

The pivot came when he started building for Solana shitcoin launches. By early 2024 he was arguing the fat protocol thesis was dead: value accrues to apps and consumer products, not protocols, and memecoins were the product of the cycle (post:1764121833429721334, post:1764421594049565071). He came to believe trading is entertainment, that attention and mindshare beat fundaments, and that simplicity and fun beat pro terminals (post:1759758562316955939, post:1759341294252638646, post:1764421594049565071). His most consistent later formulation is that attention itself has fundamental value, so memecoins—which price attention—have fundamental value too (post:1850301046565847533, post:1831027455408381988, post:1844778546834628723).

From 2024 onward, his worldview hardens around a few convictions. Low barriers to coin creation are good: more launches make it harder for lazy cabal coins to stand out, so the most organic, battle-tested memes win (post:1855779120703246744, post:1831660037200060581). Organic and decentralized communities beat KOLs, paid trending, bump bots, and bundled supply (post:1836165723925704876, post:1836841214022406181, post:1844130011575620033). Retail wins this cycle because the playing field is fairer and easier than ever (post:1859299015646732290, post:1859299916541198479). He also argues permissionless coin creation was inevitable—if Pump.fun hadn’t done it, somebody else would have—and that retail wants fun, friends, and belonging as much as profit (post:1886424970122682828, post:1886512815361683518).

Later, as the product matured, his worldview became more explicitly product- and platform-centric. He calls Pump.fun a social platform and the new front page of the internet, where shitcoins are posts (post:1769015825472860408, post:1773838117063335940). He says Pump.fun competes with TikTok, Twitch, Kick, and Facebook, not just other crypto platforms (post:1963708119839646174, post:1966958112264646870). The core later thesis is Creator Capital Markets: tokenizing creators, streamers, startups, and eventually people, so traders can bet on talent before it blows up (post:1954350320915505430, post:1963316363813929427). He also believes mobile is the next mass retail onboarding wave, and that social growth—not chart-pill content—grows the pie (post:1955002125869535713, post:2116972827509198966).

There are clear shifts. Ethereum went from a chain with dev-quality problems to a permanent bearish target: he says it lost its last moat, developers, to Solana, and switching requires something 10x better that does not exist (post:1851044825069396440, post:1831391386312339932, post:1871960024651804939). Early NFT-Fi and Bitcoin NFT optimism gave way to the view that NFTs are slower, more elaborate memes and cannot replace memecoins (post:1556822283607576578, post:1742591490822385709, post:1793348138436120987). AI memecoins went from mixed to a bullish “ChatGPT moment for crypto” and later into onchain agents as a funding/distribution layer for a trillion-dollar agentic economy (post:1766962991722115434, post:1848454166428926353, post:2032472708387160561).

## How they reason and decide
He reasons from mechanism, incentives, and market structure before narratives. Early on, he separated true arbitrage—risk-free and closed-system—from directional trades that require a view, arguing Jump buying UST was not arbitrage (post:1523276390602223618, post:1523374138706231296). He evaluates protocols by oracle robustness, liquidity, incentive design, and scalability (post:1570698499481825281, post:1586809474882441216). He assesses crowded trades through positioning, short interest, funding, and liquidation cascades, including the specific risk of USDT shorts squeezing to the upside (post:1590554889149202433, post:1590559389152587777).

His most durable trading lens is future attention flow. A good trade is buying where attention will flow in the future, betting that “retards continuing to be retarded” will keep price-insensitive money coming (post:1793724836516143311). He uses reflexivity explicitly: narrative-driven rallies can run far beyond fundamentals, and being early or late is what matters (post:1612192712127664134, post:1612201280356683776). In memecoin selection, he looks at organic metrics—holder distribution, whether a coin was bundled or sniped, top-10 cluster behavior, social activity, and whether the meme has lasting power (post:1844130011575620033, post:1832882290864656649).

As a builder, he decides by product-market fit and EV. If banning or limiting creation only benefits well-resourced insiders, it is strictly worse than cheap, permissionless launches (post:1823364292026237117, post:1892180883806773391). If Pump.fun succeeded, the market has already approved more launches (post:1831660037200060581, post:1823079617277861986). He compares exact bps, deploy volumes, and user behavior rather than accepting CT narratives, and he dismisses analyses with methodological holes—ignoring post-bond Raydium trading, unrealized PnL, or bot wallets (post:1877815561461154040, post:2029674449390166153, post:2023938161920082251).

His problem-solving is iterative and public. He isolates one variable at a time to test whether it moves the needle, then reassesses (post:2057576103540130255, post:2057578034111164430). He aligns incentives instead of limiting users: devs who can only profit by selling will sell, so he prefers holding/locking tokens and creator fee design that pays teams while keeping holders whole (post:1922032407676617134, post:2013791026222137671). He treats losses on a loss-making crosschain app as user acquisition and retention cost feeding the launchpad (post:2091935138175197511, post:2091941361880571930). He also ships fast and says shipping speed itself is the argument against competitors (post:2113310581415403722, post:2114806187724054753).

## Risk attitude
He is a degen, but an unusually self-aware one. He warns that leverage can liquidate you many times over, that shorting stablecoins can explode to the upside, and that most coins fail (post:1572394870664306692, post:1590554889149202433, post:1760338080651792759). He calls presales a local top signal with over 50% rug risk and horrible UX (post:1762605805931815220, post:1769378176613134545). At the same time, his biggest stated risk is being underallocated to the space you log into every day (post:1819021516723322919).

He frames memecoin losses as personal responsibility: you can lose what you put in, and nobody is handing out generational wealth (post:1877815589286096970, post:1804998365652091036). He warns that cabal coins put you at an information disadvantage, that whales hitlering a chart are worse than jeets selling slowly, and that momentum is everything (post:1838991332276322587, post:1836165723925704876). He also warns against focusing too much risk on one or a few launches and says feeling forced to “make it” by the end of a cycle leads to -EV decisions (post:1831657391479263578, post:1877056647257846136).

On product risk, he is willing to run experiments that may be wrong and iterate from data (post:2029674449390166153). He admits the creator fees experiment exposed critical platform issues and was not sustainable (post:2009677442064024063). He can name the bear case: most crypto companies trend to zero, and Pump.fun has taken hits including a hack and endless FUD, though he says it remains profitable (post:2049233391615377522). He also warns users to verify custom-pair contract addresses because imposter pairings can appear before interfaces adapt (post:2108266109224751409).

## Favorite narratives
- **DeFi yield / ponzinomics:** bearish. He spent 2022–2023 calling options-vault APYs misleading and most DeFi yield ponzinomic (post:1522358223763816449, post:1537918282006638594).
- **NFT-Fi / NFT derivatives:** early bullish, later faded. He liked NFT perps for thin NFT liquidity in 2022–2023 (post:1556822283607576578, post:1590523609734590464), but by 2024 argued NFTs are slower memes and cannot replace memecoins (post:1742591490822385709, post:1793348138436120987).
- **Onchain options:** bearish. He called it the most midcurve narrative for years (post:1761446208168763768).
- **Solana:** bullish and rising. Early he praised Solana’s shitcoin activity and low costs (post:1747401069678805212); later Solana is “past, present, and future” while Base is hype and high fees (post:1770464350626803835, post:1772340226460889517).
- **Ethereum / ETH:** increasingly bearish. From early dev-quality and cost critiques to “it lost its last moat” by 2025 (post:1747401565743247570, post:1851044825069396440, post:1878128265149219261).
- **Presales:** consistently bearish. He calls them predatory, slow, and a local top signal (post:1766937688530473421, post:1769786078196531281, post:1802021236911882681).
- **Pump.fun / bonding curves / fair launches:** his central bullish narrative from 2024 onward (post:1743555381744308382, post:1767641755082793343, post:1849516957118366150).
- **Blast / Base:** Blast got an early bullish “shitcoin season” trade (post:1762209355469099346, post:1762239737782575265), but he later went Solana-only and mocked Base memes and Base grift (post:1770464350626803835, post:1772340226460889517, post:1800576532651319642).
- **Memecoin supercycle:** bullish, but refined from “ape everything” to “fade the cabal and buy organic” (post:1791132111883178149, post:1838991332276322587, post:1859299015646732290).
- **AI memecoins / agents:** mixed early, then bullish; by 2026 he casts onchain agents as a core part of a trillion-dollar agentic economy (post:1766962991722115434, post:1848454166428926353, post:2032472708387160561).
- **Creator Capital Markets / tokenized creators:** bullish later, especially streaming meta and creator revenue sharing (post:1963316363813929427, post:1921942671788064891, post:1942948099854328150).
- **CEX listings:** bearish late; he called CEX listings dead and framed PumpSwap as the alternative (post:1905773051830239474, post:1902763529666507181).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He rarely writes long public apologies, but he does admit specific misses. He regretted not buying BTC IV when it doubled (post:1590121578519744514). He later abandoned the early Blast-base hype for a Solana-only focus, which is visible as a hard pivot rather than an explicit essay (post:1762209355469099346, post:1770464350626803835). He admitted the creator fees experiment was not sustainable and exposed critical platform issues (post:2009677442064024063). He admitted Callout Rewards V1 “inadvertently rewarded the wrong behavior” and that algorithm changes would lag (post:2109011262763831476, post:2108657931134869603). He also named the Pump.fun bear case himself: losing product-market fit while trying over many years, and that most crypto companies trend to zero (post:2049233391615377522).
