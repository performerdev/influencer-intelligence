# How @a1lon9 thinks

## Worldview

@a1lon9's worldview starts from a trader/market-structure skepticism and becomes a builder's thesis about attention, fair launches, and organic communities. In the 2022–2023 notes he is already dismissive of options vaults that quote "yield" while hiding tail losses, and of DeFi yields as ponzinomics (post:1506947893000916992, post:1522358223763816449, post:1537918282006638594). He calls UST short, thinks Jump buying UST was not arbitrage but a directional trade, and says regulation and CEXs are not to be trusted after FTX (post:1590552537283035139, post:1523374138706231296). Even then he sees MEV as a permanent part of the ecosystem that platforms should incorporate rather than ignore (post:1520917936621432832).

The pivot to pump dot fun and fair launches is the defining turn. By early 2024 he argues fair launches and non-predatory tokenomics should be the norm, and that bonding curves are the best mechanism for bootstrapping tiny tokens because orderbooks or self-LPing micro caps have horrible UX (post:1740484955828855262, post:1743555381744308382, post:1754382856380449233). He frames trading as entertainment and a social sport, not a serious endeavor, and says people want to copy-trade people they know and compare with friends (post:1759758562316955939, post:1761065142111142031). Attention and mindshare matter more than the fat protocol thesis; front end and fun are what win (post:1759341294252638646, post:1759343859618787451).

This matures into a full memecoin-supercycle and Solana-dominance stance. He writes that the fat protocol thesis is dead, that memecoins are the product of this cycle, and that Pump dot fun is a social platform where shitcoins are the new posts (post:1764121833429721334, post:1764421594049565071, post:1773838117063335940, post:1769015825472860408). Solana is described as the past, present, and future; Base is hype, grift, and high fees (post:1770464350626803835, post:1772340226460889517, post:1786485881903222884). Anon culture creates the best content, while institutional chains want to doxx you (post:1772685007322042402). Coin creation should be cheap and easy; accessibility grows the top of funnel (post:1767551226026332613, post:1790413302700331115).

By late 2024 the thesis becomes more philosophical: attention is fundamentally valuable, so memecoins have fundamental value; if you believe attention has value, you must believe memecoins do too (post:1850301046565847533, post:1831027455408381988, post:1844778546834628723). Retail wins this cycle because the playing field is fairer, and Ethereum is losing its last moat—developers—to Solana (post:1859299015646732290, post:1851044825069396440, post:1831391386312339932). He insists organic, decentralized communities beat cabals, KOLs, bump bots, and paid promotion (post:1836165723925704876, post:1836841214022406181, post:1844130011575620033). Memes are not a one-cycle phenomenon; good memes with strong communities live forever, like XRP (post:1863715361117368783).

From 2025 onward he defends pump fun as a net positive: it democratized coin creation, made launches safer, and burned hundreds of millions in SOL (post:1881074065860919330, post:1885072431833841874, post:1902908658129715332). Permissionless coin creation is inevitable; if pump fun didn't exist, someone else would have built it because people want to trade culture, events, and ideas onchain (post:1886424970122682828, post:1891593198574895155). Retail users aren't purely financial; they want fun, friends, and fulfillment, so crypto is entertainment as much as investing (post:1886512815361683518, post:1886530165494161645). More coins is good: competition at low market caps means better content and distribution win (post:1883237911593979933, post:1883303993478349018). Traders are the most important user group; creators and devs don't eat if traders don't see value (post:1914421121350570333, post:1914422215585456637).

The most recent 2026 notes add a mature ecosystem view: community is the moat, crypto communities need a home with mass distribution built in, and if Coin Communities win, everyone wins (post:2060410186477572542, post:2060410823491641645, post:2060410830496174106). PVP between coins benefits no one; it slows narratives and pushes people away (post:2071994266239516863, post:2071997306996478158). The Pump fun app should cover everything traders want to speculate on, crosschain, not just pump fun coins (post:2074864951185801486, post:2092613725618356659). A healthy ecosystem is successful tokens that create wealth effects and onboard new people with a positive experience, not just volume (post:2091971489285415228, post:2103284330567029137). Owning the end-user experience is a user acquisition and retention cost that helps the launchpad make more money long-term (post:2091935138175197511, post:2091941361880571930). Rewarding users is the product—callout rewards, holder rewards, creator fees, referrals—make the app the most rewarding on Earth (post:2107907448522821678, post:2111168024065540192, post:2111955227280633987).

## How they reason and decide

His reasoning style mixes market-structure analysis with product-design thinking and incentive critique. In the 2022 UST episode he distinguishes true arbitrage from directional trades: arbitrage is risk-free and closed-system; Jump buying UST was a directional view, not arbitrage (post:1523276390602223618, post:1523374138706231296). He evaluates protocols by mechanism design—oracle robustness, liquidity, incentives, and whether the product can actually scale—and questions altcoin options because liquidity is too thin across many assets (post:1570698499481825281, post:1586809474882441216, post:1586810221233586177). He uses positioning data: short interest, funding, liquidation cascades, crowded trades (post:1594633609868099585, post:1590554889149202433, post:1612202174758494209).

Once pump fun arrives, his reasoning becomes data- and EV-driven. He says to study winners and the numbers rather than narratives, compares EV of launching or trading on pump dot fun versus elsewhere, and insists transparency over everything—show the chain, don't hide behind quick fixes (post:1765456553597980851, post:1769153280297636019, post:1769459502888227244). Quick fixes like max buy limits, dev locks, and sell taxes give false protection (post:1769453529243291815, post:1787972726868541533). Discovery is the core problem, and organic discovery is harder to game than filters; social activity is harder to fake than wallets or charts (post:1783562998356795668, post:1771687748551725165). He frames trades around future attention flow and price-insensitive buyers when a topic gains attention (post:1793724836516143311). He evaluates coins by whether they can retain attention and liquidity, not by roadmap or fundamentals (post:1793348138436120987, post:1799858749236244737).

He reasons from incentives. If bad actors want to jeet, they will jeet; vesting gives an illusion of safety, so better to give information tools (post:1800269674036228360, post:1797311147554574362). Bad actors can always change wallets, so mechanisms to punish them fail; design systems that organically reward good actors instead (post:1832903228159308203, post:1832921519686602792). He uses the "market thinks it's good" test: pump fun got users and became the norm, so the market approved (post:1823079617277861986). He distrusts data that ignores unrealized PnL, post-bonding trading, bots, and farms, and tells people to look through methods and draw their own conclusions (post:1825212830439924203). Later he attacks engagement-farming analyses by pointing out methodological flaws—excluding post-bond Raydium trading, counting bot wallets as users (post:1877815561461154040, post:1877815577646907740).

As a builder, he reasons from product fundamentals and iteration. Build in a problem space you understand deeply, ship as fast as possible to confirm biases, and develop relationships with target users first (post:1882558167722393608, post:1882570288250151234). Align incentives between devs and holders; if devs can only profit by selling, they will sell (post:1922032407676617134, post:1922032406789108275). Contrarian bets pay off; being early and wrong is better than being late and right (post:1923347286471413764, post:1944497868972359702). Focus on growing the pie 10x rather than capturing value; that yields more in the long run (post:1928502626267599108). In 2026 he isolates one variable at a time to test whether it moves the needle, and iterates publicly—ship, take feedback, tweak, ship again (post:2057576103540130255, post:2060410832765227299, post:2110498484088455669). He uses data straight from the source to rebut narratives and points out how charts can be manipulated with median vs average or cherrypicking (post:2108618810420846647, post:2108632868595613789).

## Risk attitude

His risk framing is not generic risk aversion but specific tail and structural warnings. Early on he warns options vault "yields" hide downside and inevitably lose money; calls UST short but warns liquidation cascades to the upside are a primary risk (post:1522358223763816449, post:1590554889149202433, post:1590559389152587777). He says leverage can liquidate you many times over regardless of funding costs (post:1572394870664306692, post:1572395581766766593). He warns presales have a >50% rug chance and bad UX (post:1762605805931815220). He also regrets not buying BTC IV when it doubled (post:1590121578519744514).

The memecoin-era risk view is more social and structural. He warns momentum is everything and a few whales hitlering the chart is more detrimental than jeets selling slowly; always fade the cabal and buy organic (post:1838991332276322587). Buying cabal coins puts you at a disadvantage because you won't know why they go higher or when to take profit (post:1836165723925704876). Focusing on one or a few launches places too much risk on a few coins; balance quantity with collective attention span (post:1831657391479263578). Your biggest risk is being underallocated to the space you log into every day (post:1819021516723322919). Nothing is guaranteed, even buying CTOs from trusted people (post:1817549695495086143). 99.999999% of established coins are a trap; bet on new launches with real momentum and community, hold for 1 hour (post:1819830095839920156).

He holds memecoin risk as personal responsibility: what you put in can be lost, but that's the deal (post:1877815589286096970, post:1842692663155589293). He warns not to take freedoms for granted: centralized L2s can blacklist memecoins and nuke positions to 0 (post:1879038646231429332). On the platform side, he acknowledges legal/regulatory risk is a scaling constraint—hire a Chief Legal Officer to grow massively while mitigating key risks (post:2069815387881939090). Fraud and wash trading are expected attacks; screen for them and design requirements to prevent mass fraud (post:2092664010697011706, post:2111518969324577087). He warns traders to verify the CA of custom pairs after major launches, not trust image/ticker/name (post:2108266109224751409). Crosschain trading at a loss may be cost of doing business if it gives users the best price (post:2091935138175197511, post:2091941361880571930). He is willing to run experiments that may be wrong and iterate based on data, and openly names the bear case: losing product-market fit while trying over many years (post:2029674449390166153, post:2049233391615377522).

He stays bullish even in 2026 (post:1974239667248562452, post:2104306627943796773).

**Ethereum / ETH** — shifts from early ETH-vs-Solana critique to open bearishness, mocking ETH maxis and calling Ethereum a declining culture with high costs (post:1747401069678805212, post:1831391386312339932, post:1881478494074687558, post:1828823579045367907).

**Pump dot fun / fair launches** — the core builder narrative, bullish throughout, but it evolves from fair-launch mechanism to social platform to everything app. Early: bonding curves and $2 launches (post:1740484955828855262, post:1823072046500520361). **Onchain options** — bearish from 2022 on; calls it the most midcurve narrative for years (post:1761446208168763768, post:1586809474882441216).

**NFT-Fi / NFT derivatives** — early bullish, from 2022 through 2023 (post:1556822283607576578, post:1590523609734590464, post:1656283877168562178), but fades as shitcoins take over. He later says NFTs are slower and cannot replace memecoins (post:1742591490822385709, post:1793348138436120987).

**Live streaming / creator economy / creator capital markets** — emerges bullish, from "live streaming shitcoins" to a full thesis that pump fun competes with TikTok, Twitch, and Kick, and that tokenizing creators lets traders bet on talent before it blows up (post:1772065905117147237, post:1963316363813929427, post:1966958112264646870, post:1954350320915505430). **AI memecoins / onchain agents** — early mixed (post:1766962991722115434, post:1787512657224040664), then bullish: AI memecoins are a ChatGPT moment for crypto, and onchain agents will become part of a trillion-dollar agentic economy (post:1848454166428926353, post:1861493750029025661, post:2032472708387160561, post:1882139109575238086). This ties to the larger shift that incentives should favor trading and risk-taking over mass coin deployment (post:2009677442064024063, post:2023781668033802745).

**Holder rewards / custom pairs / reduced PVP** — 2026 narrative. ## Recurring mistakes they admit

He rarely concedes a core thesis, but he does admit specific tactical and product mistakes. He says he regrets not buying BTC IV when it doubled (post:1590121578519744514). He openly admits chasing Blast hype was a mistake: "it was, I openly admit that" (post:1763577656044589501). He jokes about losing money on anita max cock coin and giving up on a bag until seeing a post (post:1762583935408914735, post:1759758562316955939). He says "the market got to my head" and "I take it back" in the voice notes (post:1809677698866008486). He acknowledges the creator fees experiment wasn't sustainable and exposed critical platform issues (post:2009677442064024063). He says "I meant Q4 2026 btw" as a self-correction (post:1945238123908067530). He admits most crypto companies trend to zero and that pump fun has taken hits including a hack and endless FUD but remains profitable (post:2049233391615377522). He says feedback was valid and ships fixes fast, e.g. "you made your feedback clear and we shipped fast" and "we later updated the reward algorithm" (post:2091935138175197511, post:2110498484088455669). In 2026 he admits algorithm changes have lagging effects and historic data takes weeks or months to reflect new incentives (post:2108657931134869603, post:2108632868595613789). The pattern: he admits tactical errors, flawed experiments, and timing mistakes quickly, but defends the overall thesis unless the data forces a redesign.
