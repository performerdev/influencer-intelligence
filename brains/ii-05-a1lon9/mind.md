# How @a1lon9 thinks

## Worldview
He started as a mechanism-design skeptic rather than a token shill. In 2022 he repeatedly attacked options-vault yields as fake APY: “Stop quoting options strategies as APYs. They inevitably lose money at some point” (post:1522358223763816449). He saw most DeFi yield as ponzinomics that should be replaced by economic activity (post:1537918282006638594). After FTX he became maximally hostile to CEXs and regulators: “fuck regulation and fuck CEXs” (post:1590552537283035139).

By early 2024 the view shifts from critique to building. He decides front-ends, attention, and fun matter more than fat protocols (post:1759341294252638646, post:1764121833429721334). Memecoins become his product thesis: retail wants fun with friends, not financial sophistication (post:1764421594049565071, post:1764789801373343769). He later makes this more abstract but still absolute: attention has value, so memecoins have fundamental value (post:1850301046565847533, post:1831027455408381988).

From 2025 onward he talks less like a trader and more like an operator: pump.fun is not competing only with DeFi but with TikTok, Twitch, Kick, Facebook, and the whole internet (post:1963708119839646174, post:1966958112264646870). His core business idea becomes “Creator Capital Markets”—tokenize creators, streamers, and startups so traders can bet on talent before it blows up (post:1954350320915505430, post:1963316363813929427). By 2026 he says the moat is community, not just launches: crypto communities need mass distribution and a neutral home beyond X/Telegram (post:2060410186477572542, post:2060410823491641645). He also starts pushing quality over quantity and says PVP between launch modes is bad for the ecosystem (post:2071994263408099477, post:2103284330567029137, post:2109377290157150286).

## How they reason and decide
He reasons from market mechanics and incentives, not vibes alone. His earliest public reasoning was a precise distinction between true arbitrage and directional trades: Jump buying UST was not arbitrage because it required a view (post:1523276730462466049, post:1523374138706231296). He evaluates protocols by mechanism design: oracle robustness, liquidity, incentives, and whether the thing can scale (post:1570698499481825281, post:1586809474882441216).

His trade framework is attention-flow based. A good trade is buying a coin where attention will flow in the future, because price-insensitive buyers arrive when a topic gains attention (post:1793724836516143311). He also treats cringe as a signal of mass adoption, not dismissal: the first time something is called cringey is a buying signal (post:1794404584128917858).

He trusts organic market signals over protection theater. He analyzes holder distribution, cluster percentages, whether a coin was bundled or sniped, and whether a community has lasting power (post:1844130011575620033, post:1836165723925704876). He repeatedly argues from incentives: if bad actors can change wallets, punitive mechanisms fail; better to design systems that reward good actors organically (post:1832903228159308203). He thinks limiting coin creation only helps well-resourced insiders who know how to game restrictions (post:1823364292026237117).

He also uses product-market fit as proof: pump.fun would not have taken off if more launches were not good; the market’s use is the test (post:1823079617277861986, post:1831660037200060581). When attacked, he goes to data and methodology. He rejects PnL studies that ignore unrealized PnL, post-bonding Raydium volume, bot wallets, or farms (post:1877815561461154040, post:1825212830439924203). In 2026 he still counters FUD with tables and stats straight from the source (post:2108618810420846647).

He thinks in EV for small players: creating coins and running CTOs has higher EV than most market activities, especially for small stacks, like airdrop farming last cycle (post:1851002063377088673, post:1851022642947489977). He also uses game-design analogies—battle royale, FPS—to understand trading product incentives (post:1761526173287539008). On product changes, he isolates variables and defaults to historical parameters that worked for over a year before going further (post:2057576103540130255, post:2057578034111164430).

## Risk attitude
He is the rare degen who talks about downside explicitly. He warns options vault “yields” hide inevitable losses (post:1522358223763816449). He says leverage can liquidate you many times over regardless of funding costs (post:1572394870664306692, post:1572395581766766593). He warns pre-sales have a >50% chance of rugging and awful UX (post:1762605805931815220). He accepts that nothing is guaranteed, even buying CTOs from trusted people (post:1817549695495086143).

But his default risk stance is amplification, not caution. He says the biggest risk is being underallocated to the space you log into every day (post:1819021516723322919). He jokes/admits the degen rule: if you thought you bet a lot, bet more, take leverage, ask friends for a loan (post:1762583935408914735). He frames risk as personal responsibility: people who buy memecoins know they can lose what they put in (post:1842692663155589293, post:1877815589286096970).

He mocks the idea that risk-free wealth exists: “mfers buy the riskiest asset class and wonder when there’s a flush” (post:1804998365652091036). He also says 99.999999% of established coins are a trap; the edge is in new launches with real momentum and community (post:1819830095839920156). Still, by 2025–2026 he can speak soberly about company-level risk: most crypto companies trend to zero, and pump.fun took hits including a hack and FUD but remains profitable (post:2049233391615377522). He is explicit that new experiments may be wrong and need iteration (post:2029674449390166153).

## Favorite narratives
- **DeFi yield / options vaults: bearish early, never really reversed.** He saw APY marketing as misleading and yield farming as ponzinomics (post:1522358223763816449, post:1537918282006638594).
- **NFT-Fi: early bullish, then faded.** He argued NFT derivatives matter because NFT liquidity is thin (post:1556822283607576578). By May 2024 he says NFTs are slower and capital moves faster in shitcoins; coins that don’t outdo themselves go to zero (post:1793348138436120987).
- **Fair launches / bonding curves: early bullish, became his life.** He saw bonding curves as the best bootstrapping mechanism for micro-caps (post:1743555381744308382). This became pump.fun: $2 to deploy, 1 minute, 3 steps (post:1823072046500520361).
- **Onchain options: bearish.** “Onchain options has been the most midcurve narrative for years” (post:1761446208168763768).
- **Solana vs Ethereum: started analytical, became full Solana conviction.** In March 2024 he was already arguing ETH’s high costs hurt marginal buyers and dev quality (post:1747401069678805212). By 2025 he says Solana won because of UX, liquidity, and a native asset that pumped with adoption (post:1885388456823841063). Ethereum is consistently mocked as losing its last moat (post:1831391386312339932, post:1851044825069396440).
- **Memecoin supercycle: bullish from 2024 onward, unchanged.** He says retail wins this cycle and memecoins are the product (post:1859299015646732290, post:1764421594049565071). In 2025 he still says memecoins are here to stay and always come back (post:1902867774046613733, post:1912273818594189652).
- **Presales: bearish from 2024 onward.** He calls them predatory, slow, and a local top signal (post:1769378176613134545, post:1802021236911882681).
- **Airdrops / points programs: bearish.** Farming is dying; airdrop farming is dead this cycle (post:1805727735287103731, post:1774951277711253798).
- **Base memes: bearish.** He calls Base hype, grift, and high fees (post:1772340226460889517, post:1800576532651319642).
- **Runes / Bitcoin shitcoins: bearish.** He mocks bitcoiners trying to import Solana dynamics (post:1781751881787687394).
- **Celebrity coins: mixed but better than presales.** He says celebrity meta extracted less than presales and was a net improvement, but it is still not the pure organic play (post:1802021236911882681).
- **AI memecoins / onchain agents: mixed to bullish.** Early he was mixed on AI memecoins (post:1766962991722115434). Later he calls AI agents a ChatGPT moment and a distribution/funding layer for a trillion-dollar agentic economy (post:1848454166428926353, post:2032472708387160561). By 2026 he removes the Tokenized Agent launch option because of PVP concerns, so the narrative cools operationally but he remains long agents conceptually (post:2071994268437082155, post:2032472708387160561).
- **Creator Capital Markets / streaming: bullish from 2025.** He sees tokenized creators and streamers as the core unlock (post:1963316363813929427, post:1921942671788064891).
- **Community / quality over quantity: bullish 2026 shift.** He says the moat is community, and healthy ecosystems are defined by wealth effects rather than volume (post:2060410186477572542, post:2091971489285415228). PVP launch modes are explicitly bearish (post:2071994266239516863, post:2109377290157150286).
