# How @MustStopMurad thinks

## Worldview

His starting position was Bitcoin maximalism. He treated Bitcoin as digital gold, a Neo-Gold standard, and argued its core proposition was not quick transactions but being the best store of value (post:937401943147864066, post:985335763205283841, post:990242033112682497). He described cryptocurrencies as money-religions and private central banks competing in a Darwinian/Hayekian free market, producing 1–4 winner-take-most winners rather than 10,000 coexisting tokens (post:941500735560343552, post:990701454087413760, post:1055010807153598464). Money trends toward one due to monetary network effects and liquidity (post:1055011032081580033, post:1055065511514529792). He also saw Bitcoin as inevitable, unseizable, uncensorable, and a force that would make wars and surveillance much more expensive (post:985335762421010434, post:985335766275502081, post:1000892680275136512). "Everyone in the world will become a Bitcoin Maximalist whether they like it or not; resistance is futile and financially dangerous" (post:1028354752747327488).

By 2018–2023 he widened the monetary-history lens: money is a ledger, all money is a kind of Excel spreadsheet, and gold won because of stock-to-flow ratio rather than use value (post:1089237815437668352, post:1077645412897615872, post:1080203704941375493). Bitcoin monetizes through collectible → store of value → medium of exchange → unit of account (post:1081056089855143936, post:1081056500850835456, post:1070401154482389003). At the same time he admitted most people do not care about decentralization, self-sovereignty, privacy, or sound money; they care about getting rich quickly, and speculation is crypto's biggest use case (post:1092191814428360711, post:1092191815065837568, post:1092191815615369216).

That admission became the bridge to his major pivot. Around 2023–2024 he began saying crypto has three core use cases—money, payments, gambling—plus a long tail of bullshit, and that memecoins are the purest, most honest corner of crypto while 99.9% of everything else pretends to be honest but is a gigantic grift (post:1783698268423147968, post:1789302483388399975). He now says "It was never about the tech; it's always been about the pump" (post:1808514638859981193, post:1808535413432205662). Tech altcoins, in his telling, have always been memecoins pretending they are not; pure memecoins rip the bandaid off, have less tech/hack/execution risk, and no VC unlocks (post:1810666229973004506, post:1818745900036243790, post:1822664714163212635, post:1830349804716912895).

From 2024 onward he frames memecoins as financialized mini-religions and cults. Community is the product and the fundamentals (post:1812143581580124186, post:1815750884069933556, post:1847290720400351564, post:1850885603316867229). The demand driver is social: crisis of meaning, loneliness, loss of identity from AI and job displacement, and declining fertility push people to belong to something bigger than themselves (post:1812582844553912584, post:1813214771967058146, post:1816805875924766767, post:1819001253197664761). Cults must emerge bottom-up from poor people becoming rich; they cannot be manufactured top-down (post:1787549737953976789, post:1787535855218823332).

By 2025–2026 this hardens into a thesis that belief is the most important fundamental in economics, and the top blockchain use case is creating financial Schelling points where quality people converge (post:1932081604530495925). Finance and spirituality are merging into one (post:1935049402382417995, post:1955288533297426609). The next trillion-dollar asset will not be a company; it will be a community or movement valued by pure belief rather than cashflow (post:1932881804949651636, post:1945935544958984460, post:2094833446556152178). AI/AGI will destroy jobs, freeze social mobility, collapse profit margins, and make human labor obsolete, so belief, community, and spirituality gain a massive premium (post:1941910752580735007, post:1960069461916180902, post:2000273651619557701, post:2013965766123819162). Assets that do not depend on profits—BTC monetary goods and cults/insane communities—are the ones that will not be hypercommoditized to zero (post:1972755956711965146, post:1999574063107264769, post:1968794077727494512).

In the most recent material (2026-09 to 2026-11), he says you are not investing in coins, memes, or projects; you are investing in communities of people, and the community is the asset (post:2105997468903088370, post:2114027958100705716, post:2118348926986104936). Tokenized communities, movements, and religions will be a >$70 trillion market; the next trillion-dollar asset will be a decentralized crypto community with zero revenue (post:2115488825439076802, post:2119024636092395596, post:2115469707734347893). Post-AGI, humans will do what the children of the top 0.1% do: arts, causes, status games, spirituality (post:2116172713702101436, post:2114059925148021044). The crypto bull cycle, he insists, has not even begun; this one will be the biggest and most concentrated in history (post:2112527077840982404, post:2115856008270528547, post:2117216792674255102).

## How they reason and decide

Early reasoning was monetary and historical. He evaluated store-of-value candidates on security, censorship-resistance, immutability, disinflationary monetary policy, credibility of monetary policy, distribution, and privacy (post:1009801247090774016, post:1009936111089668097, post:1009354979281338369). He used market-cap thresholds: BTC needed $3T+ to be a store of value, $10T+ to be a good medium of exchange, $15T+ to be a good unit of account (post:977587904670322689, post:981255850005336064). He reasoned that blockchains destroy revenues rather than generate them; decentralization leads to disaggregation and no leech points to extract value (post:965820295503339520, post:1020754672569790465, post:1054286569392300032).

In 2018–2023 he became more of an indicators-and-timing trader, combining MVRV, NVT, CVDD, Reserve Risk, NUPL, stablecoin/total ratio, moving averages, diagonals, Gaussian supports, RSI, orderbooks, and funding rates (post:1544304436171907073, post:1544717652219797504, post:1543247456800055299, post:1546137511738613761, post:1071095842000515072). He is contrarian: buy when 65% of CT influencers call for a crash, sell when normies ask about XRP/IOTA (post:1164642144482713606). He cycles through fractal and cycle-length analysis, adjusts price for M1/M3 money supply, and argues cycles are lengthening (post:1084199413202018304, post:1096051705832181760, post:1539645197872762882, post:1673334040739364867). He prefers waiting for breakouts from bearish channels or key support zones before adding rather than catching knives (post:1540318524622737408, post:1560572784505339906, post:1161037640675799042).

From 2023 onward his decision-making shifts to community and on-chain social metrics. He buys memecoins where price is down and holder count is up, treating dip buyers plus resilient holders as bullish divergence (post:1789285793090990305, post:1788166599612022950). He uses BubbleMaps and HolderScan to check distribution and avoid concentrated supply (post:1809917942236172371, post:1790359427347845621). He judges coins by surviving multiple -70% drops and long sideways accumulation under $10m while the community still vibes—battle-tested equals safer (post:1811552486274085248, post:1814285849753763970, post:1828434323436921141, post:1837461598858105281, post:1839643583348982125). He also leans on "feeling the vibes" and intuition over quantitative valuation; authenticity and emotional resonance are key metrics (post:1811870495161135111, post:1816973644695757000, post:1827716192053973292).

He uses reflexive and game-theory logic: when he gets disproportionately more hate or ad-hominem, he takes it as evidence the painful scenario is more probable (post:1802077727639392556). He asks which coins inspire holders to hustle for free, because free cult labor is the growth engine (post:1818366712355733965, post:1826335817268429280, post:1868626774957187443). He asks whether a coin could become a global movement that millions identify with and whether enthusiasm will last years (post:1818764690425582048, post:1823856712132288520, post:1828886520637644915).

By 2025–2026 the reasoning is explicitly belief-versus-trade. He says trading is harder than ever—10,000 memecoins/day, 43-second average holding time, institutions only on BTC—while holding is easier because only a handful of top-100 coins beat BTC (post:1942948976610685386, post:1954286761024323640, post:1959986822127165926). His core filter: only buy a coin that already made a critical mass of poor people rich and they are still holding, survived 3+ 70% drops, and has a big inspiring mission (post:2010806643039355006). He asks what today is like BTC in 2011, and which coin will inspire movies to be made about it (post:2028150914197127182, post:2102376622074806289).

By late 2026 he says the biggest gains come in about 10 days a year, and only multi-year holding positions you for them; step-wise bursts happen overnight (post:2115436639208067419, post:2116883161183383847, post:2117888829990342793). He categorizes people into gamblers/traders, holders of consensus assets, and holders of non-consensus assets—only the third outperforms (post:2114789183814328794, post:2119764395769155634). He reads industry behavior, like VCs dropping "crypto" from fund names, as a cycle-bottom signal, and models consensus shifts through Stage 1 to Stage 7 (post:2113254118374465543, post:2117488129422541172).

## Risk attitude

Early on he warned most tokens are scams that will collapse, and non-BTC altcoins had not yet seen deep capitulation (post:943963229532950529, post:1038092379918675968, post:1044809720081633282). He expected a harsh bear market with total market cap bottom below $100B in the first half of 2019 (post:1039917243696644097, post:1052611501813510145). He also noted the risk of new technologies emerging and Bitcoin's social contract collapsing (post:970504359736946689, post:977590170122932225). But he prescribed calm and stoicism during the bear market (post:1055558974387470336, post:1063166894600019968).

In 2018–2023 he managed risk with cash tranches, no leverage on spot buys, and warnings about overexposure—if a chart makes you angry, you're overexposed; if you're overexposed, you're impatient (post:1538256335137034241, post:1594768604939317248, post:1694354911385854103). He admitted he gets a lot wrong and said paper numbers are not real because not everyone can withdraw at the top (post:1232181959454629, post:1075745935395823617). He also warned the macro environment could affect BTC differently than ever before (post:1096797523065348104, post:1096797542849880071).

From 2023–2024 his risk focus turns to memecoins: long-term stagnation is the real risk, not daily price (post:1696202072582959546, post:1696202074160087053). Max pain is ETF rejection or approval with underwhelming flows, a deflationary recession breaking the halving cycle, and heavy Fed regulation (post:1733556734323507528). He says every hard landing looks like a soft landing at first (post:1738146109061558446). He is against going all-in, against knife-catching, and warns that gambling on new memecoins is increasingly -EV while buying culty midcap category leaders that survived -70% drops is +EV (post:1804153144818930061, post:1805187864075891084, post:1790341286471348562, post:1789647774113210686). He says 95% of your profits will come from doing nothing, and the most important skill in crypto is to be able to HODL (post:1789046659965452453, post:1787612015965421817). New launches, presales, celebrity coins, cabal bundles, and PolitiFi are traps designed to extract money (post:1818729896283095248, post:1818982808439718253, post:1821586375713644692, post:1825979631574593853).

By 2024–2025 he is explicit: no leverage, no margin, no debt, no pair trading; the only way is spot, HODL, DCA, belief (post:1977015649978425759). He says 97% of traders lost money over the past year, and gambling/trading are negative-EV (post:2100277714737389753, post:2098004237636530343). He recommends cold storage, avoiding daily devices, avoiding links, updating macOS/iOS, and avoiding portfolio trackers and PnL-checking (post:1963984517116379141, post:1959262878290059549, post:1817609992486179022). He says the number one mistake people make is that they cannot hold (post:1940473255808364584, post:2093322467477537261).

In 2026 he says trading grinds you to zero and starts a gambling spiral; impatience is the main destroyer; joining the wrong memecoin will eventually mean poverty versus trillions; zero exposure to the thesis is irrational (post:2114376902655795454, post:2113581624239731092, post:2115720071503106551, post:2119887448167571949). He also warns hacks are at an all-time high from human-guided AI swarms (post:2114319722380489025).

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He is not shy about saying he gets calls wrong. In the middle period he said "I get a lot of shit wrong" and "That was a mistake," and told people not to blindly listen (post:1232181959454629, post:1166171924642312194). He also said not everyone can withdraw paper profits at the top (post:1075745935395823617). His preferred way to bound error is an invalidation attached to a scenario: "where am I wrong" (post:1696202079549714462). Later he rarely concedes in the moment; he reframes dips as tests of conviction, uses phrases like "I could be very wrong on this…" without dwelling, and says delay is accumulation: "It always takes a little longer than you think. And always goes way higher than you think." The one recurring mistake he genuinely warns against is the same mistake he attributes to everyone else: trading instead of holding, and selling too early (post:2095211000928575517, post:2094749731616346341).
