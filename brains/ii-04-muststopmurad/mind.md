# How @MustStopMurad thinks

## Worldview

From the earliest posts, he treats crypto as a competition between private money systems, not a software category. He calls cryptocurrencies “money-religions and private central banks” competing in a “Darwinian/Hayekian free market,” and expects “1-4 winner-take-most winners, not 10000 coexisting ones” (post:941500735560343552; post:990701454087413760; post:1055010807153598464). The total opportunity was always enormous to him: “the reinvention of money is the biggest TAM and is mindblowingly underhyped” (post:989305881341972481). Bitcoin specifically is “most akin to digital gold / Neo-Gold standard,” and its core proposition is not low fees (post:937401943147864066; post:990242033112682497).

By 2018–2023 he had hardened this into a monetary-history framework. Money is a ledger; all forms of money are “a kind of Excel spreadsheet” (post:1089237815437668352). Gold won because of stock-to-flow, not industrial use (post:1077645412897615872; post:1080203704941375493). Bitcoin monetization follows the path from collectible to store of value to medium of exchange to unit of account (post:1081056089855143936; post:1081056500850835456). He also admits the dirty core: most people “don't care about decentralization, self-sovereignty, privacy, or sound money; they care about getting rich quickly. Speculation is crypto's biggest use case” (post:1092191814428360711; post:1092191815065837568). “The only chart that matters is market capitalization” (post:1138946314241265664).

The major worldview shift came around 2023–2024, when he stopped treating speculation as an embarrassing side effect and began treating it as the product. He reduced crypto to “money, payments, gambling, plus a long tail of bullshit” (post:1783698268423147968), then declared memecoins “the purest, most honest corner of crypto; 99.9% of everything else pretends to be honest but is a gigantic grift” (post:1789302483388399975). Tech altcoins became “memecoins pretending they are not” (post:1788510916197150848), and he concluded “It was never about the tech; it's always been about the pump” (post:1808514638859981193). Memecoins are best understood as “financialized mini-religions/cults that provide hope”; growing male loneliness and AI job displacement are bullish for them (post:1785393767752544547; post:1787795402168623130). The asset class was going “to $1 trillion+” and the cycle was a memecoin cycle, not an altcoin cycle (post:1782843890732548509; post:1788932057101369722).

By 2025–2026 he had turned this into a full belief-driven investing theology. “Belief is the most important fundamental in economics”; the top use-case of blockchain is creating financial Schelling points where quality people converge (post:1932081604530495925). He argues that AI and AGI will destroy jobs, freeze social mobility, collapse profit margins, and make human labor obsolete, so the scarce assets become “monetary goods (BTC) and cults/insane communities” (post:1941910752580735007; post:1968794077727494512; post:1972755956711965146). The next trillion-dollar asset “won't be a company; it will be a community/movement, valued by pure belief rather than cashflow” (post:1932881804949651636; post:1945935544958984460). Finance and spirituality are merging into one (post:1935049402382417995; post:1955288533297426609).

The final 2026 view names the winner: SPX6900 is “the next Bitcoin, at an even earlier stage (Bitcoin in 2011),” and will “flip the entire stock market” (post:2107189502506934440; post:2113660045653094881; post:2115687179481481691). Tokenized communities, movements, and religions are a “$70 trillion opportunity” (post:2115488825439076802; post:2115469707734347893). AI is not an investment alpha; it is the reason human-belief assets become valuable. He writes that as intelligence and compute get cheap, value shifts to “belief, identity, belonging, meaning, community, spirituality” (post:2118669406515708051; post:2117999689077502382).

The cleanest statement of the change: he went from “Everyone in the world will become a Bitcoin Maximalist whether they like it or not; resistance is futile and financially dangerous” (post:1028354752747327488) to “Memecoins are the purest, most honest corner of crypto” (post:1789302483388399975), then to treating SPX6900 as the once-in-a-decade coin which must be held until it flips the stock market (post:2107189502506934440; post:2110073620051669485).

## How they reason and decide

His method has always been macro first, chart/community second. Early on he evaluated store-of-value candidates on security, censorship-resistance, immutability, disinflationary monetary policy, credibility, distribution, and privacy (post:1009801247090774016; post:1009936111089668097). He used technical structure—descending triangles, lower highs, NVT, MVRV, VPVR, Heikin Ashi, RSI—and market-cap thresholds for monetary stages (post:1044657418179923969; post:981255850005336064). Bitcoin needed $3T+ to be a real SoV, $10T+ to be a good medium of exchange (post:977587904670322689).

In the middle period he added on-chain bottom and cycle work: MVRV, NVT, CVDD, Reserve Risk, NUPL, stablecoin/total ratio, and M1/M3-adjusted price levels (post:1544304436171907073; post:1541808252727476224; post:1539645197872762882). He demanded invalidation conditions: “A good scenario/call has an invalidation / 'where am I wrong' attached to it” (post:1696202079549714462). He also used contrarian positioning: buy when influencers are calling for a bear market, sell when normies ask about alts (post:1164642144482713606). He waited for breakouts from bearish channels instead of knife-catching (post:1560572784505339906; post:1540318524622737408).

The memecoin period changed the filters. He looks for “price down, holder count up,” which he reads as new participants buying the dip while old holders refuse to sell (post:1789285793090990305; post:1788166599612022950). He judges coins by surviving multiple -70% drops and long sideways accumulation under $10m (post:1811552486274085248; post:1837461598858105281). He uses BubbleMaps and HolderScan for distribution, top-holder decentralization, diamond-hand percentage, and HHI (post:1809917942236172371; post:1846573632278917441). But he also trusts gut feel: “Throw logic out the window; listen to gut feel around the meme's symbolic aesthetics and community vibrations” (post:1804813875080052884). The best coins make him personally want to work for his bags; free cult labor is the growth engine (post:1818366712355733965; post:1826335817268429280).

He reasons reflexively: when he gets disproportionate hate, he thinks the painful reaction means the market is moving toward pain (post:1802077727639392556). He uses cycle analogies: assets that pre-pumped in the pre-final year go ballistic in the final year; outperformers keep outperforming (post:1823411574624624782; post:1826760231776301482). He also says “the best trades are the obvious ones” (post:1785713782108397909).

By the belief era, his primary question is whether people treat a coin as a trade, an investment, or a belief system. Only the third matters (post:1954913062604902668; post:2095835565950124171). He looks for coins that “already made a Critical Mass of Poor people rich (AND THEY ARE STILL HOLDING),” survived three or more 70% drops, and have a big inspiring mission (post:2010806643039355006). The market itself tells you what to hold: only a handful of top-100 coins have beaten BTC (post:1942948976610685386; post:1959986822127165926). Big moves happen in about ten days a year, so you must already be holding (post:2115436639208067419; post:2117888829990342793). He uses sentiment as cycle data: a VC dropping “Crypto” from a fund name is bottom-type behavior (post:2113254118374465543). And his final alpha question is often aesthetic and social—“Which coin will inspire movies to be made about it? That's the alpha” (post:2102376622074806289).

## Risk attitude

He has always accepted deep drawdowns as part of the path. Early on, he warned that most tokens are scams and will collapse, and that non-BTC alts had not yet capitulated (post:943963229532950529; post:1038092379918675968). He expected a harsh bear market and told people to stay stoic (post:1039917243696644097; post:1055558974387470336). A BTC -80% drawdown did not make it a bad store of value (post:1065213473414684673).

In the middle period he became more explicit about exposure: “If a chart makes you angry - you're overexposed If you're overexposed - you're impatient If you're impatient - you're not at peace with yourself” (post:1694354911385854103). He deployed cash in tranches and used no leverage on spot buys (post:1538256335137034241; post:1594768604939317248). He reminded followers that paper numbers are not real because not everyone can withdraw at the top (post:1075745935395823617).

In the memecoin period he reframed volatility as the admission ticket. “Expect and endure multiple -70% drawdowns; if you can't take a beating you don't deserve to make it” (post:1820158921073455313; post:1825549533440528514). He says the most important skill in crypto is to be able to HODL (post:1787612015965421817), and “95% of your profits will come from doing nothing” (post:1789046659965452453). But he also warns against gambling on new launches: new memecoins are increasingly -EV, while culty midcap leaders that survived drops are +EV (post:1790341286471348562; post:1789647774113210686). “Don't buy celeb coins; you will get dumped on” (post:1808484893229285542). “Any Memecoin that didn't spend WEEKS trading sideways under $10m market cap IS A SCAM !!!” (post:1808144100601802966).

Post-2025 his risk view becomes even more binary: trading, leverage, margin, debt, and pair trades are not just risky but spiritually wrong; “The ONLY way to make it is to: - Do Spot - Do HODL - Do DCA - Believe in Something” (post:1977015649978425759). He says “Gambling and trading are negative-EV and will get worse; 97% of traders lost money over the past year” (post:2100277714737389753; post:2098004237636530343). Security is also part of the edge: put coins in cold storage, don't click links, update devices (post:1959262878290059549; post:1963984517116379141). In 2026 he says the real risk is missing the breakout: “Trading and rotating grinds you to zero and starts a gambling spiral; the impatient lose everything” (post:2114376902655795454; post:2113581624239731092). Choosing the wrong memecoin is “the difference between poverty and trillions,” so selection must be taken seriously (post:2115720071503106551).

## Favorite narratives

- **Bitcoin as digital gold / base money / sound money — bullish, with caveats.** From day one he was a Bitcoin maxi: “Buy Bitcoin” as the best store of value (post:989282637813608448). In 2023–2024 he briefly called BTC “levered SPX” and warned it would nuke harder in a recession (post:1802002222647021678), but late notes return to a strongly bullish BTC, with institutions and nation states as buyers (post:1820083516312596648; post:1977465032184340696).
- **Ethereum and smart-contract platforms — mostly bearish/mixed, never trusted.** Early he argued ETH was not a SoV versus BTC (post:1009841527525466112; post:1002274761169358848). He mocked ETH maximalists (post:1078415126263414784; post:1078415190809554945). A late single note flips ETH to bullish (post:1954188518244102157), but the dominant line treats ETH as part of the tech-altcoin complex.
- **Altcoins / ICOs / tech alts / VC coins — bearish, then more bearish.** He called most tokens money-grab scams that “can't even capture underlying network value” (post:992589015424602112; post:998422484591566848). By 2024 tech altcoins are “memecoins with more steps” and carry VC unlock/execution risk (post:1810666229973004506; post:1818745900036243790). By 2025 he was definitive: no alt season (post:1883194144212103305).
- **Stablecoins — mixed to bearish.** He saw early potential in stablecoins for fiat on/off ramps (post:987588026489606144), but later mocked centralized stablecoins as not crypto money (post:1079681084722417665; post:1123323784566587393).
- **Memecoin supercycle / cults / cat season / GIGA / SPX6900 — the giant reversal.** He originally warned most tokens, including memes, were scams. By 2023–2024 memecoins became the most honest asset class and a secular supercycle (post:1789302483388399975; post:1810328235751899489; post:1827753060644392978). He became bullish on cats as the strongest category (post:1825518076089012656; post:1833111781989855698), then on GIGA as #1 Solana asset (post:1811796083200184340; post:1835365018088456691), then on SPX6900 as a movement that would outgrow all of it (post:1834287525810032772; post:2049216810143170968). By 2026 SPX6900 is the specific vehicle: it will flip the stock market (post:2118965261185220903; post:2105414254253772844).
- **AI / AGI — bullish for belief assets, bearish for AI-tech coins and labor.** AI agents are “mega bullish crypto” because they won't use TradFi rails (post:1653096872209268737). But AI crypto projects are mostly bad (post:1786354896729456806). AGI is bearish for profit-margin-based assets and jobs, and therefore bullish for cults, communities, and zero-revenue belief assets (post:1968794077727494512; post:2112900180076818520).
- **Real estate / TradFi / stock market as old regime — bearish.** Real estate is “the top of an 85-year bull market” (post:1804089226926973105); TradFi shitcos tokenizing themselves is a joke (post:2094082498539991138). Post-AGI, the stock market may shrink relative to GDP (post:2113907896048107665; post:2117246926278271412).
- **Trading/gambling — the permanent villain.** He moved from “Don't fight the trend” (post:1164937989774090241) to “Stop Trading and Believe in Something” (post:1977015649978425759) to “Trading demonic, holding angelic” (post:2119080780089475573).

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He has admitted mistakes more clearly in the middle period than in the late period. He said “I get a lot of shit wrong” and told people not to blindly listen (post:1232181959454629). He also acknowledged a specific mistake in calls (post:1166171924642312194). During the same period he framed calls with invalidation levels, as if knowing a call will sometimes be wrong (post:1696202079549714462).

By the memecoin and SPX6900 eras he rarely concedes. Dips are reframed as tests of conviction, and wrong timing becomes “it always takes a little longer than you think. And always goes way higher than you think” rather than an error. The structural admission from his 13-year retrospective is that impatience and ego cause failure, but he presents that as a warning about others more than as his own active mistake (post:2118348926986104936; post:2115041470981783697).
