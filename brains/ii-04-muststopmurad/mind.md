# How @MustStopMurad thinks

## Worldview
In the earliest available posts (2017–2018), @MustStopMurad was a Bitcoin-maximalist macro thinker. He saw cryptocurrencies as "money-religions and private central banks competing in a Darwinian/Hayekian free market" (post:941500735560343552, 990701454087413760). The outcome would not be 10,000 coexisting coins, but one to four winner-take-most monetary networks (post:1009936103783231488, 1055010807153598464). Bitcoin's proper analogy was digital gold or a Neo-Gold standard, not a fast payment rail (post:937401943147864066, 985335763205283841, 990242033112682497). He argued that the reinvention of money was "the biggest TAM" and "mindblowingly underhyped," eventually worth tens of trillions (post:937405934992752647, 989305881341972481, 993906998898036736). His worldview was explicitly monetary: inflation hurts the poor and benefits the rich; central banking and QE increase inequality (post:978278879168270339, 978278955101970432, 1061335863022534656). Scarce money would make war and surveillance more expensive, producing an era of peace and prosperity (post:985335762421010434, 985335766275502081, 1021770172636581888). He even described Bitcoin as a living hivemind and HODL as an emergent supply-reduction battlecry (post:994227059223343105). Any cryptocurrency pre-sold to investors had, in his view, already failed (post:1062385597841588224).

By 2024–2025, the worldview pivoted hard from sound-money fundamentalism to memecoins and belief. He described a "Memecoin Supercycle" in which memecoins would outperform everything, including altcoins (post:1843730412608335942, 1850556028774944812, 1851024284405166280). The strongest value driver was no longer monetary policy or utility, but community, belief, and cult-like conviction (post:1847290720400351564, 1864011427184259286, 1865009332309442840). He framed memecoins as a generational escape from a broken economic system, especially for young people (post:1851751444224843844, 1857135703530131928, 1871525406748402148). He also argued that AI/AGI would destroy traditional opportunities, making human connection and belief-based assets more valuable (post:1859963549881401470, 1910343480708112713, 1919480036865851825). The next trillion-dollar asset, he said, "won't be a company; it will be a community/movement, valued by pure belief rather than cashflow" (post:1932881804949651636). Finance and spirituality were merging into one enormous market (post:1935049402382417995, 1955288533297426609). In this view, assets that do not depend on profits—monetary goods like Bitcoin and cults or insane communities—are the ones that will not be hypercommoditized to zero (post:1972755956711965146, 1968794077727494512). The system itself is broken: inflation, unaffordable housing, dead social contracts, and rising inequality make SPX6900 an "escape vessel" or "lifeboat" for the left-behind 95% (post:2012933575662846382, 2013234486860038346, 2013588961877192729, 2015861365077098835). By 2026, this became even more compressed: you are not betting on coins, memes, or projects; you are betting on communities of people (post:2105997468903088370). SPX6900, he wrote, is the next Bitcoin (post:2107189502506934440).

The change is stark but he frames it as continuity: memecoins are "more Bitcoin-aligned than altcoins" and a return to cypherpunk values (post:1845222827395268648, 1853771878805975131, 1931409154238816583). He remained bullish on Bitcoin, but the center of gravity moved from sound money to social belief.

## How they reason and decide
Early reasoning was historical and monetary. He used the evolution of gold, silver, and fiat to reason about crypto winners (post:985871914034958337, 1009936071982047233, 1019913276690452482). Store-of-value candidates were judged on security, censorship-resistance, immutability, disinflationary monetary policy, credibility, distribution, and privacy (post:1009801247090774016, 1009936111089668097, 1009354979281338369). He set market-cap thresholds: Bitcoin needed roughly $3T to be a store of value, $10T to be good money, and $15T to be a unit of account (post:977587904670322689, 981255850005336064). He also used technical analysis—descending triangles, lower highs, NVT, MVRV, VPVR, Heikin Ashi, RSI (post:1044657418179923969, 1045358253570490370, 1052611501813510145, 1046454739687395328). His monetary network-effect logic said money trends toward one in a free market because of liquidity and network effects (post:1055010807153598464, 1055011032081580033, 1055065511514529792). He used game theory to think about state adoption versus attack on Bitcoin (post:1060416367906734085, 1060416767611297792).

Later reasoning shifts from monetary history to community analytics. He evaluates memecoins by community strength, holder growth, and decentralization metrics such as HHI and median holder rank (post:1841890262664020193, 1853107626550165929, 1862190404960915538). He uses on-chain tools like BubbleMaps and HolderScan to assess distribution and conviction (post:1846573632278917441, 1848739241729118225, 1857468156748718328). He identifies winners by watching which coins bounce hardest from BTC lows and hold up during dips (post:1869810281192534039, 1873425736671047724, 1877316705363329216). "Critical mass" is his tipping-point concept for exponential community growth (post:1867541129149001985, 1873056068013629554, 1890139777418043425). He asks which coins inspire holders to hustle and contribute for free, using that as a leading indicator (post:1868626774957187443, 1879171272195670394, 1897663441013272969). He studies past cycles and coins that survived multiple -70% drawdowns (post:1846265044201263517, 1853464536554058075, 1902726387200360457). By 2025–2026, he frames a simple filter: ask whether people treat a coin as a trade, an investment, or a belief system—and hold the belief-system ones (post:1954913062604902668, 2095835565950124171). Trading is harder than ever, he argues, while holding is easier than ever because the market is concentrating into a handful of outperformers (post:1942948976610685386, 1959986822127165926). His most repeated later question is: "what is today like what BTC was in 2011?" (post:2028150914197127182). He looks for the hardest asset no one understands yet, something still weird, risky, and easy to laugh at (post:2103822735780598021, 2046695932964565088). Attention is easy and short-lived; belief is hard and long-lasting, so he bets on retention and community expansion (post:2085456786488742313, 2096569748070842499).

## Risk attitude
His stated risk attitude changed from bear-market stoicism to anti-trading conviction. Early on, he warned that most tokens were scams and would collapse, and that non-BTC altcoins had yet to experience deep capitulation (post:943963229532950529, 1038092379918675968, 1044809720081633282). He acknowledged Bitcoin's brutality: an -80% drawdown did not make it a bad store of value (post:1065213473414684673). He expected a harsh bear market, with a total-market-cap bottom below $100B in the first half of 2019 (post:1039917243696644097, 1052611501813510145). He also noted the risk that new technologies could emerge and Bitcoin's social contract could collapse (post:970504359736946689, 977590170122932225). His remedy was calm and stoicism (post:1055558974387470336, 1063166894600019968).

In the memecoin era, he still warns that most coins will fail. He cautions against new launches and low-cap coins: most will go to zero (post:1843342211548615090, 1867963021068571095, 1873767417886957635). He warns that over-rotating and trading will lead to losses, and that patience is the edge (post:1861383125542068456, 1868657286497100060, 1877778698654372158). He says only a handful of cults will succeed (post:1869025177839001744, 1870870469530075607, 1872645827384287254). He also warns repeatedly about scams using his name and fake accounts (post:1842163979168215427, 1843234809520509275, 1924472787277578583). By 2025–2026, his risk rules are explicit: no leverage, no margin, no debt, no pair trades; only spot, HODL, DCA, and belief (post:1977015649978425759). He insists on cold storage and warns against phishing and social engineering (post:1959262878290059549, 1963984517116379141). Gambling and trading are negative-EV, he argues, citing that 97% of traders lost money over the past year (post:2100277714737389753, 2098004237636530343). In the latest 2026 notes, risk is reframed entirely as conviction: "Stop Trading and Believe in Something" will end up being the winning strategy (post:2105752918020300964). He posts extreme bullishness with no hedging or downside scenario (post:2108245104918188110).

## Favorite narratives (stance, and how it changed)
- **Bitcoin as digital gold / SoV:** Bullish from the earliest posts and still bullish later. Early: Bitcoin is digital gold, not a cheap transaction system; it needs to be a store of value first (post:937401943147864066, 985335763205283841, 1009801247090774016). Later, he still lists Bitcoin as bullish (post:1855588509303591291, 1864640324867383653, 1977465032184340696, 2051741031830347871). By 2026, he claims SPX6900 is the next Bitcoin (post:2107189502506934440), which is a major extension, not a rejection, of the Bitcoin narrative.
- **Ethereum and smart contract platforms:** Bearish early. In 2018 he posted "Next Target? $95" for ETH (post:1038498085322870786). He argued ETH was inferior to BTC as a store of value (post:1009841527525466112, 1009936097261080576, 1035604840175558656). By 2025–2026, Ethereum is marked bullish in the notes (post:1954188518244102157), but his public emphasis remains against utility tokens and tech projects (post:1932418070309380375, 2030013311899816297, 2063346047779221921). The change is real but not central to his later output.
- **Altcoins / ICOs:** Bearish early and bearish later, with a crucial distinction. Early: most tokens were financing grabs and would collapse (post:992589015424602112, 1038092379918675968). Later, he still says "no alt season" and mocks altcoins (post:1883194144212103305, 1846600450188951830, 1847321499272655236). Memecoins are not grouped with altcoins in his thesis; they are a separate, superior category.
- **Stablecoins:** Mixed early; he discussed them but did not make them core (post:967120275379716096, 987588026489606144, 1021732508579377153). They largely disappear from the later narrative.
- **Zcash:** Mixed early; skeptical as a store of value relative to Bitcoin (post:1038457990226108416, 1038458106504785920). Not mentioned in the later notes.
- **Memecoin Supercycle:** The defining later narrative. In 2024–2025 he says memecoins are the main narrative of the cycle and will outperform everything, including altcoins (post:1841206683600879674, 1843730412608335942, 1845105397662646470, 1874093522833596905). He says they are a new asset class heading global and mainstream, potentially trillions in market cap (post:1844420641551941670, 1853184235705622821, 1882399777876529319).
- **SPX6900 / SPX:** Bullish, escalating over time. It starts as one of several cult memecoins and becomes the center of his entire thesis. By 2025–2026 he says it is an escape vessel for the 95% (post:2012933575662846382, 2013234486860038346) and the next Bitcoin (post:2107189502506934440). In 2026, SPX6900 is the "Once-in-a-Decade Asset" (post:2106418748181598418) and will become one of the biggest assets in all of finance (post:2105414254253772844).
- **Other cult memecoins:** He was bullish on GIGA, POPCAT, APU, MOG, RETARDIO, and HarryPotterObamaSonic10Inu in 2024–2025 (post:1842272082794782724, 1842285788702089695, 1848870442997846030, 1841554446666391768, 1852071507805233284, 1861809326320407010). By 2025–2026, SPX6900 dominates and these others fade from his notes, though there is no explicit retraction.
- **AI / AGI disruption:** Bearish. He argues AI will destroy jobs, freeze social mobility, and collapse profit margins, making belief, community, and spirituality more valuable (post:1941910752580735007, 1960069461916180902, 2013965766123819162).
- **Trading / gambling:** Bearish, especially later. Trading is harder than ever and is negative-EV; the market "transfers wealth from degens to the non-degens" (post:2104629190712860827). He contrasts this with HODLing and belief.
- **Gold and silver:** Bullish later, alongside Bitcoin, as monetary goods that do not depend on cashflow (post:2016987646183297323, 1972755956711965146).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
There is almost no direct "I was wrong" in the available notes. He does not explicitly admit a recurring personal trading or investment mistake. The closest indirect admission is a shift in framework: the early technical-analysis and altcoin-capitulation posts give way to a later stance that trading and gambling are negative-EV and that the number one mistake people make in crypto is failing to HOLD (post:1940473255808364584, 2093322467477537261). He warns that over-rotating and trading will lead to losses (post:1861383125542068456, 1877778698654372158), and he says the market transfers wealth from degens to non-degens (post:2104629190712860827). Those posts function as implied corrections of a trading-era mindset, but he does not name himself as the cautionary example. When drawdowns happen, he reframes them as tests of conviction and patience rather than mistakes (post:1869715696319651893, 1878782415294157285, 2106118634271875534).
