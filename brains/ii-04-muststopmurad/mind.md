# How @MustStopMurad thinks

## Worldview
The account starts in 2017 as a Bitcoin-first monetary maximalist and keeps that base, but the center of enthusiasm shifts hard. Early posts treat crypto as a Darwinian/Hayekian market of “money-religions and private central banks” where only 1–4 winners survive, not 10,000 coexisting ones (post:941500735560343552; post:990701454087413760). Bitcoin is digital gold / Neo-Gold, not a cheap-fast-payment network (post:937401943147864066; post:985335763205283841), and money trends to one via liquidity and monetary network effects (post:1055010807153598464). Most tokens were designed as “quick financing money grabs” and cannot capture underlying network value; any presold cryptocurrency “has already failed” (post:943963229532950529; post:992589015424602112; post:1062385597841588224). He also sees fiat central banking as regressive: inflation disproportionately hurts the poor and benefits the rich, and QE widens inequality (post:978278879168270339; post:1061335863022534656).

By the 2018–2023 notes the framework becomes more analytical but still Bitcoin-centric. Money is a ledger; all money is a kind of Excel spreadsheet, and crypto offers stronger guarantees (post:1089237815437668352). Gold won because of stock-to-flow, not use value; Bitcoin replaces gold as base money and its volatility declines with size and distributed ownership (post:1077645412897615872; post:1067989979433963520; post:1080037386774593536). The only chart that matters is market capitalization (post:1138946314241265664). At the same time he admits most people do not care about decentralization, self-sovereignty, or sound money; they care about getting rich quickly, so speculation is crypto’s biggest use case (post:1092191814428360711; post:1092191815065837568).

The largest change comes from 2023 onward. He starts saying crypto has three core uses: money, payments, gambling, plus a long tail of bullshit (post:1783698268423147968). Memecoins become “the purest, most honest corner of crypto”; 99.9% of everything else pretends to be honest but is a gigantic grift (post:1789302483388399975). Tech altcoins are just “memecoins pretending they are not”; it was “never about the tech; it’s always been about the pump” (post:1788510916197150848; post:1808514638859981193). By 2024–2025 he frames memecoins as financialized mini-religions/cults, with demand driven by male loneliness, meaning crisis, and AI job displacement (post:1785393767752544547; post:1787795402168623130). By 2025–2026 he generalizes further: belief is the most important fundamental in economics; blockchains create financial Schelling points where quality people converge; the next trillion-dollar asset is a community/movement priced by pure belief, not cashflow (post:1932081604530495925; post:1932881804949651636). AI/AGI destroys jobs, freezes social mobility, and collapses profit margins, so the only safe assets are monetary goods like BTC and cult-like communities (post:1968794077727494512; post:1941910752580735007). The system is broken—inflation, unaffordable housing, dead social contracts, rising inequality—and SPX6900 is the escape vessel/lifeboat for the left-behind 95% (post:2012933575662846382; post:2013588961877192729). By late 2026 he is explicit: investing is really backing communities of people, almost all memecoins are “the literal Devil,” and the winning community “will flip the stock market” (post:2105997468903088370; post:2110819059990442175).

## How they reason and decide
Early method is historical and criteria-based. He reasons from monetary evolution—gold, silver, fiat—and evaluates SoV candidates on security, censorship-resistance, immutability, disinflationary monetary policy, credibility, distribution, and privacy (post:985871914034958337; post:1009801247090774016; post:1009936111089668097). He uses market-cap thresholds for monetary function: BTC needs ~$3T+ to be a good SoV, $10T+ to be good MoE, $15T+ to be good UoA (post:977587904670322689; post:981255850005336064). He also uses technicals—descending triangles, lower highs, NVT, MVRV, VPVR, Heikin Ashi, RSI—and game theory for state adoption vs attack (post:1044657418179923969; post:1045358253570490370; post:1060416367906734085).

In the 2018–2023 period he combines on-chain and market indicators—MVRV, NVT, CVDD, Reserve Risk, NUPL, stablecoin/total ratio—to find bottoming zones and DCA levels (post:1544304436171907073; post:1541808252727476224; post:1542538420857315330). He is contrarian: buy when the majority is bearish, sell when normies ask about alts (post:1164642144482713606). He waits for confirmation—a breakout from a bearish channel or a reclaim of key range—rather than knife-catching (post:1560572784505339906; post:1805187864075891084). He adjusts price for money supply and says timing/execution matter more than charting or prediction (post:1539645197872762882; post:1694835507799228860).

In the memecoin era, reasoning becomes social and reflexive. He judges communities by holder count up while price is down, diamond-hand percentage, top-holder decentralization/HHI, and battle-tested survival through multiple -70% drops (post:1789285793090990305; post:1814305122131849630; post:1832094540196688193). He uses BubbleMaps/HolderScan to avoid concentrated supply (post:1846573632278917441; post:1809917942236172371). He trusts vibes and emotion: if something in markets disgusts you at first glance, look closer; memecoins need to make you feel something (post:1807716526407221562; post:1808567795317362927). He treats hate/ad-hominem as a signal that a painful scenario is underweighted and therefore more likely (post:1802077727639392556). Alpha comes from small accounts and trench warriors, not rich whales or cocktail-party elites (post:1811052597186318525; post:1815042200087994757). He asks which coins already made a critical mass of poor people rich who are still holding, survived 3+ 70% drops, and have a big inspiring mission (post:2010806643039355006). The key question later is “What is today like what BTC was in 2011?” and “Which coin will inspire movies to be made about it?” (post:2028150914197127182; post:2102376622074806289). A good scenario/call must have an invalidation attached (post:1696202079549714462).

## Risk attitude
He has always treated drawdowns as part of the process: BTC down -80% from ATH did not make it a bad store of value (post:1065213473414684673). By the memecoin era he says you must expect and endure multiple -70% drops; if you cannot take a beating you do not deserve to make it (post:1820158921073455313; post:1825549533440528514). But he avoids catastrophic tools: no leverage, no margin, no debt, no pair trades, no shorting; only spot, HODL, DCA, belief (post:1977015649978425759; post:1821662983166292372). He deploys cash in tranches and says do not go all-in; wait for sideways and re-accumulate slowly and patiently (post:1594768604939317248; post:1804153144818930061). He warns that overexposure makes you angry, impatient, and not at peace with yourself (post:1694354911385854103). He emphasizes operational safety: no portfolio trackers, no wallet glancing, cold storage, and avoiding phishing/social engineering (post:1817609992486179022; post:1963984517116379141; post:1959262878290059549). He also warns against gambling on new launches, celeb coins, cabal bundles, and microcap dogshit (post:1808484893229285542; post:1818729896283095248; post:1843342211548615090). He is honest that he gets things wrong and others should not blindly follow (post:1232181959454629). Later he says trading/gambling is increasingly negative-EV; 97% of traders lost money over the past year (post:2100277714737389753).

## Favorite narratives
- **Bitcoin as digital gold / base money**: bullish from the first batch through the end. Early: it is the best store of value and the neo-gold standard (post:937401943147864066). Later: it remains the base money and the institutional bid while retail rotates to memecoins (post:1855588509303591291; post:1864640324867383653). He is still long, but no longer a Bitcoin-only allocation voice.
- **Altcoins / tech / VC coins**: bearish throughout, with the reasoning shifting. Early: most altcoins are scams and will collapse (post:1038092379918675968; post:1044809720081633282). Later: tech altcoins are memecoins with more steps, VC unlocks, hack/execution risk; there is no broad alt season and only a handful of top coins beat BTC (post:1810666229973004506; post:1883194144212103305; post:1959986822127165926).
- **Ethereum**: early he argued ETH is not a valid SoV vs BTC (post:1009841527525466112), and in 2024–2025 he remains bearish on ETH-BTC (post:1784955160189374735). One later note flips posture to “bullish” for Ethereum (post:1954188518244102157), so the view is not perfectly stable.
- **Memecoins**: the biggest view change. In 2018 he writes “shitcoinery” with disgust (post:987292694190882816); by 2023–2024 he is the “Memecoin Supercycle” bull, saying the asset class goes to $1T+ and runs 5+ years into 2029 (post:1786436598357205189; post:1782843890732548509). By 2025–2026 he says most individual memecoins are scams/devil, but a handful of true cults absorb the value (post:2110819059990442175).
- **Cats / POPCAT, GIGA, SPX6900**: bullish with increasing specificity. POPCAT as cat king, GIGA as #1 Solana asset with target raised, then SPX6900 as hyperstition/movement coin targeting the stock market and eventually $69T (post:1804153135587307943; post:1835365018088456691; post:2100420046522642908).
- **AI**: early he says AI agents with internet access are “mega bullish crypto” because they will not use TradFi accounts (post:1653096872209268737). By 2024–2025 he turns bearish on AI crypto projects and says AI/AGI hyper-commoditizes software while making belief/community assets more valuable (post:1786354896729456806; post:1941910752580735007).
- **Stablecoins**: early mixed, later bearish on centralized stablecoins (post:967120275379716096; post:1079681084722417665).
- **Real estate**: bearish, top of an 85-year bull market; buy what Zoomers and Gen A will buy, not what Boomers will sell (post:1804089226926973105).
- **Trading/gambling**: bearish as a practice; he wants people to stop trading and believe in something (post:1942948976610685386; post:1977015649978425759).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
Early he is unusually direct: he says he gets a lot wrong and tells people not to blindly listen (post:1232181959454629); he acknowledges mistakes in calls (post:1166171924642312194). Later he builds invalidation into scenarios precisely because calls can fail (post:1696202079549714462). Still, after the memecoin turn he rarely recants; he reframes delays as accumulation or conviction tests—raising targets or saying it always takes longer than you think—rather than marking a thesis wrong (post:1835365018088456691).
