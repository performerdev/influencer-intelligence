# How @MustStopMurad thinks

**Worldview**

The account began as a Bitcoin maximalist with a Hayekian/Darwinian view of money. He described cryptocurrencies as "money-religions and private central banks" competing in a free market that would produce one or a few winners, not 10,000 coexisting tokens (post:941500735560343552, post:990701454087413760, post:1055010807153598464). Bitcoin was digital gold / Neo-Gold, not primarily a quick cheap payments system (post:937401943147864066, post:985335763205283841). He believed the reinvention of money was the largest total addressable market and "mindblowingly underhyped" (post:937405934992752647, post:989305881341972481). Inflation and QE were central villains because they "disproportionately hurt the poor and benefit the rich" (post:978278879168270339, post:1061335863022534656). That early frame was moralized: sound money would force deeper due diligence and make wars and surveillance expensive (post:1114204688545669125, post:985335762421010434).

In the middle period he formalized money as a ledger (post:1089237815437668352) and a monetization ladder: collectible → store of value → medium of exchange → unit of account (post:1081056089855143936, post:1081056500850835456). He argued gold won because of its stock-to-flow ratio, not its industrial use, and that Bitcoin's volatility would decline as market cap and distributed ownership grew (post:1077645412897615872, post:1080037386774593536). A key shift appeared, though: he admitted most people don't care about decentralization, self-sovereignty, privacy, or sound money—they care about getting rich quickly, and speculation is crypto's biggest use case (post:1092191814428360711, post:1092191815065837568). That was the bridge to the memecoin turn.

From late 2023 onward he rewrote his own thesis. Crypto now has "three core use cases: money, payments, gambling, plus a long tail of bullshit" (post:1783698268423147968). Memecoins are "the purest, most honest corner of crypto," while 99.9% of everything else pretends to be honest but is a gigantic grift (post:1789302483388399975). Tech altcoins are "memecoins pretending they are not" (post:1788510916197150848); "it was never about the tech; it's always been about the pump" (post:1808514638859981193). The social driver was a crisis of meaning, male loneliness, declining fertility, and AI job displacement, which made people want to belong to something bigger than themselves (post:1785393767752544547, post:1812582844553912584, post:1816805875924766767). Wealth that would have gone to VCs flows to memecoiners instead (post:1788932057101369722). This became the Memecoin Supercycle thesis: a permanent secular shift, not a fad, with the asset class going to $1T+ (post:1782843890732548509, post:1810384045400010789, post:1845105397662646470).

By 2025-2026 the worldview became fully belief-driven and explicitly anti-cashflow. The next trillion-dollar asset "won't be a company; it will be a community/movement, valued by pure belief rather than cashflow" (post:1932881804949651636, post:1945935544958984460). Finance and spirituality are merging into one (post:1935049402382417995). AI/AGI will destroy jobs, freeze social mobility, and collapse profit margins, making belief, community, and spirituality more valuable (post:1941910752580735007, post:1968794077727494512). The system is broken for young people—unaffordable housing, dead social contracts, inflation—and SPX6900 is the "escape vessel/lifeboat for the left-behind 95%" (post:2012933575662846382, post:2013588961877192729). By late 2026 he flatly predicted SPX6900 will flip the stock market and Bitcoin (post:2105414254253772844, post:2110073620051669485). This is the clearest worldview change: from "Bitcoin is the one winner" to "belief-based community assets, especially SPX6900, are the one winner."

**How they reason and decide**

Early reasoning was analytical and historically grounded. He evaluated store-of-value candidates on security, censorship-resistance, immutability, disinflationary monetary policy, credibility, distribution, and privacy (post:1009801247090774016, post:1009936111089668097). He used market-cap thresholds: BTC needed roughly $3T to be a good SoV, $10T to be a good MoE, $15T+ to be a good UoA (post:977587904670322689, post:981255850005336064). He used technical analysis—descending triangles, lower highs, NVT, MVRV, VPVR, Heikin Ashi, RSI (post:1044657418179923969, post:1045358253570490370, post:1052611501813510145). He reasoned that money trends to one in a free market because of network effects and liquidity (post:1055010807153598464, post:1055065511514529792). He also argued blockchains destroy revenues rather than generate them, which made most tokens poor value-capture vehicles (post:965820295503339520, post:1054286569392300032).

In the 2018-2023 period he added on-chain and macro filters: MVRV, NVT, CVDD, Reserve Risk, NUPL, stablecoin/total ratio, and M1/M3-adjusted prices (post:1544304436171907073, post:1544717652219797504, post:1539645197872762882, post:1541808252727476224). He combined multiple technical confluences before acting (post:1071095842000515072, post:1124752236008153088). His most explicit contrarian rule was to buy when "65% of CT influencers calling for an e c h o b u b b l e" and sell when normies ask about alts (post:1164642144482713606). He waited for breakout confirmations rather than knife-catching (post:1540318524622737408, post:1560572784505339906). He also said timing and execution matter more than charting or prediction (post:1694835507799228860). Early in this period he attached invalidation levels to scenarios, asking "where am I wrong" (post:1696202079549714462). That habit faded as he moved into conviction investing.

From 2024 onward the method became community-centric and intuitive. He used on-chain metrics like MarketCap-to-Holders, diamond-hand %, holder growth, top-holder decentralization, and HHI to confirm "vibes" (post:1814305122131849630, post:1823378013544669475, post:1832094540196688193). He required that a coin survive multiple -70% drops and long sideways accumulation under $10m as proof of battle-testing (post:1811552486274085248, post:1828434323436921141, post:1846265044201263517). He explicitly said to "throw logic out the window" and listen to gut feel around a meme's symbolic aesthetics and community vibrations (post:1804813875080052884). He sought "free cult labor": coins that make him personally want to work for his bags (post:1818366712355733965). Alpha comes from small accounts and trench warriors, not rich whales or cocktail-party elites (post:1811052597186318525, post:1814009729397764409). He also treated hate and ad-hominem responses as a signal that his outlined scenario was becoming more likely, because painful emotional reactions mean unwillingness to consider the scenario (post:1802077727639392556).

By 2025 he asked whether a coin was treated as a trade, an investment, or a belief system—and to hold only the belief-system ones (post:1954913062604902668, post:2095835565950124171). His later filters were: already made a critical mass of poor people rich who are still holding, survived 3+ 70% drops, and a big inspiring mission (post:2010806643039355006). He asked "what is today like what BTC was in 2011?" and looked for assets that still seem weird, risky, and easy to laugh at (post:2028150914197127182, post:2103822735780598021). He increasingly reasoned by narrative inevitability rather than data: SPX6900 will flip markets because once it is large enough, portfolio managers will rotate from stocks into it, a "self-fulfilling prophecy" (post:2109046871557656956, post:2110073620051669485).

**Risk attitude**

Early on he was explicitly macro-bearish: most tokens are scams, non-BTC altcoins had not yet capitulated, and he expected a bear-market bottom below $100B total market cap (post:943963229532950529, post:1038092379918675968, post:1039917243696644097). Yet he advised staying calm and stoic during drawdowns (post:1055558974387470336, post:1063166894600019968). He acknowledged risk from new technologies and a BTC social-contract collapse (post:970504359736946689, post:977590170122932225).

In the middle period he managed risk with cash tranches ("bullets") and refused leverage on spot buys (post:1538256335137034241, post:1594768604939317248). He warned that overexposure causes anger and impatience, and that one should find peace before trading with a large percentage of net worth (post:1694354911385854103). He noted paper numbers aren't real and not everyone can withdraw profits at the top (post:1075745935395823617). He also warned the macro environment and a global financial crisis could affect BTC differently than prior cycles (post:1096797523065348104). He said "don't blindly listen; I get a lot of shit wrong" (post:1232181959454629).

The later periods turned risk management into self-discipline and operational security. He said the most important skill is HODLing and 95% of profits come from doing nothing (post:1787612015965421817, post:1789046659965452453). New memecoin launches are increasingly negative-EV; established culty midcaps that survived -70% are +EV (post:1790341286471348562, post:1789647774113210686). He warned: no leverage, no margin, no debt, no pair trades; only spot, HODL, DCA, belief (post:1977015649978425759). He said 97% of traders lost money over the past year and gambling/trading will get worse (post:2098004237636530343, post:2100277714737389753). The number one mistake is being unable to HOLD (post:1940473255808364584, post:2093322467477537261). By 2025-2026 he added operational risk: use cold storage, don't store coins on daily devices, don't click links, update MacOS and iOS (post:1959262878290059549, post:1963984517116379141). Risk became less about market drawdowns and more about overtrading, gambling, and phishing.

**Recurring mistakes they admit**

In the 2018-2023 period he admitted error directly: "I get a lot of shit wrong" and "That was a mistake" (post:1232181959454629, post:1166171924642312194). He said don't blindly listen because he gets a lot wrong (post:1232181959454629). As he shifted to memecoin/SPX conviction, explicit admissions became rare. The later notes say he "rarely admits being wrong, doubles down on belief and patience, framing dips as tests of conviction." He used phrases like "I could be very wrong on this…" but usually reframed delays as "it always takes a little longer than you think" rather than admitting a flawed thesis. That is a notable change in how he reacts when wrong.
