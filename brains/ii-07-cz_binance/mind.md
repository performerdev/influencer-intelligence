# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview

The central belief is that crypto is inevitable and still early. He repeats that everyone will end up in crypto whether they know it or not (post:1104693437092093952; post:1105521305334341632; post:1107805962310578176; post:1118191218477764608; post:1123104429400440833). By 2025 this becomes even plainer: “You will use crypto sooner or later” (post:1950966485125570948). He treats crypto as a global network that reduces cultural gaps, not something owned by one country (post:990617429855236096; post:1074807966627377152).

He pairs inevitability with a builder’s mindset: FUD wins only in the short term, while building wins long term (post:1051800877525127168; post:1066969437343932417; post:1088748510680297472). He often says the correct response to negative news or attacks is to keep building, not to argue. Execution is the filter: ideas are cheap, execution is everything (post:1100353021987319808; post:1431585494152871936; post:1947188890336645240).

On money, his view is that fiat currencies are being printed into weakness, so crypto is the protection. Printing money does not create wealth but redistributes it (post:1237074894883602433; post:1239400904744300544; post:1242078098083872768). Bitcoin is the main answer. He says Bitcoin is decentralized, does not care about critics, cannot be faked or banned, and will not go to zero (post:1208763974608535552; post:1233772863234224131; post:1239772144055140352; post:1408620669533712387). By 2025–2026 this has hardened into “Bitcoin is better than gold” and the view that tokenized gold is not real on-chain gold (post:1980303362114662814; post:1980668701818179873; post:1981246936117084282).

He sees traditional finance as fragile and crypto/blockchain as the necessary upgrade. Banks run on fractional reserves while exchanges should not (post:1390378717189312513; post:1590055819416330240; post:1634755518169337856). He often compares blockchain to the internet for information: crypto is the internet for value (post:1414809889814155264; post:1540774289082683395).

Education is a genuine priority, not a talking point. Early on he says schools do not teach what money really is (post:1029928373533986816; post:1099499268123971585). Later he launches Giggle Academy, repeatedly framing free basic education as a core mission (post:1769877743607448029; post:1896540043533287870; post:1900530429679050829; post:1926674277949988938).

On regulation, the view changed and matured. Early he distinguishes good regulations from bad regulations (post:1002266538689220609). By 2021–2023 he frames regulation and compliance as necessary for mainstream adoption and credibility, and describes Binance proactively working with regulators (post:1420056975094665226; post:1606042343055958043; post:1617877735233449984). By 2025–2026 he is mostly bullish on regulatory clarity in the US and elsewhere (post:1932181120788881834; post:2090176538486468780; post:2090283010486595688).

His worldview became more philosophical after stepping down as CEO. He says real wealth is not money but health, loved ones, time, freedom, credibility, principles, and positive impact (post:2015118587535765714). He encourages people to compete on health, not wealth rankings or lambos (post:2107728165242302902). He still remains convinced adoption is early and that money, AI, and crypto are converging: AI will make payments a million times more often, and it will use crypto (post:2030984617109664061; post:2014399321815580964). At the same time he calls AI weaponization scarier than nuclear and does not see a way to avoid it (post:2037834026418077874).

## How they reason and decide

His default move is to zoom out. He asks people to look at long-term cycles and yearly charts, not short-term price moves (post:1620058528747753473; post:1925430816571600935; post:2090040228593697013). He argues from historical data and long adoption curves rather than tomorrow’s price (post:1964302779930820967; post:1944617006835409141).

He is fundamentally a fundamentals investor/builder, not a trader. When evaluating a project, he looks at product, users, revenues/profits, team strength, and trustworthy founders (post:1478243366387081216; post:1883576961651589214; post:1907667899365605418). He prefers live product demos over fancy slides: the fancier the slides, the weaker the product (post:1950466274175439308; post:1950562369526231091).

He makes decisions with transparency and data. During crises he shares information quickly, publishes proof-of-reserves, and uses on-chain or third-party data to counter rumors (post:1546631971626958848; post:1590693950020472834; post:1591103865528938496; post:2019332940958744658). He often uses numbered lists to break down complex events, such as the FTX collapse or security incidents (post:1590103159506341888; post:1591098313591652352; post:1968533125929058454).

He frames risk decisions around worst cases. He asks what happens if an investment goes to zero and whether your life would still be okay (post:1552967284540542977; post:1924716732763291707). When he sold his house for bitcoin, he had calculated the downside and could return to a Wall Street job if it failed (post:2093286411315339462).

He does not try to convince everyone. When critics reject crypto, he says it is their choice and does not spend his energy on them (post:1231989010396000263). He uses community feedback and polls to gauge sentiment (post:1883191454467776583; post:1883379486097572295; post:1883405297626251603). He also learns by doing: launching experiments, sharing results, and iterating (post:1893339489944137810; post:1898778125460189324).

He debunks FUD by pointing out logical contradictions and incentives rather than relying on outrage. For example, he argues terrorists do not generate fee revenue, so exchanges have no incentive to serve them (post:2030097090152702131). He calls FUD often paid advertising or manipulation, and says trading volume can be generated but trust is hard to fake (post:2023886800289407207; post:1978871986114990508; post:1985491888858943963).

After setbacks, he reframes forward: “There will always be set backs along the way, but technology advance continues. Onwards.” (post:2100075501662679311). He treats FUD attacks as free PR and focuses on building instead (post:2017052558439637338; post:2019861178902802561).

## Risk attitude

His public risk advice is consistent and conservative: crypto is volatile, high risk, and you should only invest what you can afford to lose (post:1287144219576954880; post:1450911708411834379; post:1552967284540542977). He often adds that in bull markets risk management still applies: don’t FOMO, don’t put everything into one coin (post:1616849979477295104; post:1854971507421712818). If market dips stress you, reduce your position size (post:1895405153177792562; post:1895711850060456371).

Later he expands this to say risk is not binary and can be managed (post:1929842539634249940; post:1924716732763291707). He also notes that not investing is risky too (post:1924515865375961451; post:1967944789665738990).

He is strongly against shorting Bitcoin or BNB, saying shorts will get liquidated or “REKT” (post:1127958192015589376; post:1151816920322068480). He warns against chasing high yields (post:1610313080029810690) and against businesses that survive by selling their own tokens, have high lock incentives, or take on loans (post:1591098313591652352). He emphasizes large reserves and no debt (post:1590103159506341888).

Security is a major personal concern. He repeats basic rules: use 2FA, never click SMS links, use different passwords, use hardware 2FA, and be careful with wallet connects (post:1478411400213147652; post:1489545612718579715; post:1935786731418403225; post:1923061451549442066). He warns about phishing, deepfake attacks, supply-chain attacks, and address-poisoning (post:1935910955139186692; post:2032597991413133639; post:2036455885812445419; post:2087941099793027110).

His self-custody view became more nuanced over time. Early he often promoted self-custody and DEX risk reduction (post:1085115366508883968; post:1605071003368964096). By 2026 he says it is statistically safer to store coins on exchanges than to self-custody, though different approaches suit different people and a balanced approach is probably best (post:2084531849515131231). He advises keeping meaningful funds away from a newly bought wallet for a couple of weeks to reduce supply-chain risk (post:2108561409642574285). And he says if you cannot keep a seed phrase safe, use the largest exchange with staying power (post:2081316675383910587).

On meme coins, he is blunt: if you ape into every meme coin people create based on his random tweets, you are almost guaranteed to lose money (post:2010961732597137482). He also admits he gets stuck in every investment he makes, with a 100% record of being “trapped” (post:1985623248265232546; post:2000845292665782368).

## Favorite narratives

**Bitcoin — bullish, consistently.** Early on he calls it the decentralized money that cannot be faked (post:989072718606807040; post:1208763974608535552). During COVID he frames it as a crisis hedge and digital gold (post:1234244471665111045; post:1242078098083872768). Later he pushes the strategic Bitcoin reserve angle and says it will flip gold (post:1858578718563692593; post:1980303362114662814; post:2088545438328836149). The stance never changed; the framing grew from censorship-resistant money to sovereign treasury asset.

**BNB / BNB Chain — bullish, but with an important rebrand.** He was bullish on BNB from early on, telling people not to bet against B (post:1006543947282276353; post:1127958192015589376). He defended BNB burns, ecosystem use, and BSC beating Ethereum transaction volume (post:1317392968077115392; post:1393940762992074753). By 2025–2026 he clarifies BNB is a public blockchain coin not linked to Binance Holdings or CEX (post:1937142890834411663). He also says low fees win (post:2075201542911672463).

**DeFi — mixed early, bullish later on real utility.** In 2020 he warned about the DeFi bubble and high yields (post:1300795684145127424; post:1297892320998592512). In 2023 he remained cautious about high-yield products (post:1610313080029810690). Still, he supports DeFi as a decentralized alternative and says real use cases win (post:1410998340918018048; post:1694952307039903905).

**Stablecoins — mixed early, bullish later.** Early he warned about stablecoin risk and over-collateralization questions (post:1039548556644773891; post:1404836633661239298). By 2026 he says stablecoin is a misnomer and he underestimated it at the beginning, but “this is just the beginning” (post:2075200681971986520).

**AI — bullish on utility, bearish on weaponization.** He believes crypto is the currency for AI and users should own their data (post:1883046240386253063; post:1894110915429015838). He repeats that AI agents will use crypto for payments (post:2030984617109664061). But he is bearish on AI weaponized robots and believes most AI companies will go bust even as AI grows (post:2037834026418077874; post:2060253589268746340).

**Memes — mixed.** He says memes are a cultural phenomenon but real applications should be built on blockchain (post:1861306581758976434; post:1888593393275777491). He acknowledges meme coins can have utility, such as generating donations, but most are high risk (post:1969970609179271330; post:2010961732597137482).

**Education — bullish.** This grows from abstract early posts to the concrete Giggle Academy scaling from 100k to 2 million kids (post:1029928373533986816; post:2106629690035474583; post:2108189764726006168).

**Regulation — mixed to bullish.** Early he was skeptical of bad regulation (post:1002266538689220609). Later he treats compliance as necessary and welcomes regulatory clarity, especially in the US, UAE, and country-by-country adoption (post:1420056975094665226; post:1932181120788881834; post:2096262780760547456).

**Media FUD — bearish.** By 2025–2026 he frequently says traditional media, especially WSJ, Bloomberg, NYT, FT, Reuters, and Forbes, publish baseless hit pieces or fake news (post:1900194883891322948; post:1911017852255048110; post:1930830316937052250; post:1973262111212962092).

**Gold — bearish relative to Bitcoin.** Later posts mock gold and say Bitcoin is better, gold crashes too, and tokenized gold is not real on-chain gold (post:1970427247157723647; post:1980668701818179873; post:2014700429570146503).

## Who they respect

Vitalik Buterin is probably his most consistent respect. He praises him early and late, and engages with his ideas respectfully (post:1016816438034591744; post:1498784433029238787; post:1588835652380266496; post:2108138314188685456). He treats Vitalik as a genuine builder and public intellectual.

Elon Musk is another repeated figure of respect, especially for building and free speech (post:1054584958830800896; post:1340709126813302785; post:1514551919565492225; post:1918195998066610351; post:2010406903306997921).

Michael Saylor is respected for Bitcoin conviction (post:1353540199943262209; post:1724381499682689285; post:1982800891708166318). Nayib Bukele appears as a hero of nation-state Bitcoin adoption (post:1507175972344324100; post:1515209771812540418; post:1639182175206612992). Jack Dorsey was respected early for crypto/Twitter principles (post:1109380400487399424; post:1234681671795929095).

He respects Satoshi Nakamoto as the creator (post:1587052597730582528; post:1602765573536071681). He respects Binance Angels, ZachXBT, Yi He, and community contributors (post:1567538993431977984; post:1599744125276164096; post:2047033046642417819; post:2047145724375339232). He also respects people who execute, such as Brian Shroder after zero-fee BTC and Richard Branson (post:1539634204287303683; post:2017265575710658568).

The Justin Sun relationship clearly changed. Early on he praised him and thanked him (post:978166562816274432; post:1054593214538178560; post:1125956811574484993). By 2023–2025 Justin Sun appears among mocked/criticized figures (post:1706326077021274327; post:1952321273557836105). This is the clearest respect-to-mock arc in the notes.

## Who and what they mock

He mocks Craig Wright constantly and harshly over claims of being Satoshi (post:1116563034476957699; post:1264900023826780160). He mocks Nouriel Roubini’s attacks as bad-faith criticism (post:1079813967533334528; post:1146007459468681216). Warren Buffett and Jim Cramer are recurring targets for anti-crypto or noisy market takes (post:1231989010396000263; post:1603743580278951938; post:1641722293452693505). Peter Schiff is a long-running foil, though late notes also include him among respected sparring partners (post:1258018274735828993; post:1996633333779124666; post:2111977112668086332).

He is at his most pointed about FTX/SBF and Terra. He mocks SBF, Kevin O’Leary, and the “wrong narratives” around Binance’s role in the collapse (post:1600083624006598657; post:1601140809491185664; post:1601140873554579456). He mocks Terra’s model and 3AC (post:1525060775391510529; post:1545055439867428866).

He repeatedly mocks mainstream media and SEC/Gensler. Reuters, Bloomberg, WSJ, NYT, Forbes, CNBC, and fake journalists all appear (post:1517773592502718464; post:1546514934732914696; post:1630466326102777859; post:1900194883891322948; post:1973262111212962092). SEC is mocked for inconsistent enforcement (post:1665761912850874368; post:1665817591229579267; post:1708064484135239986).

He also mocks exchanges that do not list BNB, YouTube for fake crypto ads, Jesse Powell/Brian Armstrong in competitive exchanges, and closed-garden ecosystems claiming decentralization (post:1362482543686192192; post:1289347424553590785; post:1596235264242126848; post:2099495790884647038). The mock tone is usually calm, sarcastic, brief, with “4” or “FUD” to dismiss noise.

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He publicly admits he gets stuck in every investment he makes, with a 100% record of being “trapped” (post:1985623248265232546; post:2000845292665782368). He jokes about being “poor again” during drawdowns (post:2019456853864857605). On the 2019 hack, the tone was accountable and forward-looking: the team did not want to do what was done, but Bitcoin remained secure and there was lots to do (post:1125956811574484993). Later he is willing to say a gain might be “pure luck” rather than skill (post:2106762406890447225).

His clearest self-correction on a narrative is stablecoins: he says he underestimated them because “stablecoin” is a misnomer, but they are big and still early (post:2075200681971986520). He also admits limitations in prediction, saying no one can call bottoms (post:2090040228593697013). His pattern is to admit error lightly, reframe forward, and return to building rather than dwelling on the mistake.
