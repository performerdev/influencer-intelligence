# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview
He treats crypto as an inevitability, not a trade. From the earliest notes he is already repeating that everyone will be in crypto “sooner or later” and that blockchain is a global network rather than one country’s project (post:1104693437092093952, post:1105521305334341632, post:1118191218477764608, post:990617429855236096). That conviction does not weaken. By 2026 he is still arguing the market is tiny compared with its potential and that you will use crypto sooner or later (post:1966389909222289514, post:2007664922159902750, post:1950966485125570948). At the same time, he is not promiscuously bullish on every project: most projects fail, and only the few successes perform exponentially (post:2016285435568504989).

His central split is between building and FUD. In 2018 he framed it as FUD winning only short term, while building wins long term (post:1051800877525127168, post:1066969437343932417). That remains his explanation for media attacks and price fear: most people in crypto want a quick buck, but the real money is in building (post:1905994147544088728). Even when he was CEO of Binance and then after leaving, “keep building” is the default answer to bad news (post:1605651313240391681, post:1899035759887188434).

Decentralization is an ideal, but he defines it in a practical, almost anti-purist way. Bitcoin is decentralized and does not care about critics; no central figure defends it, and that is the point (post:1208763974608535552). Later he argues that complaining others are not decentralized enough is itself a centralized mindset, because decentralization means everyone has their own choices (post:2009687975291166816, post:2009690354350800974).

He sees trust and credibility as the real scarce assets. Credibility is “the fundamental currency” and is earned over long periods through consistent positive actions (post:1598709944907223040, post:1586795986378170373). The best way to gain trust is during a crisis (post:2029876802852946196). That connects to his repeated emphasis on protecting users: that value, he says, is what built Binance and the BNB ecosystem (post:1978174580864045479).

He also has a strong low-fee, high-access worldview. High fees exclude people, and low fees win (post:1300826955449729025, post:2075201542911672463). Education is not just PR; he treats free basic education, especially through Giggle Academy, as a core mission (post:1769877743607448029, post:1774141533618352581, post:2097266218290008325). Regulation is not one thing: good regulations are good, bad regulations are bad (post:1002266538689220609). Over time he moves from that quip to more proactive compliance and law-enforcement cooperation language (post:1420056975094665226, post:1606042343055958043, post:1902772815763476703).

## How they reason and decide
He almost always zooms out. His preferred frame is long-term historical data, not the current candle: look at yearly charts, not one-minute charts, and ask where the industry will be in five to ten years (post:1052859966984347648, post:1005689649493209090, post:1620058528747753473, post:1692442108672491840, post:1925430816571600935). That is not just a slogan; he uses it to argue against panic selling and against trying to call exact bottoms, since nobody can call bottoms (post:2090040228593697013).

He reasons with simple supply-and-demand and basic arithmetic. He explains Bitcoin price as supply and demand, compares crypto with traditional finance, and uses “if you double the fee, you double the revenue” as math that breaks down only because human behavior changes (post:1242078098083872768, post:2031580229001744437, post:2023015324812230685). When Forbes publishes a net-worth number, he points out the internal contradiction with crypto prices dropping half (post:2031891308047175872).

He likes data and incentive analysis over narrative. To counter FUD he points to inflows, market share, reserves, and third-party reports (post:2019332940958744658, post:2023886800289407207, post:2025746601256612120). He reasons from incentives: terrorists do not generate fee revenue, so exchanges have no motive to serve them (post:2030097090152702131). He also reads market reactions as a vote: users vote with their money, and net inflows after a crisis are trust (post:2118154471872086024, post:2116954044988354798).

He is consistently execution-first. Ideas are worthless without execution; do not pitch projects, show execution (post:1100353021987319808, post:1431585494152871936, post:1947188890336645240). He applies the same standard to pitches: the fancier the slides, the weaker the product; prefer live product demos (post:1950466274175439308, post:1950562369526231091).

In a crisis, his decision pattern is to communicate fast, admit the failure, cover losses from the balance sheet, avoid borrowing or selling assets, and keep operating (post:1578190677241778177, post:2117302637574144147, post:2115993068507897936). He also weighs scale carefully: how many users were affected, how large the loss was relative to total supply, and whether the platform covered it itself (post:2116198836729520420, post:2115932500006756686).

He does not try to convince everyone. He says it is their choice, and he will not spend energy on people who are already hostile (post:1231989010396000263). But he does take local and cultural context seriously, adapting to local customs and avoiding one-size-fits-all conclusions (post:1074807966627377152, post:990178420876304384, post:2111157487726453177).

## Risk attitude
His risk attitude is not “risk on” or “risk off”; it is “manage the risk.” He explicitly says risk is not binary and can be managed (post:1929842539634249940, post:1924716732763291707). The most repeated rule is to invest only what you can afford to lose and, in bull markets, ask what happens if everything goes to zero (post:1287144219576954880, post:1552967284540542977). If a position causes stress, the fix is not more information; it is to reduce the size (post:1895405153177792562).

He is very security-focused. Funds must be SAFU and anti-fraud systems are key (post:1065272836032389120, post:1090491816888029186). That shows up as practical instructions: use 2FA, avoid SMS 2FA, use unique email aliases per exchange, do not click email links or attachments, do not publicize holdings, and wait a few weeks before trusting a new hardware wallet (post:1478411400213147652, post:2115304943934672928, post:2115516716331536682, post:2108561409642574285). After seeing exchange failures, he also warns against businesses that are not profitable or that survive by selling their own tokens (post:1591098313591652352).

His FTX-era lesson is one of the clearest risk rules: never use a token you created as collateral, and do not borrow if you run a crypto business (post:1590103159506341888). He extends that to ordinary users: do not chase high yields, and do not ape into every meme coin based on his tweets because you are almost guaranteed to lose money (post:1610313080029810690, post:2010961732597137482).

His custody view changed with the market. Early on he said to store coins yourself or use reputable exchanges, with DEX reducing custody risk (post:1085115366508883968). By the 2026 wave of wallet and supply-chain scares, he says exchange custody is statistically safer for many people, self-custody means self-responsibility, and a balanced approach is probably best (post:2084531849515131231, post:2081316675383910587, post:2084018586541273200). He also accepts that not investing is itself risky, because fiat and standing still have risks (post:1924515865375961451, post:1967944789665738990).

## Favorite narratives
- **Bitcoin: bullish throughout.** Early: do not bet against Bitcoin (post:1127958192015589376). He later frames it as crisis hedge, inflation protection, and hard asset (post:1234244471665111045, post:1242078098083872768, post:1980303362114662814). In 2025–2026 he argues “everything before the next ATH is a dip” and that “bitcoin is dead” calls have been the best buying times (post:1936992556254187736, post:2113699694245556532, post:2118077202764013695). No one he knows lost money holding bitcoin or BNB for four-plus years (post:1882138060034642397).
- **BNB: bullish throughout.** He linked it with Bitcoin early: do not bet against Bitcoin and do not bet against BNB (post:1127958192015589376). Later he pushed BNB as an ecosystem and public-chain native coin, distinct from the CEX (post:1937142890834411663).
- **Ethereum and DeFi: early Ethereum bullish, then BSC emphasis, DeFi mixed-to-bullish.** He praised Ethereum early (post:1120267491073073153), then pushed BNB Chain as faster and cheaper (post:1321673456665067521, post:1393940762992074753). DeFi was exciting but he warned about bubbles and high-yield games (post:1300795684145127424, post:1610313080029810690).
- **Stablecoins: mixed early, bullish later.** Early stablecoin view was mixed (post:1039548556644773891). By 2026 he admits he underestimated them because “stablecoin is a misnomer,” but says they are big and “this is just the beginning” (post:2075200681971986520). He also says AI agents will use crypto and stablecoins for millions of payments (post:2030984617109664061).
- **NFTs and metaverse: bullish in 2021, then faded.** He called NFTs a new frontier for art and culture (post:1366219549977178112) and was bullish on the metaverse (post:1430878136107868170). Later notes mostly drop the subject.
- **Meme coins: mixed.** He says memes are a cultural phenomenon but real applications should be built on blockchain (post:1861306581758976434, post:1888593393275777491). He accepts they can have utility, such as generating donations, but warns they are extremely high risk (post:1969970609179271330, post:2010961732597137482).
- **AI: bullish with hard risk exceptions.** He is bullish on AI as a tool and on AI agents using crypto (post:1901542080649781487, post:2014399321815580964, post:2115229579434746138). But he is bearish on weaponized robots and says most AI companies will go bust even though AI itself will stay and grow (post:2037834026418077874, post:2060253589268746340).
- **Prediction markets, tokenization/RWA, and privacy: bullish later.** He backs prediction markets (post:1979204734125748615), tokenized assets on-chain (post:1922309166221124070, post:2090590687657922579), and privacy as crypto’s missing link for payments (post:2023016538677371079).
- **Gold as crypto alternative: bearish.** He repeatedly mocks goldbug arguments and says Bitcoin is better than gold and will eventually flip it (post:1980303362114662814, post:1981246936117084282, post:2014700429570146503).

## Who they respect
He consistently respects builders and people who take real risk or make real tools. Vitalik Buterin appears across every period, from early Ethereum praise to later math jokes (post:1016816438034591744, post:1685684907106881536, post:2108138314188685456). Elon Musk is a recurring reference for markets and tech execution (post:1054584958830800896, post:1692571034354946265, post:2010406903306997921). Michael Saylor and Nayib Bukele are respected for Bitcoin conviction and adoption (post:1353540199943262209, post:1507175972344324100, post:1982800891708166318). He respects Satoshi as the absent founder whose design cannot be defended by a central figure (post:1587052597730582528, post:1602765573536071681). Inside Binance, he respects He Yi for standing in front of users during crises (post:2047145724375339232, post:2115993068507897936). He also respects ZachXBT and law enforcement for recovery work (post:2047033046642417819, post:2116768459451469945, post:2115965174243033143). Early on he respected Jack Dorsey, Tim Draper, and PancakeSwap’s team (post:1109380400487399424, post:1072483396645994498, post:1361682447386124293). He respects Arthur Hayes/BitMEX as an exchange pioneer and later offers respect when BitMEX shuts down (post:2022175247194116488, post:2080279259701002381).

## Who and what they mock
He mocks skeptics, bad actors, and most of all manufactured FUD. Early targets include Nouriel Roubini (post:1079813967533334528, post:1146007459468681216), Craig Wright (post:1116563034476957699, post:1231846420392685569), Warren Buffett on crypto dismissal (post:1231989010396000263), Peter Schiff (post:1218975130216361984, post:2111977112668086332), CNBC and The Block (post:1119952463866449920, post:1198092367103807493). In the 2022 fallout he mocks SBF, Kevin O’Leary, Jim Cramer, and FTX-linked media narratives (post:1600083624006598657, post:1601140809491185664, post:1603743580278951938). He repeatedly mocks traditional media—Reuters, Bloomberg, Forbes, WSJ, NYT—over what he sees as distorted or baseless hit pieces (post:1517773592502718464, post:1630466326102777859, post:1900194883891322948, post:1930830316937052250). He mocks exchange competitors who pay for FUD, saying paying to FUD a competitor destroys your own trust and advertises the victim (post:2109718347646714002, post:2109394672053608813). His mockery is usually dry and logic-based rather than purely insulting: he uses “4” or “FUD” to dismiss, and tells people to unfollow weak minds (post:1937633684020244686).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He admits security incidents are real failures that should have been prevented, even when the response is to cover losses himself (post:1578190677241778177, post:2117846768666755491, post:2117302637574144147). After FTX he states two big lessons: never use a token you created as collateral, and do not borrow if you run a crypto business; Binance had never used BNB for collateral or taken on debt (post:1590103159506341888). He admits he underestimated stablecoins because the name misled him (post:2075200681971986520). He also uses self-deprecating admissions about markets: he gets stuck in every investment he makes and has a 100% record of being “trapped” (post:1985623248265232546, post:2000845292665782368). He frames these not as shame but as lessons: it hurts, but we survive; onwards (post:2115993068507897936).
