# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview
The core belief has been stable from the earliest posts to the most recent: crypto is inevitable. In 2018–2019 he framed it as everyone ending up in crypto "whether they know it or not" (post:1104693437092093952). By 2026 he kept the same frame: "You will use crypto sooner or later" (post:1950966485125570948). This is not a trading call; it is a thesis about technology replacing money rails. He compares blockchain to the internet: "You don't exit the internet, you don't exit AI, and you don't exit money" (post:2079482970436419836).

He thinks in builders' terms, not traders' terms. FUD "wins only in the short term" while building wins long term (post:1051800877525127168). That is one of his most repeated ideas: keep building, ignore hype and fake news (post:1582062717447245827, 1883576961651589214). He often says real wealth comes from consistency and execution, not from being especially smart (post:2021513212085284933).

On money itself, he believes fiat printing redistributes wealth rather than creating it, and crypto is the protection (post:1237074894883602433, 1380510077807853571). Bitcoin specifically is a hard asset and crisis hedge: by 2020 he declared it was "created in response to financial crises" and would outperform fiat and stocks during crises (post:1234244471665111045). He repeated the "Bitcoin won't go to zero" line through multiple cycles (post:1239772144055140352, 1350353181197312000, 1408620669533712387), and in 2026 still said "Bitcoin won't be 'dead' for too long; don't panic" (post:2064365489674170779).

On decentralization, his view evolved but kept the same anti-authority core. Early on he said decentralization means "no central figure defends it; that's the point" (post:1208763974608535552). By 2025 he had sharpened it into a point about choice: complaining that others are not decentralized enough is itself "a centralized mindset" (post:2009687975291166816).

He sees blockchain as a global, neutral technology. It is not tied to one country, and it reduces cultural gaps (post:990617429855236096). At the same time, regulation is not one thing: "good regulations are good, bad regulations are bad" (post:1002266538689220609). Later he became more explicit that compliance and working with regulators are necessary for mainstream adoption (post:1606042343055958043, 1420056975094665226).

Education is a genuine personal mission, not just a talking point. He launched Giggle Academy as free basic education for all, with no in-app purchases and no premium features (post:1769877743607448029, 2106993383986377213). He ties this to crypto: crypto can fund education (post:1976595446849007748).

On AI, he is bullish on the intersection but careful about the industry: AI agents will make "a million times more payments than humans, and they will use crypto" (post:2030984617109664061). But he also says most AI companies will go bust and survivors will see huge price swings (post:2060253589268746340), and AI "eats software" (post:2025043537507553493).

## How they reason and decide
He reasons from long-term charts and historical cycles, not short-term price action. He routinely tells people to "zoom out" and look at yearly charts, not 1-minute charts (post:1692442108672491840, 1925430816571600935). In 2026 he argued against panic with Bitcoin's 17-year history and supply math: 20.07M mined, 4.4% supply left, 10–20% lost (post:2088545438328836149).

He is aggressively data-driven when defending Binance or crypto. During proof-of-reserves debates he published the argument that banks run on fractional reserves while exchanges should hold 1:1 (post:1590055819416330240, 1590693950020472834). He uses third-party reports and reserve data to counter FUD rather than just denying it (post:2019332940958744658, 2025746601256612120).

He loves basic math and common sense. When Forbes net worth estimates didn't reconcile with a 50% crypto price drop, he mocked the logic (post:2031580229001744437, 2031891308047175872). He reasons from incentives: for example, terrorists don't generate fee revenue, so exchanges have zero motive to serve them (post:2030097090152702131). And on pricing, "double the fee, double the revenue is math; in human behavior, double the fee halves the revenue" (post:2023015324812230685).

He is a worst-case planner. He frames risk decisions around a single question: if everything goes to 0, can your life still be ok? (post:1552967284540542977, 1924716732763291707). He applied the same logic to himself when he sold his house for BTC: he had calculated the downside and could have returned to a Wall Street job (post:2093286411315339462).

He judges projects by fundamentals, not narratives: product, users, revenue/profits, team strength (post:1907667899365605418). He says execution is key and ideas are worthless (post:1431585494152871936, 1947188890336645240). In practice he prefers live product demos over fancy slides: "The fancier the slides, the weaker the product" (post:1950466274175439308).

He makes decisions through community feedback too. In 2025 he ran polls to gauge sentiment on memes and token issuance (post:1883191454467776583, 1883379486097572295). He also treats market temperature as a filter: when hype leaves and real builders stay, that is when it's easier to invest (post:2017499475921801623, 2095933513258393923).

He avoids trying to call bottoms: "Pass (Not calling any bottoms. No one can. But try to adopt a yearly horizon.)" (post:2090040228593697013). That yearly horizon is his preferred substitute for price predictions.

## Risk attitude
He is permanently risk-aware but not risk-averse in the bankable sense. He is all-in on crypto personally, having "sold all my fiat years ago" and converting only what he needs each month (post:1145184650416947200). At the same time he repeats "only invest what you can afford to lose" as a hard rule (post:1287144219576954880).

His signature risk exercise is the zero-scenario: "If everything went to 0, will your life still be ok? If no, you invested too much. Reduce it by half and ask again" (post:1552967284540542977). He extended that to a blunt prompt: "what is the worst case scenario? if it goes to 0, can you survive? keep your life style?" (post:1924716732763291707).

He treats leverage and debt as toxic. After FTX he stated two big lessons: "Never use a token you created as collateral. Don't borrow if you run a crypto business. Don't use capital 'efficiently'. Have a large reserve" (post:1590103159506341888). He also listed red-flag businesses to avoid: unprofitable, surviving by selling their own tokens, high lock-up incentives, tiny circulation supply, loans (post:1591098313591652352).

Security is a recurring theme. Funds must be SAFU (post:1065272836032389120). He warns constantly about phishing, SMS links, fake accounts, deepfake hacks, and supply-chain attacks (post:1489545612718579715, 1935910955139186692, 2036455885812445419). His practical advice evolved from simple 2FA (post:1478411400213147652) to hardware 2FA and different passwords per site (post:1935786731418403225, 1923061451549442066).

His view on custody is the clearest change in the notes. Early he leaned self-custody and DEX to reduce custody risk (post:1085115366508883968). By 2026 he had moved to a balanced stance: self-custody has its own risks, and "it is statistically safer to store coins on exchanges than to self-custody, though different approaches suit different people and a balanced approach is probably best" (post:2084531849515131231). He still supports self-custody as a right, but with care (post:2081316675383910587, 2083446448461606930).

On market risk, he accepts volatility as normal and says dips are part of free markets (post:1895405153177792562). If you are stressed about a dip, he says reduce your investment size (post:1895405153177792562, 1895711850060456371).

## Favorite narratives
**Bitcoin** — consistently bullish from 2018 to 2026. He called it a crisis hedge, digital gold, and a hard asset (post:1242078098083872768, 1980303362114662814, 2035940587988550080). He never wavered on "not going to zero," and in 2026 argued it could solve most public debt (post:1945491587087425945). He also pushed "don't bet against Bitcoin" early (post:1127958192015589376).

**BNB / BNB Chain** — bullish throughout. He promoted BNB as a top-tier ecosystem asset (post:1251343273441943552), defended team token transparency (post:1383793547183222791), and later clarified BNB is "a public blockchain native coin, not linked to Binance Holdings or CEX" (post:1937142890834411663). He repeatedly pushed BNB Chain as faster and cheaper, with low fees as a weapon (post:1321673456665067521, 2075201542911672463).

**Stablecoins** — this is an admitted evolution. He was mixed early (post:1039548556644773891), then became bullish, and in 2026 admitted: "Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why he underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning" (post:2075200681971986520). The change is from skepticism to strong backing.

**Memes** — mixed, with a hard pragmatic edge. He saw memes as a cultural phenomenon but said real applications should be built on blockchain (post:1861306581758976434). After becoming a meme himself (Broccoli the dog), he clarified and warned: "if you are going to ape into every meme coin people create based on my random tweets, you are almost guaranteed to lose money" (post:2010961732597137482). He also noted meme volume had decreased significantly (post:1896457484119756855).

**AI** — bullish on adoption, bearish on most AI companies. He calls AI "the currency for AI" and says users should own their data (post:1883046240386253063, 1894110915429015838). He later split the thesis: AI agents will use crypto (post:2030984617109664061), but "most AI companies will go bust" (post:2060253589268746340). He is also worried about AI weaponization (post:2037834026418077874).

**Gold** — turned bearish later. He argued Bitcoin is better than gold and tokenized gold is not real on-chain gold (post:1980303362114662814, 1981246936117084282). He even blamed gold and silver crashes on overhyped "safe assets" (post:2017453575467897021).

**DeFi, NFTs, education, regulation** — generally bullish, with DeFi carrying risk warnings about bad incentives (post:1300795684145127424, 1591098313591652352).

## Who and what they mock
Peter Schiff is his favorite gold-bug target, mocked from 2019 to 2026 (post:1218975130216361984, 1970427247157723647, 1996633333779124666). Craig Wright/CSW was attacked hard and early, with Binance even offering legal support to fight him (post:1116563034476957699, 1264900023826780160). Jim Cramer is treated as a running joke about bad predictions (post:1608181941156593664, 1699143872918548634). Nouriel Roubini gets short, annoyed dismissals (post:1079813967533334528).

He mocks media misinformation relentlessly: WSJ, Reuters, Bloomberg, Forbes, NYT (post:1900194883891322948, 1517773592502718464, 1630466326102777859). He calls out fake news, anonymous sources, and disgruntled ex-employees as the source of most FUD (post:2022366141927100726, 2025746601256612120).

After FTX, he targeted SBF and his defenders with long critical threads (post:1600083624006598657, 1601140809491185664). He also mocked SEC enforcement under Gary Gensler (post:1708064484135239986, 1889217075262214494).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He has admitted underestimating stablecoins: "Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why he underestimated it at the beginning" (post:2075200681971986520). He jokes about a 100% record of being "trapped" in investments: "I get stuck in every investment I make" (post:1985623248265232546, 2000845292665782368). He says he "can't predict anything" when reflecting on super cycles, and has admitted, "I am not smart. I am not good-looking" with self-deprecating honesty. On product decisions he admitted "I mis-judged that" about a wallet spam feature. He also regularly admits he doesn't know everything: "I don't know the answer, other than keep building."
