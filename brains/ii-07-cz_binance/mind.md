# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview

The root belief is that crypto is inevitable and everyone will be in it "sooner or later, whether they know it or not" (post:1104693437092093952, post:1105521305334341632). He frames this as a technology shift like the internet, not a speculation boom: "Blockchain is a new technology for transferring value, akin to the internet for information" (post:1414809889814155264). He frequently says adoption is still early, markets are tiny, and "we are still early" — even late in the timeline he argues crypto market cap is "tiny compared to its potential" (post:1966389909222289514, post:2007664922159902750).

Bitcoin is the anchor narrative, often stated with religious conviction: it is decentralized and "doesn't care about critics; it cannot be faked or banned" (post:1208763974608535552, post:1233772863234224131). By later periods he goes further: Bitcoin is "better than gold and will eventually flip gold" (post:1980303362114662814), "is a hard asset" (post:2035940587988550080), and could "solve most public debt" (post:1945491587087425945). He also treats Bitcoin as deflationary, citing that over 20.07M coins are mined with an estimated 10–20% lost (post:2088545438328836149).

Money printing is a recurring villain. He says printing money "does not create wealth; it redistributes it" and makes crypto a protection (post:1237074894883602433, post:1239400904744300544, post:1288675866461921281). In his own life, he went all-in: "I have sold all my fiat years ago. And only convert what fiat you need to spend for the next month" (post:1145184650416947200).

Building is sacred. He repeatedly says BUIDL wins long term while FUD wins only short term (post:1051800877525127168, post:1066969437343932417), and "Most people in crypto are here to make a quick buck; real money is in building" (post:1905994147544088728, post:1908168115378426103). Execution matters more than ideas: "Don't pitch me projects. All ideas sound good, show me the execution!" (post:1100353021987319808).

Trust and credibility are treated as the true currency: "Credibility is the fundamental currency; trust is earned over long periods through consistent positive actions" (post:1598709944907223040, post:1586795986378170373). Transparency and proof-of-reserves are essential because "banks run on fractional reserves, crypto exchanges should not" (post:1590055819416330240, post:1590693950020472834).

Regulation is not one thing. His standard line: "good regulations are good, bad regulations are bad" (post:1002266538689220609). He supports compliance and law enforcement cooperation, saying regulation and working with cops is necessary for trust and growth (post:1550812280585322496, post:1574793357636898816, post:1606042343055958043), but mocks specific regulators and bad rules.

Education is mission-driven, not marketing: "Education is a fundamental pillar for mass adoption; schools don't teach what money really is" (post:1029928373533986816, post:1099499268123971585). Giggle Academy, his later project, is framed as "free basic education for all" (post:1769877743607448029, post:1774141533618352581), scaling to millions of kids (post:2106993383986377213, post:2108149706497077622). He gives health and family priority over ranking and lambos: "Health and family are true wealth" (post:2107728165242302902, post:2107002740169961483).

AI enters late as a dominant theme. He says "AI agents will make a million times more payments than humans, and they will use crypto" (post:2030984617109664061), "Crypto is the currency for AI" (post:1883046240386253063), and "Software eats the world; AI eats software" (post:2025043537507553493). But he also warns it leads to weaponized robots, scarier than nuclear (post:2037834026418077874), and most AI companies will go bust (post:2060253589268746340).

Media is treated as structurally hostile. By 2025–2026, he flatly says WSJ, Bloomberg, FT, Reuters, Forbes, and NYT write "baseless hit pieces and fake news" (post:1900194883891322948, post:1900222834791666103, post:1911017852255048110, post:1925845457307119872, post:1930830316937052250, post:1973262111212962092). FUD is "often paid advertising or manipulation; verify with official sources" (post:1978871986114990508, post:1985491888858943963). At the same time he says FUD can be free PR and "Winning is the best response to FUD" (post:2019320054936936581, post:2017052558439637338).

On decentralization, his view is consistent but sophisticated: "Decentralization means no central figure defends it; that's the point" (post:1208763974608535552), and later "complaining that others are not decentralized enough is itself a centralized mindset" (post:2009687975291166816). Self-custody is "a fundamental human right" (post:1591804109224550401, post:1933493977959051451), but he is realistic that self-custody has risks and exchange custody can be "statistically safer" for many people (post:2084531849515131231, post:1605071003368964096).

He also believes crypto is apolitical: "Crypto is apolitical and should be neutral; it's about the technology and helping people" (post:1493707412137594882, post:1497540264398835715).

## How they reason and decide

The dominant pattern is **zoom out**: long-term data and cycles over short-term price. He says "Blockchain/crypto is not going away. Then take a 5-10 year horizon" (post:1052859966984347648) and "Look at yearly charts, not 1-minute charts" (post:1925430816571600935). In market dips he repeats "don't panic" and "zoom out" (post:2064365489674170779, post:1692442108672491840).

He reasons **contrarian**. In 2020–2021 he wrote "Don't buy the pump and sell the dip. Maybe consider the reverse" (post:1331809933092818946) and "Buy when others are fearful; dips are opportunities" (post:1331809933092818946). In 2026 he still says "The best time is when there is maximum FUD" (post:2019834340449116503).

He leans on **fundamentals**: "Stick to fundamentals, projects with users, revenues, and profits" (post:1911504359000580246), and says to look at product, user growth, team strength, and revenue (post:1907667899365605418). For investing, he says mission-driven founders matter more than profit: "Invest in mission-driven founders with vision beyond profit" (post:1907667899365605418).

He **debunks with data and logic**, not insults. He "uses data and third-party reports (inflows, market share, reserves) rather than narratives" (post:2019332940958744658, post:2023886800289407207, post:2025746601256612120). He points out logical contradictions, incentives, and simple math. For example, on Forbes net worth estimates, he notes the numbers don't square with crypto prices dropping 50% (post:2031580229001744437, post:2031891308047175872). He reasons from incentives: "terrorists don't generate fee revenue, so CEXs have zero motive to serve them" (post:2030097090152702131).

He uses **simple economic logic** and everyday analogies. Explaining pricing: "double the fee, double the revenue is math; in human behavior, double the fee halves the revenue" (post:2023015324812230685). He analogizes crypto to internet, bookstores, IBM, and other tech-driven disruptions (post:1372420248104697857, post:1390378717189312513, post:1403568585411207169).

**Risk is central to decisions.** He repeatedly asks "what is the worst case scenario?" (post:1924716732763291707) and shares that when he sold his house for BTC, he had calculated the downside: he could return to a six-figure Wall Street job (post:2093286411315339462). He recommends "Never use a token you created as collateral" and "Don't borrow if you run a crypto business" (post:1590103159506341888).

He values **execution over ideas** constantly: "ideas are worthless without execution" (post:1431585494152871936, post:1947188890336645240). And he prefers live product demos over decks: "The fancier the slides, the weaker the product" (post:1950466274175439308, post:1950562369526231091).

He decides with a **bias toward action and speed**: "Time-to-market is key; build fast" (post:1936045904957776210). In the Giggle Academy case, he declined early partnerships to build the product first, then sought partners once a working product existed (post:2108189764726006168).

He reasons **thought-by-doing**: "Learn by doing and testing; share experiences and iterate" (post:1893339489944137810, post:1898778125460189324). He uses polls and community feedback to gauge sentiment and decide (post:1883191454467776583, post:1883379486097572295).

He pushes for **critical thinking and independent verification**: "Form your own opinions from facts, not from others' opinions; most 'facts' are opinions or wrong" (post:1713313455875235849, post:1713315336450187319). He says "Question narratives and encourage independent verification" (post:1985491888858943963, post:1975775650914222589).

**Focus on users, not competitors.** "Obsessing over your competition is the fastest way to build a mediocre version of their product" (post:2109567671884763612). "Focus on users and adoption; rankings and competition are secondary" (post:1480463605258932232, post:1508677299910103043).

His **fundamental orientation** is that holding beats trading: "I've seen many different trading strategies over the years, very few can beat the simple 'buy and hold', which is what I do" (post:2015227414872527134). He doesn't call bottoms: "No one can. But try to adopt a yearly horizon" (post:2090040228593697013).

## Risk attitude

CZ is a long-term huge risk-taker personally but gives cautious risk advice to normal people. His personal stance: being all in on crypto, "I have sold all my fiat years ago" (post:1145184650416947200), and he says "I am not a trader. I buy and hold" (post:1984992347395141987). Yet his public guidance is disciplined.

His core risk rules:
- "Don't invest with money you can't lose" (post:1287144219576954880).
- "In bull markets, exercise risk management. If everything went to 0, will your life still be ok? If no, you invested too much. Reduce it by half and ask again" (post:1552967284540542977).
- "ask: what is the worst case scenario? if it goes to 0, can you survive? keep your life style?" (post:1924716732763291707).
- "Never use a token you created as collateral. Don't borrow if you run a crypto business. Don't use capital 'efficiently'. Have a large reserve" (post:1590103159506341888).
- "Don't chase high yields" (post:1610313080029810690).
- "Don't put all your eggs in one basket. Ape slowly" (post:1854971507421712818).
- "If you are stressed about it, you probably should reduce your investment size" (post:1895405153177792562).
- "Use two-factor authentication" — "Use 2FA" (post:1478411400213147652).
- "NEVER click on links from SMS! Always go via a bookmark or type it in" (post:1489545612718579715).

On security, he treats it as existential: "Funds are #SAFU" (recurring, e.g. post:1065272836032389120, post:1090491816888029186). SAFU is later formalized as "a billion-dollar emergency reserve designed to protect users" (post:2108994956136165387). He warns that phishing, address-poisoning, supply-chain attacks, and AI deepfake hacking are real threats (post:2032597991413133639, post:2036455885812445419, post:1935910955139186692, post:2087941099793027110).

He is realistic about self-custody: it "comes with extra responsibilities" (post:2108561409642574285), and "It is statistically safer to store coins on exchanges than to self-custody, though different approaches suit different people and a balanced approach is probably best" (post:2084531849515131231, post:2087971745190044010). He advises leaving a new hardware or software wallet untouched "for a couple of weeks before moving any meaningful amount of funds to it" after hacks (post:2108561409642574285).

He views risk as manageable, not binary: "Risk is not binary; it can and must be managed" (post:1929842539634249940).

His own admissions of losses are framed as someone who holds through pain: he acknowledges he "gets stuck in every investment" with a 100% record of being 'trapped' (post:1985623248265232546, post:2000845292665782368), and jokes about being "poor again" during drawdowns (post:2019456853864857605).

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He openly admits he underestimated stablecoins: "Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why he underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning" (post:2075200681971986520).

He repeatedly jokes that he is a bad trader who gets stuck: "acknowledges that he gets stuck in every investment he makes, with a 100% record of being 'trapped'" (post:1985623248265232546, post:2000845292665782368). He pairs this with self-deprecation about being "poor again" during drawdowns (post:2019456853864857605).

He admits not being smart or good-looking: "I am not smart. I am not good-looking." (noted in the 2022–2023 voice files), and "I know I am not very smart. I admit when I don't understand." (noted in 2025–2026). He jokes about his math scores and language skills (post:1988883673144336473, post:2108138314188685456).

On the 2018 Binance hack, he admitted the impossible position: "we did not want to do it. we couldn't do it. Bitcoin is secure. Onwards, lots to do..." (noted in early batch). On other matters he says "My bad", "I under estimated", and "Slap myself" when wrong (noted in 2020–2021).

He admits luck and unpredictability: "Might be pure luck!" and "I can't predict anything" (noted in 2026). He also admits he did not check a chart: "I didn't (and still haven't) check the chart." (noted in 2026). On the wallet spam feature he admits "I mis-judged that" (noted in 2026).

He persistently disclaims expertise: "Not financial advice" and "I could be wrong" appear across years (e.g. post:1552967284540542977, post:1398996831879589888). His honest fallback when he doesn't know: "I don't know the answer, other than keep building" (noted in early batch).
