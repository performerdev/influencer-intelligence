# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview
Crypto is not a phase, a single chain, or a competitor to be beaten. It is an inevitable technology, and adoption is simply a matter of time. This belief runs unchanged from the earliest notes to the latest: blockchain is the internet of value, and everyone will use it whether they know it or not (post:1104693437092093952, post:1966071743476543825). He says plainly that any person or company not using the internet, blockchain, or AI will be less competitive (post:2101513693205024858), and that banks using blockchain are not an attack on crypto but just fintech (post:2100816055979642901). Adoption is now something he tracks country by country through regulation, stablecoins, and banking rails (post:2096262780760547456, post:2106003031838138411).

Bitcoin and hard assets sit at the center of that worldview. Money printing does not create wealth; it redistributes it and causes inflation, making crypto a protection (post:1239400904744300544, post:1380510077807853571). Bitcoin was created in response to financial crises and will outperform fiat and stocks, especially during crises (post:1242078098083872768, post:1322464874950139906). He later sharpens this into a direct comparison: bitcoin is better than gold, and tokenized gold is not real on-chain gold (post:1980303362114662814). He still separates the asset from the price panic: bitcoin won’t be dead for long; don’t panic (post:2064365489674170779).

The other fixed axis is building. FUD wins only in the short term; hard work and building win long term (post:1051800877525127168). Ideas are cheap; execution is what matters (post:1431585494152871936). Even at the very end of the notes, he says 99% of ideas are good but 98% of teams fail at execution (post:2113611119445373422). That is why he sees competing chains as the wrong focus: obsessing over competitors builds a mediocre copy of their product; build instead of FUD (post:2109567671884763612).

Regulation shifted from a mixed early view—“good regulations are good, bad regulations are bad” (post:1002266538689220609)—to a more consistently pro-compliance position after the 2022 exchange failures. He treats regulation and compliance as necessary for mainstream credibility (post:1420056975094665226), and later praises regulatory clarity as advancing globally (post:2034137025075671348). Transparency and proof-of-reserves became non-negotiable: banks run on fractional reserves, crypto exchanges should not (post:1590055819416330240, post:1596240115911000064).

His later worldview adds softer but consistent values: real wealth includes health, loved ones, time, freedom, credibility, principles, and impact (post:2015118587535765714). He tells people to compete on health, not wealth rankings or lambos (post:2107728165242302902). Education became a core mission, first as a pillar for adoption and then through Giggle Academy’s free basic education for all (post:1769877743607448029, post:2106993383986377213). Privacy is a missing link for crypto payments; on-chain salaries are visible to everyone (post:2023016538677371079). AI is both opportunity and danger: AI agents will make many more payments than humans and will use crypto (post:2030984617109664061), but AI inevitably leads to weaponized robots (post:2037834026418077874). He remains a technology optimist with a black-swan awareness.

## How they reason and decide
His default move is to zoom out. He repeatedly tells people to look at long-term historical data rather than short-term price, and to adopt a yearly horizon rather than try to call bottoms (post:1692442108672491840, post:1925430816571600935, post:2090040228593697013). He uses history as evidence: bitcoin has been declared dead many times, and those were the best times to buy (post:2113699694245556532). His own early bitcoin history fits the same pattern: buyers who stayed through crashes did not lose if they held long enough (post:1944617006835409141).

He reasons from simple supply-and-demand logic, economic incentives, and basic math. Double the fee is double the revenue in arithmetic, but in human behavior double the fee can cut revenue by half (post:2023015324812230685). He debunks hit pieces by pointing out internal contradictions and comparing claimed numbers with actual flows (post:2019332940958744658, post:2030990452166644202). When a hack happens, he sizes it against MtGox in BTC terms and asks whether there is a domino effect (post:2116198836729520420). User behavior is the verdict: if deposits exceed withdrawals after a crisis, users have voted with their money (post:2116954044988354798, post:2117532388641136767).

A major decision rule is execution over pitch. He wants live product demos, not beautiful slides; the fancier the slides, the weaker the product (post:1907667899365605418, post:1950466274175439308). He also invests in founders, missions, and fundamentals rather than narratives: product, community, revenue, and trustworthy teams (post:1896116179325817172, post:1911504359000580246). He will state that he invests early, bets long-term, and holds, rather than trying to be a trader (post:2015227414872527134).

He is contrarian but not impulsive. The best time to buy is when there is maximum FUD (post:2019834340449116503). He says don’t buy the pump and sell the dip; consider the reverse (post:1331809933092818946). At the same time, he calculates the downside before acting: when he sold his house for bitcoin, he knew he could return to a six-figure Wall Street job if wrong (post:2093286411315339462). That is consistent with his repeated advice to ask what happens if an investment goes to zero (post:1924716732763291707). He treats risk as a profile to manage, not a binary to avoid (post:1929842539634249940).

He wants decisions evidence-based and transparent. During incidents he shares information quickly and explains the fix (post:1546631971626958848, post:1578190677241778177). When FUD appears, he prefers data and third-party reports over narratives (post:1590693950020472834, post:2019332940958744658). He also understands second-order effects: policy applied to one company applies to the whole industry (post:2090283010486595688), and paying to FUD a competitor destroys your own trust (post:2109718347646714002).

## Risk attitude
His public risk stance is conservative for ordinary users and structured for himself. Crypto is volatile; only invest what you can afford to lose (post:1588876673248006145, post:1287144219576954880). If everything went to zero, your life should still be okay; if not, reduce the investment (post:1552967284540542977). He repeats the worst-case question: if it goes to zero, can you survive and keep your lifestyle? (post:1924716732763291707). He tells people who are stressed by dips to reduce their size (post:1895405153177792562).

He is not anti-risk; he is anti-leverage and anti-stupid-risk. He warns against shorting BNB or betting against Bitcoin, saying shorters get rekt (post:1151816920322068480, post:1127958192015589376). He says don’t chase high yields because the lesson keeps returning (post:1610313080029810690). The FTX collapse hardened several rules: never use a token you created as collateral, don’t borrow if you run a crypto business, don’t run on capital “efficiency,” keep a large reserve (post:1590103159506341888). He also tells people to avoid unprofitable businesses that survive by selling their own tokens or locking customer tokens with high incentives (post:1591098313591652352).

On custody, his view became more nuanced over time. Early notes say store coins yourself or use reputable exchanges, and DEX reduces custody risk (post:1085115366508883968). Much later, he says it is statistically safer to store coins on exchanges than to self-custody for most people, though a balanced approach is probably best (post:2084531849515131231). He still treats self-custody as a fundamental right but warns that securing a seed phrase against theft, fire, flood, and your own mistakes is hard (post:2084018586541273200, post:2083621786005152101). Security details matter: unique email aliases, no SMS 2FA, hardware keys, withdrawal whitelisting, no API keys with third parties, keep the OS updated (post:2115304943934672928, post:2118298620797812955, post:2118966640486555947).

He personally accepts crypto’s drawdowns but does not hide them. He jokes about being “poor again” during drawdowns and about being stuck in every investment he makes (post:2019456853864857605, post:1985623248265232546). That is not a contradiction of his advice; it is his way of saying that even committed holders feel pain.

## Favorite narratives (stance, and how it changed)
- **Bitcoin: bullish throughout.** Early as digital gold and inflation hedge (post:1234244471665111045, post:1242674194296344576), then legal tender and resilient asset (post:1408620669533712387, post:1542817444636524544), later store of value and better than gold (post:1920505488283709512, post:1980303362114662814). He never shifted to bearish; he only added strategic-reserve and nation-state adoption arguments.
- **BNB: bullish throughout.** From top-tier ecosystem token and burn tokenomics (post:1317392968077115392, post:1383793547183222791) to public-chain native coin (post:1546376013948518400, post:1968776498183291072). Later he emphasizes BNB is a public blockchain coin, not linked to Binance Holdings or CEX (post:1937142890834411663), and the BNB ecosystem is open (post:2099495790884647038).
- **BNB Chain/BSC: bullish.** It began as the faster, cheaper alternative to Ethereum (post:1321673456665067521, post:1393940762992074753) and stayed a low-fee, builder-friendly chain (post:1899035759887188434, post:2111157487726453177).
- **Stablecoins: mixed to bullish.** Early notes show caution around stablecoin risk (post:1404836633661239298). He later admits he underestimated them: “Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why he underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning” (post:2075200681971986520). He also says Binance users hold the largest share of most stablecoins (post:2021157754342142193).
- **DeFi: bullish but cautions on bad yields.** He supports DeFi and self-custody as a direction (post:1410998340918018048), but he warned about DeFi bubbles and high-risk projects (post:1300795684145127424). After FTX he remained pro-DeFi while stressing transparency and safe custody.
- **Memes: mixed/bearish.** He says memes are a cultural phenomenon but real applications should be built on blockchain (post:1861306581758976434). He warns almost everyone not to ape into meme coins based on his random tweets because you are almost guaranteed to lose money (post:2010961732597137482). He allows that meme coins can generate donations (post:1969970609179271330), so his stance is not pure disdain; it is risk-first.
- **AI: bullish on utility, bearish on weaponization and most AI companies.** AI agents should focus on utility first and will use crypto for payments (post:1901542080649781487, post:2030984617109664061). But he also says AI inevitably leads to weaponized robots (post:2037834026418077874), and most AI companies will go bust despite AI staying and growing exponentially (post:2060253589268746340).
- **Education: bullish.** Education is a pillar for adoption and later a personal mission through Giggle Academy (post:1769877743607448029, post:1900530429679050829, post:2106993383986377213).
- **Regulation/compliance: mixed to bullish.** Early he split good and bad regulation (post:1002266538689220609); later he says regulatory clarity and pro-crypto policies are advancing globally and necessary for adoption (post:1420056975094665226, post:2034137025075671348).
- **Traditional media: bearish throughout.** He mocks WSJ, Bloomberg, NYT, FT, Reuters, and Forbes as baseless hit pieces and fake news (post:1900194883891322948, post:1930830316937052250, post:1973262111212962092).
- **Terra/FTX: bearish after collapse.** He treated Terra as an over-leveraged stablecoin collapse (post:1525060769771048960, post:1525223086638899203) and FTX as fraud/trust failure (post:1590013613586411520, post:1600083616003854336).

## Who they respect
He respects builders, long-term thinkers, and people who execute or support adoption. Vitalik Buterin appears consistently, often with admiration for his thinking (post:1498784433029238787, post:2108138314188685456). Elon Musk is respected as a builder and platform owner (post:1692571034354946265, post:1936341458317095189). Michael Saylor is credited for clear bitcoin conviction (post:1353540199943262209, post:1982800891708166318). Nayib Bukele is praised for El Salvador’s bitcoin adoption (post:1507175972344324100, post:1639182175206612992). He also respects Jack Dorsey (post:1234681671795929095), Ray Dalio (post:1621523900281806855), Satoshi Nakamoto (post:1587052597730582528), the Uniswap team for security response (post:1546631971626958848), Binance Angels (post:1567538993431977984, post:1599744125276164096), and later ZachXBT for on-chain investigation work (post:2047033046642417819, post:2051789092258447820). His respect is not personality-cult: he respects execution and user protection, which is why he also praises partners and even former competitors when they build.

## Who and what they mock
He mocks people he sees as bad-faith critics, fakes, and frauds. Early targets include Nouriel Roubini (post:1079813967533334528, post:1146007459468681216), Craig Wright (post:1116563034476957699, post:1264900023826780160), Warren Buffett (post:1180534216720674816, post:1520821354710409216), and Peter Schiff (post:1218975130216361984, post:1970427247157723647). He repeatedly mocks Jim Cramer as contrarian indicator (post:1603743580278951938, post:1699143872918548634). During and after FTX, SBF and Kevin O’Leary are treated with open contempt (post:1600083624006598657, post:1601140809491185664). He mocks the SEC and Gary Gensler (post:1708064484135239986, post:1722500524027568573), and mainstream media broadly: WSJ, Bloomberg, NYT, FT, Reuters, Forbes (post:1900194883891322948, post:1930830316937052250, post:1973262111212962092). He also mocks fake FUDders, paid fudders, and exchanges that pay to FUD competitors (post:2019317100204961815, post:2109718347646714002, post:2109738885697683801). His mockery is usually dry, factual, and brief, often with a joke or emoji rather than a long takedown.

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He admits specific misjudgments rather than vague failures. He says he underestimated stablecoins early: “Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why he underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning” (post:2075200681971986520). He also jokes that he gets stuck in every investment he makes and has a 100% record of being “trapped” (post:1985623248265232546, post:2000845292665782368). On security incidents he is direct: “serious security failures occurred” and “Some details are embarrassing,” taking responsibility and promising to learn (post:2117846768666755491). After the BNB Chain exploit he was transparent and humble about what happened and what the team did next (post:1578190677241778177). He also admits he cannot call bottoms and no one can, so he uses a yearly horizon instead (post:2090040228593697013). The FTX lessons are framed as personal and industry failures: don’t use your token as collateral, don’t borrow, keep a large reserve (post:1590103159506341888).
