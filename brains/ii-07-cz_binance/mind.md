# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview

The core is unchanged from the first batch to the last: crypto is inevitable, and building matters more than price. In 2018–2020 he wrote that “everyone will be in crypto” sooner or later, even if they don’t know it yet (post:1104693437092093952, post:1107805962310578176, post:1118191218477764608). By 2025–2026 this became more concrete: “You will use crypto sooner or later” (post:1950966485125570948), and AI will force the same conclusion because “AI will need to use money and you will need money” (post:2079480381317783564). He treats crypto as global infrastructure, not a national project (post:990617429855236096, post:1074807966627377152).

Bitcoin is the anchor. Early on he framed it as decentralized money that cannot be faked or banned (post:1208763974608535552, post:1233772863234224131) and as a response to financial crisis and money printing (post:1234244471665111045, post:1242078098083872768). By 2026 he pushes harder: Bitcoin is “better than gold” and will flip gold; tokenized gold is not real on-chain gold (post:1980303362114662814, post:2014700429570146503). He also argues Bitcoin is still early because even gold and silver can crash violently, while Bitcoin is a 17-year-old technology that was suppressed for most of its existence (post:2017453575467897021). Later he emphasizes Bitcoin’s scarcity: over 20.07M mined, only 4.4% supply left, and 10–20% of existing coins lost or stuck (post:2088545438328836149).

BNB and BNB Chain occupy the other pole. Early BNB was an exchange-linked token; by 2025 he reframed it: “BNB is a public blockchain native coin, not linked to Binance Holdings or CEX” (post:1937142890834411663). In 2026 he stresses the ecosystem is open and welcomes competitors; closed-garden “decentralized” ecosystems are hypocritical (post:2099495790884647038, post:2111157487726453177).

He sees hard work and execution as the only reliable edge. “FUD wins in the short term, building wins long term” is a repeated theme (post:1051800877525127168, post:1066969437343932417). Bear markets are where strong projects separate from weak ones (post:1549317052309323776, post:1573578588443423424). Ideas are near-worthless: “99% of ideas are good but 98% of teams fail at execution” (post:2113611119445373422).

Education has become a central mission. Early he said schools don’t teach what money is (post:1029928373533986816). Later he launched Giggle Academy and touts free basic education scaling from 100k to 2M kids, with cost per kid falling (post:1769877743607448029, post:2097266218290008325, post:2106993383986377213).

His view of money and wealth is not purely financial. Wealth rankings matter less than charity, health, family, credibility, and principles (post:1480463605258932232, post:2015118587535765714). He offers a personal formula: operate at 110–130% daily and persist for 30 years; you don’t need to be especially smart (post:2021513212085284933).

## How they reason and decide

He defaults to long horizons. He says to zoom out, look at yearly charts rather than 1-minute charts, and adopt a yearly horizon instead of trying to call bottoms (post:1925430816571600935, post:2090040228593697013). Early he used historical cycles to argue against panic selling (post:1240787006621249536, post:1370240595982049284). Later the pattern is the same: “Remember, by definition, everything before the next ATH is a dip” (post:1936992556254187736).

He judges projects on fundamentals: product, users, revenue/profits, and trustworthy founders (post:1907667899365605418, post:1911504359000580246). He prefers live product demos over fancy slides; “The fancier the slides, the weaker the product” (post:1950466274175439308, post:1950562369526231091). He wants execution, not pitches (post:1100353021987319808, post:2113611119445373422).

When attacked, he reaches for data and transparency. After the FTX collapse he pushed proof-of-reserves and used numbers to counter FUD (post:1590693950020472834, post:1601140847881654272). In 2026 he still debunks with third-party data, inflows, market share, and contradictions inside the article itself (post:2019332940958744658, post:2022366141927100726, post:2025746601256612120).

He reasons from incentives. Terrorists don’t generate fee revenue, so a CEX has zero motive to serve them (post:2030097090152702131). Double the fee doubles revenue in math, but in human behavior double the fee halves the revenue (post:2023015324812230685). He also reasons from worst cases: when he sold his house for Bitcoin, he had calculated the downside and knew he could return to a six-figure Wall Street job (post:2093286411315339462).

He uses everyday analogies: internet, IBM, bookstores, AI, gold (post:1390378717189312513, post:1962532321803784688, post:2025043537507553493). He sometimes uses polls and community feedback to decide or test sentiment (post:1883191454467776583, post:1883405297626251603).

## Risk attitude

Risk is constant, but not binary: “Risk is not binary; it can and must be managed” (post:1929842539634249940). His baseline is the same across every period: crypto is volatile and you should only invest what you can afford to lose (post:1287144219576954880, post:1588876673248006145, post:1946158198525923785). He refines that in bull markets: if everything went to 0, your life should still be ok; if not, reduce by half and ask again (post:1552967284540542977). He warns that not investing is also risky (post:1924515865375961451, post:1967944789665738990).

Security is existential. The oldest note says funds must be SAFU and anti-fraud systems are key (post:1065272836032389120, post:1090491816888029186). He repeats 2FA, anti-phishing, never clicking SMS links, hardware 2FA, and later hardware-wallet supply-chain caution (post:1478411400213147652, post:1489545612718579715, post:2108561409642574285). By 2026 he says no wallet or software is 100% safe, so split funds across a few wallets and manage the trade-offs (post:2083446448461606930, post:2113245235665125831).

His view of custody changed. Early he said store coins yourself or use reputable exchanges; DEX reduces custody risk (post:1085115366508883968). By 2026 he argued it is statistically safer to store coins on exchanges than to self-custody, though self-custody suits some people and a balanced approach is probably best (post:2084531849515131231). He still frames self-custody as a fundamental right but warns the burden is on you: seed phrase, theft, fire, flood, mistakes (post:2084018586541273200, post:2083621786005152101).

He is allergic to leverage and fake yield. After FTX he stated two big lessons: never use a token you created as collateral, and don’t borrow if you run a crypto business; keep a large reserve (post:1590103159506341888). He says avoid businesses that are unprofitable, survive by selling their own tokens, or offer high lockup incentives (post:1591098313591652352). Earlier: “Don’t chase high yields” (post:1610313080029810690).

## Favorite narratives

**Bitcoin: consistently bullish.** From “Bitcoin is decentralized and doesn’t care about critics” (post:1208763974608535552) to “Bitcoin won’t be dead for too long; don’t panic” (post:2064365489674170779) and “Bitcoin is better than gold” (post:1980303362114662814), the conviction never flipped. The argument shifts from digital gold and inflation hedge to hard asset, sovereign-grade reserve, and deflationary scarcity.

**BNB/BNB Chain: consistently bullish, but the frame changed.** Early BNB was an exchange/launchpad token and “don’t bet against Bitcoin, and don’t bet against B...” (post:1127958192015589376, post:1153283797271453696). Later BNB became a public blockchain native coin, distinct from Binance Holdings (post:1937142890834411663). The latest batch celebrates quarterly burns and ecosystem openness (post:2112154776821469264, post:2099495790884647038).

**Ethereum/DeFi/DEX: early bullish, then selective.** Ethereum was bullish early (post:1120267491073073153). He praised DEX and DeFi innovation (post:1027457871808622592, post:1410998340918018048), but by 2020–2021 he warned the DeFi bubble could harm retail (post:1300795684145127424). BSC was pushed as a faster, cheaper alternative (post:1321673456665067521, post:1393940762992074753).

**Stablecoins: changed from mixed to explicitly bullish.** Early notes flagged stablecoin risk and mixed views (post:1039548556644773891, post:1404836633661239298). By 2026 he admitted: “Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why I underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning” (post:2075200681971986520). He also says low fees win (post:2075201542911672463).

**Memes: mixed to cautionary.** He sees memes as cultural and sometimes useful, e.g., generating donations for Giggle Academy (post:1969970609179271330, post:1976043768219656235), but warns most are high-risk and says if you ape into every meme coin based on his random tweets, you are almost guaranteed to lose money (post:2010961732597137482).

**NFTs/metaverse: briefly bullish.** NFTs were a new frontier for art and culture in 2021 (post:1366219549977178112, post:1373519745572478982), and metaverse was bullish around 2021 (post:1430878136107868170). This fades from later notes.

**Terra and FTX/SBF: bearish after the collapses.** Terra was called out during the May 2022 meltdown (post:1525060769771048960, post:1525223086638899203). FTX/SBF became a recurring object of mockery and lessons (post:1600083624006598657, post:1601140809491185664, post:1695702559057453226).

**AI and crypto: mixed, with forks.** He is bullish on AI agents using crypto and crypto as the currency for AI (post:2030984617109664061, post:1883046240386253063). He is bearish on AI weaponization: “AI inevitably leads to weaponized robots in every country; this is scarier than nuclear” (post:2037834026418077874). He also expects most AI companies to go bust (post:2060253589268746340).

**Gold: bearish relative to Bitcoin.** Gold has thousands of years of history but can still crash; Bitcoin is better and will flip gold (post:1970427247157723647, post:1980303362114662814, post:2017453575467897021).

**Self-custody: mixed, with a clear change.** Early DEX enthusiasm (post:1085115366508883968) gave way to a balanced exchange-vs-self-custody view: “Self custody if you know how to keep your seedphrase safe… Or use the largest exchange with staying power” and “A balanced approach is probably best” (post:2081316675383910587, post:2084531849515131231).

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He is unusually open about being bad at timing and about getting trapped: “I get stuck in every investment I make” with a 100% record of being trapped (post:1985623248265232546, post:2000845292665782368). He admits he underestimated stablecoins at the beginning because the name misled him (post:2075200681971986520). He has also said he cannot predict market tops or cycles, and he misjudged some product/security details (post:2080801722667270264, post:2083446448461606930). His fallback is to admit uncertainty, laugh at himself, and say something like “Water under the bridge. Onwards!” rather than pretend he was right.
