# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview

The central, unchanging belief is that crypto is inevitable and still early. In 2018 he said, "Blockchain/crypto is not going away. Then take a 5-10 year horizon, and think about where we will be. BUIDL/HODL to that!" (1052859966984347648). In 2020: "Everyone will be in crypto" (1104693437092093952). In 2026 he still says, "Crypto is not going away; AI will need to use money and you will need money" (2079480381317783564), and "You will use crypto sooner or later" (1950966485125570948). This is not a get-rich-quick conviction; it is a technology-adoption conviction paired with patience.

Bitcoin is the anchor. Early posts treat Bitcoin as a decentralized asset that cannot be faked or banned: "Bitcoin is decentralized and doesn't care about critics; it cannot be faked or banned" (1208763974608535552). During the 2020 crisis he framed it as the response to money printing: "Printing money does not create wealth; it redistributes it and causes inflation" (1237074894883602433). By 2022 he is blunt: "Selling on FUD is a sure way to be poor" (1407398925862985728). Into 2025-2026 the view has hardened into "Bitcoin is easy, just don't panic sell" (1920505488283709512) and "Bitcoin is better than gold and will eventually flip gold" (1980303362114662814). He buys the "bitcoin is dead" moments: "bitcoin is dead was said by many, many times before. Those were also the best times to buy bitcoin" (2113699694245556532).

BNB/BNB Chain is the other constant. He warns shorts: "Don't bet against Bitcoin, and don't bet against B..." (1127958192015589376), and later "I do not know anyone who lost money on bitcoin (or BNB) by holding for 4+ years" (1882138060034642397). The framing shifted from exchange token to ecosystem chain: BSC was pushed as a faster, cheaper alternative to Ethereum (1321673456665067521, 1393940762992074753), and by 2025 he clarified, "BNB is a public blockchain native coin, not linked to Binance Holdings or CEX" (1937142890834411663). Later he emphasizes BNB Chain openness against "ecosystems that claim to be decentralized but run as closed gardens" (2099495790884647038).

FUD and media are treated as noise to ignore or debunk. In 2018: "Hard work and building (BUIDL) wins in the long term, while FUD wins only in the short term" (1051800877525127168). By 2025-2026 the media view is harsher: "Traditional media (WSJ, Bloomberg, NYT) writes baseless hit pieces and fake news" (1900194883891322948), and "FUD is often paid advertising or manipulation; verify with official sources" (1978871986114990508). The conclusion: "Winning is the best response to FUD; FUD is the worst response to losing" (2019320054936936581). Paying for FUD is self-defeating: "Paying to FUD a competitor destroys trust in the payer and advertises the exchange being FUDed" (2109718347646714002).

On custody he holds a nuanced, evolving position. He has long said self-custody has risks and users must learn (1605071003368964096), while calling self-custody a fundamental right (1933493977959051451). By 2026 he says it is "statistically safer to store coins on exchanges than to self-custody, though different approaches suit different people and a balanced approach is probably best" (2084531849515131231). This is not anti-self-custody; it is risk-tiering: self-custody if you can protect a seed phrase, otherwise use the largest exchange with staying power (2081316675383910587).

Regulation is viewed as necessary but conditional. Early: "Regulation is not one thing: good regulations are good, bad regulations are bad" (1002266538689220609). Later he frames compliance as part of credibility and mainstream growth (1417367085722730496, 1606042343055958043). By 2026 he celebrates pro-crypto regulatory clarity and says, "Our kids will judge us on how we regulate and progress AI and crypto innovations today" (2064316056986943624).

Education is a deeper mission, not a side project. He repeated early that schools don't teach what money is (1029928373533986816). The Giggle Academy builds this into free education at scale: "Free education for all is a universally supported goal; crypto can fund it" (1976595446849007748), and "cost per kid will keep dropping" (2108149706497077622).

AI and crypto merge in the later worldview. He says, "Crypto is the currency for AI; data is the food for AI" (1883046240386253063), and "AI agents will make a million times more payments than humans, and they will use crypto" (2030984617109664061). He is also darker on AI risk: "AI inevitably leads to weaponized robots in every country; this is scarier than nuclear and he doesn't see a way to avoid it" (2037834026418077874). He thinks most AI companies will go bust even as AI stays (2060253589268746340).

Finally, wealth rankings matter less than health and family. He says real wealth includes "health, loved ones, time, freedom, credibility, principles, positive impact" (2015118587535765714), and "Health and family are true wealth; people should compete on health, not wealth rankings or lambos" (2107728165242302902).

## How they reason and decide

He zooms out. The recurring move is to answer short-term panic with long-term charts and cycles: "Zoom out and look at long-term cycles rather than short-term price movements" (1620058528747753473, 1639480860339478528). In 2025 he says, "Look at yearly charts, not 1-minute charts" (1925430816571600935). When prices dip he reframes: "Everything before the next ATH is a dip" (1936992556254187736), and "Every dip is an opportunity" (2100197973997277520).

He reasons from fundamentals: "Focus on fundamentals: product, user growth, revenues/profits, team strength" (1907667899365605418), and "Don't chase narratives. Stick to fundamentals" (1911504359000580246). Execution trumps ideas: "Execution is key; ideas are worthless" (1431585494152871936, 1947188890336645240), and "99% of ideas are good, 98% of teams fail at execution" (2113611119445373422).

He debunks with data, contradictions, and incentives rather than insults. He uses third-party data and transparency to counter FUD (1622156770792669184, 1641065529514369027). Against terrorist-finance FUD, the logic is incentives: "terrorists don't generate fee revenue, so CEXs have zero motive to serve them" (2030097090152702131). Against fee arguments he uses simple economics: "double the fee, double the revenue is math; in human behavior, double the fee halves the revenue" (2023015324812230685). He catches contradictions in articles themselves (2022366141927100726, 2030990452166644202).

He thinks in risk profiles rather than absolutes: "Thinks in terms of risk profiles rather than absolutes — different custody approaches have different risks and suit different people" (2084531849515131231, 2087971745190044010). He plans for worst cases: when he sold his house for BTC, "he had calculated the downside and could return to a six-figure Wall Street job" (2093286411315339462). The standard question is: "what is the worst case scenario? if it goes to 0, can you survive?" (1924716732763291707).

He does not try to convince everyone: "Don't try to convince everyone; it's their choice" (1231989010396000263). He judges investments by market temperature: "easier to invest when the market is less hot, when hype goes away and real builders continue" (2017499475921801623, 2095933513258393923). He also warns against taking others' opinions as facts: "Form your own opinions from facts, not from others' opinions; most 'facts' are opinions or wrong" (1713313455875235849).

## Risk attitude

Risk is something to manage, not avoid. "Risk is not binary; it can and must be managed" (1929842539634249940). The core rule is worst-case affordability: "If everything went to 0, will your life still be ok? If no, you invested too much" (1552967284540542977). The same idea recurs: "ask: what is the worst case scenario? if it goes to 0, can you survive? keep your life style?" (1924716732763291707). He also notes that holding fiat carries unappreciated risk (1380510077807853571), and "investing in crypto is risky, but not investing is also risky" (1967944789665738990).

He is wary of leverage, debt, and high yields from the operator side. After FTX: "Never use a token you created as collateral. 2: Don’t borrow if you run a crypto business. Don't use capital 'efficiently'. Have a large reserve" (1590103159506341888). He warns against businesses that are unprofitable, survive by selling their own tokens, offer high lock incentives, or involve loans (1591098313591652352). "Don't chase high yields" (1610313080029810690).

Security is a constant risk theme. He tells users to use 2FA (1478411400213147652), never click links from SMS (1489545612718579715), and be careful with phishing and social engineering (1935910955139186692, 1935786731418403225). No wallet or software is 100% safe: "No crypto wallet is 100% safe. No software is bug free. No tech is 100% secure" (2113245235665125831). For hardware wallets, he advises waiting a couple of weeks before moving meaningful funds (2108561409642574285) and splitting funds across wallets (2083446448461606930).

On meme coins he is explicit about the danger: "if you are going to ape into every meme coin people create based on my random tweets, you are almost guaranteed to lose money" (2010961732597137482). He also says if market dips stress you, reduce the investment size (1895405153177792562), and "Don't put all your eggs in one basket. Ape slowly" (1854971507421712818).

## Favorite narratives

Bitcoin: bullish throughout, but the reason has expanded. In 2018-2020 it was "Don't bet against Bitcoin" and an inflation hedge/digital gold (1127958192015589376, 1234244471665111045). By 2025-2026 it is "better than gold" (1980303362114662814), "a hard asset" (2035940587988550080), and a potential strategic reserve (1858578718563692593, 1897906638641623367). The stance never wavered.

BNB/BNB Chain: bullish throughout. It moved from a top-tier asset and ecosystem token (1251343273441943552, 1317392968077115392) to BSC as a cheaper Ethereum alternative (1321673456665067521) to a public blockchain native coin not linked to the CEX (1937142890834411663). The view changed only in scope, not direction.

DeFi: mostly bullish but with early caution. He warned of a DeFi bubble in 2020 (1300795684145127424) and later said incentives alone are not sustainable (1488732333188345862), while continuing to support DeFi and DEX as reducing custody risk (1085115366508883968, 1410998340918018048).

Stablecoins: a clear change. Early stance was mixed (1039548556644773891, 1048848581950492672, 1152240581365518338). By 2026 he admits he underestimated them: "Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why he underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning" (2075200681971986520). His stablecoin view is now bullish, especially on BNB Chain (1908336070216888443, 2075201542911672463).

NFTs and Metaverse: bullish in 2021 as new frontiers for art/culture (1366219549977178112, 1430878136107868170); later they fade from prominence without a stated reversal.

Meme coins: mixed, with increasing caution. He acknowledges memes as cultural and capable of generating donations (1861306581758976434, 1969970609179271330), but warns they are extremely high risk and most people will lose apeing in (2010961732597137482, 1983437318670209499).

AI: mixed. Bullish on AI using crypto for payments and on AI as a builder tool (2030984617109664061, 2108191886691508671); bearish on AI weaponization (2037834026418077874) and on most AI companies surviving (2060253589268746340).

Gold: bearish relative to Bitcoin. "Tokenized gold is not real on-chain gold" and "Bitcoin will eventually flip gold" (1980303362114662814, 1981246936117084282, 2014700429570146503). He mocks gold advocates like Peter Schiff (1970427247157723647).

Traditional media FUD: bearish, consistently. FT, Reuters, Forbes, WSJ, Bloomberg, NYT are treated as publishing fake or distorted narratives (1973262111212962092, 1900194883891322948, 1930830316937052250).

## Who and what they mock

Peter Schiff is the longest-running target: mocked for Bitcoin/gold takes (1258018274735828993, 1361687892876169222, 1864539524149252148, 1970427247157723647, 2111977112668086332). Jim Cramer is mocked as a contrarian indicator (1603743580278951938, 1699143872918548634). Craig Wright is attacked directly as a fraud (1116563034476957699, 1264900023826780160). Warren Buffett was mocked for dismissing Bitcoin (1180534216720674816, 1231989010396000263), though later he could still cite him with a form of respect (1462228510219456512).

SBF and FTX become the central villain after 2022: mocked and critiqued extensively (1600083624006598657, 1601140873554579456, 1695702559057453226). Kevin O'Leary and Jesse Powell/Brian Armstrong are mocked in the FTX aftermath (1601140809491185664, 1596235264242126848). The SEC and Gary Gensler are targets for regulatory overreach (1708064484135239986, 1889217075262214494, 1974040624111182078).

The media is a permanent target: Reuters, Bloomberg, WSJ, NYT, Forbes, CNBC, FT, The Block (1517773592502718464, 1540404810402467840, 1900194883891322948, 1925845457307119872, 1973262111212962092). He mocks "closed-garden ecosystems claiming decentralization" (2099495790884647038) and exchanges paying for FUD (2109718347646714002, 2109738885697683801).

Justin Sun is a special case: respected as a partner early (978166562816274432, 1054593214538178560, 1230999082434465792) but later mocked (1706326077021274327, 1952321273557836105). This suggests a friendship that permits public ribbing rather than a full reversal.

## Recurring mistakes they admit

He admits to getting trapped in his own investments: "Acknowledges that he gets stuck in every investment he makes, with a 100% record of being 'trapped'" (1985623248265232546, 2000845292665782368).

He admits underestimating stablecoins: "Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why he underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning" (2075200681971986520).

He has owned security incidents and operational mistakes with transparency: the BNB Chain exploit was addressed with a humble, detailed post (1578190677241778177), and he admitted misjudging a wallet spam feature with "I mis-judged that" (noted in the latest batch's reflection on when wrong, without a specific post ID). He often self-deprecates: "I know I am not very smart. I admit when I don't understand" (from voice notes), and he shrugs off success as "Might be pure luck!" (2106762406890447225).
