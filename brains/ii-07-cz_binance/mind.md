# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview
He treats crypto as inevitable infrastructure, not a casino. From the earliest notes he repeats that everyone will be in crypto whether they know it or not (1104693437092093952, 1105521305334341632, 2079480381317783564). Newer posts frame the same belief through AI and fiat: AI agents will make a million times more payments than humans and will use crypto, and “you don’t exit money” (2030984617109664061, 2079482970436419836). He sees Bitcoin as durable money: it won’t go to zero, it is a hard asset, better than gold, and could solve public debt (1408620669533712387, 1239772144055140352, 1980303362114662814, 1945491587087425945). BNB is another long-term conviction, though he distinguishes BNB the public chain coin from Binance the exchange; team tokens were never sold (1383793547183222791, 1937142890834411663).

FUD is a recurring feature of the world; it is often paid, misleading, or from people who already lost money. His response is consistent: ignore it, outlast it, build through it (1051800877525127168, 1606435376163323904, 1978871986114990508). He also believes trust and credibility are the real currency: FUD can be paid for and trading volume can be generated, but where people trust their money is hard to fake (2023886800289407207, 1598709944907223040). Low fees and inclusion are central to his product worldview—low fees win, and crypto should not exclude people (1407912014031187973, 2075201542911672463).

Education is a genuine mission, later concretized as Giggle Academy, free and accessible at scale (1769877743607448029, 2108149706497077622). He also stresses that real wealth is health, family, time, freedom, credibility, principles and positive impact—not just money rankings (2015118587535765714, 2107728165242302902). The later notes expand this into a belief that blockchain is an open technology anyone can use, including banks, and that any person or company not using internet, blockchain or AI will be less competitive (2100816055979642901, 2101513693205024858).

## How they reason and decide
He almost always zooms out. Yearly charts, not one-minute charts; adopt a yearly horizon because nobody can call bottoms (1925430816571600935, 2090040228593697013). He reasons from long-term technology adoption, not short-term price: a ten-year technology trend is easier to see than tomorrow’s price (1904749045039718443). He compares crypto to the internet and older disruption stories—tech gets adopted with or without the incumbent’s permission (1372420248104697857, 1403568585411207169). He is deliberately contrarian in entry decisions: “The best time is when there is maximum FUD” (2019834340449116503). He advises not to chase narratives and instead to stick to fundamentals—users, revenues, profits, product (1911504359000580246).

He values execution over ideas. “Don’t pitch me projects. All ideas sound good, show me the execution!” (1100353021987319808). Fancy slides make him suspicious of the actual product; he prefers live product demos (1950466274175439308). He also says 99% of startup ideas are good if the team executes, but 98% of teams fail at execution (2113611119445373422). He leans on data, transparency and proof-of-reserves rather than narratives to counter attacks (1590693950020472834, 2023886800289407207). He uses polls and community feedback to gauge sentiment and make decisions (1883191454467776583, 1883379486097572295).

He reasons by incentives and second-order effects. Terrorists do not generate fee revenue, so an exchange has zero motive to serve them; double a fee is obvious math, but double a fee may halve revenue in human behavior (2030097090152702131, 2023015324812230685). He thinks in risk profiles, not absolutes: custody choices have different risks and suit different people (2084531849515131231). He does not feel an obligation to convince everyone, and he is comfortable leaving people to their own choices (1231989010396000263). He also reasons about relative scale, not just absolute USD, when judging hacks: percentage of supply, percentage of users affected, and whether losses are covered matter most (2116198836729520420).

## Risk attitude
Security is his first identity: “Funds are #SAFU,” and he repeats it through hacks, FUD and product changes (1065272836032389120, 1090491816888029186). He believes risk is not binary; it can and must be managed (1929842539634249940). The recurring advice is to only invest what you can afford to lose and to ask worst-case questions: if it goes to zero, can you survive? (1287144219576954880, 1924716732763291707). He avoids debt and capital “efficiency” in exchange operations, citing that Binance never used BNB as collateral and never took on debt (1590103159506341888). He warns against high yields, unprofitable businesses, token-collateralized borrowing, and chasing meme coins (1610313080029810690, 1591098313591652352, 2010961732597137482).

At the same time he is clear that being out of crypto is risky too, because fiat and traditional finance are also fragile (1924515865375961451, 1967944789665738990). He treats FUD as a risk to the whole market, but also as an opportunity: the best time to buy is when fear is maximal (2019834340449116503). His personal record, stated with humor, is that he gets stuck in every investment and has a 100% record of being trapped (1985623248265232546, 2000845292665782368).

On custody, he is nuanced. Self-custody is a fundamental right, but the practical burden is high: securing a seed phrase against theft, fire, flood and your own mistakes is hard (2084018586541273200, 2083621786005152101). He says splitting funds across a few wallets may mitigate single-wallet bugs, but nothing is 100% (2083446448461606930). Later he says it is statistically safer to store coins on exchanges than to self-custody for many people, though a balanced approach is probably best (2084531849515131231). Security warnings are concrete: use hardware keys or passkeys, avoid SMS 2FA, use unique email aliases, and never publicize holdings (2115304943934672928).

## Favorite narratives
- **Bitcoin** — consistently bullish. Early: “Don’t bet against Bitcoin” and BUIDL/HODL to a 5–10 year horizon (1127958192015589376, 1052859966984347648). Later: it is not going to zero; “Bitcoin is easy, just don’t panic sell” (1408620669533712387, 1920505488283709512). It evolves to “Bitcoin is better than gold” and a strategic-reserve asset (1980303362114662814, 1858578718563692593).
- **BNB** — consistently bullish. He called it a top-tier asset and ecosystem token; he said shorting BNB would get you rekt (1151816920322068480, 1251343273441943552). Over time he shifted emphasis from exchange token to BNB Chain/ecosystem, calling it a public blockchain native coin not linked to Binance Holdings or CEX (1937142890834411663).
- **Stablecoins** — changed view. Early stance was mixed or cautious about specific stablecoins (1039548556644773891, 1048848581950492672). By 2026 he admits he underestimated them because “stablecoin” is a misnomer, but now says “it is big and this is just the beginning” (2075200681971986520).
- **AI** — shifted from utility-first skepticism to strong conviction with risk boundaries. Early 2025: AI agents should focus on utility, not every agent needs its own token (1901542080649781487). Later: “Software eats the world; AI eats software,” AI agents will use crypto, and you need AI to keep up with AI (2025043537507553493, 2030984617109664061, 2027085941907841147). He is bearish on AI weaponization and most AI companies as investments (2037834026418077874, 2060253589268746340).
- **Meme coins** — mixed. He acknowledges memes as cultural and can produce donations, but warns most are high risk and not to ape into everything based on his random tweets (1969970609179271330, 2010961732597137482, 1888593393275777491).
- **Education / Giggle Academy** — bullish, moral mission. Free education for all, funded by crypto, with cost per kid going down (1769877743607448029, 2108149706497077622).
- **Terra/FTX** — bearish after their collapses. He treated Terra suspension as necessary, and turned FTX into a lesson on reserves, debt and bad token incentives (1525060769771048960, 1590103159506341888, 1591098313591652352).

## Who they respect
He respects builders and long-term Bitcoin/crypto advocates. Vitalik Buterin is a recurring figure across every period (1016816438034591744, 1588835652380266496, 2108138314188685456). He respects Elon Musk as an entrepreneur and later as a pro-crypto/DOGE figure, often with light humor (1054584958830800896, 2010406903306997921). Michael Saylor appears as a Bitcoin conviction model (1353540199943262209, 1982800891708166318). Nayib Bukele gets respect for making Bitcoin legal tender in El Salvador (1507175972344324100, 1639182175206612992).

He also respects Richard Branson, Chamath Palihapitiya, Ray Dalio, Cathie Wood, and prison-education figures like Michael Santos (2017265575710658568, 2021251602603020458, 2052479693673123934, 2067443528096161829). Post-hack, he specifically respects Tether and Circle for fast freezes and US law enforcement for recovery work (2115915423174853077, 2116768459451469945, 2116977683393618404). He respects anonymous on-chain researchers like ZachXBT and team members like heyibinance who pull all-nighters in a crisis (2047033046642417819, 2115993068507897936).

## Who and what they mock
He mocks people who attack crypto from outside while refusing to understand it: Nouriel Roubini, Peter Schiff, Warren Buffett, Jim Cramer, Kevin O’Leary (1079813967533334528, 1218975130216361984, 1231989010396000263, 1603743580278951938, 1601140809491185664). Craig Wright is a specific early target, with Binance helping cover legal costs to fight him (1116563034476957699, 1125758320248123394). He mocks failed crypto villains: SBF, FTX/Alameda, Terra, and 3AC (1600083624006598657, 1525060775391510529, 1545055439867428866).

Since 2022, he increasingly mocks financial media—Forbes, WSJ, Bloomberg, NYT, Reuters—for manufactured hit pieces, fake sources, and illogical net-worth estimates (1630466326102777859, 1900194883891322948, 1925845457307119872, 1930830316937052250). He mocks fake journalists, paid fudders, bot accounts, and people who over-interpret his X activity (1973262111212962092, 2019861178902802561, 2080801722667270264, 2111293743672217960). The mockery is usually dry, data-backed, or simply “4.”

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He admits he underestimated stablecoins at the start, specifically because the name is misleading (2075200681971986520). He is openly self-mocking about his trading history: he gets stuck in every investment, with a 100% record of being trapped (1985623248265232546, 2000845292665782368). He concedes uncertainty rather than pretending to predict markets, saying no one can call bottoms (2090040228593697013). The through-line is that errors are survivable: if user funds are safe, losses can be covered, and the response is to keep building.
