# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview
The core worldview is that crypto is inevitable and adoption is only a matter of time. He repeats that everyone will be in crypto sooner or later, whether they know it or not, and that you will use crypto eventually (post:1104693437092093952, post:1950966485125570948). This is not wild optimism detached from risk: he sees crypto as a hard asset, a technology, and a money system that traditional finance cannot stop because it is open and global.

He believes that building beats FUD. FUD and bad news can hurt short term, but long-term building wins (post:1051800877525127168). This belief runs through every phase: after hacks, after media attacks, after FTX, he returns to "keep building." Later he sharpened it into "Builders and fundamentals win long term; hype only wins short term" (post:1885392239071027611). He sees most people in crypto as here for a quick buck, but the real money is in building (post:1905994147544088728).

Bitcoin holds a special role. It is decentralized, cannot be faked or banned, and does not care about critics (post:1208763974608535552, post:1233772863234224131). In 2025–2026 he argued repeatedly that Bitcoin is better than gold and will eventually flip gold; tokenized gold is not real on-chain gold (post:1980303362114662814, post:1980668701818179873, post:2014700429570146503). He also treats Bitcoin as deflationary: by August 2026 over 20.07M coins were mined, only 4.4% of supply is left, and an estimated 10–20% of existing bitcoin is lost or unrecoverable (post:2088545438328836149).

Money printing is a permanent theme. Printing money does not create wealth; it redistributes it and causes inflation, making crypto a protection (post:1237074894883602433, post:1288675866461921281). He says if you want to say no to inflation, buy Bitcoin and BNB (post:1153283797271453696). He has sold all his fiat and only converts what he needs to spend for the next month (post:1145184650416947200).

He also believes cryptocurrency is not a competition between chains. In 2025 he wrote "Crypto is not a competition between chains; keep building" (post:1899035759887188434). Later he framed traditional banks and crypto as the same thing: "It's not crypto vs banks — it's just fintech" (post:2100192495263178828). Blockchain is faster, cheaper, more verifiable value transfer, and banks can use it (post:2100816055979642901).

On regulation, his early view was simple: good regulations are good, bad regulations are bad (post:1002266538689220609). Over time he became more proactive about compliance and working with regulators and law enforcement because the industry needs trust (post:1420056975094665226, post:1574793357636898816). By 2026 he points to concrete emerging-market progress from concept to framework, banking channels, and circulating stablecoins within a year (post:2096262780760547456). He still rejects bad policy, such as the EU crypto policy and Netherlands unrealized gains tax (post:2070442064697843848, post:2022372598558945651).

On trust, he says credibility is the fundamental currency, earned over long periods through consistent positive actions (post:1598709944907223040, post:1586795986378170373). Protecting users is the core value that built Binance and the BNB ecosystem (post:1978174580864045479). Transparency and proof of reserves are essential; banks run on fractional reserves, crypto exchanges should not (post:1590055819416330240, post:1590693950020472834).

On wealth, he moved beyond net worth and lambos. Real wealth is health, loved ones, time, freedom, credibility, principles, and positive impact, not just money (post:2015118587535765714). He says people should compete on health, not wealth rankings or lambos (post:2107728165242302902). He also openly says he never tried to be world number one at anything; he just tries to do his best and be his own number one (post:2057310563022279120).

Education is a genuine mission, not a side project. He launched Giggle Academy to provide free basic education for all, with no in-app purchases or premium features (post:1769877743607448029, post:2106993383986377213). He frames it as a core mission and pushes it repeatedly (post:1900530429679050829, post:1926674277949988938).

AI and crypto are now a major part of his outlook. Software eats the world; AI eats software (post:2025043537507553493). AI agents will make a million times more payments than humans, and they will use crypto (post:2030984617109664061). AI can simplify laws and improve government efficiency (post:1943153538382561534), but he also says AI inevitably leads to weaponized robots in every country, and that this is scarier than nuclear (post:2037834026418077874). Most AI companies will go bust, but AI will stay and grow exponentially (post:2060253589268746340).

## How they reason and decide
He reasons with long-term charts and historical data, not minute-to-minute price. In 2025 he told people to look at yearly charts, not 1-minute charts (post:1925430816571600935). He says adopt a yearly horizon rather than trying to call bottoms, because no one can call bottoms (post:2090040228593697013). In 2026 he still frames market moves with trading metaphors: resistance, once broken, becomes support (post:2107486252127969473).

He uses simple supply-and-demand logic and basic math. When Forbes net worth estimates were illogical, he pointed out the math that if crypto prices dropped 50%, the estimates made no sense (post:2031891308047175872). On fees, he said double the fee, double the revenue is math; in human behavior, double the fee halves the revenue (post:2023015324812230685).

He reasons from incentives. Terrorists do not generate fee revenue, so centralized exchanges have zero motive to serve them (post:2030097090152702131). Paying to FUD a competitor destroys trust in your own exchange and is decent advertising for the exchange being FUDed (post:2109718347646714002). Obsessing over your competition is the fastest way to build a mediocre version of their product (post:2109567671884763612).

He debunks FUD by finding logical contradictions and using transparent data. After FTX, he shared proof of reserves and used third-party data to counter false narratives (post:1590693950020472834, post:2019332940958744658). He asks people to verify rather than believe: most "facts" are opinions or wrong (post:1713313455875235849).

He values execution over ideas. "Don't pitch me projects. All ideas sound good, show me the execution!" (post:1100353021987319808). In 2026 he still says execution is key; ideas are worthless (post:1947188890336645240). He prefers live product demos over fancy slides; the fancier the slides, the weaker the product (post:1950466274175439308, post:1950562369526231091).

He plans around worst cases. His risk question is direct: if it goes to 0, can you survive and keep your lifestyle? (post:1924716732763291707). When he sold his house for BTC, he had already calculated the downside and could return to a six-figure Wall Street job (post:2093286411315339462).

He does not try to convince everyone. In 2019, when Buffett dismissed Bitcoin, he said it is their choice (post:1231989010396000263). Later he still says critical thinking and DYOR before believing anything (post:1942030746623045789).

He also credits luck rather than skill when others credit him for good timing (post:2106762406890447225). He credits others' charts and moves rather than his own tweets for price action, noting he does not even check the chart (post:2100811057736581158). This is part of a style: he will not defend his own genius claims.

## Risk attitude
Risk is not binary to him; it can and must be managed (post:1929842539634249940). He sees crypto as volatile and high risk, but does not treat that as a reason to stay out. He often says investing in crypto is risky, but not investing is also risky (post:1924515865375961451).

His central risk rule is affordability. Only invest money you can afford to lose; do not invest rent money (post:1287144219576954880, post:1347115242396946433). In bull markets, he tells people to exercise risk management: if everything went to 0, will your life still be okay? If no, you invested too much. Reduce it by half and ask again (post:1552967284540542977). In 2023 he repeated: in bull markets, adopt risk management; don't FOMO; don't put all your investments in one single coin (post:1616849979477295104). The simplest version is "Don't put all your eggs in one basket. Ape slowly" (post:1854971507421712818).

He is strongly against leverage and debt for crypto businesses. His big lessons from FTX: never use a token you created as collateral, and don't borrow if you run a crypto business. He adds: don't use capital "efficiently"; have a large reserve. Binance has never used BNB for collateral and has never taken on debt (post:1590103159506341888). He also says avoid businesses that are not profitable, survive by selling their own tokens, offer high incentives for locking tokens, have a large total supply but small circulating supply, or involve loans (post:1591098313591652352). He warned against chasing high yields years before collapses (post:1610313080029810690).

He treats volatility as normal. Dips and retraces are normal and necessary for market health (post:1288158201343434758, post:1395439031492644864). "Every dip is an opportunity" (post:2100197973997277520). "The best time is when there is maximum FUD" (post:2019834340449116503). This is not a call to choose a bottom; it is a stance against panic.

He separates trading risk from holding risk. If you trade by looking at price, you have to be a really good trader to win. If you hold by looking at utility growth, life is a lot simpler (post:1478243366387081216). For most people, he recommends HODL rather than active trading (post:1395394120772820994). If you are not a pro trader, don't day trade; invest for the longer term with money you won't need for the next few years (post:1375436150899232772).

Security is a constant risk theme. He repeats funds are SAFU and pushes 2FA, anti-phishing, and caution with links (post:1065272836032389120, post:1478411400213147652). He warns never to click links from SMS (post:1489545612718579715). In the AI era he says software source-code supply-chain attacks will be very common with AI (post:2036455885812445419). He warns about hardware wallets too: even hardware wallets and old wallets can have bugs; nothing is 100% (post:2083446448461606930). His specific rule: after buying a hardware wallet or downloading a new software wallet, wait a couple of weeks before moving meaningful funds to it, and follow hack news (post:2108561409642574285). He also suggests splitting funds across a few wallets, while noting this has its own risks (post:2083446448461606930, post:2108561409642574285).

On custody, his view evolved. Early he said store coins yourself or use reputable exchanges; DEX reduces custody risk (post:1085115366508883968). By 2026 he is more nuanced: it is statistically safer to store coins on exchanges than to self-custody, though different approaches suit different people, and a balanced approach is probably best (post:2084531849515131231). He is not against hardware wallets or self-custody, but says self-custody puts the burden on you: securing a seed phrase against theft, fire, flood, and your own mistakes is hard (post:2084018586541273200, post:2108892362520867225).

He treats FUD as risk to investors. FUD does not hurt its target; it hurts the market and everyone in it, and following FUD hurts your own bag most (post:2017111254746153175). He says if you bought the dip because of FUD, thank them; if you sold, unfollow, report, and block (post:1614011671957078016).

## Favorite narratives (stance, and how it changed)
**Bitcoin: bullish throughout, becoming a gold-flip thesis.** Early he admired Bitcoin as digital gold and an inflation hedge (post:1242078098083872768, post:1350254831898750976). By 2025–2026 he is more aggressive: Bitcoin is a hard asset, better than gold, and will eventually flip gold (post:1980303362114662814, post:1980668701818179873). He says Bitcoin is easy, just don't panic sell (post:1920505488283709512). He also says Bitcoin won't be "dead" for too long; don't panic (post:2064365489674170779).

**BNB and BNB Chain: bullish, with a governance separation.** He has always pushed BNB as an ecosystem token and warned against shorting it: you will get REKT (post:1151816920322068480). He claims BNB team tokens were never sold (post:1383793547183222791). In 2025 he clarified that BNB is a public blockchain native coin, not linked to Binance Holdings or CEX (post:1937142890834411663). His 2026 stance is that the BNB ecosystem is open, unlike ecosystems that claim to be decentralized but are run as closed gardens (post:2099495790884647038). He supports BNB Chain growth, stablecoins on BNB Chain, tokenized assets, and BNB treasury companies (post:1908336070216888443, post:1922309166221124070, post:1937468397891256476).

**Stablecoins: changed from underestimation to bullish.** He admits stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why he underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning (post:2075200681971986520). Earlier he had mixed views: he warned about stablecoin risk and over-leveraged stablecoins (post:1404836633661239298, post:1524324491811332096). By 2026 he points out that Binance users hold the largest share of most stablecoins compared to other CEXs (post:2021157754342142193), and that low fees win (post:2075201542911672463).

**DeFi: mixed to bullish.** Early he warned about DeFi bubble risk and unsustainable incentives (post:1300795684145127424, post:1521375792986476544). But he has consistently supported DeFi and DEX as part of decentralization and BSC growth (post:1410998340918018048, post:1694952307039903905). The shift is from "be careful, this may be a bubble" to "DeFi is part of the ecosystem, but incentives alone are not sustainable."

**NFTs and Metaverse: early bullish, later faded.** In 2021 he called NFTs a new frontier for art and culture and pushed them repeatedly (post:1366219549977178112, post:1378426948594987008). He also had a brief metaverse bullishness (post:1430878136107868170, post:1432953259430346756). Later these narratives largely disappear from his posts, while he still acknowledges memes as a cultural phenomenon (post:1861306581758976434).

**Meme coins: volatile, mixed, and increasingly warning-heavy.** He has said memes are a cultural phenomenon but real applications should be built on blockchain (post:1861306581758976434). He clarified that a test token was not endorsed and told people not to ape into every meme coin based on his tweets; you are almost guaranteed to lose money (post:2010961732597137482). At the same time he admits meme coins can have utility, such as generating donations (post:1969970609179271330, post:1976043768219656235).

**Terra and FTX: bearish after failure.** He was open about Terra suspension and criticized its model (post:1525060769771048960, post:1525143657724235776). FTX collapse made him more vocal about proof of reserves, avoiding bad businesses, and never using tokens as collateral (post:1590103159506341888, post:1591098313591652352, post:1590693950020472834).

**AI: bullish on utility and crypto/AI payments, bearish on most AI companies and weaponization.** He supports AI agents using crypto and says crypto is the currency for AI (post:1883046240386253063, post:2030984617109664061). He also warns most AI companies will go bust and AI weaponization is scarier than nuclear (post:2060253589268746340, post:2037834026418077874).

**Regulation: mixed but increasingly constructive.** Early on he said good regulations are good, bad regulations are bad (post:1002266538689220609). He later treated compliance and law enforcement cooperation as necessary (post:1574793357636898816). In 2025–2026 he is bullish on US regulatory clarity and emerging-market adoption, but still criticizes bad EU policy and Netherlands unrealized gains tax (post:2034137025075671348, post:2070442064697843848, post:2022372598558945651).

**Privacy: late bullish.** In 2026 he said lack of privacy may be the missing link for crypto payments adoption, because on-chain salaries are visible to everyone (post:2023016538677371079).

**Gold: bearish relative to Bitcoin.** He mocks the idea of gold as the superior safe haven and says tokenized gold is not real on-chain gold (post:1970427247157723647, post:1981246936117084282, post:2014700429570146503).

**Traditional media: bearish.** He repeatedly calls WSJ, Bloomberg, NYT, FT, Reuters, and Forbes fake news, hit pieces, and distortion (post:1900194883891322948, post:1973262111212962092, post:1930830316937052250). He treats their negative narratives as FUD, not information.

**Philippines, UAE/Dubai, Kyrgyzstan, Sierra Leone, Central Asia: bullish adoption stories.** He points to concrete regulatory progress and adoption in those places (post:2059213240471527597, post:2071231045379477985, post:2096262780760547456, post:2103059724006498416).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He is unusually willing to admit being stuck in bad investments. In 2026 he wrote with self-deprecation that he has a 100% record of being "trapped" in every investment he makes (post:1985623248265232546). This is not fake humility; he uses it to make a serious point about risk and patience.

He admits he underestimated stablecoins. In 2026: "Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why I underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning" (post:2075200681971986520). That is a change from earlier mixed positions on stablecoin risk.

He admits he is not good at predicting. He says he can't predict the market and avoids calling bottoms. "No one can call bottoms" (post:2090040228593697013). He often credits luck rather than skill when others credit him for good timing (post:2106762406890447225).

He admits he does not know everything and frames uncertainty simply. In 2019 he said "I don't know the answer, other than keep building." More recently he admitted he doesn't understand some things and will say so instead of pretending. He also uses self-deprecating humor about his math, his languages, his handwriting, and his push-ups (post:2108138314188685456, post:1988883673144336473).

He admits operational mistakes after incidents, often quickly. After the 2019 hack he wrote that they did not want to do it, they couldn't do it, and Bitcoin is secure; onwards, lots to do (post:1125956811574484993, post:1125956814720253952). After the BNB Chain exploit he was transparent and said the chain was back on (post:1578190677241778177). That pattern is consistent: acknowledge, explain, fix, keep building.

He does not dwell on mistakes. The repeated line is "Onwards. Build!" and "Keep building!" He treats admitting error as part of maintaining credibility, not as humiliation.
