# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview
The core belief from 2018 to 2026 is that crypto is inevitable and mass adoption is coming. Early posts repeat "Everyone will be in crypto" (post:1104693437092093952), "Blockchain/crypto is not going away" (post:1052859966984347648), and "Crypto is staying and will become ubiquitous; embrace it" (post:1413051680279515141). By the latest batch the line is virtually unchanged: "You will use crypto sooner or later" (post:1950966485125570948) and "Crypto is not going away; AI will need to use money and you will need money" (post:2079480381317783564). He constantly frames crypto as still tiny: "Crypto market cap is tiny compared to its potential; the technology is still early and unrealized" (post:1966389909222289514, post:2007664922159902750), and warns banks got in later than retail: "Crypto is probably the only place retail had an earlier start than the banks" (post:2021325532319842392).

Money printing is an early obsession that never fades. He argues "Printing money does not create wealth; it redistributes it and causes inflation, making crypto a protection" (post:1237074894883602433, post:1242078098083872768, post:1242351171018223620), and "Bitcoin was created in response to financial crises and will outperform fiat and stocks" (post:1234244471665111045, post:1239400904744300544). In the latest years this hardens into "Bitcoin is better than gold and will eventually flip gold" (post:1980303362114662814, post:1980668701818179873, post:2014700429570146503) and "Gold and silver can crash violently; Bitcoin is a 17-year-old technology... we are still early" (post:2017453575467897021).

FUD is both an enemy and a signal. The early stance is "FUD and negative news can hurt short-term but long-term building wins" (post:1051800877525127168, post:1066969437343932417). By 2026 he is more analytical: "FUD doesn't hurt its target; it hurts the market and everyone in it" (post:2017111254746153175) and "Winning is the best response to FUD" (post:2019320054936936581). He treats most big-media coverage as fabricated: "Traditional media (WSJ, Bloomberg, NYT) writes baseless hit pieces and fake news" (post:1900194883891322948, post:1925845457307119872, post:1973262111212962092), and tells readers "verify with official sources" (post:1978871986114990508, post:1985491888858943963).

Transparency and credibility became central after FTX. He insists "banks run on fractional reserves, crypto exchanges should not" (post:1590055819416330240, post:1590693950020472834), and "Credibility is the fundamental currency; trust is earned over long periods" (post:1598709944907223040, post:1592727073545912320). Binance pushed proof-of-reserves as a direct response (post:1590055819416330240, post:1600567141887000580).

His view on custody changed materially. The oldest note says "Store coins yourself or use reputable exchanges; DEX reduces custody risk" (post:1085115366508883968). For years he calls self-custody "a fundamental human right" (post:1591804109224550401) and "hardware wallets fundamental tools" (post:1933493977959051451). But the 2026 batches, after Coldcard and other wallet scares, flip the emphasis: "It is statistically safer to store coins on exchanges than to self-custody" (post:2084531849515131231), while still granting "self-custody puts the burden on you" (post:2084018586541273200). The final position is a balanced, risk-profile view: "different approaches suit different people and a balanced approach is probably best" (post:2084531849515131231).

Wealth is rewritten over time. Early posts joke about money; later posts are explicit: "Real wealth is not just money: health, loved ones, time, freedom, credibility, principles, positive impact matter more" (post:2015118587535765714), and "Health and family are true wealth, more than wealth rankings or lambos" (post:2107728165242302902). He also embraces anti-narrative: "We are trained to want more of everything and end up less happy" (post:2057905736836137179).

AI becomes a dominant theme. He believes "Crypto is the currency for AI; data is the food for AI and users should own their data" (post:1883046240386253063), and "AI agents will make a million times more payments than humans, and they will use crypto" (post:2030984617109664061). At the same time he is bearish on most AI companies and scared of weaponized robots: "AI inevitably leads to weaponized robots in every country... scarier than nuclear" (post:2037834026418077874), and "most AI companies will go bust" (post:2060253589268746340).

## How they reason and decide
He defaults to the longest possible horizon. "Take a 5-10 year horizon, and think about where we will be" (post:1052859966984347648). "Technology trends over 10 years are easier to see than tomorrow's price" (post:1904749045039718443). In 2026: "Look at yearly charts, not 1-minute charts" (post:1925430816571600935) and "Adopt a yearly horizon rather than trying to call bottoms. No one can call bottoms" (post:2090040228593697013).

He reasons from cycles and history, but with a caveat. He says "Learn from history; market cycles repeat, and bear markets are opportunities" (post:1417124284070125568, post:1442818323637424128, post:1537674040206405632), but also "Don't rely on it to predict the future" (post:1639547484337762304). His most repeated reasoning move is to zoom out: "Zoom out and look at long-term cycles rather than short-term price movements" (post:1620058528747753473, post:1639480860339478528, post:1648941336513458176).

He is contrarian about crowds. "Markets overreact; do the opposite of the crowd" (post:1008915844963840000, post:1029928373533986816). "Don't buy the pump and sell the dip. Maybe consider the reverse" (post:1331809933092818946). The latest version is a pristine line: "For every seller there is a buyer; a trade requires both" (post:2019139670270218581).

Execution beats ideas, always. "Don't pitch me projects. All ideas sound good, show me the execution!" (post:1100353021987319808). "Execution is key; ideas are worthless without execution" (post:1431585494152871936, post:1907667899365605418). "The fancier the slides, the weaker the product; prefer live product demos" (post:1950466274175439308). He extends this to AI: "Time-to-market is key; build fast" (post:1936045904957776210).

He leans on data and transparency to fight narrative. "Use data and transparency to counter FUD" (post:1622156770792669184, post:1641065529514369027, post:1667554966662266883). After FTX he publishes liabilities and reserves rather than argue (post:1590693950020472834). In 2026 he debunks by contradiction: "FUD can be paid for and trading volume can be generated, but which exchange people trust to store their money is hard to fake" (post:2023886800289407207).

He reasons from incentives. "Terrorists don't generate fee revenue, so CEXs have zero motive to serve them" (post:2030097090152702131). "Double the fee, double the revenue is math; in human behavior, double the fee halves the revenue" (post:2023015324812230685). "Paying to FUD a competitor destroys trust in the payer and is decent advertising for the target" (post:2109718347646714002).

He asks worst-case questions before acting. "Ask: what is the worst case scenario? If it goes to 0, can you survive?" (post:1924716732763291707). His house-to-Bitcoin story is framed this way: he calculated the downside and could return to a six-figure Wall Street job (post:2093286411315339462).

## Risk attitude
Security is the first rule. "Security is paramount; funds must be SAFU" (post:1065272836032389120, post:1090491816888029186). His specific security advice is obsessive: use 2FA (post:1478411400213147652), never click SMS links (post:1489545612718579715), use different passwords and hardware 2FA (post:1935786731418403225, post:1923061451549442066), never give up private key recovery phrases (post:1921799301375918252), use a separate computer for wallets (post:1917835198835593293), don't install software from non-official links (post:1935910955139186692), and split funds across wallets because "Nothing is 100%" (post:2083446448461606930). His most recent wallet rule is to wait weeks before moving meaningful funds to a new wallet (post:2108561409642574285).

He preaches capital preservation. "Don't invest more than you can lose" appears across eras (post:1287144219576954880, post:1450911708411834379, post:1552967284540542977). His bull-market test is explicit: "If everything went to 0, will your life still be ok? If no, you invested too much. Reduce it by half and ask again" (post:1552967284540542977).

He warns against leverage, debt, and yield-chasing. "Never use a token you created as collateral. Don't borrow if you run a crypto business. Have a large reserve" (post:1590103159506341888). "Don't chase high yields" (post:1610313080029810690). "Avoid businesses that are not profitable, survive by selling their own tokens, or give high incentives for locking tokens" (post:1591098313591652352).

His own risk footprint is extreme but self-aware. He claims "I have sold all my fiat years ago. And only convert what fiat I need to spend for the next month" (post:1145184650416947200). Yet he jokes "I get stuck in every investment I make, 100% record of being trapped" (post:1985623248265232546, post:2000845292665782368) and warns people not to ape into meme coins from his tweets: "you are almost guaranteed to lose money" (post:2010961732597137482).

## Favorite narratives
- **Bitcoin:** Consistently bullish. Early: "Don't bet against Bitcoin" (post:1127958192015589376), "Bitcoin is decentralized and cannot be faked or banned" (post:1208763974608535552, post:1233772863234224131). Middle: "Bitcoin is now legal tender in some countries and adoption is growing" (post:1408620669533712387). Late: "Bitcoin is better than gold" and "Bitcoin won't be dead for long" (post:2064365489674170779).
- **BNB:** Consistently bullish, from "Don't bet against B..." (post:1127958192015589376) to "Buy Bitcoin and BNB to say no to inflation" (post:1153283797271453696) to "BNB is a public blockchain native coin, not linked to Binance Holdings or CEX" (post:1937142890834411663).
- **BNB Smart Chain / BNB Chain:** Bullish throughout. Early as "faster, cheaper alternative to Ethereum" (post:1321673456665067521, post:1346000721313861632). Late as "an open ecosystem, in contrast to closed gardens claiming decentralization" (post:2099495790884647038, post:2112161808127561780).
- **Stablecoins:** Changed from mixed to strong bullish. Early notes are cautious (post:1039548556644773891, post:1404836633661239298). By 2026 he admits "Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why I underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning" (post:2075200681971986520). "Low fees win" (post:2075201542911672463).
- **Self-custody vs exchange custody:** Changed. Early pro-DEX/self-custody (post:1085115366508883968), then as "fundamental human right" (post:1591804109224550401), then late nuanced reversal: "statistically safer to store coins on exchanges... balanced approach is probably best" (post:2084531849515131231).
- **Traditional media / FUD:** Bearish throughout, but the target sharpens from "CNBC" and "The Block" to "FT/Reuters/Forbes" (post:1973262111212962092, post:1630466326102777859). He calls it "paid advertising or manipulation" (post:1985491888858943963).
- **AI:** Mixed-to-bullish on crypto-AI intersection, bearish on most AI companies (post:2060253589268746340). "AI is great but does not protect you against inflation; Bitcoin does" (post:2077659139854004689).
- **Meme coins:** Mixed. He sees them as cultural but risky: "Memes are a cultural phenomenon but real applications should be built on blockchain" (post:1888593393275777491). By 2026: "Meme coins can have utility, e.g., generating donations, though most are high risk" (post:1976043768219656235, post:2010961732597137482).
- **Gold:** Bearish. "Tokenized gold is not real on-chain gold" (post:1981246936117084282) and "Gold and silver... can crash violently" (post:2017453575467897021).
