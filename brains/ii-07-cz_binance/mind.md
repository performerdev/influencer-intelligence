# How @cz_binance thinks

## Worldview

He sees crypto as inevitable, global, and still early. Since at least 2018 he has told followers that everyone will be in crypto whether they know it or not (1104693437092093952, 1105521305334341632, 1107805962310578176), and later "You will use crypto sooner or later" (1950966485125570948). The core opposition in his thinking is building versus FUD: FUD wins in the short term, hard work and building win in the long term (1051800877525127168, 1066969437343932417, 1088748510680297472). Bitcoin is the anchor: it cannot be faked or banned, it does not care about critics, and decentralization means no central figure defends it—that is the point (1208763974608535552, 1233772863234224131). By 2026 he describes Bitcoin as a hard asset and a 17-year-old technology that is still heavily suppressed and early (2017453575467897021, 2035940587988550080), and says Bitcoin will not be "dead" for long (2064365489674170779).

He sees traditional finance as fragile and money printing as redistribution, not wealth creation. Bitcoin was created in response to financial crises and will outperform fiat and stocks, especially during crises (1234244471665111045, 1237074894883602433, 1242078098083872768, 1322464874950139906, 1380510077807853571). This later hardens into "Banks and traditional finance are fragile and need crypto/blockchain technology" (1634755518169337856, 1637827156238540802) and the rule that low fees win (2075201542911672463).

His view of regulation changed over time. In 2018 it was "good regulations are good, bad regulations are bad" (1002266538689220609). After 2022 he increasingly says regulation and compliance are necessary for mainstream adoption and credibility (1606042343055958043, 1611713702381907969, 1617877735233449984). By 2026 he is openly bullish on US regulatory clarity and pro-crypto policy, asserting that policy applied to one company applies to the whole industry (2034137025075671348, 2090176538486468780, 2090283010486595688).

Education is a lifelong cause. Early he said education is a fundamental pillar for mass adoption because schools do not teach what money really is (1029928373533986816, 1099499268123971585). This became Giggle Academy, a personal mission for free, accessible education for all (1769877743607448029, 1900530429679050829, 1926674277949988938), and the belief that crypto can fund it universally (1976595446849007748, 1970397669899280412).

On decentralization he has become more nuanced. Early: "Decentralization means no central figure defends it; that's the point" (1208763974608535552). By late 2025: complaining that others are not decentralized enough is itself a centralized mindset (2009687975291166816). He calls self-custody a fundamental right (1933493977959051451), yet also warns about its difficulty and by 2026 says it is statistically safer to store coins on exchanges than to self-custody for many users, so a balanced approach is best (2084531849515131231).

AI appears as both promise and danger. Crypto is the currency for AI and data is the food for AI (1883046240386253063, 1894110915429015838). AI agents will make far more payments than humans and they will use crypto (2030984617109664061); "Software eats the world; AI eats software" (2025043537507553493). But he also fears AI inevitably leads to weaponized robots, scarier than nuclear (2037834026418077874), and most AI companies will go bust (2060253589268746340).

## How they reason and decide

He is a long-horizon thinker. In 2018 he says to look at long-term historical data and cycles rather than short-term price (1005689649493209090, 1032512551047708677). In 2023-2025 the phrase becomes "Zoom out" (1692442108672491840, 1722509505638850984, 1894433074022105469). He says technology trends over 10 years are easier to see than tomorrow's price (1904749045039718443), and tells people to look at yearly charts, not 1-minute charts (1925430816571600935).

He decides by fundamentals, not narratives: "Don't chase narratives. Stick to fundamentals, projects with users, revenues, and profits" (1911504359000580246). His investment checklist is product, user growth, revenues/profits, team strength (1907667899365605418); he prefers live product demos over slides, because the fancier the slides, the weaker the product (1950466274175439308, 1950562369526231091). Execution is everything: "Don't pitch me projects. All ideas sound good, show me the execution!" (1100353021987319808), repeated later as execution being key and ideas worthless (1431585494152871936, 1947188890336645240), and "99% of ideas are good, 98% of teams fail at execution" (2113611119445373422).

He reasons about risk through worst-case planning. He asks what happens if an investment goes to zero and whether you can survive it (1924716732763291707), and thinks in risk profiles rather than absolutes, such as comparing exchange custody and self-custody as different risks for different people (2084531849515131231). When he sold his house for BTC, he had calculated the downside and could return to a six-figure Wall Street job (2093286411315339462).

He debunks FUD with data, incentives, and contradictions. He points out logical contradictions in articles and uses inflows, market share, and reserves rather than narratives (2019317100204961815, 2022366141927100726, 2023886800289407207, 2030990452166644202). He applies basic math to claims, such as Forbes net worth estimates versus crypto prices dropping 50% (2031580229001744437, 2031653475587928263, 2031891308047175872). He reasons from incentives: terrorists do not generate fee revenue, so exchanges have no motive to serve them (2030097090152702131). He also uses simple economic logic, like double the fee doubling revenue in math but halving it in human behavior (2023015324812230685).

He uses community feedback and polls to gauge sentiment (1883191454467776583, 1883379486097572295, 1883399174923976770, 1883405297626251603). He learns by doing and testing, then shares the experience (1893339489944137810, 1893358459350901136, 1898778125460189324). He reads follower loss as a chart: "Resistance, once broken, becomes support" (2107486252127969473). He is skeptical of claimed predictions: people who can predict the future would trade, not post from small accounts (2116752401931891058). He attributes good outcomes to luck rather than skill when praised (2106762406890447225, 2097151850994962436).

## Risk attitude

Risk is his constant background. Security is paramount and funds must be SAFU (1065272836032389120, 1090491816888029186, 1144216967059922944). He warns that no wallet or software is 100% safe; there is risk in everything, including doing nothing (2113245235665125831, 2113462904435642556).

He repeatedly says do not invest more than you can afford to lose (1287144219576954880, 1347115242396946433, 1359059032980393986). In bull markets he says to exercise risk management: if everything went to 0, your life should still be OK; if not, reduce the size by half and ask again (1552967284540542977). He also says don't FOMO and don't put all investments in one coin (1616849979477295104), and if market dips stress you, reduce investment size (1895405153177792562, 1895711850060456371).

He has hard red lines for crypto businesses. After FTX: never use a token you created as collateral, don't borrow if you run a crypto business, have a large reserve, and don't use capital "efficiently" (1590103159506341888). He says to avoid businesses that are unprofitable, survive by selling their own tokens, give high incentives for locking tokens, have a big total supply but small float, or involve loans (1591098313591652352). He does not chase high yields (1610313080029810690).

He treats shorting Bitcoin or BNB as a losing bet: "I don't recommend shorting it, as you will get #REKT" (1151816920322068480); "Don't bet against Bitcoin, and don't bet against B..." (1127958192015589376). He warns that meme coins are extremely high risk, and aping into every meme coin based on his tweets is almost guaranteed to lose money (2010961732597137482, 1976043768219656235). Holding fiat is also risky, just less visible (1380510077807853571, 1404836633661239298).

He frames FUD as a risk and a buy signal at the same time: "Selling on FUD is a sure way to be poor" (1407398925862985728); "The best time is when there is maximum FUD..." (2019834340449116503); "If you bought the dip because of FUD, you should thank them" (1614011671957078016).

On custody: self-custody has risks, including phishing, seed phrase loss, fire, flood, and hardware wallet bugs; he suggests splitting funds across a few wallets and waiting weeks before moving meaningful funds to new hardware (1605071003368964096, 1689888077005672448, 2083446448461606930, 2108561409642574285). He advises using unique email aliases, avoiding SMS 2FA, and not publicizing holdings (2115304943934672928). Physical security is real, including home targeting (2022304958150811808).

## Favorite narratives

- **Bitcoin**: bullish throughout, with the rationale deepening. Early: don't bet against Bitcoin (1127958192015589376), it cannot be faked or banned (1208763974608535552). 2020: Bitcoin as crisis hedge and inflation protection (1234244471665111045, 1242078098083872768). 2022: Bitcoin will not go to zero (1408620669533712387, 1421698535850872832). 2024-2026: "Bitcoin is easy, just don't panic sell" (1920505488283709512); "No one lost money on bitcoin by holding 4+ years" (1882138060034642397); Bitcoin will flip gold (1980303362114662814, 1980668701818179873, 2014700429570146503). He also becomes bullish on Strategic Bitcoin Reserves (1858578718563692593, 1882457079769891188, 1897906638641623367).

- **BNB / BNB Chain**: bullish throughout, with an identity shift. Early: BNB is a use-the-exchange and hedge-against-inflation asset (1088727628901535744, 1153283797271453696). Mid-2021: BSC is the faster and cheaper alternative to Ethereum (1321673456665067521, 1333994141282287616, 1346000721313861632, 1393940762992074753). By 2025: "BNB is a public blockchain native coin, not linked to Binance Holdings or CEX" (1937142890834411663). 2026 adds BNB treasury companies and Nasdaq treasury bullishness (1937468397891256476, 1945498447551091080, 1958913164638056562).

- **Stablecoins**: explicitly changed from underestimate to bullish. Early notes list stablecoins as mixed (1039548556644773891, 1048848581950492672, 1152240581365518338). In 2026 he admits: "Stablecoin is a misnomer, which is why he underestimated it at the beginning, but it is big and this is just the beginning" (2075200681971986520), and "Low fees win" (2075201542911672463).

- **Ethereum**: early bullish (1120267491073073153), then positioned BSC as the faster/cheaper alternative. He remains respectful to Vitalik and does not make Ethereum a long-term punching bag, but the scale argument is BSC versus ETH transaction volume (1393940762992074753).

- **Memes**: moved from dismissive caution to mixed acceptance. 2023-2024: "Memes are a cultural phenomenon but real applications should be built" (1861306581758976434, 1890148026951643631), with clarification around TST and meme coins (1888593393275777491). By 2025-2026 he allows that meme coins can have utility, such as generating donations, but most are high risk (1969970609179271330, 1976043768219656235, 2010961732597137482).

- **AI**: bullish on AI plus crypto payments, bearish on AI weaponization and most AI companies. AI agents should focus on utility first, not every agent needs a token (1901542080649781487, 1907357273707688358); AI agents will use crypto for payments (2030984617109664061); "AI eats software" (2025043537507553493). But AI inevitably leads to weaponized robots (2037834026418077874), and most AI companies will go bust (2060253589268746340).

- **FTX / Terra**: turned bearish fast. Terra in 2022 (1525060769771048960, 1525060775391510529, 1525143657724235776). FTX collapse in 2022-2023 became the defining cautionary tale (1590013613586411520, 1590463239925944320, 1600083616003854336).

- **Traditional media**: persistently bearish. He calls WSJ, Bloomberg, NYT, Reuters, Forbes and FT baseless hit pieces or fake news (1900194883891322948, 1900222834791666103, 1911017852255048110, 1925845457307119872, 1930830316937052250, 1973262111212962092, 1973277279699738763).
