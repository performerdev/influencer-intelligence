# How @blknoiz06 thinks

## Worldview
He treats crypto as a generational escape hatch from a broken fiat system and traditional gatekeepers. Early on he wrote that capitalism is structurally bad for people without privilege (872008521885396992) and later hammered the Fed for printing money and buying corporate debt while leaving consumers behind (1241944033632620544, 1248263939068170241). By 2026 this had hardened into a core belief: more money will live on blockchains than in banks before 2040 (1859194463588929607), and the dollar is being devalued so aggressively that ordinary people are forced to speculate just to keep up (1954298796847825156, 1930320665456062547). He calls crypto the only market where small capital can become millions quickly if you have skill, which is also why it is full of degens and scammers (2077870417486700999, 2077892426992136278).

He does not separate markets from attention. He often says crypto trades heavily off narratives, mindshare, and community rather than clean fundamentals (1743056312613687439). His clearest statement of this: “fundamentals are a meme” because purest value comes from community and collective belief (1446969044641452032, 1446967755312349187). Later he refined it into two kinds of crypto fundamental analysis: technical merit and the likelihood something pumps, with “meme-ability, community, narrative” often deciding the latter (1763709541261484302, 1763707944682840220). By 2026 he is explicit that attention frontruns value (2075363288594985140, 2077773554033037509).

Solana is the longest-running serious conviction. In 2021 he was already calling Solana undervalued and arguing it would outperform Ethereum on user experience and speed (1372539997262389249, 1380905704064696322). By 2023-2024 this became maximal: Solana is the mispriced asset of the cycle, the best venue for onchain users, and the SOL/ETH pair is the trade (1683915979485503498, 1704610441429729350). He consistently warred with Ethereum maxis, arguing ETH fees are not a feature and modular fragmentation spreads value away from ETH (1724482705939689476, 1788161996711399831). At the same time he rejects being a pure maximalist because a trader should never become one (2101894967710937558).

Memecoins and social tokens are not jokes to him. He calls Bitcoin the first memecoin and says memecoins financialize internet culture and viral attention (1783491024524083342, 1842095971607445635). He defends pump.fun and permissionless launches as one of the most consistent businesses in crypto (2079362694222618888, 2087335011804487872). The next step in his view is social trading and tokenized attention: traders become the next generation of creators, and crypto social meets finance (2086584870642528603, 2093118390185431348).

He is political only when it touches crypto. He said he was voting red because Republicans in Congress were clearly better on crypto legislation, even if he had no illusions about Trump knowing crypto deeply (1829212882396962828, 1829226729434689665). He believes stablecoins extend US dollar dominance and a pro-crypto US admin is a structural tailwind (1879294791135138028, 2090592377996026005). He also frames crypto as financial sovereignty for Black people who face discrimination in traditional finance (1476687726980251658).

On personal life he is louder than most traders: he values friends over roles, treats cooking as meditation, and promotes positivity maxxing, but admits trust is hard after being burned (1178412859174330371, 1059176632353742849, 1672652044723965953, 1822012430353859013). He sees social media as both the best alpha source and a disease of validation addiction (1440390569612623879, 1828915864072016255).

## How they reason and decide
He is chart-first and pattern-first. He says technical analysis beats fundamental analysis in crypto because nobody reads whitepapers; price action, volume, support/resistance are the real edge (949503841891946496). He prefers price action over price and warns against getting attached to specific levels instead of watching structure (1371224638865473538, 1366308660993998848). His main tools are higher-timeframe structure, the 200-day EMA, and the Ichimoku cloud; he repeatedly tells traders to use the H6 cloud bear-to-bull flip for alt entries (1885799696960045214, 1360960453900447749). He also uses volume and volume profile as confirmation because “volume doesn’t lie” (953470265538818049).

He fades consensus and treats extreme sentiment as a signal. His own reputation is a contrarian indicator: bid when people call him a scammer and take profit when they call him the black Jesus Christ (1782983518659752073). More generally, best trades are when the majority disagrees at entry, and once everyone agrees you should derisk (1509950754752286726). He repeatedly warns that “everyone is bearish/bullish” is worthless without your own positions and invalidations (2111910811153105043).

He is an early-research trader. He scans for assets the crowd has not priced yet, reads docs and dev notes before CT talks about them, and tracks social/onchain signals like holder growth and volume (1475615264989401102, 1722643858427687302, 1735526103215665540). He also buys before institutions start talking and sells when they do (1989348773404029334). He says his edge is 10,000 hours navigating the internet and understanding human nature (1705412432393367836). He makes decisions in second-order terms: if ETH wins a lawsuit, that is third-order bullish for Solana (1806344838863794178).

He runs a barbell portfolio: 60-70% high-conviction, long-term spot, 30-40% degen rotations/perps/onchain risk (1757810215083282753, 2090342180938190978). He sizes by downside, not upside, and says if you have no edge the correct size is “don’t buy” (1913122044645958083). He journals trades, writes thesis and invalidation, and stresses flipping bias quickly when the market changes (2080414456136212591, 1921662836566110717). He forces himself to argue against his own positions to cover blind spots (1919275286094360699). He plans scenarios and never assigns 0% probability (1505295528766156806).

## Risk attitude
His default advice is spot, not leverage. He says every major fuck-up came from overconfidence with leverage (1462475458432352260), and that leverage makes no sense for ~90% of people because coins already 10x (1731675741949985214). He uses invalidation levels and stop loss discipline for active trades, but for high-conviction spot he holds through deep drawdowns rather than panic sell (1805102613102211218, 1887537447585591581). He explains that you cannot get liquidated in spot (2091174674566664330), and tells newer traders to avoid leverage on alts especially mid-trend (1747169912125485061).

At the same time, his risk appetite is genuinely high. He says he plays the extreme side of the risk curve and only wants 100xs (1708487108980060260). He accepts that 100x-to-1000x hunting requires sizing to survive 70% drawdowns (2098462838721589509). He has been liquidated, stopped out, and down millions in a month, and he talks about it with humor rather than hiding it (1985556224411386226, 1976786281993756895). His survival rule is the 70/30 split plus rolling degen gains into long-term bags (1824075797344215363).

He hedges instead of selling core bags. He describes holding spot in one coin and shorting another as the way to stay exposed without unloading (1708905074490036690). He also derisks near resistance with close invalidation rather than waiting until after a selloff (1813046730390188114, 1801256479174898159). He warns against hero-longing bottoms, martingaling longs without trailing stops, and revenge trading after liquidation (1805102613102211218, 1742587760790106362, 1742588299091284313).

Security is a real risk practice for him. He recommends hardware wallets, test transactions, burners for ponzis, and multiple addresses for airdrops (1687493802489962496, 1769032362401124539, 1733586429383459223). He has been impersonated and phished, so he repeatedly tells followers not to click random links (1461026175468412940).

## Favorite narratives (stance, and how it changed)
Early on he was an altcoin-cycle bull: NEO flips ETH, Skycoin is Bitcoin 2012, privacy coins like Monero and Zcash are the future (942478824368951296, 925884355989385216, 941067540113051648). He was also clearly bearish ETH: Ethereum was technically weak and its value came from ICOs on top of it (937550668553957376). This was refined but never abandoned.

In 2020-2021 he shifted into DeFi and alt L1s, especially Solana. He declared DeFi a double-bubble and alt season real (1362310422041362432, 1362129345062572045). He was early to play-to-earn, NFTs, Terra, and meme coins like Doge, though he changed tone on meme coins repeatedly: first skeptical, then “ponzis are bullish,” then fully memecoin supercycle (1446967755312349187, 1763394466876965008).

From 2023 onward the main narratives became Solana, memecoins, Coinbase, and airdrops. He argued retail was already here on Solana, not Coinbase, and that WIF/BONK were legitimate attention assets (1799626373809795391, 1735694318747234622). Later, he merged memes with social trading and tokenized attention into “memefi” (2090719481546899904). In 2025-2026 he added Hyperliquid, Zcash privacy resurgence, revenue/buyback meta, tokenized stocks/RWAs, and AI x crypto. This marks a change: from pure narrative/TA to demanding cash-flow, revenue, and trust premium for longer-term allocations (1970107553079079341, 2102395246298427480, 2077773554033037509). He also explicitly rejects the old four-year cycle frame, preferring four-month cycles and taking profits into momentum (1974156647850184863).

## Recurring mistakes they admit
He admits overconfidence with leverage is his biggest recurring failure (1462475458432352260). He says he used to hero-long bottoms and got liquidated unnecessarily (1805102613102211218). He overtraded and round-tripped gains by re-entering on bounces that looked like bull resumption; the worst ever was shorting LUNA, being right eventually but waking up down way too much (1490098060734652421, 1887898949840756920, 2080448877388238622). He repeatedly fumbled bags by selling too early, including WNXM and HOOD (1305500132125872128, 1942375800793432146). He admits being too early is expensive: the thesis can be right for years before price confirms (1962213802167968230). He also says he was wrong on celebrity coins, “maybe the most wrong” he had ever been (2075349615612703040), and that using CT sentiment to trade is a one-way ticket to poverty (1789923905961906312). His practice is to own it, joke about it, and not delete the miss: “I am wrong a fuckton bros” (1776670703422525659, 1736606007063621817).
