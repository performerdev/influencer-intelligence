# How @blknoiz06 thinks

## Worldview
He is an anti-system, pro-crypto contrarian. Before crypto took over the timeline, he wrote about capitalism being structurally bad for people without privilege (872008521885396992), state violence ending only when the system is dismantled (877274576559931392), and self-security meaning caring little what others say (873109674060505089). He trusted Black women and older Black women explicitly and kept other groups at a distance early on (872579473291579393, 873784179384885248, 875747516293292032). That same contrarian instinct became his market personality: he assumes consensus is usually wrong, retail panic is an entry, and the most hated trade is often the best one.

By late 2017, crypto became the center of his public thinking: a way out of broken fiat, a path to wealth, and a game of positioning. He wrote "if it don't make money, it don't make sense" (1053055838317895685). He sees markets as reflexive and driven by human emotion, not clean fundamentals. In 2020 he said the Fed was lying and printing trillions while monetary policy was the least-discussed story of the year (1266457173577674752). He saw asset ownership as the only protection when wages lag and the dollar is debased; by 2024 he framed the economy as forcing people to "hypergamble" to leapfrog socioeconomic tiers (1751670060001775723, 1764565397687435576).

He believes crypto is still early and information asymmetry is massive (1730098080576389193). He says crypto is one of the only places a small stack can become life-changing if you outwork the crowd, and that top participants share ideas in public (1813568069815751151, 1839329481083007000). He often says the only people who truly understand are those doing onchain work, not credentialists (1473163155039830020).

His view of his own role is part prophet, part degenerate, part coach. He says his edge is being early to things most people write off or ignore (1863084457953014202). He treats social capital and attention as tradable assets (2075363288594985140, 2095591087104487619). That is why he moved from mocking memecoins to calling them a supercycle: attention financialized by community became the core product.

## How they reason and decide
He reasons through price action first, then narrative and macro. In early notes he repeatedly says technicals front-run news and news is priced in (1138139421193228288, 1800560864853967300). He watches market structure, ranges, invalidation levels, volume, open interest, funding, and relative strength. His consistent method is to look for higher lows on alt/BTC or alt/ETH pairs after forced selling, then bid the leaders (1805317500516843973, 1827023695459627446).

He is explicit about scenarios: "when looking at a chart, always look at a bullish & a bearish scenario regardless of your position" (958173438748909570). He treats diagonal trendlines alone as fake and horizontal levels plus volume profile as real (960143109375123456). He uses the H6/H12 Ichimoku cloud flip for alt momentum, later repeating it for years (1360960453900447749, 1804577553413226709).

His most durable decision rule is contrarian: "bid when ppl are calling me a scammer and start taking profit when ppl are calling me the black jesus christ" (1782983518659752073). He fades sentiment extremes when positioning is one-sided. He buys fear and sells euphoria, and he judges a new idea by whether it makes no sense to the majority at the start (1798726157036380233).

He also front-runs narratives by reading docs, blogs, and onchain data before the crowd. He says "in crypto doing things that are annoying for alpha before the market makes it easier for everyone else usually pays for itself pretty quickly" (1479189546319454214). His conclusion is sharp: "if you can chart it you're late" (1479191847369072643).

He segments his portfolio by role: long-term conviction spot bags plus a smaller degen allocation. He wants a best long idea and best short idea at all times (1874177609090949621). He journals trades, writes theses with invalidations, and says being comfortable with being wrong is part of being profitable (1683890895735730176, 2080414456136212591).

He updates bias when price invalidates the setup. He does not assign 0% probability to anything because it leaves no gameplan (1505295528766156806). In late 2025 he warned against using "everyone" as an analytical category: "there is no 'everyone' there's your own positions and your invalidations, nothing more nothing less" (2111910811153105043).

## Risk attitude
His risk attitude changed over time. In 2017-2018 he used high leverage on BitMEX, acknowledged liquidations, and joked about 100x (923039394721861632, 962358455154696192). By 2021 he was warning new traders not to trade alts with leverage in their first cycle (1382305212816318465). By 2023-2025 he said repeatedly that spot cannot be liquidated and that leverage is the main cause of his major mistakes (1462475458432352260, 2091174674566664330).

He still takes aggressive risks, but mostly through small onchain positions. He says a 70/30 split—high-conviction spot plus degen—is the correct framework (1776671011196264929, 2112890596272792053). He accepts that memecoins and 100x runners can draw down 70-90%, but he sizes to survive the volatility (1790891965778678160, 2098462838721589509). He also says being all in spot is its own risk management: "you can't get liquidated if you're sitting in spot" (1734045147531272265).

He protects himself with invalidation levels and plans. He would rather be sidelined than force a trade: "no position is a position" (1991236649150202211). He warns against martingaling leveraged longs into pullbacks and says position sizing is determined by downside (1742587760790106362, 1913122044645958083). He also tells people to keep a separate account with no cards to avoid lifestyle inflation (1828911238140563677).
